# Private label vs. brand head-to-head

## The idea

For a set of paired products — same category, near-identical functional product, one branded and one private-label — show the cost stack side by side. Where does the price difference actually go?

Pair candidates:
- Coca-Cola vs. Costco Kirkland Signature Cola
- Kraft Mac & Cheese vs. Trader Joe's Mac & Cheese
- Tide vs. Costco Kirkland laundry detergent
- Cheerios vs. generic store-brand toasted oat cereal
- Bayer aspirin vs. CVS aspirin
- Q-tips vs. store-brand cotton swabs
- Ziploc vs. Glad vs. store-brand sandwich bags
- Heinz ketchup vs. Hunt's vs. Whole Foods 365 ketchup

## The graph

For each pair, stacked bars showing build / sell / overhead / profit per dollar of consumer spend. The branded product almost certainly has a giant "sell" slice that the private-label doesn't. The hypothesis: branded products are paying for the brand promise, not the product itself, and the gap is dramatic.

## Why it's interesting

Direct comparison nukes the "but maybe Coke is actually different" defense. If the Kirkland cola is 90% as good for 60% of the price, you can see exactly where the missing 40% went on the brand-side bar. Visceral and direct.
