# Ticket 021 — Fluoxetine (Prozac → Sarafem)

## Demon identity

Not a street drug. **Prozac** (fluoxetine HCl). FDA approved December 1987 for major depression. Eli Lilly. Cultural baggage: Prozac became *the* cultural shorthand for SSRI antidepressants in the 1990s (Peter Kramer's *Listening to Prozac*, 1993). Some controversy around suicidality / behavioral effects, but not "demonized" in any meaningful sense.

## Medicine identity

**Sarafem** (fluoxetine HCl, same molecule, repackaged in lavender-and-pink capsules). FDA approved July 2000 for premenstrual dysphoric disorder (PMDD). Eli Lilly's evergreening play, launched just as Prozac patent protection was ending (generic fluoxetine launched August 2001).

## Arc type

`failed_medicine_rebranded`. The cleanest evergreening case in the project — a same-molecule rebrand explicitly timed to patent expiry, for a closely-related indication, with a cosmetic redesign (pink/purple pills, feminine branding) to differentiate.

## Chemistry hypothesis

**Identical.** Fluoxetine HCl in both. The Sarafem capsule is literally generic fluoxetine in different-colored shells. PubChem confirms.

## Source hierarchy

1. **FDA Prozac approval (1987), Sarafem approval (2000), fluoxetine generic launch (2001).**
2. **Eli Lilly historical materials** for the Sarafem strategy.
3. **PMDD treatment guidelines** showing fluoxetine's role.
4. **Public Citizen / consumer-advocate commentary** on the Sarafem evergreening — well-documented at the time.
5. **IQVIA data** showing Sarafem's commercial performance (modest — most PMDD prescribing went to generic fluoxetine).

## Caveats

- Sarafem is one of the *worst* commercial rebrand performances in the project. The same-molecule-with-new-pink-pill strategy didn't fool prescribers; most just prescribed generic fluoxetine. Worth highlighting as a *failed evergreen*.
- The honesty section should note: Sarafem the brand essentially failed even though the rebrand-as-strategy is a textbook case.
- Awareness that Sarafem is Prozac is probably near 100% among prescribers and near 0% among general public who never heard of Sarafem.
