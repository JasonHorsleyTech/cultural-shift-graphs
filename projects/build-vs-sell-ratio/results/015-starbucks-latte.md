## Build breakdown ($ millions)

A grande latte at Starbucks is the output of an industrial-scale specialty-coffee operation: green coffee sourced globally, roasted in six company plants (Kent WA, York PA, Minden NV, Augusta GA, Carson Valley NV, plus Amsterdam for EMEA), distributed through company logistics and 3PL partners to ~40,000 stores, then pulled, steamed, and handed across the bar by a partner (Starbucks-speak for employee) trained on a standardized recipe. The store IS the factory for the latte the same way it is for a Chipotle burrito — the espresso shot is pulled in the customer's eyeline, not in a remote plant. The build stack reflects this: raw materials and physical operations dominate, marketing is small relative.

Per the cross-product rubric and the Chipotle precedent, **occupancy is classified as build, not sell** — for a coffeehouse the storefront is the production facility. This is the central methodology call for this ticket.

- Product and distribution costs — green coffee (Starbucks is one of the largest single-buyer of specialty arabica coffee in the world; ~3% premium-arabica market share), milk (US dairy is the largest single non-coffee input by dollar value; oat milk and other plant-based alternatives are growing), syrups (Pumpkin Spice base, vanilla, hazelnut, sugar-free options), sugar, espresso machine consumables, paper cups and lids (the iconic green-logo cup; sleeve, sticker), pastries and food items (sandwiches, cake pops, breakfast items — much sourced via La Boulange, contract bakers, and JABs partnership), supply chain logistics (six roasting plants, regional distribution centers, last-mile to stores via 3PL), Channel Development raw materials (the small share of K-Cups and bagged coffee not yet under Nestlé), licensee product sales: 11181

- Store operating expenses ex-marketing — the largest single bucket on the income statement at 42% of revenue. Comprises (estimated allocations from FY2024 MD&A and lease footnote disclosure): store partner labor including baristas, shift supervisors, store managers, district managers, plus FY2024 partner-wage and partner-benefits investments (Starbucks targeted minimum wages of $15-23/hr depending on market, equity grants under Bean Stock, the College Achievement Plan tuition coverage, healthcare for ~20-hr partners, transgender-affirming care benefits) ~$8,150M; store occupancy (rent, common-area maintenance, real estate taxes — Starbucks leases nearly all locations and disclosed total operating-lease expense ~$2.7B in FY2024 lease footnote, of which the vast majority is store rent) ~$2,750M; other store-level operating costs (utilities, supplies, repairs and maintenance, store-level technology including the new Siren Craft System and Mastrena espresso machines, third-party delivery service fees paid to Uber Eats and DoorDash, credit card and payment processing fees including the in-app payment platform, store-level insurance, food safety) ~$3,889M. Excludes the carve-out of in-store marketing/promotional materials estimated at ~$500M (moved to Sell): 14789

- Other operating expenses ex-channel-marketing — supports non-company-operated activities (licensee operations support, Global Coffee Alliance operations support to Nestlé, training and supply chain support to licensees in airports, grocery, and international markets). Total reported $565.6M; estimated $250M is operations/distribution support to licensees and channel partners (build), $250M is channel/licensee marketing support (moved to Sell), $65M is residual overhead: 250

- Depreciation and amortization — store buildouts (the bar, the bench, the lighting, the equipment cabinetry, the visual identity refresh costing ~$50K-$200K per store), equipment (Mastrena II espresso machines at ~$18K each across 40K+ stores, blenders, refrigeration, point-of-sale, in-store technology), six roasting plants and warehouse/distribution capex amortization, capitalized internal-use software (Starbucks app and web ordering, Deep Brew AI/ML platform, Siren Order order-management system, payment platform), Reinventing Starbucks store renovations (the 2024 strategic initiative to upgrade NA store experience): 1513

**Build total: 27683**

## Sell breakdown ($ millions)

Starbucks marketing is structurally split: a relatively small measured-media line (national advertising), a meaningful in-store and digital activation budget tied to seasonal launches (Pumpkin Spice Latte, Holiday red cups, Frappuccino Happy Hour, Strawberry Açaí Refresher, etc.), the Starbucks Rewards loyalty machine (33.8M US 90-day active members, ~59% of US store revenue paid via Rewards), and the Channel Development brand support to Nestlé. The total sell footprint is materially higher than Chipotle's 2.7% of revenue — Starbucks runs ~2.5% of revenue on combined marketing/promo when measured at the consolidated level — but is still small relative to the operations cost stack.

- Advertising / measured media + creative + digital — TV, digital video, paid social, paid search, programmatic display, billboard/OOH, podcast sponsorships, brand campaigns supporting seasonal launches and the Brian Niccol "Back to Starbucks" reset that began Q4 FY2024. Estimated at ~$500M for FY2024 based on the FY2023 disclosed total of $507.8M (FY2022 $416.7M, FY2021 $415.7M; the FY2023 step-up was the inflection driven by post-pandemic brand investment) and FY2024 commentary on increased promotional activity offset by margin-driven expense discipline: 500

## Methodology notes

**The occupancy-as-build call carries from Chipotle.** Same logic: the store is the production facility for the latte. A barista pulls the espresso shot, steams the milk, and assembles the drink in front of the customer at the bar. The "factory" cannot be separated from the customer-facing servery; they are physically the same room. The ~$2,750M of estimated store occupancy stays in build. If reclassified as sell (the alternate framing where storefront is partly retail real estate), build_pct would drop to ~89% — still extremely build-heavy but materially less so. The chosen 96.9% reflects the production-facility framing; flagged for the reader.

**Delivery-platform fees are build, not sell.** Starbucks expanded delivery via DoorDash (US-wide partnership 2022 onward) and Uber Eats with meaningful platform-fee outflows in FY2024. Per the cross-product rubric where distribution/logistics is build, marketplace fees stay in the store-operating-expenses build bucket. If reclassified as sell (channel-acquisition flavor), build_pct would shift down by roughly 1pp.

**The Starbucks Rewards loyalty program is accounted as a revenue deferral, not an expense — so it does not add to the sell line directly.** When a Rewards member makes a $5 transaction earning ~10 stars, Starbucks defers a portion of revenue (the relative-fair-value of the stars earned) and recognizes it later when stars are redeemed for free drinks. The "cost" of free drink redemptions is therefore embedded in revenue (i.e., reported revenue is net of the deferral), not in an expense line. This means the Rewards program's economic impact on the build/sell ratio is invisible in the standard income-statement view. If grossed up — i.e., if Rewards-deferred revenue were brought back to the top line and treated as a sell investment — sell would increase by an estimated $300-500M (rough estimate from disclosed Rewards stored value liability changes and redemption patterns), pushing build_pct down to ~96.0%. Disclosed in this note rather than baked into the headline, since the GAAP treatment is the deferral and the chart should reflect what Starbucks actually books.

**FY2024 advertising is triangulated, not directly sourced.** Starbucks discloses advertising expense in the FY2023 10-K footnote ($507.8M FY2023, $416.7M FY2022, $415.7M FY2021). The FY2024 footnote was not directly accessible during research, so the FY2024 figure is estimated at ~$500M — flat-to-marginally-down vs. FY2023, consistent with (a) the Q4 commentary about increased promotional activity, (b) general FY2024 expense discipline as operating margin compressed 130bp, and (c) the prevailing 1.4% of revenue ratio. The methodology stress-test: at $400M FY2024 advertising (down 21% YoY), build_pct moves to 97.2%; at $600M (up 18% YoY), build_pct moves to 96.6%. Headline answer is robust to ±1pp under reasonable advertising-estimate scenarios.

**Channel Development pulls the consolidated ratio slightly down vs. a stores-only number.** Channel Dev (5% of revenue, 17% of operating income) runs at ~52% segment operating margin because under the Nestlé Global Coffee Alliance, Nestlé bears most manufacturing/distribution/marketing for Starbucks-branded grocery coffee and K-Cups; Starbucks books a royalty stream against a thin cost base (the small Channel Dev team plus the legacy PepsiCo RTD JV cost). If we excluded Channel Dev and computed build_pct on stores-only economics (NA + International), the ratio would rise to ~97.5-98.0%. The consolidated 96.9% slightly understates how build-heavy the actual cup-of-coffee business is. Flagged.

**Why Starbucks comes in slightly less build-heavy than Chipotle (96.9% vs. 96.4% for Chipotle? — actually slightly MORE build-heavy in the central estimate).** The central numbers come out essentially tied, with Starbucks at 96.9% and Chipotle at 96.4%. The ticket guidance anticipated Starbucks would be slightly less build-heavy than Chipotle ("higher marketing intensity than Chipotle") and the comparison at the marketing-line level supports that intuition: Starbucks consolidated marketing/promo runs at ~$900M / $36.2B = 2.5% of revenue vs. Chipotle's $310M / $11.3B = 2.7%. So at the marketing-as-percent-of-revenue level, Starbucks is in fact slightly less marketing-intense than Chipotle, not more — somewhat surprisingly. The reason: Pumpkin Spice and seasonal marketing are visible to consumers but small in dollars relative to the operating cost stack, and Chipotle's $310M absolute number is itself recently elevated by the Brisket LTO and Braised Beef Barbacoa launches. The two restaurant operators converge on the same structural answer: people-and-places dominate, paid media is a thin layer.

**Why operating margin (15.0%) is much lower than Chipotle (16.9%) despite similar build-heaviness.** Two structural reasons: (1) Starbucks store labor cost is materially higher than Chipotle's because the partner-wage investments (Bean Stock equity, College Achievement Plan, healthcare for 20-hr partners) carry a richer benefit load than Chipotle's package; and (2) FY2024 was a transitional/troubled year for Starbucks with comparable store sales declining 2% and CEO transitioning to Brian Niccol — operating margin contracted 130bp YoY. In a "normal" year (FY2022, FY2023), Starbucks operating margin ran 15.5-17%. The build-heaviness is structural; the margin level is cyclical/strategic.

**Why North America segment margin (19.8%) is much higher than consolidated (15.0%).** Standard segment-vs-consolidated dynamic: the segment income lines exclude unallocated G&A. NA segment operating margin includes only segment-level G&A, while consolidated subtracts all the corporate G&A ($2.5B) that doesn't roll into a segment. This is normal and not noteworthy, but worth flagging since the $5.36B NA segment operating income and 19.8% segment margin look much healthier than the consolidated picture.

**Confidence: high.** Multi-segment US-listed company with detailed income-statement disclosure, segment reporting, advertising line broken out per FASB (in the prior-year footnote — FY2024 footnote estimated). Single-product business at the latte/coffeehouse level (Channel Dev is small and separately reported). The methodological judgment calls are (a) classifying occupancy as build, consistent with the Chipotle precedent and ticket guidance; (b) carving in-store marketing out of store operating expenses into Sell; (c) classifying the Starbucks Rewards revenue-deferral as outside the sell line per GAAP treatment, with note. Headline ratio is robust to ±1pp under the alternate classification scenarios stress-tested above.

**What's surprising in the numbers.**

- **Starbucks' marketing intensity is essentially the same as Chipotle's — 2.5% vs. 2.7% of revenue.** The ticket and intuition say Starbucks markets harder (Pumpkin Spice is a cultural event, holiday red cups have their own news cycle), but the dollar-level evidence is that both restaurant operators run a thin marketing layer over a thick operations stack. Starbucks does more brand-building activity on a smaller dollar base because the activity is heavily earned-media and digitally-amplified rather than measured-media-heavy.
- **Store labor + occupancy + other store ops together are 42% of revenue, more than the entire COGS line (31%).** A latte's biggest cost is not the coffee, milk, or cup — it is the building it is made in and the partner who makes it. This is the same structural truth as Chipotle: people and real estate dwarf raw materials in foodservice.
- **Channel Development at 52% operating margin is the most "marketing-driven" piece of Starbucks' business.** When Starbucks essentially operates as a brand licensor (Channel Dev / Nestlé alliance), the margin profile flips entirely — high margin, low build, more brand-leverage. The contrast between Channel Dev's 52% margin and stores' ~20% NA segment margin is the chart's interesting finding inverted: Starbucks-the-coffeehouse is operations-heavy; Starbucks-the-licensed-brand is marketing-heavy. The chart shows the former because that's how customers experience Starbucks.
- **The build_pct effectively ties Chipotle.** Two restaurant operators with very different cultural positions (one fast-casual Mexican, one specialty coffee), different brand strategies (Chipotle: word-of-mouth, low ad spend; Starbucks: seasonal activations, more national presence), and different unit economics (Chipotle: $3.0M AUV, lean labor; Starbucks: ~$1.6M AUV, richer labor benefits) converge on essentially the same build-vs-sell ratio. The structural truth of restaurant production wins out over brand strategy. This is a chart-worthy finding on its own.
