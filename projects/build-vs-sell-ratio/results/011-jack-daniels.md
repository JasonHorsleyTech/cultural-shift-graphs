## Build breakdown ($ millions)

Cost of sales for Brown-Forman includes (per the 10-K accounting policies and historical notes): grain (corn ~80% of mash bill for JDTW, plus rye and malted barley), sugar maple charcoal for the Lincoln County Process mellowing, water, yeast; new American oak barrels (Brown-Forman Cooperage in Louisville plus historically the Jack Daniel Cooperage in Trinity, Alabama which was being wound down through FY24); aging warehouse depreciation across the multi-decade rickhouse complex in Lynchburg, TN; bottling line labor and depreciation; glass, labels, closures, secondary packaging; outbound freight to distributors; production overhead and quality control. Aged whiskey COGS in any given fiscal year reflects barrel inputs and warehouse depreciation from 4+ years prior — the "current year" view of cost of sales mixes the cost of liquid laid down in roughly FY20 with the cost of FY24 packaging and bottling.

- Cost of sales (grain, charcoal, water, yeast, glass and packaging, new American oak barrels, distillery and bottling labor, plant and warehouse depreciation including barrel/rickhouse D&A, outbound freight to distributors, production overhead): 1,652
- R&D / engineering: 0 (Brown-Forman does not break out R&D as a separate income-statement line; product development for new line extensions — Triple Mash, Bonded, JD & Coca-Cola RTD, etc. — is bundled into innovation/marketing teams within SG&A and into the bottling line capex/expensing in COGS. Immaterial as a separate bucket.)
- Manufacturing capex (depreciation share): already inside cost of sales (FY24 D&A was $87M, the bulk flowing through COGS as plant/warehouse depreciation; current-year capex of $228M sits on the balance sheet and is not double-counted in build)
- Distribution / logistics: already inside cost of sales (Brown-Forman ships from Lynchburg/Louisville/elsewhere to distributors at FOB; outbound freight is in COGS rather than SG&A)
- Customer support: 0 (B2B customer is the wholesaler/distributor; consumer relations is a tiny corporate function bundled in G&A)

**Build total: 1,652**

## Sell breakdown ($ millions)

Brown-Forman is one of the cleaner spirits comparables because it discloses **Advertising expenses as a separate income-statement line** (most CPG peers fold advertising into SG&A and require Kantar triangulation). Advertising captures measured media (TV, digital, OOH, sports/music sponsorships including the JDTW NASCAR program and various NFL/college football activations), brand activation, and the launch spend on the Jack Daniel's & Coca-Cola RTD globally. The non-advertising portion of SG&A — sales force, trade promotion, brand-management payroll, plus corporate G&A — is presented as a single $826M line and split here per the plan.md fallback.

- Advertising expenses (FY24 disclosed line — measured media, sports and music sponsorships, RTD launch spend for JD & Coca-Cola, brand activation across the Jack family, Woodford Reserve, Herradura, Diplomático, Gin Mare): 529
- Sell-side share of residual SG&A (50% of $826M — sales-force compensation supporting the three-tier distribution model in the US and direct sales in controlled markets internationally, brand-management payroll, trade promotion and on-premise activation, in-store and on-premise activations including bar/restaurant sponsored programs, point-of-sale materials): 413

**Sell total: 942**

## Methodology notes

**Single-segment company; no segment carve needed.** Brown-Forman discloses one reportable segment in the FY2024 10-K. Geographic disclosures (United States, Developed International, Emerging, Travel Retail) are presented for revenue color but not as separate segments. This is the cleanest possible setup for the build vs. sell project's purposes — the consolidated income statement *is* the segment-level income statement, and the only triangulation step is the SG&A sub-line allocation. The single-segment structure also reflects the strategic reality: Brown-Forman is a spirits-and-occasional-wine pure-play that just simplified further in FY24 by divesting Finlandia and Sonoma-Cutrer. The remaining business is overwhelmingly American whiskey, anchored by Jack Daniel's.

**Jack Daniel's family share — approximately 60%.** Brown-Forman doesn't publish a precise brand-level revenue split, but management commentary and analyst coverage consistently put the Jack Daniel's family of brands at roughly 60% of consolidated net sales — sometimes higher when the Tennessee Whiskey base is having a strong year, sometimes a touch lower as Woodford Reserve, Herradura tequila, Diplomático rum, Gin Mare, and El Jimador grow into the mix. JDTW Old No. 7 alone is the single largest brand. For a 60% Jack-family revenue share against a consolidated cost structure that is dominated by Jack manufacturing (the JDTW distillery and warehousing complex in Lynchburg is Brown-Forman's largest single-asset cluster), brand-specific economics for the Jack family are unlikely to differ materially from consolidated — the same plants, the same sales force, the same advertising agencies. Confidence is high that the consolidated build_pct of 63.7% is within ±2 points of a true Jack-family-only build_pct.

**Advertising-to-revenue is high — 12.7% — but COGS is also chunky.** Spirits is famously marketing-intensive, and Brown-Forman's 12.7% advertising-to-revenue ratio sits in the meaty middle of category benchmarks (DISCUS shows premium spirits typically 10-20%; Diageo runs around 17%; Pernod Ricard around 15-16%; Constellation Brands beer-heavy mix around 8-9%). The flip side is that aged whiskey has *meaningful* COGS — barrels, warehousing, multi-year working-capital tie-up — pushing cost of sales to 39.5% of revenue (gross margin 60.5%, lower than vodka or gin operations which can run 70%+ gross margin). The build/sell ratio reflects both forces: high advertising pulls toward sell, high COGS pulls toward build, and the result lands at 63.7% — squarely inside the ticket's 50-65% prediction range.

**SG&A 50/50 split assumption — sensitivity check.** SG&A excluding advertising is $826M. Per plan.md's fallback rubric, this is split 50/50 between sell and overhead, yielding $413M each. Sensitivity:
- 60/40 sell-heavy ($496M sell / $330M overhead) → build_pct = 1,652 / (1,652 + 1,025) = **61.7%**
- 40/60 overhead-heavy ($330M sell / $496M overhead) → build_pct = 1,652 / (1,652 + 859) = **65.8%**

The "true" split is probably *modestly* sell-heavy for Brown-Forman: a focused single-segment spirits company has a substantial brand-management and sales-force payroll inside SG&A (key-account selling to chains, on-premise activation managers in major markets, brand ambassadors for the Jack Daniel's "house" experience), plus trade-promotional activity that doesn't always sit on the advertising line. The corporate G&A footprint, while real, is moderate — Louisville HQ, regional offices, no acquisitions-driven sprawl. A 55/45 sell-heavy split (slightly more honest than 50/50) would yield build_pct = **62.7%**. Reporting 63.7% as the headline using the 50/50 default for cross-product consistency.

**Gain on divestitures is non-recurring and structural.** The $267M of operating-line gain (Finlandia + Sonoma-Cutrer) is a one-time portfolio-cleaning event in FY24. It boosts reported operating income from a "true operating" ~$1,147M to the GAAP $1,414M. For the build vs. sell ratio specifically, the gain doesn't move the numerator or denominator — it's neither build nor sell. Treating it as a negative in overhead so the four buckets reconcile to revenue, but readers comparing operating margins year-over-year should understand that FY24's 33.8% operating margin is inflated by the gain; underlying operating margin is closer to 27-28%. Per the press release, organic operating income was -2% vs. FY23, confirming that the underlying business was modestly soft after stripping out the divestiture credit and FX. Brown-Forman's FY25 outlook implicitly normalizes back to the no-gain baseline.

**Three-tier distribution caveat — parent vs. shelf-price bottle.** Per the ticket spec, the 10-K-anchored ratio captures only the brand-owner's cost structure. A consumer paying ~$24 for a 750ml bottle of JDTW at retail decomposes very differently: Brown-Forman captures (very roughly, with state-by-state variation in markup and tax) maybe $9-11 of that $24; the wholesaler/distributor takes ~$3-4; the retailer takes ~$5-7; federal and state excise plus sales tax take ~$3-5. Brown-Forman is the brand-owner step only. The "consumer dollar anatomy" decomposition (filed as a separate idea per plan.md) would attribute the rest of the dollar to wholesaler and retailer markups and tax. This is the same caveat Coca-Cola (ticket 005), Bud Light (ticket 010), and other CPG entries carry — relevant directionally but not specific to Brown-Forman.

**Aged-whiskey inventory accounting.** JDTW is a minimum 4-year aged Tennessee whiskey (most of the bourbon released as Old No. 7 is in the 4-7 year range). The barrel and grain costs incurred in fiscal years 2018-2020 sit on the balance sheet as inventory through aging, and only flow into COGS when the bottled product is sold to a distributor. Brown-Forman's $228M of FY24 capex is partly continued investment in barrels-in-aging that won't appear in COGS until the late 2020s. Two implications for the build vs. sell ratio:
1. The current-year COGS of $1,652M reflects production decisions made years ago, not FY24 input costs. If grain/oak costs spiked in FY22-FY23 (which they did, per industry reports on bourbon raw materials), the bulk of that won't hit COGS until FY26-FY28.
2. The $228M capex / $87M D&A spread (~2.6×) reflects Brown-Forman continuing to lay down aged inventory faster than it's depreciating existing warehouses — a long-cycle investment posture characteristic of premium aged spirits.
For the FY24 ratio specifically, neither effect changes the reported number; both are timing-of-cash-vs-P&L issues that are fully consistent with the "current operating expenses" framing of the project.

**Cooperage restructuring (announced January 2025, post-FY24).** In January 2025 Brown-Forman announced closure of the Jack Daniel Cooperage (Trinity, AL — recently renamed Alabama Cooperage) and a 12% global workforce reduction, projecting $70-80M in annualized cost savings. None of this is in FY24 P&L (the announcement post-dates the April 30, 2024 fiscal year end), but it's worth flagging that FY24 is the *peak headcount and footprint* baseline for Brown-Forman; future years will show modest cost-line reductions concentrated in COGS as the cooperage exit lowers barrel-sourcing costs (Brown-Forman will use third-party cooperages and the remaining Louisville cooperage for the full barrel supply going forward).

**Excise taxes are netted against revenue.** Net sales of $4,178M is "net of excise taxes." Federal excise on distilled spirits is $13.50/proof gallon (with a small reduction for the first 100,000 proof gallons under the Craft Beverage Modernization Act); state excise varies dramatically (highest in Washington at ~$36/gal effective, lowest in Wyoming at $0). Brown-Forman collects and remits these as a pass-through. The presentation convention is consistent across spirits peers and doesn't change the build_pct, just the absolute scale of revenue and the implicit "gross sales to distributors" number that's not separately disclosed.

**Comparison with Bud Light (ticket 010) and Coca-Cola (ticket 005).** AB InBev North America came in at 81.9% build_pct under the plan.md classification (and ~66% under an "industry intuitive" frame that treats Distribution as sell). Coca-Cola was 66.8%. Jack Daniel's at 63.7% sits modestly lower (more sell-heavy) than both, driven primarily by the higher advertising-to-revenue ratio (12.7% for Brown-Forman vs. 8-9% for Coca-Cola and ~12% for AB InBev). Spirits-vs-beer-vs-soft-drinks shows roughly that beverage CPG clusters in the 60-85% build range with the spread driven mainly by COGS intensity (spirits and beer high; soda mid; aged spirits with imported inputs higher than domestic) and advertising intensity (spirits highest; beer high; soda variable by region).

**Brown-Forman vs. Diageo / Pernod Ricard sanity check.** Diageo and Pernod Ricard, the two larger spirits peers, both report similarly structured income statements with COGS, advertising/promotion, and SG&A. Diageo's published net-sales-to-marketing-investment ratio runs 16-17%, Pernod 15-16%. Brown-Forman's 12.7% advertising-to-revenue is at the lower end of premium-spirits peers but well above CPG averages. Build_pct ranges across the three majors: Diageo's published cost structure (FY24 underlying) implies a build_pct in the 58-62% range; Pernod Ricard's around 60-63%. Brown-Forman's 63.7% is consistent with the peer spread and the slightly lower marketing intensity that distinguishes it from the two European-headquartered majors.

**Confidence: high.** All four primary buckets are sourced directly from disclosed line items: cost of sales ($1,652M), advertising expense ($529M, separately disclosed — this is the high-confidence anchor), SG&A ($826M), gain on divestitures ($267M), other expense ($24M), and operating income ($1,414M) all map cleanly off the FY24 consolidated statements of operations. The single-segment company structure means no segment-allocation guesswork. The largest interpretive choice is the SG&A 50/50 split (sell vs. overhead), which moves the headline build_pct by at most ±2 points across reasonable alternatives. The Jack Daniel's-specific approximation (consolidated → Jack family) is robust because Brown-Forman is essentially a "Jack Daniel's company plus extensions," with the Jack family ~60% of revenue and proportionally larger of the manufacturing footprint. Calling this "high" reflects clean, primary-source, cleanly-classified data.
