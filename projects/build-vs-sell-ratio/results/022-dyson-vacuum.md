## Build breakdown ($ millions)

Dyson is genuinely a build-heavy brand by the standard of consumer-appliance peers. Three structural reasons drive this: (a) Dyson designs and tools its own digital motors, cyclone separation systems, and battery packs in-house — most premium consumer-appliance peers (SharkNinja, Eufy, Roborock) source these from upstream suppliers; (b) R&D intensity at ~6-7% of revenue (~£416m / $533m on £6.6bn) is structurally above category peers (SharkNinja ~3-4%, iRobot ~12% but on a much smaller base); (c) own/contract manufacturing footprint in Singapore, Malaysia, and the Philippines with significant capex in factory infrastructure (£166m Philippines campus, "biggest investment in Dyson's history" in Singapore for batteries, £100m Bristol UK technology centre). At the same time, Dyson is materially less build-dominated than Apple-segment products or pure semiconductor businesses because Dyson's marketing intensity and 200+ owned Demo stores push the sell side meaningfully higher than commodity-CPG levels.

- **COGS (raw materials, contract manufacturing, depreciation share)** — aluminium, steel, polycarbonate and ABS plastics for housings, lithium-ion battery cells (sourced cells assembled into Dyson-designed packs at Singapore facility), digital-motor components (rotors, stators, bearings — manufactured to Dyson spec), HEPA filtration media for vacuum and purifier categories, glass and electronics for hair-care heating elements, contract assembly fees at Philippines/Malaysia/Singapore plants, in-bound freight for components from Asia component suppliers, primary and secondary packaging, depreciation of Dyson's own manufacturing tooling and capex (Bristol technology centre, Hullavington UK robotics campus, Singapore battery facility, Philippines campus). Estimated at gross margin ~48% (consistent with SharkNinja and slightly below traditional premium small-appliance peers like KitchenAid which have higher labor-cost domestic manufacturing). FY2024 COGS ≈ £3,432M / 0.78 GBP per USD = **$4,400**

- **R&D / engineering** — disclosed at ~£8m/week in 2024 (down from ~£9m/week in 2023), implying ~£416m annual. Spans digital-motor design (Dyson V-series motors generating 110,000 RPM are core IP), battery-pack engineering (Singapore facility for solid-state and lithium battery development), cyclone separation R&D (legacy core IP for floor care), hair-care fluid dynamics (Coanda effect for Airwrap, the engineering mathematics that drives the styler's curl mechanism), HEPA and air-flow engineering (purifier line), AI and software (Dyson Link app, robot vacuum SLAM, on-device AI), robotics (Hullavington team), wearables (Zone air-purifying headphones, the Supersonic r). R&D facilities span UK (Malmesbury HQ, Bristol new centre, Hullavington Airfield robotics, London AI lab), Singapore (battery), Malaysia, Philippines. Dyson Institute UK trains undergraduate engineers (~156 students). 6,500 engineers globally is the consistently-cited figure. £416M / 0.78 = **$533**

- **Distribution / logistics** — third-party ocean freight from Singapore/Malaysia/Philippines manufacturing hubs to regional distribution centres (Americas via West Coast US ports, Europe via Rotterdam/Felixstowe/Hamburg, EMEA via UAE, Asia regional), regional warehouse operations, last-mile e-commerce fulfilment for direct-to-consumer dyson.com sales (which is a meaningful and growing share of mix), retail-channel logistics to Best Buy / Currys / John Lewis / MediaMarkt / Amazon / department stores. Estimated at ~3% of revenue, a normal level for an Asia-manufactured globally-distributed durable-goods brand. ≈ £200M / 0.78 = **$255**

- **Customer support** — Dyson Customer Service operates regional call centres and online chat support across English, French, German, Spanish, Italian, Dutch, Mandarin, Japanese, Korean, Russian, Polish covering hardware warranty (battery degradation in cordless vacuums is a material warranty driver, brushbar replacements, motor failures in older units), Dyson Account/app support, in-warranty repair coordination (Dyson runs both mail-in repair service and authorised service-centre network), out-of-warranty paid repair (a real business — Dyson sells a "Dyson Service" repair option as well as parts), and trade-in/upgrade promotion handling. Estimated ~1.5% of revenue, normal for a premium hardware brand with own-store retail channel and direct-to-consumer presence. ≈ £100M / 0.78 = **$130**

**Build total: 5,310** (rounded; sum 4,400 + 533 + 255 + 130 = 5,318)

## Sell breakdown ($ millions)

## Methodology notes

**Why consolidated, not segment.** Dyson does not publish category-level segment financials (floor care vs hair care vs air treatment vs lighting vs wearables). Geographic disclosure is also limited in the public press releases; the most granular available data is consolidated group revenue and profit. The build/sell ratio is therefore computed at the group level and presented as a Dyson-vacuum proxy on the basis that the underlying cost-structure mechanics — Dyson's own digital motors, batteries, cyclone tech, the heavy R&D, the Asia contract manufacturing, the global retail and DTC infrastructure — are common across the categories. The vacuum SKU is an honest proxy for Dyson's cost structure even though hair care has become the highest-growth category and vacuums face the most competitive pressure.

**Why fiscal_year 2024 (calendar year ending December 2024).** Dyson reports on calendar year. The 2024 results were announced via press release in early 2025 and the underlying UK Companies House annual report and accounts were filed in October 2025. This is the most recent full year. Note: 2024 was a structurally noisy year for Dyson — revenue declined from £7.1bn to £6.6bn (-7%), EBITDA declined from £1.4bn to £900m (-36%), PBT halved from £1.1bn to £561m. The decline was attributed to (a) currency headwinds in Asia and Turkey, (b) a Philippines factory fire one-off, (c) ~£100m+ in restructuring costs from the global headcount reduction announced mid-2024, and (d) consumer-confidence weakness. For directional cost-structure interpretation, 2023 (£7.1bn revenue, £1.4bn EBITDA, ~19.7% margin) is more representative of steady-state Dyson — in a 2023 framing, build_pct shifts modestly because higher operating profit lowers both the SG&A residual and the implied sell number. Re-running the same classification on 2023: revenue £7.1bn ($9.1bn), build ~$5,650 (R&D was ~£468M in 2023 vs £416M in 2024, COGS proportional), sell ~$1,750 (advertising scaled with revenue), overhead ~$700, profit ~$1,000 (using £700M operating profit as PBT £1.1bn less interest). Build_pct in 2023 ≈ 76.4% — within 0.2pp of the 2024 number. The directional finding is robust to the 2024 vs 2023 endpoint choice.

**UK Companies House vs Singapore-filed accounts — entity scope.** Dyson Limited (UK Companies House 02627406) is a UK trading entity. The wider Dyson Group is now consolidated through Singapore-based holding companies (Dyson Holdings Pte Ltd; Weybourne Holdings Pte Ltd). The 2019 HQ relocation from Malmesbury UK to Singapore put the corporate centre and meaningful intercompany economics in Singapore. The £6.6bn group revenue figure cited in the press release is the global group consolidation, which agrees within FX-translation tolerance with the $8.3bn Singapore-filed group figure (at ~1.26 GBP/USD). The UK Companies House Dyson Limited annual report is a substantial document (48 pages for FY2024) but is one entity within the group, not the consolidated group. For build/sell purposes I have used the group consolidated figure to capture the full economic picture; classifying within UK-only would understate global infrastructure (Singapore battery facility, Philippines campus, US Demo stores, etc.).

**Gross margin assumption — central uncertainty.** Dyson does not publicly disclose its gross margin. I have assumed ~48%, based on (a) SharkNinja FY2024 disclosed gross margin of 48-49% as the closest current public peer (premium contract-manufactured small-appliance brand with own-design IP), (b) Dyson's premium pricing and own-motor own-cyclone IP would support gross margin slightly above SharkNinja under a "more vertically integrated, more IP-protected" logic, but (c) Dyson's own factories and labor costs partly offset this advantage. A range of 45-52% is plausible. At 45% gross margin, COGS shifts up by £200M and build_pct rises to ~78%. At 52%, COGS shifts down by £265M and build_pct falls to ~74%. Either way the directional finding holds — Dyson is build-dominated in the 74-78% range.

**R&D classification — clean.** Dyson discloses R&D running at ~£8m/week in 2024 (~£416m annual). All R&D is classified as build per the project rubric. This is consistent with the rubric's treatment of Apple, Samsung, and other technology-led peers. Dyson R&D includes both pure engineering (motor design, battery, cyclone, fluid dynamics) and software/AI (robot vacuum SLAM, MyDyson app, on-device AI) — both flow to build.

**Marketing intensity — triangulated, not disclosed.** Dyson does not publicly disclose advertising or marketing spend. Estimated at ~10-12% of revenue based on (a) SharkNinja's disclosed marketing intensity of ~10%, (b) Dyson's known position as a heavy TV and digital advertiser in major markets, (c) the hair-care category's particular dependence on sustained brand-building investment given the Airwrap/Supersonic premium pricing requires ongoing TikTok/YouTube/influencer presence. The £750M / $960M estimate is the central case; range £600-900M ($770-1,150M). At the low end of the marketing range, build_pct rises ~1pp; at the high end, falls ~1pp.

**Demo stores — material sell-side number.** 200+ owned-and-operated Dyson Demo stores globally is structurally distinctive — most premium appliance brands do not operate own-retail at this scale (KitchenAid does not; Whirlpool does not; SharkNinja does not; Apple does, which is the closest analog). The estimated $285M annual store operating cost (rent, staff, utilities, demo equipment, store-level marketing) is a material part of the sell side and a structural feature of Dyson's go-to-market that pushes the sell number meaningfully higher than peer brands that distribute exclusively through third-party retail. Per-store annual cost of ~$1.0-1.5M is a midpoint estimate based on premium-mall retail rent in major markets (some flagships — Fifth Avenue, Champs-Élysées, Kurfürstendamm — are materially more expensive than this; tail stores in regional shopping malls are materially less). The number could plausibly range $200-350M.

**Customer support is meaningful.** Dyson runs a large customer-service operation because (a) cordless vacuum batteries degrade and warranty replacement is a real cost driver, (b) the Demo store network requires staff-driven demo and after-sales support, (c) the DTC channel requires direct customer-service infrastructure, (d) Dyson sells out-of-warranty paid repair as a service. Estimated ~1.5% of revenue is the central case; this is in line with hardware-brand norms.

**Excluded overhead is somewhat elevated for a private global manufacturer.** Dyson runs ~8% of revenue in corporate G&A, which is high for a public consumer-products company (P&G ~5%, Apple ~6%) but reasonable for a privately-held global manufacturer with extensive own-infrastructure (multiple manufacturing campuses, own technology centres, complex IP enforcement, multi-jurisdiction tax/legal structure including the UK-Singapore restructuring, family-office coordination through Weybourne). The Dyson Foundation overhead is excluded from this number (it's a separate charitable structure).

**The Singapore HQ distortion.** Dyson moved corporate HQ from Malmesbury UK to Singapore in 2019. This shifted some intercompany economics — IP licensing, treasury, certain executive functions — from UK to Singapore. The consolidated group figures used here capture the full economic picture regardless of where individual entities sit; the build/sell classification is unchanged by the geographic distribution of overhead. However, anyone looking at UK-only Dyson Limited filings would see a different and incomplete picture.

**Manufacturing footprint clarification.** Despite Dyson being culturally identified with British engineering, Dyson has not manufactured products in the UK since 2002 (vacuum production moved to Malaysia in that year; subsequent product lines launched in Singapore/Malaysia/Philippines from inception). UK operations are R&D, design, corporate functions, and a UK commercial organisation. The build-side cost classification reflects this — manufacturing economics are Asia-based contract assembly with own tooling, not UK-domestic labour. This keeps Dyson's COGS competitive with SharkNinja, Eufy, and other Asia-manufactured premium peers.

**Confidence: medium.** The directional finding (Dyson sits in the 74-78% build_pct range) is robust to the main classification uncertainties (gross margin 45-52%, advertising 10-12% of revenue, demo store cost $200-350M). What anchors the medium-confidence rating is that the headline group financials are real and disclosed (revenue, EBITDA, PBT, R&D approximate) — not estimated like Liquid Death or Stanley Tumbler. What prevents a high-confidence rating is that the SG&A sub-classification (advertising vs sales force vs trade promo vs store ops vs corporate G&A) is triangulated from peers and trade press rather than disclosed by Dyson, and that no segment-level disclosure exists to validate the vacuum-category proxy assumption.

**What's surprising in the numbers.**

- **Dyson's build_pct of 76% is meaningfully lower than Apple iPhone (~85%) or PlayStation (~89%) despite Dyson being culturally identified as an engineering-first brand.** The mechanism is the unusually heavy sell-side infrastructure — 200+ owned Demo stores plus aggressive global advertising — that pushes the sell side higher than Apple's per-revenue equivalent (Apple Stores are smaller as a percentage of Apple's much-larger revenue base). Dyson at $8.5bn revenue running 200 own-stores has more retail-real-estate intensity per revenue dollar than Apple at $390bn revenue running ~520 stores.

- **R&D intensity at ~6.3% of revenue is high for an appliance company but lower than the cultural narrative suggests.** "Dyson is an engineering company" is a true brand positioning, but at ~6% R&D-to-revenue Dyson runs above appliance peers (Whirlpool ~1-2%, Electrolux ~2%) and SharkNinja (~3-4%), but well below pure-tech peers (semiconductors at 15-20%, large-pharma at 15-20%, Nintendo at 12.4% in trough year). The R&D investment is genuine and material — but it's not Apple-or-Microsoft-scale R&D.

- **The 2024 profit halving was structural noise, not steady-state.** Currency headwinds (~£100M+), Philippines factory fire one-off, restructuring charges (~£100M+), and consumer-confidence-driven softness combined to halve PBT. The underlying cost structure is reasonably stable — re-running 2023 yields the same build_pct within 1pp. The headline "Dyson profits halved in difficult year" was real but doesn't reflect a permanent shift in cost structure.

- **The dividend up to Weybourne fell 71% in 2024.** Dividend from Dyson trading group up to the family holding (Weybourne) fell from $890M in 2023 to $254M in 2024 (per Fortune coverage), with a further $286M paid in early 2025. This is downstream of the profit decline but exaggerated — Dyson Group retained more cash on balance sheet to fund the restructuring and the continued capex programs (Singapore battery facility, Philippines campus, Bristol UK centre). Has no impact on the build/sell ratio (dividends are post-profit), but is the most visible signal of the 2024 noise.

- **Dyson does not manufacture in the UK and hasn't since 2002.** This is widely misunderstood by consumers who associate Dyson with British engineering. UK operations are entirely R&D, design, corporate, and commercial — manufacturing is in Singapore/Malaysia/Philippines. The cost-structure classification reflects Asia-based contract manufacturing economics, not UK labour costs. This keeps Dyson cost-competitive with SharkNinja and Asian peers — but means any "British manufacturing" framing of the brand is marketing, not operational reality.

- **Hair care has become a structural growth driver and may eventually exceed floor care in revenue mix.** Dyson does not disclose category mix, but trade press coverage consistently identifies hair care (Supersonic, Airwrap, Airstrait, the new Supersonic r and Chitosan formulation launched 2024) as the highest-growth and highest-margin category. Floor care (legacy vacuums, robot vacs, wet cleaners) faces price pressure from Roborock, Dreame, Eufy/Anker, and other Chinese premium brands. The build/sell ratio used here is a fair proxy for the vacuum SKU because the cost-structure mechanics are common across categories — but the long-run mix shift toward hair care is a real strategic question Dyson is navigating.
