# Princess Peach

## Character Summary
- **Source work:** Mario franchise (Nintendo). First appeared as the kidnapped princess in Super Mario Bros. (1985, NES). Recurring across the entire Mario continuity for 40+ years: playable hero in Super Mario Bros. 2 (1988), Super Mario RPG (1996), Paper Mario (2000), Super Princess Peach (2005, DS), Super Mario 3D World (2013), Princess Peach: Showtime! (2024); plus universal-roster appearances across Mario Kart (1992+), Mario Party (1998+), Super Smash Bros. (Melee 2001, Brawl 2008, Wii U / 3DS 2014, Ultimate 2018), Mario Tennis, Mario Golf, Mario Strikers, and dozens of other spin-offs. Major film appearances: Super Mario Bros. (1993, Samantha Mathis), The Super Mario Bros. Movie (2023, Anya Taylor-Joy). The "eternal damsel" coding is the foundational male-fantasy projection on the character; the Smash Bros. era (2001+) added the "playable cosplay-icon Peach" projection that anchors her 2000s decade-slot placement.
- **Audience:** male-targeted
- **Decade slot:** 2000s
- **Cultural reach proxy:** Mario is the **best-selling video-game franchise of all time** at over **930 million units sold** cumulatively across all titles (Nintendo annual reports through FY2025). Peach is a top-tier playable across the Mario sub-franchises: Super Smash Bros. Ultimate (2018) is the best-selling fighting game ever at ~38M units with Peach as a top-third roster pick by play rate; Mario Kart 8 Deluxe (2017) is the best-selling racing game ever at ~75M units with Peach as a perennial top-character pick; Mario Party titles cumulatively past 60M units. The Super Mario Bros. Movie (2023) grossed **~$1.36 billion worldwide**, the highest-grossing video-game film ever, with Peach as a co-leading character. Princess Peach: Showtime! (2024) sold over 3M units in its first month. Cosplay presence has been continuously top-tier since the early-2000s Smash Bros. era — Peach is one of the three most-cosplayed female video-game characters of all time alongside Lara Croft and Zelda. By recognition-and-reach metrics, Peach is essentially the most universally-known female fictional character ever depicted in video games, recognizable to billions globally.
- **Canonical version used:** The **modern Mario design** of Princess Peach (Super Mario 64 onward, 1996+) — pink ball-gown princess aesthetic, blonde hair, blue eyes, blue earrings, gold crown with jewel, white gloves. The Smash Bros. Melee (2001) and Brawl (2008) era is the load-bearing 2000s reference because that's when Peach became established as a male-fantasy / cosplay icon beyond the kidnapped-princess plot role. Per ticket instruction: cartoon character with stylized proportions, profile what she represents culturally rather than literal trait values. Per heartthrobs.md operational definition, Peach is included as "fictional female character functioning as fantasy object regardless of strict plot role" — same operational basis as Lara Croft / Buffy / Bayonetta / Ciri.

## Personality / Characterization (Excluded from Calc)

- **Damsel-in-distress / eternal-rescue dynamic:** THE single most defining characterization beat for Peach across 40 years of Mario games. Per plan, this is the canonical example of vulnerability/damsel-coding flagged as personality and explicitly excluded from the calc. For Peach this is the load-bearing male-fantasy projection — the rescue/reward dynamic IS the character's male-target appeal mechanism. Some male audiences find the perpetual-damsel framing attractive; some find it regressive. Preferences split → personality, not universal slider.
- **Devoted-to-protagonist / first-love coding:** Peach is the platonic example of the "she would build her remaining life around the protagonist" archetype — and crucially, she has done so for 40 years across hundreds of canonical encounters. This overlaps with the Cool Girl Agreeableness filter but the magnitude of devotion and the perpetual-waiting framing exceed the universal-desirable filter. Personality-coded.
- **Royal status / Mushroom Kingdom princess:** Wealth-and-status fantasy — but per plan, **wealth/income is on the FEMALE-target stack only — not part of the male-target filter list.** Peach's royal-princess status (Mushroom Kingdom monarch with a castle, retainers, throne room) is therefore NOT counted in the male-target calc. Same structural asymmetry as Lara Croft (Lord Henshingly Croft heiress), Black Widow / Wonder Woman / Ciri. Acknowledged as personality / status coding here. Note: this is one of the most-flagged "wealth fantasy" characters in the project — explicitly noted in the ticket as "Royalty (wealth fantasy)."
- **Pink-princess fairy-tale aesthetic:** Hyper-conventional-feminine visual coding (pink ball gown, parasol, crown, white gloves, blonde hair). Some male audiences find this attractive; some find it juvenile. Preferences split → personality, not slider.
- **Cake-baking / hostess / domestic-feminine archetype:** "Welcome to the Princess Toadstool's castle, Mario! It's me, Peach! I baked you a cake!" The domestic-feminine framing is load-bearing for the character's male-fantasy projection but maps to traditional gender-role preferences that split sharply across the male audience.
- **Cartoon stylized proportions:** Peach's body is rendered in cartoon-fantasy convention (large head, simplified limbs, idealized but non-anatomical figure). Per ticket instruction, the cartoon-archetype mapping uses cultural projection (petite-elegant princess body) rather than literal anthropometric measurement.
- **Multiplayer-roster ensemble appeal:** Peach's playable status across Mario Kart / Tennis / Golf / Smash Bros / Party is ensemble-roster framing rather than aligned-hobby coding. Some male audiences find the playable-Peach framing more attractive than the static-damsel framing; some prefer the rescue-archetype. Preferences split → personality, not slider.
- **Vagueness of Peach's autonomy / self-rescue capacity (varies by game):** Peach has been depicted across the spectrum from pure damsel (most platformers) to capable-protagonist (Super Mario Bros. 2, Super Princess Peach, Princess Peach: Showtime!, Paper Mario). The varying-canonical-agency dimension is characterization rather than universal-desirable trait.

## Joint Probability

- **Method:** filter (male-target stack)
- **Naive product of marginals:**
  0.20 × 0.22 × 0.34 × 0.70 × 0.10 × 0.25
  = (0.20 × 0.22) = 0.044
  × 0.34 = 0.01496
  × 0.70 = 0.01047
  × 0.10 = 0.001047
  × 0.25 = **2.62e-4**
  (≈ 1 in 3,820, applied within a base population of US women aged 20–28)
  Note: the body-composition factor (0.22) is the BMI marginal alone, NOT the BMI+DD+ joint rate (0.02) that buxom-coded characters trigger. Peach's design is petite-elegant-princess, NOT buxom — same arithmetic treatment as Yuna. ALSO note: the "doesn't follow girl culture" filter (0.25) is OMITTED for Peach because she is the platonic embodiment of conventional-feminine girl culture rather than an alternative-coded character — this is the dominant arithmetic difference between Peach's stack and Yuna's stack (which had the not-girly filter included via spiritual-modest archetype coding).
- **Correlation adjustment:** Mixed-direction adjustments. Pairs:
  - Top-quintile attractiveness ↔ low neuroticism: positive halo (r ≈ 0.10–0.15). Naive product OVERSTATES rarity by ~1.2x.
  - Top-quintile attractiveness ↔ Cool Girl Agreeableness: positive halo (r ≈ 0.10–0.15). OVERSTATES by ~1.2x.
  - Cool Girl Agreeableness ↔ low neuroticism: positive (both load on stability factor; r ≈ 0.20–0.25). OVERSTATES by ~1.5x.
  - No-kids ↔ currently single: positive (r ≈ 0.20). OVERSTATES by ~1.2x.
  - Top-quintile attractiveness ↔ currently single: NEGATIVE in real world (more attractive women more likely partnered). UNDERSTATES rarity by ~1.2–1.4x.
  - **Net direction:** Multiple positive halos (attractiveness↔low-N, attractiveness↔Agreeableness, low-N↔Agreeableness, no-kids↔single) collectively meaningful. Only one mild negative correlation (attractiveness↔single). **Critically, Peach AVOIDS the negative-correlation pair that drives most male-target adjustments toward "rarer than naive product" — namely the attractiveness↔not-girly violation — because she does NOT clear the not-girly filter at all.** This is structurally the same situation as Yuna, but more pronounced: Peach is conventional-feminine, so the male-target trait combination is *more* aligned with real-world correlations (positive halos dominate, negative violations minimal). Net adjustment: roughly **1.5–2x LESS rare than naive product**.
- **Adjusted estimate:** **~1.5e-4** (≈ 1 in 6,700 women aged 20–28). Bracket: [7e-5, 3e-4].
- **Population implication:** Out of ~14M US women aged 20–28, roughly **2,100** match Princess Peach's universal-desirable stack as captured by the male-target methodology (range ~900–4,500 across the bracket). Peach lands at the **LESS-rare end of the male-target distribution** — comparable to Yuna (~660 matches) but actually slightly less rare because Peach lacks the not-girly filter that Yuna clears. Peach's male-fantasy projection rides predominantly on **damsel/rescue dynamics + devoted-first-love coding + royal-princess wealth-and-status** — ALL of which are excluded from the universal-desirable calc per project methodology. The arithmetic correctly captures that Peach's stack on universal-desirable sliders is relatively modest; the load-bearing fantasy material is on the EXCLUDED personality and EXCLUDED-by-asymmetry wealth axes. This is a genre-coherent finding parallel to Yuna's: cartoon / video-game female-leads in family-friendly franchises stack LESS aggressively on universal-desirable sliders than late-night-cable heartthrobs (CJ Parker, Mikaela) or action-genre female protagonists (Black Widow, Wonder Woman, Lara Croft).

- **Correlation adjustments:** medium — the directional claim that the positive halos dominate the (single mild) negative correlation for this stack is well-supported in mate-market and Big-5 literature, but the magnitude (1.5–2x net less rare) is judgment, not a measured joint distribution. ## Notes / Edge Cases

- **THE central trait-list-asymmetry caveat for Peach:** Princess Peach's male-fantasy projection is **dominantly load-bearing on traits the project deliberately excludes from the calc**:
  1. **Vulnerability / damsel-rescue dynamic** — explicitly excluded by plan as personality/characterization, not quantified. For Peach this is THE central appeal mechanism for 40 years of Mario games.
  2. **Royal-princess wealth-and-status (Mushroom Kingdom monarch)** — excluded because wealth/income is on the FEMALE-target stack only, not part of the male-target filter list. Same structural asymmetry as Lara Croft / Black Widow / Wonder Woman / Ciri.
  3. **Devoted-first-love / 40-year-perpetual-monogamous-waiting** — overlaps with Cool Girl Agreeableness but exceeds the filter at a magnitude that the universal-desirable methodology doesn't capture.
  4. **Pink-princess hyper-conventional-feminine aesthetic** — preferences split → personality, not universal slider.

  As a result, Peach's adjusted joint of ~1.5e-4 reflects only the *universal-slider* portion of her male-fantasy projection. The total fantasy projection is much larger but is concentrated on excluded axes. **Flag this as one of the cleanest cases in the dataset of the universal-vs-personality split being load-bearing for cross-character comparison** — directly parallel to Yuna's caveat (ticket 018) but more pronounced because Peach's damsel-rescue + royal-status combination is more dominantly the appeal mechanism than Yuna's first-love coding.

- **Why Peach is even LESS stacked than Yuna on universal sliders:** Yuna cleared 7 universal-desirable filters (incl. "doesn't follow girl culture" via spiritual-modest archetype coding); Peach clears 6 (drops the not-girly filter because she's the apotheosis of conventional-feminine pink-princess girl culture). The arithmetic difference is ~4x rarer for Yuna (~5e-5) vs Peach (~1.5e-4). This is consistent with the broader genre pattern — characters whose male-fantasy projection rides on damsel/rescue + royal-status + devoted-first-love (Peach, Zelda template) stack LESS on universal sliders than characters who add tomboy-coded interest profiles (Mikaela, Lara, Black Widow). Peach is approximately the LEAST-stacked male-target character in the dataset on universal-desirable axes — but is paradoxically among the MOST-canonical male-fantasy figures by recognition, reach, and longevity. This dissociation between universal-slider stacking and male-fantasy-iconicity is itself a finding for the final graph.

- **Cartoon-archetype mapping per ticket instruction:** Per ticket: "cartoon character — physical traits are stylized. Use 'cartoon archetype' mapping where direct measurement doesn't apply. Profile what she represents culturally rather than literal trait values."
