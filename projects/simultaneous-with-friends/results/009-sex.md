## Setup

Partnered sex (intercourse only — solo orgasm is ticket 008). At roughly once a week for the average partnered adult, with a duration of about 7 minutes and a ferociously tight time-of-day cluster, sex is one of the most synchronized activities in the human behavioral repertoire. Everyone's doing it (or not) at nearly the same hour.

## Inputs

**Frequency:** The General Social Survey (2024) finds that 37% of Americans ages 18–64 report weekly sex, down from 55% in 1990. For partnered adults specifically, ~46% of married and ~34% of cohabiting/dating adults report weekly sex. A reasonable cross-demographic average for active partnered adults lands at ~1×/week = 0.143/day. The sex recession is real and the number has cratered — in 1990 this would have been closer to 1.5×/week.

**Duration:** Multiple research lines converge on the penetrative act itself lasting 5–7 minutes. Sex therapists classify "adequate" as 3–7 minutes and "desirable" as 7–13 minutes. Self-report studies (Clement 2002; Waldinger multinational 2005) put the mean around 5.4–7.9 minutes. Using 7 minutes = 420 seconds for the act itself (foreplay excluded — that's a different coordination problem).

**Clustering:** The nycthemeral distribution is striking. PMC research (Refinetti & Menaker 2005; rubenarslan.github.io diary study) shows:
- Night (11pm–1am): ~65% of encounters
- Morning (~6am): ~20%
- Evening (9–11pm): ~10%
- Afternoon: ~5%

The dominant mass is a ~2-hour window around midnight. Use h = 2 hours as the effective cluster window.

**Caveats:** Frequency drops sharply with age (couples 60+ average once every 2 weeks), parenting young children (~30% less frequent), and is higher for cohabiting couples than married ones of the same age. The 0.143/day estimate is conservative for younger partnered adults and generous for older ones.

## Math

Using the time-clustered formula with h = 2 hours:

```
r = f_day / (h × 3600)
  = 0.143 / (2 × 3600)
  = 0.143 / 7200
  = 1.986 × 10⁻⁵ per second

2 × r² × d × N = 2 × (1.986e-5)² × 420 × 50
               = 2 × 3.944e-10 × 420 × 50
               = 2 × 3.944e-10 × 21000
               = 1.656e-5

t_50 = (24/h) × 1 / (1.656e-5)
     = 12 × 60,362
     = 724,344 seconds
     ≈ 8.4 days

t_1 = t_50 × 50
    = 36,217,200 seconds
    ≈ 419 days ≈ 1.15 years
```

## Result

**With any of your 50 friends:** You are both having partnered sex at the same time roughly **once every 8–9 days**.

**With your best friend specifically:** The two of you are simultaneously having sex roughly **once every 14 months**.

## Surprise check

This is far more frequent than naïve intuition would suggest — especially the any-of-50 result. The tight midnight cluster is doing enormous work here. By compressing nearly two-thirds of all sex into a 2-hour window (vs. spreading it across 16 waking hours), the effective rate during that window is ~8× higher than a flat-day assumption would produce. If we had used h = 16 (uniformly distributed), t_50 would balloon to ~67 days. The clustering creates a pseudo-coordinated behavior: the whole group is basically trying to have sex at the same time, even though nobody arranged it.

The 14-month gap for your single best friend feels right — you know they're probably getting lucky sometime this year, but you wouldn't bet on it being the exact same Saturday night.
