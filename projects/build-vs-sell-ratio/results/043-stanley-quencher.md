## Methodology notes

**Revenue basis.** PMI Worldwide is private. No public filing exists. The $750M figure for FY2023 is the most frequently cited trade-press estimate, reported consistently by CNBC, Forbes, and Fast Company and attributed to industry sources. The 2022 estimate of ~$402M provides a double-check: revenue approximately doubled in one year, which is consistent with the documented viral explosion driven by TikTok in late 2022 and 2023. All numbers in this result carry a ±20–30% uncertainty band; this is a low-confidence triangulation, not a disclosure.

**Yeti as cost-structure comparable.** Yeti Holdings (NYSE: YETI) is the closest public analog — a premium, brand-led, stainless-steel insulated drinkware company selling through wholesale retail and its own DTC channels. Yeti FY2024 10-K (fiscal year ended December 2024): revenue $1,829.9M, COGS 41.9% of revenue, gross margin 58.1%, SG&A 44.7% of revenue (including $21.1M disclosed R&D), operating income 13.4% of revenue. The COGS ratio (41.9%) is the strongest anchor in this analysis — it reflects the actual cost of manufacturing, shipping, and getting premium insulated drinkware from Asian factories to US retailers.

Three adjustments were made from Yeti to Stanley:

1. *COGS held at ~42% (same).* Both companies manufacture in China with similar materials and supply-chain configurations. Stanley's higher unit volume (30M+ units in 2023) gives manufacturing cost advantages, but the product is slightly simpler. Roughly a wash; Yeti's ratio is applied directly.

2. *R&D reduced (1.15% → 0.7%).* Stanley's "innovation" during the Quencher era was primarily aesthetic — new colors, limited-edition finishes, collabs. The engineering is mature (vacuum-insulated double-wall stainless is established technology). Yeti invests more in product platforms (coolers, bags, drinkware). Estimate $5M R&D for Stanley; this is speculative.

3. *Marketing intensity adjusted upward; DTC store costs removed.* Yeti's SG&A is inflated by 24 company-owned retail stores and a large e-commerce fulfillment operation. Stanley has neither — it sells wholesale (Target, REI, Dick's) and runs a direct web store but no physical footprint. That removes a meaningful sell-side cost that Yeti carries. Partially offsetting this: Stanley's paid influencer program was substantial during the viral peak (see below). Net result: Stanley's sell ratio is modeled at roughly similar to Yeti (23% to 26% of revenue vs. an estimated 20–25% for Yeti), producing build_pct of 63.6% vs. Yeti's estimated ~60%.

**Influencer spend sizing — "viral doesn't mean free."** The Quencher story is often told as an organic miracle. It is not. Terence Reilly (CEO, hired from Crocs in 2020) explicitly built a paid influencer strategy: mass product seeding to thousands of TikTok and Instagram creators, structured affiliate partnerships at a disclosed 10% commission rate, and paid sponsorships with select high-reach creators. Evidence of scale: tracked "#ad" disclosures in Stanley posts jumped 4,480% during the 2022–2023 viral period (Influencer Marketing Hub data), indicating a dramatic expansion of paid partnerships alongside the organic mentions. In November 2023 alone, Stanley generated $7.0M estimated media value (EMV) with 251.5M impressions — some organic, some paid.

Estimates here: $52M in creator fees/seeding + $20M in affiliate commissions + $13M in other PR/events = $85M in influencer-category spend = 11.3% of revenue. This is substantially higher than the typical CPG advertising-only benchmark (~4–8%) but below the share implied by the extreme narrative version of the story. Total sell (including trade promotion and salesforce) is modeled at $183M = 24.4% of revenue.

**Trade promotion and retail exclusives.** Stanley's key distribution was through Target (exclusive colorways, prominent shelf placement, seasonal resets). These exclusive programs are not free — retailers extract co-marketing spend, and Target's exclusive colorway model typically involves negotiated marketing support. The $48M in this line is estimated; the true figure may be significantly higher or lower depending on the specific commercial terms.

**Operating margin implication.** The residual profit of $190M = 25.3% operating margin. This is high relative to Yeti's 13.4%, but plausible for a brand in its viral peak: revenue approximately doubled in FY2023 while fixed-cost infrastructure grew more slowly. Stanley did not have to build retail stores or expand corporate headcount proportionally to capture revenue upside. If the actual margin was closer to 18–20% (more conservative), that would push sell costs to $225–$245M and bring build_pct down to approximately 57–59%. The margin uncertainty is the single largest driver of uncertainty in the ratio.

**What this result does NOT capture.** PMI Worldwide owns brands beyond Stanley (Aladdin, others). The consolidated overhead here is estimated as entirely attributable to Stanley, since Stanley dominated PMI's revenue and growth in 2023. If PMI's other brands carried meaningful overhead, Stanley's share would be lower and operating profit correspondingly higher.

**Confidence: low.** Every number here except the COGS ratio (anchored to Yeti's public filing) is an estimate from trade press narratives, industry benchmarks, or residual inference. The $750M revenue itself is a journalist estimate, not a disclosed figure. Results should be treated as directionally correct, not arithmetically precise. The build_pct of 63.6% has an honest uncertainty range of ±8–10 percentage points.
