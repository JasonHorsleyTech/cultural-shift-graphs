# Ticket 043 — Stanley Quencher tumbler

## Product

The Stanley Quencher H2.0 FlowState tumbler — the 30-40oz insulated cup that became a viral phenomenon in 2023-2024 driven by TikTok and influencer marketing. Iconic example of a "viral DTC" product where marketing intensity is visibly central to the product's success.

## Parent company

Pacific Market International (PMI Worldwide), the Seattle-based company that owns the Stanley brand. **Private company.**

## Panel

consumer

## Segment to use

**PMI Worldwide consolidated.** PMI is private and doesn't break out segments. The Stanley brand is the dominant product line (PMI also owns Aladdin and a few smaller brands), so consolidated PMI is a reasonable proxy for Stanley economics.

## Source hierarchy

1. **Trade press estimates** of Stanley revenue (frequently cited at $750M in 2023, ~$94M in 2019 — explosive growth). Sources: CNBC, Forbes, WSJ, Adweek.
2. **PMI Worldwide press releases and leadership interviews** (CEO Terence Reilly, formerly of Crocs, gave several interviews about the Quencher's marketing strategy).
3. **Industry benchmarks** — IBISWorld "Houseware and Kitchenware Manufacturing", or use Yeti Holdings 10-K (NYSE: YETI) as the closest public comparable for premium drinkware cost structure.
4. **Influencer marketing trade press** (Influencer Marketing Hub, Adweek) for sizing the influencer/TikTok spend that drove the phenomenon.

## Specific caveats for this product

- **Confidence is low.** PMI is private; revenue estimates come from trade press, cost breakdowns are unavailable. Triangulate via Yeti as a public comparable, but adjust:
  - Yeti is a publicly listed brand-led company with relatively heavy traditional advertising. PMI/Stanley relies more on influencer + TikTok-driven viral marketing, which is cheaper per impression but still a meaningful sell line.
  - Stanley's cost of goods is largely manufactured in China — typical premium-drinkware COGS for Yeti is in the 45-55% of revenue range; reasonable to assume similar for Stanley.
- **The "viral" framing matters.** Stanley's growth was largely "earned" media (TikTok creators posted unsponsored videos), but Stanley also paid for: massive influencer seeding, partnerships (Starbucks collab, Target exclusive colorways), retail trade promotion. The "we did it organically" narrative understates actual paid sell spend. Note this honestly.
- Distribution: sold through Target, Dick's, REI, etc. — wholesale, not DTC stores. Limited retail real estate burden.
- **This is the kind of ticket where the headline number (high sell ratio) supports the project's hypothesis, so be especially careful not to inflate it.** If Yeti's actual ratio comes out at 60% build, Stanley is likely similar or slightly more sell-tilted, not dramatically so.
