# Selene

## Character Summary
- **Source work:** Underworld franchise (Screen Gems / Lakeshore Entertainment), debuting with Underworld (2003, dir. Len Wiseman) and continuing across Underworld: Evolution (2006), Underworld: Awakening (2012), and Underworld: Blood Wars (2016) — Kate Beckinsale plays Selene in four of the five franchise films (she is absent only from the Rise of the Lycans 2009 prequel, which centers on the Sonja / Lucian backstory). Per the project's anchor-on-original convention, the **Underworld (2003) Selene** is the canonical reference for the 2000s decade-slot.
- **Audience:** male-targeted
- **Decade slot:** 2000s
- **Cultural reach proxy:** The Underworld franchise has grossed approximately **$539M worldwide across five films** on a combined ~$207M budget (Box Office Mojo / The Numbers cumulative reporting). The original Underworld (2003) grossed $95.7M worldwide on a $22M budget (a 4.4x ROI), Underworld: Evolution (2006) grossed $111.5M, and Underworld: Awakening (2012) is the franchise's highest-grossing entry at $160M worldwide. Critically panned but commercially successful — the franchise developed a substantial cult-genre following and became one of the defining male-fantasy / horror-action hybrids of the 2000s. Selene specifically is consistently cited in retrospective rankings as one of the iconic male-fantasy figures of the 2000s action / horror genre — recurringly placed on FHM, Maxim, Empire, IGN, and Complex "sexiest action heroine" / "hottest movie vampires" / "best leather-clad action characters" lists 2003–2020. Kate Beckinsale's career-defining role; she was named Esquire's "Sexiest Woman Alive" candidate multiple times and Empire's "Sexiest Movie Stars" 2003 list specifically credited the Selene role. The black leather catsuit + dual pistols + pale skin + dark hair + cold-warrior expression silhouette is one of the most-recognizable female action character designs of the 2000s.
- **Canonical version used:** Per ticket instruction and the project's anchor-on-original convention, the **Underworld (2003) Selene** is the canonical reference. Per the Underworld: Evolution novelization and franchise wiki canon, Selene was born to Hungarian parents in **1383** and turned into a vampire by Viktor at age 19 (~1402); by the events of Underworld (2003) she is canonically ~620 years old as a vampire but **physically presents as a young adult woman** (Kate Beckinsale was 30 at filming in 2003). Following the dataset convention used for Black Widow (ticket 010, age drift), Wonder Woman (ticket 020, immortal), and Wanda Maximoff (ticket 012, age drift), the joint is computed using the 20–28 base population framing because the male-fantasy projection treats her as the archetypal "young attractive adult woman" regardless of canonical numerical age. The 2003 Underworld Selene is the load-bearing male-fantasy projection (black leather catsuit, dual pistols, ankle-length leather coat, pale-vampire complexion, ice-blue contact lenses).

## Notes / Edge Cases

- **Methodological asymmetry — central caveat for Selene:** Selene is a master Death Dealer with ~600 years of weapons / firearms / close-combat training, vampire-supernatural physical capabilities (enhanced strength, speed, reflexes, accelerated healing, daywalker abilities by later films), polyglot capacity implied by 600 years of European existence, and an orphaned-by-her-sire backstory. **The male-target trait list captures NONE of these as universal-desirable filters** because the project's premise is that male-target media doesn't typically *stack* skills onto female love interests as universal-desirable sliders the way female-target media stacks them onto male leads. If a SYMMETRIC trait-list analysis were applied — counting expert-combatant ~1e-3 (per the base-rates.md "expert combatant" entry from ticket 017), top-quintile fitness ~0.20 (vampire-enhanced), family-unencumbered ~0.05 (orphaned by Viktor's massacre), plausibly multilingual ~0.03–0.20 (600 years of European life), and arguably IQ ≥130 ~0.02 (tactical mastery + Death Dealer command rank) — Selene would gain roughly 4–5 additional rarity multipliers and her joint probability would collapse by **3–6 orders of magnitude** to ~1e-9 to ~1e-11. This would migrate her toward the rarest tier of the entire dataset, comparable to mid-pack female-target heartthrobs like Bridgerton-lead (ticket 011), Edward Cullen (ticket 007), Aragorn (ticket 017), or Rhysand (ticket 021). The plan's call (asymmetric trait lists) is taken because the male-target *audience* does not appear to stack those traits as universal sliders — Selene is a male-fantasy figure first because she is hot-AND-leather-clad-AND-stoic-AND-not-girly, second because she can outshoot and outfight an entire Lycan pack. The math captures the sliders that actually drive male-fantasy projection. **Flag this asymmetry in the final-graph annotations.** Same structural caveat as Black Widow / Wonder Woman / Ciri / Lara Croft / 2B / Tifa — Selene's symmetric-analysis stack is comparable in rarity to Lara Croft's (most heavily skill-stacked male-target character in the dataset) once the 600-year skill accumulation is counted.
