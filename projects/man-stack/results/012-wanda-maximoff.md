# Wanda Maximoff / Scarlet Witch

## Character Summary
- **Source work:** Marvel Cinematic Universe — Avengers: Age of Ultron (2015), Captain America: Civil War (2016), Avengers: Infinity War (2018), Avengers: Endgame (2019), **WandaVision (Disney+, 2021)** and **Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness (2022)**. Portrayed by Elizabeth Olsen. Per the ticket, the 2020s canonical reference is WandaVision and Multiverse of Madness.
- **Audience:** male-targeted
- **Decade slot:** 2020s
- **Cultural reach proxy:** WandaVision became the most-watched series globally on Disney+ during its January–March 2021 run, drove a major surge in Disney+ subscriptions, and earned 23 Primetime Emmy nominations. Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness (2022) grossed ~$956M worldwide, the 6th-highest-grossing film of 2022. Elizabeth Olsen's Wanda is consistently cited as the breakout character of MCU Phase 4 and one of the defining female-coded leads of the 2020s superhero decade. The "Wanda hot witch" cultural footprint (red leather, levitation, cottagecore-Sokovian aesthetic, and Westview "1950s housewife" iconography) is extensively documented across Tumblr, Reddit, TikTok, and fan-cosplay metrics.
- **Canonical version used:** MCU Earth-199999 across the 2014–2022 run, with primary reference to WandaVision (2021) and Multiverse of Madness (2022) per the ticket's "especially" clause. Birth year 1989 (per Marvel Cinematic Universe Wiki / Marvel Database / multiple critical sources), making her ~26 in Age of Ultron (2015), ~28–29 at the time of the Snap (Infinity War, 2018), and ~33–34 in WandaVision and Multiverse of Madness (in-universe 2023). Elizabeth Olsen was 25 at filming Age of Ultron and 31–33 across WandaVision / MoM filming.

## Personality / Characterization (Excluded from Calc)

- **Tragic widow / grief arc:** The defining 2020s characterization. WandaVision is structurally a grief allegory — the entire show is Wanda's psychological response to Vision's death. Per plan, this is personality, not universal — the trauma-coded arc splits male-audience preference (some find tragic-vulnerability attractive, some find it exhausting).
- **Magic / chaos magic / reality warping:** Fantasy framing, per ticket note. Not counted as a universal-desirable trait. Some male audience finds the "powerful witch" framing attractive; preferences split.
- **Sokovian Eastern-European accent / heritage:** Aesthetic characterization. Some viewers find the accent attractive; preferences split. Not a universal-desirable slider.
- **Mysterious dark past (Sokovian orphan, HYDRA experimentation, twin-loss):** Per plan, "mysterious dark past" is explicitly flagged as personality and excluded from the calc.
- **Maternal-grief arc / Billy and Tommy:** The single most load-bearing personality beat of Multiverse of Madness — Wanda's antagonist arc is driven entirely by her desire to reach her hex-fabricated children in another universe. Heavy narrative material. Personality, not universal.
- **Sitcom-aesthetic / Westview 1950s-2000s nostalgia:** Aesthetic personality coding from WandaVision. Not a universal-desirable slider on a real partner.
- **High emotional intensity / possessiveness / capacity for villainous breakdown:** Personality. Some viewers find the intensity / "girl who could destroy the multiverse for love" framing attractive; many find it terrifying. Preferences split.
- **Witch / occult aesthetic (red leather, levitation, demon-summoning, Darkhold):** Aesthetic characterization, not a slider.
- **Sisterhood / friendship arc with Agatha Harkness (antagonistic) and with Monica Rambeau:** Plot/relationship characterization, not a universal-desirable trait.
- **Sacrificial-villain arc (collapses Mt. Wundagore on herself in MoM):** Plot resolution, not a stacked trait.

## Joint Probability

- **Method:** filter (male-target stack)
- **Naive product of marginals:**
  0.20 × 0.22 × 0.34 × 0.70 × 0.25
  = (0.20 × 0.22) = 0.044
  × 0.34 = 0.01496
  × 0.70 = 0.01047
  × 0.25 = **2.62e-3**
  (≈ 1 in 380, applied within a base population of US women aged 20–28)
- **Correlation adjustment:** Mixed-direction adjustments. Net: roughly neutral with a slight toward-rarer tilt because the male-fantasy "hot AND alternative-coded" combo violates a real-world negative correlation:
  - **Top-quintile attractiveness ↔ BMI 18.5–22:** positive (slim figures rate higher; r ≈ 0.20–0.30). Naive product OVERSTATES rarity by ~1.2–1.5x for this pair.
  - **No-kids ↔ currently single:** positive (single women more likely childless; r ≈ 0.20–0.30 in life-stage data). Naive product OVERSTATES rarity by ~1.2x.
  - **Top-quintile attractiveness ↔ currently single:** NEGATIVE in real world (more attractive women more likely partnered). Naive product UNDERSTATES rarity by ~1.2–1.4x.
  - **Top-quintile attractiveness ↔ doesn't-follow-girl-culture:** NEGATIVE in real world (Mean-Girls / popular-girl framing maps onto high mainstream-girl-culture engagement; conventionally attractive women socialize more in mainstream-girl-culture contexts on average). Naive product UNDERSTATES rarity by ~1.3–1.5x — and Wanda's stack DELIBERATELY violates this real-world correlation (the male-fantasy "top-tier hot AND alternative-witch-coded" combination is the same genre-violation as Mikaela Banes' "hot AND mechanic" or Black Widow's "hot AND not-girly" patterns).
  - **Net direction:** The two negative-correlation violations (attractiveness↔single, attractiveness↔not-girly) push toward "rarer than naive product"; the two positive halos (attractiveness↔BMI, no-kids↔single) push toward "less rare than naive product." For the FICTIONAL Wanda stack, the negative-correlation effect modestly DOMINATES because the alternative-witch-coded archetype is constructed precisely on the violation of the real-world tradeoff between conventional attractiveness and mainstream-girly engagement. Net adjustment: roughly **1.2x rarer** than the naive product.
- **Adjusted estimate:** **~3.1e-3** (≈ 1 in 320 women aged 20–28). Bracket: [1.5e-3, 6e-3].
- **Population implication:** Out of ~14M US women aged 20–28, roughly **44,000** match Wanda Maximoff's universal-desirable stack (range 23,000–84,000 across the bracket). This places Wanda in the **less-rare** band of the male-target distribution — substantially less rare than Black Widow (~1,190 matching women), Mikaela Banes (~24), or Christian Grey-style stacks. The 5-trait stack (attractiveness, BMI, single, no-kids, not-girly) is **shorter than Black Widow's 7-trait stack** because Wanda explicitly DOES NOT clear the Cool Girl Agreeableness filter or the low-neuroticism filter — those are precisely the personality dimensions where her characterization runs in the opposite direction. **Wanda's pop-culture appeal is structurally weighted toward the personality bucket (tragic-widow + witch + maternal-grief + power-fantasy) rather than the universal-desirable bucket.** This is the most informative finding for this character and probably the most interesting data point in the dataset for the methodological discussion: characters whose appeal is heavily personality-coded (vulnerability, mystery, power) score as LESS rare on this metric than characters whose appeal is more strongly stacked on universal-desirable axes (Cool Girl, low-N, frictionlessness). This is not a flaw in the methodology — it is the methodology *working as designed*. The plan deliberately separates universal-desirable sliders from personality coding, and Wanda is a clean illustration of why that distinction matters.

## Confidence Assessment
- **Overall:** medium-low
- **Trait identification:** high — Wanda's MCU traits are extensively documented across 6 films, one Disney+ series, Marvel Cinematic Universe Wiki, Marvel Database, multiple critical analyses, and academic / cultural-criticism coverage of WandaVision specifically (which has a substantial scholarly footprint as a grief allegory). Canon is unambiguous on tragic-widow status across 2020s, no-kids in real-world terms (Hex twins are explicitly fabricated), tragic / vulnerable / not-Cool-Girl personality, alternative-witch interest profile, and slim attractive body type.
- **Base rates:** medium — all base rates reused from existing base-rates.md entries with NHANES / Pew / Census / Big 5 norms provenance. No new entries added.
- **Correlation adjustments:** medium-low — the directional claim that the fictional Wanda stack violates real-world negative correlations between attractiveness and alternative-coded interest profiles is consistent with mate-market literature, but exact magnitude (~1.2x net rarer) is judgment, not measured. Bracketing of [1.5e-3, 6e-3] is wide.
- **Age-window mismatch:** **medium-low confidence reduction.** Wanda is canonically ~33–34 in the 2020s reference works, outside the 20–28 base population. Per dataset convention used for Black Widow, the joint is computed against the 20–28 base anyway, but a reviewer enforcing strict age-window correctness would either expand the base or reject the character from the population. This is the largest single confidence concern for this profile.

## Notes / Edge Cases

- **Age-window mismatch (the most consequential issue for this character):** The 2020s canonical Wanda (WandaVision 2021, MoM 2022) is ~33–34, clearly outside the 20–28 base population. The dataset convention (per Black Widow's treatment) is to use the 20–28 base anyway because the male-fantasy projection treats her as the archetypal "young attractive adult woman" regardless of canonical numerical age. Elizabeth Olsen was 31–33 during these films and visually reads as the late-20s / early-30s archetype. A stricter reviewer would either: (a) shift to a 28–38 base (~9.4% of women, ~12M in the US, with broadly similar conditional rates per filter) and recompute, in which case the joint stays in roughly the same neighborhood; or (b) reject the character from the 2020s slot for age reasons and substitute a younger 2020s love-interest archetype. Option (a) gives essentially the same answer; option (b) is a list-modification call for Jason. This is a meaningful methodological footnote but does not invalidate the joint estimate.

- **The Hex children / "no-kids" filter caveat:** WandaVision's plot creates two hex-fabricated children (Billy and Tommy) who exist for a few episodes, then cease to exist when the Hex collapses. Multiverse of Madness's plot is driven by Wanda's attempt to reach alternate-universe versions of these children. In strict filter terms — population-level "has biological children currently" — Wanda is unambiguously childless (the Hex twins were never biologically real, and the alternate-universe versions are not hers). The maternal-grief arc is the single largest characterization beat of the 2020s run, but it is in the personality bucket per plan and does not enter the joint calc. A reviewer who reads the Hex twins as canonically "Wanda's children" would zero out the no-kids filter and pull the joint to ~4.4e-3 (still in the same band). Not adjusted in the calc.

- **The personality-stack vs. universal-desirable-stack asymmetry — most important methodological finding for this character:** Wanda is structurally distinct from the dataset's other 2020s/2010s male-target characters in that her appeal is *disproportionately* loaded onto the personality bucket (tragic-widow + witch + maternal-grief + power-fantasy + Sokovian-accent + reality-warping) and *under*-loaded onto the universal-desirable bucket (only 5 filters clear, vs. 7 for Black Widow, 7+ for typical Cool Girl archetypes). This is not a flaw — it is what makes Wanda a *character* rather than a slider-maxed fantasy. The methodology is working as designed: it is correctly differentiating "Wanda is a hot witch with extremely specific characterization that some male audience finds attractive" from "Wanda's universal-desirable trait stack is unusually impressive." She scores LESS rare on the universal-desirable axis precisely because her appeal is genuinely about the witchy / vulnerable / tragic personality rather than about stacking universal sliders. This is the cleanest illustration in the dataset so far of why separating personality from universal-desirable matters: a character can be culturally enormous (WandaVision was the most-watched Disney+ release; MoM grossed nearly $1B) without being a universal-desirable slider-stack. **Flag prominently for final-graph annotations.** It is also a useful counterpoint to Black Widow (ticket 010), whose Cool Girl + low-N + spy-archetype profile loads more heavily onto universal sliders.

- **Cool Girl filter explicitly NOT cleared — distinguishes from Black Widow:** Wanda is structurally NOT a Cool Girl. She is willful, possessive, escalates conflict, and becomes the *primary antagonist* of MoM. She kills the Illuminati. This is the structural difference between her and Black Widow's stack — Black Widow clears Cool Girl + low-N + tomboy-coded; Wanda clears tomboy-coded only (the alternative-girl-culture filter). The 2020s decade slot includes both archetypes (Wanda the tragic powerful witch, alongside other 2020s heartthrobs), and the ticket order produces a useful within-decade comparison.

- **Low-neuroticism filter explicitly NOT cleared:** Wanda is the most emotionally turbulent female lead in the MCU. The grief response in WandaVision is *literally* a clinical-level grief breakdown that becomes a public crisis. Marvel itself consulted a grief counselor in WandaVision's production. Bottom-quartile neuroticism is not a defensible read. This is also a structural distinction from Black Widow (whose Red Room conditioning produces extreme emotional regulation).

- **Elizabeth Olsen conflation:** Same caveat as Megan Fox / Mikaela, Jennifer Aniston / Rachel, Scarlett Johansson / Black Widow. A meaningful share of Wanda's "cultural reach" is partly Elizabeth Olsen's career-wide reach (sister to Mary-Kate and Ashley Olsen, lead of Sorry For Your Loss, Martha Marcy May Marlene). For trait-stack purposes, the calc is on the character — the reach annotation conflates somewhat with the actress.

- **Multiverse of Madness as villain-arc:** In MoM, Wanda is the villain. Some male audience members find this disqualifying; others find it *more* attractive (the "girl who would burn down the multiverse for love" framing is a recurring fan-discourse trope). Per plan, this is personality / characterization and not a universal-desirable slider. Not adjusted in the calc; flagged as one of the more genre-violating personality coding choices in the male-target dataset.

- **Power fantasy as personality:** The "she has reality-warping magic" element is per the ticket explicitly fantasy framing, not a universal-desirable trait. This is the right call — magic powers are characterization, not a slider the median male audience would universally push up on a real partner. (Compare to the female-target stack, where Edward Cullen's vampirism and Christian Grey's billionaire-status are quantified differently because they map to wealth/status thresholds; magic does not have a real-world analog in the universal-desirable trait list.)

- **Comparison to other 2020s and prior heartthrobs:** Wanda's adjusted joint (~3.1e-3) is substantially less rare than Black Widow (~8.5e-5, ticket 010) and Mikaela Banes (~1.7e-6, ticket 008), comparable to Rachel Green (~1.3e-2, ticket 006) but slightly rarer due to the additional "alternative-witch-coded" filter. The 2020s decade signal: Wanda represents the *personality-heavy* path to male-fantasy iconography (tragic + powerful + alternative), distinct from the *universal-stacked* path represented by characters like Black Widow. Both paths produce huge cultural reach. The dataset captures this distinction by design — that's the point of separating personality from universal traits.
