## Methodology notes

**Single-segment — no carve-out needed.** Blue Bird Corporation reports as a single operating segment: all bus manufacturing. The consolidated income statement IS the school bus business. No pro-rata allocation required; all line items are used directly. This is the cleanest structure in the B2B panel (cleaner than Boeing, Deere, or GE Vernova, which all require segment extraction).

**FY2024 income statement (from 10-K and earnings press release):**
- Revenue: $1,347.2M (+19% YoY, driven by 9,000 units sold, 6% unit increase, and mix shift toward higher-ASP EV buses)
- COGS: $1,091.0M (derived: revenue minus gross profit of $256.2M)
- Gross margin: 19.0%
- SG&A: $116.8M (increase of $29.6M from FY2023's $87.2M; SG&A growth attributed in MD&A to labor cost increases, share-based compensation, and higher R&D)
- Operating income: $139.3M (vs. $51.7M in FY2023; +169% — the EV transition is in its payoff phase)

**R&D not separately disclosed — biggest data gap.** Blue Bird does not break out R&D as a distinct income-statement line; it is folded into SG&A. The FY2024 MD&A explicitly attributes part of the $29.6M SG&A increase to "research and development expense increases," confirming R&D growth but providing no dollar figure. Estimate of ~$20M (approximately 1.5% of revenue) is derived from: (a) the fact that SG&A grew $29.6M YoY with R&D cited as one of three drivers, (b) Blue Bird's EV program scale (704 EV buses sold in FY2024, 2,000th EV bus delivered — meaningful engineering investment required), and (c) typical industrial OEM R&D ratios of 1–2% of revenue for active product-line investment. The R&D estimate has a plausible range of $13–30M; even at the extremes, build_pct shifts only ±0.5 percentage points (from ~94.8% to ~95.8%) because COGS is so dominant.

**Independent dealer invisibility.** Blue Bird's ~50 North American dealers are independent businesses — their cost structures (showrooms, inventory carrying, local marketing, service bays) do not appear on Blue Bird's books. This is the same structural blind spot as Deere's dealer network. The sell-side total here represents only what Blue Bird spends to support and enable the dealer channel, not what dealers themselves spend. Net effect: actual sell-side cost in the full distribution chain is higher than reported, though for B2B school-bus sales, dealer cost structures are lean relative to consumer retail.

**Sell side is near-zero advertising.** School districts buy buses through formal public-procurement RFPs — not brand awareness or consumer advertising. Blue Bird spends essentially nothing on paid media. The "sell" spend is entirely personnel-and-support: regional sales reps working district relationships, bid/proposal preparation, dealer co-op and training programs, and trade-show presence (National Association for Pupil Transportation, etc.). This is the structural floor for the sell side; there is no analog to Tide's $3B+ advertising budget here.

**EPA Clean School Bus Program context.** The federal EPA Clean School Bus Program distributes grants directly to school districts to purchase zero-emission and low-emission buses. Blue Bird is a major beneficiary of this demand-pull program (record EV order book in FY2024 driven partly by EPA grant rounds). However, the grant flows to districts as buyers, not to Blue Bird directly — it does not appear in Blue Bird's revenue or cost structure. The $80M DOE manufacturing grant announced July 2024 is a capital subsidy for EV plant expansion; it hits the balance sheet (capital), not the income statement, and does not affect the build/sell split. Both programs are noted as demand drivers, not cost-structure factors.

**COGS classification.** Blue Bird's COGS includes: raw materials and purchased components (chassis, powertrain, body panels), direct assembly labor, manufacturing overhead (plant depreciation, utilities, tooling amortization), and distribution to dealers. Per plan.md rubric, distribution costs inside COGS are classified as build. All of COGS = build.

**Aftermarket parts.** Blue Bird operates a modest parts business (spare parts for the existing fleet of ~480,000 school buses it has built over the decades). This revenue and its associated COGS are included in consolidated figures and classified as build, consistent with ticket instructions.

**Confidence: high.** Revenue, COGS, SG&A, and operating income are directly from the FY2024 10-K of a US-listed single-segment company. The only estimation involved is splitting SG&A among R&D (build), sell, and overhead — and because COGS at $1,091M (81% of revenue) dominates the numerator, the ratio is highly insensitive to that split. A 100% error in the R&D estimate ($20M vs. $0M or $40M) moves build_pct by less than 1 percentage point. Calling this "high" is warranted.
