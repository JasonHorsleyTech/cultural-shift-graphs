## Build breakdown ($ millions)

A KitchenAid stand mixer is a premium small-domestic-appliance manufactured in the US (Greenville Ohio plant for the global stand-mixer supply) with a heavy zinc die-cast metal construction (the iconic tilt-head body and bowl-lift platform are zinc/aluminium die-cast rather than plastic, which is part of why the units are heavy and durable — typical Artisan unit ~22 lbs and Professional bowl-lift ~26 lbs), a 250-575 watt direct-drive motor depending on tier, a planetary mixing action (the trademark slow-rotation-of-the-attachment combined with planetary orbit around the bowl), enamelled or polished stainless-steel bowls, and the famous power hub on the front for attachments (meat grinder, pasta roller, food processor, ice cream maker — the 'lifetime of attachments' upsell strategy is central to the brand's economic model). The SDA Global segment EBIT margin of 12.3% in FY2024 reflects a premium small-appliance category with healthy gross-margin economics anchored by the KitchenAid brand. The cost structure differs from Whirlpool's major-appliance MDA NA segment (6.7% EBIT margin) primarily through (a) higher gross margin reflecting brand pricing power, (b) somewhat higher consumer-marketing intensity supporting the gifting/aspirational positioning, (c) lower channel-mediated sales-force intensity since stand mixers sell heavily through gift channels (Williams-Sonoma, Crate & Barrel, Sur La Table, Amazon Wedding Registry, online direct) where the Whirlpool national-account team has less primary involvement.

- **COGS — raw materials** (zinc die-cast metal alloy for the body and head — the heavy zinc construction is part of brand equity and meaningfully raises the BOM cost versus plastic-bodied competitor mixers; aluminium die-cast components for the planetary mechanism and motor housing; copper motor windings; permanent-magnet motor components; planetary gears and pinion gears; stainless-steel mixing bowl and lid; enamel-coated bowl variants for premium tiers; rubber feet and seal components; electronic speed-control board for variable-speed models with sensor feedback; flat beater, dough hook, and wire whip standard attachments — all metal, machined or formed; power-hub coupling mechanics; cord and plug; powder-coat paint in the famous KitchenAid colour palette — Empire Red, Onyx Black, White, Silver Metallic, plus the seasonal 'colour of the year' SKUs which drive holiday-gifting demand; packaging — the iconic KitchenAid retail box is a premium presentation with the dedicated insert tray, instruction booklet, and recipe insert): estimated at ~56% of SDA Global revenue. **$870**

- **COGS — direct labor and factory overhead** (US plant labor at Greenville Ohio for KitchenAid stand mixers, plus US/Mexico/Asia manufacturing labor for other SDA Global products including food processors, blenders, espresso machines, attachments; plant utilities, maintenance, indirect labor, quality assurance, plant management; the Greenville plant is a unionised US facility with average loaded labour cost meaningfully above the segment average — but for the KitchenAid stand mixer brand-equity mode, the 'made in Ohio' status is part of the value proposition): ~9% of revenue. **$140**

- **COGS — manufacturing capex amortization** (depreciation share for Greenville Ohio plant tooling, die-casting equipment, paint lines, assembly lines, plus tooling and capex at other SDA Global plants for other product lines; the Greenville plant has been continuously upgraded over the past two decades with newer die-casting and finishing equipment): ~3% of revenue. **$50**

- **Inbound freight and component logistics** (component shipments from suppliers — motors, electronic boards, attachments, packaging — to the Greenville Ohio plant and to other SDA Global manufacturing locations): ~2% of revenue. **$30**

- **R&D / product engineering** (NA and global share of consolidated R&D for SDA Global product lines; small-appliance R&D is structurally lighter than major-appliance R&D — KitchenAid stand mixer platform is a mature design with incremental refresh cadence focused on attachments, motor power and noise reduction, smart-connectivity features for newer 'KitchenAid Yummly' connected appliances, and refresh of the food processor and blender lines; espresso/coffee products are newer and carry meaningful engineering load; product styling and colour development for the seasonal colour-of-the-year strategy): **$30**

- **Distribution and outbound logistics** (finished-goods shipping from Greenville Ohio and other SDA plants to retail distribution centres and direct-to-consumer fulfilment; KitchenAid stand mixers are bulky relative to their value but parcel-shippable rather than LTL — a key economic distinction from major appliances; e-commerce direct-to-consumer fulfillment via the kitchenaid.com site is increasingly meaningful, plus Williams-Sonoma, Crate & Barrel, Sur La Table, Amazon, Costco, Bed Bath & Beyond's successor channels, Target, Walmart): ~3% of revenue. **$50**

- **Customer support / warranty service** (KitchenAid warranty operations covering the standard 1-year hassle-free replacement policy on stand mixers — a notable feature being that within the warranty period, KitchenAid will ship a full replacement unit rather than repair, which is unusually consumer-friendly for the category; the longer-tail repair operations through KitchenAid authorised service centres for out-of-warranty repairs which are surprisingly common given the 20-40 year typical lifecycle of a stand mixer; the KitchenAid Customer eXperience Center call-centre handling stand-mixer questions, attachment compatibility queries, recipe support, colour-availability questions; warranty parts inventory; service tech training for repair partners; the unique 'lifetime of attachments' relationship that keeps consumers returning to the brand for new attachments and accessories on stand mixers they bought decades ago): **$25**

- **Quality and engineering assurance non-plant** (regulatory testing and certification — UL safety certification, FCC for connected models, energy compliance; reliability engineering for the long-life-cycle product positioning that is a core part of KitchenAid brand equity; food-safety regulatory compliance for food-contact components like bowls and beaters — NSF certification for some product lines): **$15**

- **Brand-equity and product-design investment** (KitchenAid product styling, the colour-development team responsible for the seasonal colour-of-the-year strategy, the design refresh cycle that keeps the iconic silhouette while incorporating modern elements, partnerships with culinary institutions like the CIA and James Beard Foundation, brand-equity stewardship — these are typically classified as marketing in pure FAS categories but I have classified them as build because they are about the *product itself* rather than about advertising, similar to how we treat industrial design at Apple): **$13**

## Sell breakdown ($ millions)

KitchenAid brand marketing in the small-appliance category is structurally distinct from major-appliance marketing: the purchase decision is gifting/aspirational rather than replacement-driven (the typical replacement cycle for a stand mixer is 20-40 years per longstanding industry observation — many consumers receive the product as a wedding or graduation gift and never replace it during their lifetime), the channel mix is heavily weighted toward gift retail (Williams-Sonoma, Crate & Barrel, Sur La Table, department-store kitchen sections, online direct, Amazon Wedding Registry, Bridge.com and similar registry platforms) rather than the major-appliance national-accounts channel, and the brand consideration is anchored to long-form content marketing (recipes, cooking content, partnerships with chefs and content creators) rather than purely broadcast advertising. The result is that sell-side spending is concentrated in (a) brand-led marketing including measured media and content marketing, (b) retail-channel co-marketing with the gift-channel retailers, (c) seasonal trade promotion concentrated in Q4 holiday and Q2 registry windows.

- **Advertising and measured media** — KitchenAid brand advertising including TV (the iconic 'red mixer on the kitchen counter' creative IP across the brand's history), digital video and connected-TV (YouTube cooking-content channel partnerships, Meta and Instagram where the colour-of-the-year creative drives gifting-season engagement, TikTok where #StandMixer and #KitchenAid hashtags drive significant organic and paid reach), paid search (heavy Google Ads presence on category terms — 'best stand mixer', 'KitchenAid Artisan vs Pro', 'wedding gift kitchen' — plus brand defensive bidding), retail-media networks (significant spend with Williams-Sonoma media, Amazon Ads, Target's Roundel, Walmart Connect where KitchenAid SKUs are sold), display and programmatic (with sophisticated audience segmentation around wedding-registry intent, gifting moments, cooking-content engagement, demographic and life-stage audiences), partnerships with cooking-content creators and chefs (Joanna Gaines, Stanley Tucci, various Top Chef alumni, food-content YouTubers), seasonal colour-of-the-year campaign creative including the annual launch event and PR push. Brand-level measured-media spend estimated globally at $35-45M with heaviest weighting in US and Europe. **$40**

- **Sales force compensation and channel marketing team** (the smaller commercial organisation calling on gift-channel retailers Williams-Sonoma, Crate & Barrel, Sur La Table, department-store kitchen buyers at Macy's and Bloomingdale's, registry-platform partnerships at Bridge/Zola/The Knot, plus the Amazon and Costco accounts that overlap with major-appliance national-account team; international commercial team for KitchenAid distribution in 100+ markets; the Inside Sales team that handles smaller specialty retail and the e-commerce direct-to-consumer business): **$45**

- **Trade promotion, retail co-op, and gifting-season activation** (vendor allowances and market-development funds paid to gift-channel and mass retailers for promotional pricing support during Mother's Day, Father's Day, July 4, holiday gifting, and Black Friday windows — note that stand mixers often see very-targeted promotional pricing during these windows because the gifting market is concentrated; in-store endcap and counter-display fees in the kitchen sections of department stores; co-marketing with Williams-Sonoma and similar specialty channels for cooking-class events and product-demonstration days; the registry-channel marketing investments — KitchenAid is the iconic wedding-registry product and the brand invests in registry-platform marketing and event sponsorship; the seasonal colour-of-the-year activation including PR launches and influencer-seeded units; cooking-event sponsorships at culinary schools and food festivals; the KitchenAid Cookbook program and recipe-content production that feeds the content-marketing engine). ## Methodology notes

**Why SDA Global, not MDA NA — segment correction from ticket spec.** The ticket specification originally suggested 'MDA North America (small appliances within)' on the assumption that small appliances are a sub-line within MDA NA. This was incorrect: per Whirlpool's FY2024 reporting structure, small kitchen appliances report in the SDA Global segment, which is structurally distinct from MDA NA. KitchenAid as a brand spans both segments — KitchenAid major appliances (refrigerators, dishwashers, ranges, ovens, wall ovens, cooktops, hoods) report in MDA North America, while KitchenAid small appliances (stand mixers, food processors, blenders, attachments, espresso machines) report in SDA Global. The KitchenAid stand mixer is unambiguously a small appliance and belongs to SDA Global. Using SDA Global as the segment is the correct unit-of-analysis and produces meaningfully different (and more honest) economics than using MDA NA — SDA Global has 12.3% EBIT margin versus MDA NA's 6.7%, reflecting the structurally different cost structure of premium small appliances. This correction is acknowledged transparently rather than shoehorning the analysis into the originally-specified-but-wrong segment.

**Differentiation from Whirlpool washer (ticket 023).** Both products are Whirlpool segment-level units of analysis, but they differ across nearly every cost-structure dimension:

- **Gross margin**: SDA Global ~33% (premium small appliance) vs MDA NA ~18% (mass-market major appliance). The KitchenAid brand carries pricing power that the Whirlpool/Maytag/Amana mass-market brands do not.
- **EBIT margin**: SDA Global 12.3% vs MDA NA 6.7%. The premium positioning translates through to operating margin.
- **Marketing intensity**: SDA Global ~10.4% of revenue (sell + overhead) vs MDA NA ~9.1%. Higher consumer-marketing intensity to support gifting/aspirational positioning, but only modestly higher because the small-appliance category overall has lower marketing intensity than CPG.
- **Channel mix**: SDA Global is gift/specialty/online (Williams-Sonoma, Crate & Barrel, Amazon registry, online direct) vs MDA NA is national-accounts retail (Lowe's, Home Depot, Best Buy, regional dealers).
- **Purchase psychology**: SDA Global is gifting/aspirational (typical replacement cycle 20-40 years, often gifted at major life events) vs MDA NA is replacement-driven (typical 8-12 year cycle, replace-when-broken).
- **Build_pct**: 87.5% for KitchenAid stand mixer vs 92.6% for Whirlpool washing machine. The KitchenAid build_pct is meaningfully lower (5 points) because of the higher consumer-marketing intensity of the premium gifting brand. Both are still high (above 85%) compared with CPG-style products in the 50-70% range, but the differentiation between sibling Whirlpool products is real and meaningful.

**Why fiscal year 2024.** The FY2024 10-K is the most recent annual filing (filed February 13, 2025). The SDA Global segment EBIT margin of 12.3% in 2024 is down from 13.9% in 2023, reflecting post-pandemic normalization in the small-appliance gifting category and continued competitive pressure from Breville (premium peer) and SharkNinja (value disruptor). The 12.3% datapoint is at the lower end of the historical SDA margin band (which has run ~13-15%) but the structural cost-ratio differentiation versus MDA NA remains stable.

**Brand-level vs segment-level — the SDA Global segment is heavily concentrated in KitchenAid.** SDA Global as a segment includes KitchenAid stand mixers plus KitchenAid food processors, blenders, espresso machines, and attachments, plus other small-domestic-appliance brand product lines outside of KitchenAid. KitchenAid is the dominant brand within SDA Global; the stand mixer is the iconic SKU and likely the single largest revenue line; the segment ratio is therefore a reasonable proxy for the KitchenAid stand mixer specifically. Within SDA Global, the stand-mixer line probably has somewhat better unit economics than the average — higher gross margin from brand pricing power and the long-tail attachment-upsell economics — but probably similar marketing intensity given the brand-led nature of the entire KitchenAid line. The directional finding (build_pct in the 86-89% range) is robust to whether we use SDA Global or attempt to isolate the stand mixer specifically.

**KitchenAid brand advertising — Kantar-confirmed ~$25M US, ~$40M global.** The brand-level Kantar measured-media data has historically run $20-30M in the US (peak $28M in 2022 during the pandemic-cooking demand). For 2024 the US figure likely declined to $20-25M with the post-pandemic small-appliance demand normalization. International markets — KitchenAid sells in 100+ countries with strong presence in UK, Germany, France, Italy, Australia, Canada — likely add another $15-20M of measured-media spend. Total brand-level advertising estimated at $35-45M, classified as the bulk of SDA Global advertising in the build/sell breakdown.

**R&D classification — embedded in COGS per Whirlpool 10-K convention.** Whirlpool reports R&D inside cost of products sold per the accounting-policies footnote rather than on a separate income-statement line. SDA-attributable share of consolidated R&D is estimated at ~$30M, classified as build per the project rubric. SDA R&D is structurally lighter than major-appliance R&D — the stand-mixer platform is a mature design with incremental refresh; new-product engineering centres on attachments, motor refresh, smart-connectivity features for newer connected products, and refresh of the food-processor/blender/espresso lines.

**Greenville Ohio plant — domestic manufacturing as brand-equity.** All KitchenAid stand mixers worldwide are manufactured at the Greenville Ohio plant. This is a deliberate brand-equity positioning — 'made in Greenville Ohio USA since 1941' is part of retail messaging and consumer perception. The plant employs ~1,000 hourly workers (United Steelworkers represented per public reporting) and the loaded US labour cost is meaningfully above what Asia-manufactured competitors face. This pushes the build-side number up in absolute dollars but is offset by the brand pricing power that allows KitchenAid to charge a premium for a 'real metal' stand mixer ($300-700 retail) versus plastic-bodied competitors ($80-200 retail). Other SDA Global product lines (food processors, blenders, attachments, espresso) are manufactured in a mix of US, Mexico, and Asia depending on product line and historical sourcing decisions.

**Trade promotion concentration in Q4 holiday and Q2 registry season.** The stand-mixer category is structurally seasonal — Q4 holiday gifting accounts for ~40-50% of annual unit sales, Q2 wedding-registry / Mother's Day accounts for another ~20-25%. Trade promotion and retail co-op spend is concentrated in these windows. The colour-of-the-year programme is timed to peak gifting season, with the annual colour announcement in spring driving registry/Mother's Day demand and the PR-led colour features driving holiday demand. KitchenAid's promotional pricing strategy uses the seasonal-colour rotation to maintain price discipline on core SKUs while creating limited-edition variants that command premium pricing.

**Hassle-free replacement warranty as a build-side investment.** KitchenAid's 1-year hassle-free replacement warranty (the brand will ship a complete replacement unit rather than repair an in-warranty unit) is unusually consumer-friendly for the category and is part of brand equity. The cost of this policy is captured in the customer-support/warranty line ($25M) and is materially higher in cost-per-unit than a typical repair-only warranty would be. This is part of the premium positioning and the long-life-cycle brand promise.

**'Lifetime of attachments' as long-tail revenue.** KitchenAid stand mixers have a power-hub on the front that accepts a lineup of attachments (meat grinder, pasta roller, food processor add-on, ice cream maker, vegetable sheet cutter, juicer, sausage stuffer, etc.). The attachment economics are part of SDA Global revenue — many consumers buy attachments years or decades after the original mixer purchase, generating long-tail revenue from the installed base. The attachment line is structurally high-margin (relatively simple metal-and-plastic accessories at premium pricing) and contributes positively to SDA Global gross margin. This 'attachment ecosystem' is a meaningful contributor to the segment economics versus a stand-mixer-only product line.

**What's surprising in the numbers.**

- **KitchenAid build_pct of 87.5% is meaningfully lower than Whirlpool washer's 92.6%, but still high by consumer-products standards.** The 5-point gap represents the meaningful difference in marketing intensity between a premium gifting/aspirational brand (KitchenAid) and a mass-market replacement-driven brand (Whirlpool washer). Both are still in the high-build territory characteristic of durable goods — neither is anywhere near the 50-70% build_pct range typical of CPG products. The differentiation between sibling Whirlpool brands is real but smaller than you might expect from the brand-positioning gap.

- **SDA Global segment EBIT margin of 12.3% is materially better than MDA NA (6.7%) — the brand premium translates clearly through the income statement.** The premium-small-appliance category has structurally better unit economics than mass-market major appliances, and KitchenAid as the dominant brand within SDA Global is the principal driver. This is consistent with the build_pct gap (87.5% vs 92.6%) — the lower build_pct for KitchenAid means more sell-side spending per unit of revenue, but the brand pricing power more than compensates so that EBIT margin is substantially higher.

- **The 'made in Greenville Ohio' positioning is real and economically meaningful.** Unlike most consumer-electronics or small-appliance peers who have moved manufacturing offshore, KitchenAid has continuously manufactured stand mixers at a single US plant since 1941. The loaded US labour cost is high, but the brand pricing power justifies it economically. The stand-mixer line is one of the few remaining fully US-manufactured premium consumer-durables of its scale.

- **Stand-mixer category has unusually long product lifecycle (20-40 years typical).** This drives the gifting/aspirational positioning — purchases are infrequent and emotionally weighted, often associated with major life events (weddings, graduations, milestone birthdays, housewarmings). The KitchenAid brand has institutionalised this positioning through the Wedding Registry channel partnerships and the colour-of-the-year programme that creates collectibility/giftability around what would otherwise be a utilitarian appliance. This is structurally distinct from the replacement-driven economics of major appliances.

- **Trade promotion is the largest single sell-side line ($75M), not advertising ($40M) — same inversion as MDA NA but for different reasons.** For major appliances, trade promotion dominates because the channel-mediated purchase requires getting on the showroom floor at Lowe's/Home Depot. For KitchenAid stand mixers, trade promotion dominates because the gifting-season concentration (Q4 holiday + Q2 registry) requires retailer-channel investment to drive promotional pricing during those windows. Different channel logic, similar quantitative outcome.

- **The colour-of-the-year programme is essentially a marketing-as-product strategy.** KitchenAid releases a new featured colour each year (recent: Empire Red, Hibiscus, Pistachio, Beetroot), drives PR around the launch, and channels demand through the colour-rotation that creates limited-edition urgency. This blurs the build/sell boundary — colour development is product-design work (build) but the colour-of-the-year activation is marketing (sell). I have classified the colour-development team as build (~$13M brand-equity investment) and the activation/PR/launch event as sell (within trade promotion line). A different classification choice would shift ~$10-20M between build and sell but does not change the build_pct meaningfully.

**Confidence: medium.** Major inputs (segment net sales, segment EBIT, consolidated COGS and SG&A) come from Whirlpool's own filings. The major triangulations (KitchenAid brand-level advertising, SDA share of consolidated R&D, gross-margin assumption for SDA Global, brand-vs-segment classification for KitchenAid stand mixer specifically) are estimated within reasonable bands and the directional finding (build_pct in the 86-89% range) is robust to those classification choices. The medium-confidence rating reflects the brand-vs-segment triangulation (KitchenAid stand mixer specifically vs SDA Global as a whole) and the necessity of estimating Kantar brand-level advertising rather than reading it directly from a financial filing. The high-quality segment disclosure for SDA Global makes this a more confident analysis than would otherwise be possible for a brand-level question, but the residual brand-vs-segment uncertainty keeps it below the 'high' bar.
