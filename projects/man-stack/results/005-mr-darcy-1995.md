# Fitzwilliam Darcy

## Character Summary
- **Source work:** Pride and Prejudice (BBC, 1995, six-episode miniseries; Andrew Davies screenplay, Simon Langton dir., Colin Firth as Darcy). Source novel: Jane Austen, *Pride and Prejudice* (1813).
- **Audience:** female-targeted
- **Decade slot:** 1990s
- **Cultural reach proxy:** UK BBC One peak ~10–11M viewers for the final episode (Oct 1995); US A&E premiere ~3.7M households (Jan 1996, A&E's highest-rated programming at the time); UK VHS sold out within ~2 hours of release. Triggered the "Darcymania" phenomenon (Firth on tabloid covers; the lake-plunge scene voted #1 "most memorable TV moment" in multiple UK polls). Recurrent presence on every "best book/screen boyfriend" list for 30+ years.
- **Canonical version used:** 1995 BBC adaptation, Colin Firth's portrayal. Where the 1995 adaptation adds material (the lake scene, the on-screen fencing practice), those additions are treated as canonical to *this* version. Novel-only details used only when the adaptation faithfully carries them through.

Traits considered and excluded:
- **Intelligence ≥ 130 IQ:** Darcy is well-read, articulate, and exercises sound estate management, but the 1995 adaptation does not depict genius-tier intellect (no on-screen scholarly display, no Latin/Greek demonstration, no problem-solving feats). His intelligence reads as "cultured Cambridge-educated gentleman of his class" rather than top-2%-by-IQ. Excluded for conservatism; flagged as low-confidence call.
- **Musical / artistic skill:** Darcy explicitly does not play. Georgiana and Elizabeth play piano; Darcy listens. No painting, no composition. Excluded.
- **Languages beyond English:** Not depicted speaking French, Latin, or Greek on screen in the 1995 adaptation. A Cambridge-educated gentleman of 1813 would almost certainly have classical Latin/Greek and at least passable French, but those aren't canonical to the *adaptation*. Excluded for strict reading; would add 0.20–0.03 if included (one or two languages).
- **Additional expert skills:** Estate management at Pemberley scale is functionally CEO-tier work, but it's intrinsically tied to inherited wealth and would double-count income. Riding/fencing/dancing are common-class skills for Regency gentlemen, not "expert in X" in the Han Solo sense. None counted.

## Joint Probability

- **Method:** threshold (female-target stack)
- **Naive product of marginals:**
  0.04 × 2e-4 × 0.20 × 0.094 × 0.05
  ≈ 7.52e-9
  (≈ 1 in 130 million)
- **Correlation adjustment:**
  - Height ↔ income: weakly POSITIVELY correlated in real-world labor data (~1–2% earnings lift per inch above mean; Persico-Postlewaite-Silverman 2004, Case-Paxson 2008). Naive product slightly OVERSTATES rarity for this pair (~1.3–1.5x). Pulls joint *less rare* than naive.
  - Income ≥$10M ↔ age 28–38: NEGATIVELY correlated. The marginal income rate is dominated by older filers (most $10M+ earners are 45+); among men aged 28–38 specifically, the conditional rate of $10M+ income is roughly 3–5x lower than the marginal. Naive product UNDERSTATES rarity for this pair. Pulls joint *more rare* than naive.
  - Family-unencumbered ↔ age 28–38: weakly negative (older = more likely orphan, but 28–38 is mostly independent). Approximately neutral here.
  - Fitness ↔ age 28–38: weakly positive (peak fitness years overlap with prime window). Modestly *less rare* than naive.
  - Net: the income↔age negative correlation dominates the female-side bias direction (per plan methodology note: female-side stacks tend to be *more rare* than naive product). Estimated ~2–3x rarer than naive.
- **Adjusted estimate:** ~2.5e-9 (≈ 1 in 400 million). Bracket: [1e-9, 7e-9].
- **Population implication:** Out of ~127M US adult men, expected matches ≈ 0.3 — i.e. fewer than one US adult man matches Darcy's universal-desirable stack at the *conservative* $10M-income threshold. If the implied threshold is raised to $30M+ (top 0.01%, more faithful to Darcy's top-of-Regency-society economic position), expected matches drop to ~0.06 — effectively zero in the US, ~2–3 globally.

## Confidence Assessment
- **Overall:** medium
- **Trait identification:** high — Darcy's traits are well-documented across 200+ years of canon analysis and the 1995 adaptation specifically (riding, fencing, swimming all on-screen; canon age 28; orphan + Georgiana well-established; £10K/year explicit in the novel). Height is the softest call (Firth listed at 6'2" but some sources say 6'1.5", borderline at threshold).
- **Base rates:** medium — height (NHANES) and age (Census) are well-grounded. Fitness top-quintile and family-unencumbered carry the same uncertainty as in earlier tickets. Income ≥ $10M is solid for the marginal; the main uncertainty is which threshold to use for Darcy (default $10M chosen for conservatism; implied threshold is meaningfully higher).
- **Correlation adjustments:** medium — directional reasoning is well-supported (height-income positive, young-rich negative are both established in labor economics), but the magnitude (~2–3x) is judgment, not a measured joint distribution.

## Notes / Edge Cases

- **Wealth threshold choice:** £10,000/year in 1813 placed Darcy in roughly the top 0.01% of the British population (Hume 2013, Copeland; ~400 families nationally with £5K+, ~200 with £10K+). MeasuringWorth's relative-income calculation puts this at ~£15–20M/year (~$18–25M USD) modern equivalent — well above the default $10M threshold but below "billionaire tier." The default $10M was used for the calc because it's the plan's documented threshold and gives a *conservative* (less rare) estimate. If a reviewer reads Darcy as $30M+ tier (top 0.01%, base rate ~1e-4), the joint becomes ~5e-9; if they read him as $100M+ tier, ~2.5e-9. The "most defensible" estimate is roughly halfway between $10M and $30M; using $10M anchors the calc to a verifiable IRS-cited base rate.
- **Height edge case:** Colin Firth is listed at 6'2" by IMDb and Wikipedia but at 6'1" or 6'1.5" by some other sources. Counted as meeting the ≥6'2" threshold given the most common public listing; if a reviewer reads Firth at 6'1.5" (≈186 cm), height is borderline and would shift the base rate from 0.04 to ~0.07. This adjustment alone would roughly halve the calculated rarity. The novel's "tall" descriptor is uninformative on the exact threshold.
- **Family-unencumbered partial:** Darcy is a true orphan (both parents dead, no living spouse, no children) but is co-guardian of his 16-year-old sister Georgiana. Per the plan's "orphan / estranged / no obligations" predicate, this is a *partial* match — he has a meaningful ongoing dependent-relationship even though he has no parents/spouse/kids. Counted as meeting the trait at the standard 0.05 base rate; a stricter reading that excluded sibling-guardianship would *raise* the base rate (more men have "no parents/spouse/kids" than "no parents/spouse/kids/dependents") and reduce calculated rarity ~30–50%.
- **Fitness call:** The 1995 adaptation deliberately physicalizes Darcy in ways the novel doesn't — fencing scene (Davies/Langton invention), lake plunge (Davies invention), aggressive riding. These are canonical to *this* version and justify top-quintile fitness even though the novel itself is silent on Darcy's physicality. A pure-novel reader might exclude this trait.
- **Cambridge-educated gentleman intelligence:** Excluded for conservatism, but in 1813 a Cambridge admittee (~250–300/year for England's ~10M population) was extraordinary educational selection. If "Cambridge admit" maps to top 1–2% intellectual capacity in modern terms, intelligence ≥130 IQ would be added at base rate 0.02, dropping the joint to ~5e-11. Flagged but not included to avoid retrospective IQ-fitting.
- **Compared to other 1990s female-targeted leads:** Darcy's stack is wealth-and-status-heavy, modest on the skill-stacking dimension (no virtuoso-pianist-pilot-mechanic stacking like Christian Grey or Han Solo). His rarity comes overwhelmingly from the income trait (2e-4 alone dominates). This is a meaningful pattern signal — Regency-rooted heartthrobs lean on inherited rank rather than slider-maxed skill stacks.
- **Personality carries enormous load:** Like Han Solo, much of Darcy's appeal is *personality and arc* (pride, humbling, the Hunsford letter, the bridge-of-tenderness in Episode 6) rather than universal-desirable slider count. The trait stack alone undersells why Darcy has been the female-targeted heartthrob benchmark for 200+ years. This is the same finding flagged in the Han Solo result: some heartthrobs land on charisma + archetype + arc, others on stack rarity.
