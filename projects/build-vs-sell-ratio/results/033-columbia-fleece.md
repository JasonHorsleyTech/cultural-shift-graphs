## Methodology notes

**Segment selection.** Columbia Sportswear reports as a single operating segment. The consolidated income statement is the correct and only source — no segment allocation required. The company owns Columbia, SOREL, Mountain Hardwear, and prAna brands, but Columbia is overwhelmingly dominant (~85%+ of revenue per management commentary), so the consolidated figures are a good proxy for the Columbia fleece jacket.

**COGS classification.** The full cost of sales line ($1,677.5M, producing 50.2% gross margin) is classified as build. Per the 10-K MD&A, Columbia's cost of sales includes contract manufacturing costs, inbound freight, customs/duties, quality control, and outbound distribution to wholesale customers. The ticket note confirms "COGS includes freight." This is a contract-manufacturing model — Columbia designs and outsources production globally (primarily Asia), so "manufacturing capex" is essentially zero; the owned-asset depreciation related to product is minimal and captured inside COGS.

**Advertising classification.** Columbia does not break out advertising expense as a separate line item in the 10-K income statement or in the accounting policy footnotes. The $199M figure (5.9% of net sales) is sourced from management commentary on the FY2024 earnings call. This is the primary reason for a "medium" rather than "high" confidence rating — the figure is disclosed but not auditable from the 10-K alone. Columbia indicated plans to increase marketing spend to 6.5% of sales in 2025, suggesting FY2024's 5.9% is accurate management guidance rather than a rough estimate.

**DTC retail operations classification.** Running ~500 owned retail stores worldwide is a selling activity, not a product-building activity. The ~5,630 DTC/retail employees plus store lease obligations represent the cost of maintaining the direct selling channel. Estimated at ~$450M, derived from: ~5,630 retail employees × ~$45k average annual cost = ~$253M in compensation, plus ~500 stores × ~$300k average annual occupancy = ~$150M in leases and utilities, plus $47M in other DTC operating costs (e-commerce platform, POS systems, packaging). This estimate is the most uncertain item in the breakdown.

**Distribution center operations.** The 10-K employee breakdown lists approximately 1,080 distribution center employees, and the MD&A confirms distribution costs are embedded in SG&A (not COGS). Estimated DC operating cost of $141M includes: ~1,080 DC employees × ~$50k average annual cost = ~$54M in labor, plus DC facility leases, equipment depreciation, and systems. Columbia operates distribution centers in Portland (OR), Robards (KY), and international facilities.

**Product design / R&D.** Columbia does not separately disclose R&D. Product design and technical innovation (OutDry, Omni-Heat, Omni-Tech fabric technologies) is embedded in SG&A. Estimated at ~$45M (1.3% of revenue), consistent with industry norms for technical apparel brands that invest meaningfully in material science but do not operate pharmaceutical-scale labs.

**SG&A residual → overhead.** After removing advertising ($199M), DTC retail ($450M), wholesale selling ($110M), distribution center operations ($141M), and product design ($45M), the remaining SG&A is $498.9M — classified as overhead (corporate G&A, executive compensation, finance, legal, IR, IT infrastructure, and non-manufacturing depreciation). Compared to revenue, this is 14.8%, elevated but plausible for a mid-size company with a relatively complex DTC + wholesale + international structure. P&G achieves better overhead leverage at 2-3× Columbia's scale.

**D&A treatment.** Total D&A is $130.4M (per cash flow statement). Manufacturing-related D&A on owned assets is minimal (contract mfg model). The bulk of D&A is for owned retail stores, distribution centers, and IT — flowing through SG&A and allocated within my sell/build/overhead buckets above. No double-counting with capex ($59.8M in FY2024).

**Confidence: medium.** Columbia is a US-listed, single-segment company — favorable for clean data. However, advertising is not disclosed as a separate line item in the 10-K (only via management commentary), and the SG&A breakdown into DTC/wholesale/distribution/design/overhead requires estimation without line-item disclosure. The COGS line is clean. The income statement itself is not in question; the uncertainty is in the classification of $1,443.9M of SG&A.
