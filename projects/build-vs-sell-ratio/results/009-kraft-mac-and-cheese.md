## Methodology notes

**Brand-level vs. consolidated framing — the deliberate choice that differentiates this ticket from 008.** KHC reports financials at the consolidated and three-segment level (North America, International Developed Markets, Emerging Markets); it does not break out Heinz ketchup, Kraft Mac & Cheese, or any other brand as a reporting unit. For ticket 008 (Heinz ketchup), the published headline used KHC consolidated FY2024 financials directly, yielding a build_pct of 88.3% with brand-specific economics discussed only in methodology notes. For this ticket, the plan-level guidance and the ticket-specific guidance both call for brand-level differentiation: "the segment-level ratio will be the same as Heinz ketchup unless we have brand-level Kantar data to differentiate" and "Build_pct probably 70-80% (slightly more build-heavy than Heinz ketchup if M&C marketing is lower)." Using consolidated numbers here would produce 88.3% identical to ticket 008 — a methodological tie that obscures whatever real differentiation exists between the two brands. So this ticket follows the brand-level approach: scale KHC's cost structure to the brand's disclosed $1.3-1.5B revenue range, and adjust the advertising line based on external brand-specific evidence (MediaRadar "under $100M") and KHC management commentary on Mac & Cheese's Brand Growth System / Win Big designation.

**Where the differentiation comes from — and where it doesn't.** The two brands sit inside the same parent, share the same SG&A infrastructure, and use the same parent-level tax and corporate cost allocations. The differentiation is structurally limited to two channels:

1. *Brand revenue size.* Heinz brand globally is estimated at $7-8B; Kraft Mac & Cheese is $1.3-1.5B. This affects absolute dollars but not the build_pct ratio.
2. *Brand-specific advertising intensity.* This is where the ticket asked us to look. Heinz ketchup has ~70% US category market share and a near-monopoly foodservice position — promotional intensity is structurally low. Kraft Mac & Cheese has ~41% category share (down ~2 percentage points since 2021), faces increasing private-label competition with quality parity in blind taste tests, and is locked in active competition with Annie's, Velveeta, Banza, Goodles, and store brands. KHC has explicitly designated Kraft Mac & Cheese as a Brand Growth System / Win Big brand requiring elevated marketing investment to defend share, and announced in 2024-2025 a 75% marketing increase across kid-favored brands including Mac & Cheese.

If the ticket's hypothetical were inverted — "Mac & Cheese marketing is *lower* than Heinz ketchup, so build% should be higher" — that's not what the evidence shows. MediaRadar's "under $100M" anchor (working estimate $75M on $1,400M revenue = 5.4%) is *higher* than the parent's 4.0% consolidated advertising-to-revenue, and is comparable to or slightly above the Heinz brand-specific allocation discussed in ticket 008's methodology notes (Heinz brand-specific advertising estimated at 5-7% of brand revenue). Net result: Kraft Mac & Cheese brand-level build_pct of 86.9% comes in *below* Heinz ketchup's published 88.3% (consolidated) and is approximately equal to Heinz ketchup's brand-specific 86% estimate noted in 008. The two brands have nearly identical brand-level cost economics, with the small remaining gap explained by Kraft Mac & Cheese's slightly higher advertising intensity.

**MediaRadar "under $100M" — interpretation.** MediaRadar tracks measured-media advertising (national TV, digital display, programmatic, paid search, magazine, OOH). It does not include trade promotion, slotting fees, or in-store shopper marketing, all of which are sell-side spend that flows through SG&A but isn't "advertising" in the MediaRadar sense. The $75M working estimate is the MediaRadar-bracket measured-media line. Adding shopper marketing and trade activation (typically 1.5-3x measured media for a high-velocity grocery brand fighting private-label competition) would push the total brand-level "sell" investment higher; the residual SG&A allocation is intended to capture this. Sensitivity: setting brand-specific measured-media advertising at $50M (low end of plausible) yields build_pct of 87.6%; setting at $100M (top of MediaRadar's bracket) yields 85.7%. The headline 86.9% is the midpoint, consistent with a $75M measured-media line.

**Brand revenue $1,400M — disclosure source and uncertainty.** The $1.3-1.5B North America net sales figure for Kraft Mac & Cheese was disclosed publicly during the September 2025 separation discussion (Food Business News, "A closer look at the Kraft Heinz split"), in the context of where Mac & Cheese should sit between Global Taste Elevation Co. and North American Grocery Co. The brand is produced predominantly inside Taste Elevation plants (significant production overlap with Heinz pasta sauces and Kraft cheese powders), but its consumer-facing positioning is closer to NA Grocery convenience meals. The disclosure is recent and credible, but it's a single data point — the brand revenue could plausibly be anywhere from $1.2B to $1.6B depending on how KHC defines the brand boundary (boxed shelf-stable only vs. including Mac & Cheese Cups, Deluxe, frozen, and licensed Mac & Cheese sauces in jars). The $1,400M midpoint balances the disclosed range; sensitivity is bounded — the build_pct is dominated by COGS-and-advertising ratios, both of which scale roughly proportionally with brand revenue.

**COGS-to-revenue at 62% — modestly tighter than parent's 65.3%.** Mac & Cheese economics are simpler than the parent's commodity-meat-and-cheese-heavy mix. The product is durum wheat semolina pasta plus a dehydrated cheese/whey/salt sauce powder — high yield on a per-pound basis, low input cost per box (estimated $0.30-0.45 box-level COGS at retail price points of $1.49-2.99), strong gross margin. Public commentary positions Mac & Cheese inside the Taste Elevation plant network where EBITDA margins (26%) run higher than the NA Grocery network (22%). 62% of revenue is a midpoint estimate — using parent-proportional 65.3% would shift $46M from profit to build, raising build_pct to 87.7% (still tightly clustered with 86.9% headline). Using a more aggressive 58% (best-case for a high-velocity branded staple) would shift the same dollars the other direction, giving 86.0%. The COGS uncertainty is the second-largest source of error after advertising.

**No brand-specific impairment.** KHC's FY2024 GAAP impairments totaling $3,669M (goodwill $1,638M + intangible asset $2,031M) were predominantly tied to legacy Kraft Foods brands acquired at the 2015 merger price — disclosed targets in prior years' impairments include Oscar Mayer trademarks, Lunchables trademarks, Maxwell House, and natural cheese trademarks. Kraft Mac & Cheese, as an actively defended Brand Growth System / Win Big brand on which KHC is increasing marketing investment, is not in the disclosed impairment list. Allocating a proportional share of impairment to this brand would mis-attribute parent-level accounting reversals to a brand that's still considered an asset KHC is investing behind. The brand-level allocation excludes impairment from overhead and assigns the dollars to profit instead — yielding implied operating margin of 23.9% rather than KHC consolidated GAAP 6.5%. This is closer to the parent's Adjusted Operating Income margin (23.1%), which is the right reference frame.

The headline 86.9% is bracketed by ~85-88% across reasonable advertising assumptions. The implied range of ~3-5 percentage points of variance is the dominant uncertainty in this brand-level estimate.

**Trade promo netting applies symmetrically.** As noted in ticket 008, KHC consolidated net sales of $25,846M are after-trade-promotion. Adding trade promo back to both revenue and to sell-side spend would bring the consolidated build_pct down from 88.3% to roughly 74-76%. The same dynamic applies at the brand level: if brand revenue of $1.3-1.5B is after-trade-promo, then gross sales would be $1.5-1.8B and the brand-level build_pct of 86.9% would compress toward 73-76% under a gross-sales framing. Following the as-disclosed convention used across all CPG entries (Cheerios, Oreos, Coca-Cola, Heinz ketchup) for cross-product comparability.

**Why Kraft Mac & Cheese build_pct lands at 86.9% — the three-factor explanation in shorthand:**

1. *Marginal lift from elevated brand-specific advertising.* Brand-level advertising is ~5.4% of revenue vs. parent-consolidated 4.0%. This shifts ~1.4 percentage points of revenue from build to sell, dropping build_pct by approximately 1.4 points from the parent's 88.3% baseline.
2. *Slightly tighter COGS than parent.* Mac & Cheese gross margin is structurally a few points better than KHC's commodity-heavy mix. This pushes build_pct up by approximately 0.3-0.5 points.
3. *Net effect.* 88.3% (parent consolidated) − 1.4 (advertising lift) + 0.0 (COGS approximately offsetting) = ~86.9%.

**Confidence: medium.** Three layers of uncertainty:
1. Brand revenue ($1,400M midpoint) is disclosed but bracketed at $1.3-1.5B — ~7% one-sided error band.
2. Brand-specific advertising ($75M working estimate) is anchored to MediaRadar's "under $100M" qualitative bracket plus KHC's Win Big designation — could plausibly be $50M to $100M, generating ~2 percentage points of build_pct variance.
3. COGS-to-revenue (62%) is a triangulation between parent's 65.3% consolidated and the Taste Elevation plant network's higher gross margin — could plausibly be 58-66%, generating ~2 percentage points of variance.

Combined sensitivity: build_pct robust to ~85-89% range across reasonable assumptions. The headline 86.9% sits comfortably in the middle of that range.

**Comparison to ticket 008 prediction.** The ticket guidance suggested KMC build_pct of "70-80% (slightly more build-heavy than Heinz ketchup if M&C marketing is lower)." The actual brand-level estimate of 86.9% is *outside* the predicted range and is *below* (not above) the published Heinz ketchup consolidated number of 88.3%. The reason: the ticket's prior assumption was that M&C marketing would be lower than Heinz ketchup. Evidence shows the opposite — M&C is in a more competitive category (41% share vs. 70%) and is an actively invested-behind brand getting elevated marketing intensity. The 86.9% headline is approximately equal to ticket 008's brand-specific Heinz estimate (86%) noted in methodology, indicating the two flagship KHC brands have nearly identical brand-level cost economics — exactly the "if they're identical" outcome the ticket flagged as a possibility.
