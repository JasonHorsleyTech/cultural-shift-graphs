## Methodology notes

**Segment selection.** Used the combined Wind segment as reported in Note 25. Revenue breaks out as: Onshore Wind $7,781M (80%), Offshore Wind $1,377M (14%), LM Wind Power (blade manufacturing) $542M (6%). The 10-K does not break out cost of revenues, SG&A, or R&D by sub-segment (onshore vs. offshore), so a combined segment approach was the only option using disclosed data. The ticket correctly anticipated this — combined-segment was the right call.

**Wind is loss-making — reported honestly.** The segment EBITDA is $(588)M on $9,701M revenue (−6.1% margin). After adding D&A of $350M, the segment operating loss is $(938)M. This is reported faithfully as profit_millions = −938. Build + Sell + Overhead exceed Revenue by $938M — the segment is absorbing more cost than it is collecting in revenue. This is the honest picture.

**Offshore Wind is the primary loss center.** Offshore (Vineyard Wind, Dogger Bank) contributed roughly $700M in charges in 2024 alone from blade events, execution delays, and contract loss provisions. Onshore Wind turned a corner — management reported it delivered "high single-digit EBITDA margin" in 2024 and its most profitable quarter in three years in Q4. The combined segment loss is real but masks a bifurcated picture: Onshore is trending toward profitability, Offshore is still hemorrhaging.

**Segment EBITDA vs. GAAP operating loss.** GE Vernova's segment reporting uses EBITDA (excluding D&A, restructuring, and "significant higher-cost restructuring programs"). The segment EBITDA table in Note 25 also excludes acquisition/disposition gains and certain non-operational items. Adding back Wind D&A of $350M gives an approximate segment operating loss of $(938)M. This excludes $141M of Wind restructuring charges reported separately in Note 23 — those are excluded from segment EBITDA by policy and not included in profit_millions to stay consistent with the segment-EBITDA framework.

**LTSA (long-term service agreement) revenue treatment.** Wind services revenue of $1,642M is substantially LTSA income — GE Vernova services an installed base of ~23,000 wind turbines under service agreements. Per the plan.md rubric, service revenue from keeping products working is "build." The costs of servicing (labor, parts, warranty) are embedded in Cost of revenues ($9,513M, which combines equipment and services costs at the segment level). Classified as build throughout — consistent and directionally correct.

**Cost of revenues line excludes D&A.** Per 10-K Note 25 footnote (b): "Excludes depreciation and amortization expenses." D&A is separately disclosed in the segment assets table at $350M for Wind FY2024. Added to COGS as a build cost — manufacturing depreciation is unambiguously a cost of building turbines.

**Advertising.** No advertising expense is disclosed anywhere in the Wind segment reporting, nor in the consolidated financial statements. Wind turbines are sold via direct enterprise sales to a small number of utility and IPP customers — the addressable buyer universe globally is probably 300-500 organizations. There is no consumer advertising, no trade promotion, and no slotting-equivalent. Advertising is effectively $0. Classified as $0 in sell.

**SG&A split (60% sell / 40% overhead).** The segment SG&A of $566M (also excluding D&A per footnote b) contains: direct sales force compensation and commissions, technical sales support and bid engineering, regional account management, and back-office G&A (finance, legal, HR, IT). For a capital-equipment OEM with a direct B2B sales model and a long sales cycle (18–36 months from lead to order), bid/proposal and technical sales support typically dominate SG&A. A 60/40 sell/overhead split is a reasonable midpoint estimate; the uncertainty range is probably 50-70% sell. A 10-percentage-point shift in the split moves build_pct by roughly 0.3 percentage points — immaterial.

**Standalone spin-off caveat.** GE Vernova completed its spin-off from GE on April 2, 2024. This is the first full fiscal year as a standalone public company. Prior to the spin-off, GE allocated certain corporate costs (IT, HR, legal, R&D infrastructure) to GE Vernova via allocation methodologies disclosed in Note 24. These allocations may have differed from what GE Vernova now incurs independently. For the three months pre-spin (Jan–Mar 2024), segment costs include some allocated GE corporate costs; post-spin, these are GE Vernova's own costs. This creates a minor discontinuity in the 2024 cost base, which the company has not quantified segment-by-segment. The overall effect is likely small (management noted GE Vernova had already established standalone capabilities for centralized services before the spin). Confidence rating reflects this residual allocation uncertainty.

**Confidence: medium.** Wind segment revenue and EBITDA are directly reported as a named segment in a US 10-K — high confidence on those figures. D&A by segment is also directly disclosed. The uncertainty sources are: (1) the SG&A sell/overhead split is estimated, not disclosed; (2) the combined segment mixes a (slightly profitable) Onshore business with a deeply loss-making Offshore business, so the build_pct reflects a blended picture that doesn't represent either sub-business cleanly; (3) the spin-off transition period creates minor cost-allocation uncertainty. Medium is the right call.
