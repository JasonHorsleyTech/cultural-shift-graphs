# Yennefer of Vengerberg

## Character Summary
- **Source work:** The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt (CD Projekt Red, 2015) — the canonical anchor — plus its expansions Hearts of Stone (2015) and Blood and Wine (2016). Originally a literary character from Andrzej Sapkowski's novel cycle (1986–1999, *The Last Wish* through *The Lady of the Lake*). Subsequently in the Netflix Witcher series (2019+, Anya Chalotra). Background presence in The Witcher 2 (2011, mentioned but not playable), but the W3 portrayal is the load-bearing pop-culture anchor.
- **Audience:** male-targeted
- **Decade slot:** 2010s (per heartthrobs.md — locked at slot 43)
- **Cultural reach proxy:** The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt has sold 60M+ copies as of 2024 (CD Projekt Red FY2023 reports cited 50M+ in May 2023, with continued growth); the Witcher franchise (games + books) has surpassed 85M units. W3 holds 250+ Game of the Year awards across publications, and the romance with Yennefer is the canonical "true" romance per CDPR's narrative design (Triss is the alternate, and the canonical ending sequence requires the Yennefer route). Netflix's Witcher Season 1 (2019) was Netflix's most-watched original series at launch with ~76M household views in its first 28 days. Yennefer is consistently in the top tier of "best video game waifus" / "hottest female video game characters" rankings across IGN, GameRant, ScreenRant, GameSpot, and TheGamer retrospectives 2015–2024, and is one of the most-cosplayed female video-game characters of the late 2010s. The "Yennefer vs. Triss" online discourse — which woman should Geralt end up with — was a defining 2015–2016 gaming-culture phenomenon and a key driver of the character's male-fantasy footprint.
- **Canonical version used:** Per ticket instruction, the **Witcher 3 era** (post-transformation adult sorceress) anchored on the W3 game, with the Netflix Anya Chalotra portrayal as a secondary canonical reference. Yennefer is canonically born ~1173 AR and W3 is set in 1272 AR, placing her at canonical age ~99 — but she appears as a young adult woman in the late-20s/early-30s range due to the slowed-aging effect of mage trance / magical training (canonically, sorceresses arrest visible aging in their 20s and remain visually that age for ~3 centuries). The post-transformation visual presentation — raven-black hair, violet/indigo eyes, slim-elegant figure, signature lilac-and-gooseberries scent — is the canonical iconography. The pre-transformation hunchback / half-elf origin is foundational characterization but the male-fantasy iconicity is exclusively the post-transformation form. The Netflix Anya Chalotra portrayal (2019+) is consistent with the W3 anchor on all load-bearing trait fronts (post-transformation beauty, sorceress profession, infertility, Geralt-romance) and shifts the imagery toward a more grounded live-action aesthetic but does not contradict the W3 anchor.

## Joint Probability

- **Method:** filter (male-target stack)
- **Naive product of marginals:**
  0.20 × 0.22 × 0.70 × 0.25
  = (0.20 × 0.22) = 0.044
  × 0.70 = 0.0308
  × 0.25 = **7.70e-3**
  (≈ 1 in 130, applied within a base population of US women aged 20–28)
- **Correlation adjustment:** Mixed-direction adjustments. Sparser stack than Black Widow / Wonder Woman because Yennefer fails Cool Girl, low neuroticism, and currently-single (canonically partnered with Geralt):
  - Top-quintile attractiveness ↔ BMI 18.5–22: positive (slim figures rate higher; r ≈ 0.20–0.30). Naive product OVERSTATES rarity by ~1.2–1.5x for this pair.
  - No-kids alone (no single-status to pair with — single is excluded from this stack).
  - Top-quintile attractiveness ↔ doesn't-follow-girl-culture: NEGATIVE in real world (Mean-Girls / popular-girl framing maps onto high mainstream-girl-culture engagement). Naive product UNDERSTATES rarity by ~1.3–1.5x — and the Yennefer stack DELIBERATELY violates this real-world correlation (the male-fantasy "top-tier hot AND aristocratic-haute-couture-NOT-mass-market-girl-culture" combination is the same genre-violation pattern as Black Widow's "hot AND not-girly," translated to the high-femme-aristocratic register rather than the tomboy register).
  - **Net direction:** The positive halo (attractiveness ↔ BMI) and the negative correlation (attractiveness ↔ not-girly) roughly cancel, with the negative correlation effect slightly dominating because the Yennefer stack is genre-violating in the same direction as the warrior-princess archetypes (just at the high-femme rather than tomboy register). Net: roughly **1.1x rarer** than the naive product (very mild adjustment given the thinner stack).
- **Adjusted estimate:** **~8.5e-3** (≈ 1 in 120 women aged 20–28). Bracket: [4e-3, 1.5e-2].
- **Population implication:** Out of ~14M US women aged 20–28, roughly **120,000** match Yennefer's universal-desirable stack (range 56,000–210,000 across the bracket). This places Yennefer in the **moderate band** of the male-target distribution — substantially less rare than Black Widow / Wonder Woman (~1,190 matches each) and Ciri (~48,000 matches), and roughly comparable in stack-thinness to Princess Leia (~175,000 matches). The structural pattern: Yennefer's appeal is overwhelmingly *characterization-heavy* (sorceress profession, mage longevity, signature visual styling, complex personality, canonical Geralt-romance soul-bond, Aretuza-transformation arc, infertility trauma, Ciri co-parenting) and *under*-loaded on universal-desirable sliders. The methodology correctly differentiates the *type* of appeal: Yennefer is a "complex sorceress fans love for her arc and her relationship" rather than a "Cool Girl optimized for low-friction male fantasy" or a "warrior-princess slider-stack." Notably, this places her LESS rare on the universal-slider axis than Ciri, which is a counterintuitive finding driven primarily by Yennefer's exclusion from the currently-single filter (canonical Geralt-partnered) — Ciri has no canonical partner and thus clears the single filter that Yennefer fails. **A reviewer who reads "currently single" as "modally available across the run" rather than "canonically partnered at the anchor moment" would re-include the filter and pull Yennefer's adjusted joint to ~2.9e-3 (~1 in 350), placing her in the same Wanda/Ciri band.** Both interpretations are flagged.

## Notes / Edge Cases

- **Currently-single filter — the central methodological caveat:** Yennefer is canonically Geralt's romantic partner across the books, the canonical W3 romance route (which CDPR has identified as the "true" / soulmate ending — anchored on the djinn-wish soul-bond from Sapkowski's *The Last Wish*), and the Netflix series. The W3 plot is centrally about Geralt searching for Ciri *with and for* Yennefer; they reunite early in the game and remain coupled through the canonical ending. Strictly applying the "currently single" filter would zero out the calc for a character who is canonically partnered. **Per the Princess Leia precedent (ticket 004 — canonically with Han Solo at the ROTJ moment, "currently single" excluded from calc), the single filter is excluded here.** A reviewer who applied the filter on a "modally available across the run" basis would re-include it (Yennefer and Geralt do separate at points across the books, and there is a Triss-romance branch in W3 where Yennefer is not Geralt's partner), pulling the joint to ~2.9e-3 (~1 in 350) before correlation adjustment — which would place Yennefer in the same band as Wanda / Ciri rather than the Leia band. **The principal interpretation excludes the filter; the alternative interpretation is documented.** This is the single most consequential methodological choice for this character.

- **"Doesn't follow mainstream girl culture" — the high-femme-aristocratic interpretation:** Yennefer is highly fashion-engaged in-universe (signature attire of black-and-white sorceress robes, signature lilac-and-gooseberries scent, refined aristocratic bearing). She is NOT tomboy-coded — she is high-femme-aristocratic-coded. The base-rate definition for this filter explicitly covers "anti-mainstream aesthetic" alongside tomboy-coded hobbies, and Yennefer's haute-couture-sorceress aesthetic qualifies as anti-mass-market-mainstream even though it operates at a different femininity register than Black Widow / Wonder Woman / Ciri. **A stricter reviewer interpretation that limited the filter to tomboy-coded characters would exclude Yennefer here**, dropping her stack to 3 traits (attractiveness, BMI, no-kids) and pulling the naive joint to ~3.1e-2 (~1 in 32) before adjustment. The principal interpretation includes the filter on the anti-mass-market-mainstream basis; the stricter interpretation is flagged.

- **Multiple canonical versions and the W3 anchor:** Yennefer exists in three main canonical layers: Sapkowski's books (1986–1999, where she is the central love interest of Geralt across multiple novels), The Witcher 3 (2015, where she is the canonical "true" romance), and the Netflix series (2019+, Anya Chalotra). The book version and the W3 version are highly consistent on character traits; the Netflix version diverges somewhat in characterization (more political-rebellion-coded, more visibly traumatized, somewhat more Cool-Girl-adjacent than the books) but does not contradict the W3 anchor on universal-desirable traits. For Yennefer specifically, this asymmetry is testable: her magical capability IS part of her appeal, but the question is whether the median male audience reads it as universal-desirable (slider up = better) or as characterization (a feature of her specific archetype).
