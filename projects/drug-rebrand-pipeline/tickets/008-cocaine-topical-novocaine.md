# Ticket 008 — Cocaine → topical anesthetic + Novocaine/Lidocaine genealogy

## Demon identity

Cocaine (coke, blow, snow, powder). Schedule II in the US. Demonization peaks: 1980s crack epidemic (see ticket 033 for the crack-specific arc), 1986 Anti-Drug Abuse Act, Len Bias's death (June 1986) as cultural inflection point. Powder cocaine cultural meaning has softened since — wealthier connotation than crack — but remains schedule II and culturally illegal.

## Medicine identity

Two arms:
1. **Cocaine hydrochloride topical solution** — still FDA-approved and prescribed for nasal/ENT surgery as a topical anesthetic and vasoconstrictor. Lannett Company / Genus Lifesciences (the brand "Numbrino" was approved 2019; Goprelto was approved 2017). Literally cocaine, still used in operating rooms.
2. **Novocaine / Lidocaine / -caine family** — synthetic descendants. Procaine (Novocain) was developed by Einhorn in 1905 specifically as a cocaine alternative. Lidocaine (Xylocaine) 1948. Articaine 1969. These are chemically *descended from* cocaine but not the same molecule — they retain the local-anesthetic action without the dopamine-reuptake CNS effects.

## Arc type

`street_to_medicine` for arm 1 (cocaine HCl topical — identical molecule, same name, just clinical context). For arm 2 (Novocaine/Lidocaine) the relationship is genealogical, not identical — this is closer to "pharma improved on the demon drug and kept the local-anesthetic part." Important to capture both.

The historical arc also has cocaine-in-Coca-Cola (1886–1903 roughly), so cocaine itself spans `failed_medicine_rebranded` (was a tonic and cure-all; pulled) too.

## Chemistry hypothesis

- Cocaine HCl topical: **identical** to street cocaine. Same molecule, same salt form even.
- Novocaine / Lidocaine: **cousin** at best — they retain the ester / amide local-anesthetic scaffold but lack the tropane structure and the monoamine reuptake action. Functionally cousins for the local-anesthetic job; entirely unrelated for the CNS-stimulant job. Mark in research that the cousin classification only holds for the anesthetic mechanism.

## Source hierarchy

1. **FDA approval letters** for Goprelto (2017) and Numbrino (2019) — the modern cocaine HCl prescription products.
2. **DEA cocaine prescription / medical-use data** — small but real numbers, available via DEA quotas.
3. **Historical medical literature on cocaine as an anesthetic** — Koller 1884, Halsted's nerve block work, the original medical use.
4. **Novocaine and Lidocaine development history** — Einhorn 1905, Löfgren 1943.
5. **Coca-Cola formulation history** — well-documented; cocaine removed in 1903–1929 depending on source.
6. **CDC cocaine use stats and overdose data** for demon-era signals.

## Caveats specific to this drug

- Powder cocaine demonization is genuinely lighter than crack demonization in US culture — this is a 1980s race/class disparity. Note in research.
- Cocaine remains the *only* DEA Schedule II local anesthetic still in clinical use. Most ENT surgery now uses lidocaine + epinephrine, but cocaine HCl is still on hospital formularies. Very low awareness of this in the public.
- Novocaine/Lidocaine awareness is high (everyone has had a dentist say "I'm giving you Novocaine") but the genealogical link to cocaine is poorly known.
- This is *two* rebrand stories: the medical-cocaine arm (identical, low awareness) and the dental-anesthetic-descendants arm (sibling-or-cousin, high awareness of medicine name but not the link).
