# CJ Parker

## Character Summary
- **Source work:** Baywatch (1989–2001 syndicated run; CJ Parker present from season 3, 1992–1997), syndicated US TV drama; portrayed by Pamela Anderson. CJ is one of the longest-tenured female lifeguards on the show and the character most directly responsible for Baywatch's status as the iconic male-fantasy program of the 1990s.
- **Audience:** male-targeted
- **Decade slot:** 1990s
- **Cultural reach proxy:** At its peak in the mid-1990s, Baywatch was reportedly the most-watched television show in the world, syndicated in 140+ countries with weekly viewership estimates around 1.1 billion (Guinness World Records 1996 entry for largest TV audience). Pamela Anderson became the defining male-fantasy figure of the decade — multiple Playboy covers (a record 13 covers, more than any other model), recurring placement on FHM and Maxim "sexiest" lists throughout the 1990s, and the 1996 V.I.P. spin-off built largely around her star power. The slow-motion red-swimsuit beach run (popularly called the "Baywatch run") is one of the most-parodied pieces of male-gaze visual grammar in modern television.
- **Canonical version used:** Per ticket instruction, the Pamela Anderson tenure on Baywatch (1992–1997, seasons 3–7). CJ is coded as a young adult lifeguard throughout this run — Anderson herself was 25 at her introduction and 30 at her departure. Character age is consistently coded as mid-20s without ever being pinned to a specific number; she is treated as in the 20–28 base-population window for filter purposes.

  Note: the body-composition factor (0.02) is already a joint rate for BMI 18.5–22 AND natural DD+, NOT a product of marginals. Multiplying the separate marginals would have given ~5–10x rarer than the joint.
- **Correlation adjustment:** Mixed-direction adjustments, with the body-composition joint already applied at the joint rate (so no further BMI×bust adjustment needed). Remaining pairs:
  - Top-quintile attractiveness ↔ low neuroticism: positive halo (r ≈ 0.10–0.15). Naive product overstates rarity.
  - Top-quintile attractiveness ↔ Cool Girl Agreeableness: positive halo (r ≈ 0.10–0.15). Overstates.
  - Top-quintile attractiveness ↔ high SOI: positive (more attractive women report more short-term mating opportunities; r ≈ 0.10–0.20). Overstates by ~1.2–1.3x.
  - Cool Girl Agreeableness ↔ low neuroticism: positive (both load on stability factor; r ≈ 0.20–0.25). Overstates.
  - No-kids ↔ currently single: positive (~1.2x overstatement).
  - High SOI ↔ Cool Girl Agreeableness: weak NEGATIVE (Cool Girl/agreeable women lean serial-monogamous; r ≈ -0.10 to -0.15). Naive product UNDERSTATES rarity for this pair by ~1.2x.
  - Top-quintile attractiveness ↔ currently single: NEGATIVE in real world (more attractive women more likely partnered). Understates by ~1.2–1.4x.
  - Top-quintile attractiveness ↔ doesn't-follow-girl-culture: NEGATIVE in real world (Mean-Girls / popular-girl framing maps onto high mainstream-girl-culture engagement). Understates by ~1.3–1.5x — but the magnitude of this fictional violation is MILDER for CJ than for Mikaela, because beach-spiritual-girl is closer to conventional-femininity-positive than tomboy-mechanic.
  - **Net direction:** The positive halos (attractiveness↔low-N, attractiveness↔Agreeableness, attractiveness↔high-SOI, low-N↔Agreeableness, no-kids↔single) are several pairs and collectively meaningful. The negative correlations (attractiveness↔single, attractiveness↔not-girly, SOI↔Agreeableness) cut the other direction but are fewer and milder for this character. Net adjustment: roughly 1.2–1.4x LESS rare than naive product (positive halo dominates, partly because the "not-girly" violation is mild for new-age-coded CJ).
- **Adjusted estimate:** ~1.1e-6 (≈ 1 in 920,000 women aged 20–28). Bracket: [5e-7, 3e-6].
- **Population implication:** Out of ~14M US women aged 20–28, roughly **15** match CJ Parker's universal-desirable stack (range 7–42 across the bracket). CJ lands in the rare end of the 1990s male-target distribution, structurally similar in joint probability to Mikaela Banes (~24 matching) but driven by a DIFFERENT critical-rarity trait: the body-composition joint (0.02) is the dominant scarcity contributor for CJ, where Mikaela's was the car-expertise (0.02). Both characters ride a single 0.02-tier rarity factor on top of the standard "attractive, slim, single, no kids, Cool Girl, low-N" base. The 1990s "buxom blonde lifeguard" archetype and the 2000s "hot-girl-mechanic" archetype are arithmetically close — they just stack rarity on different axes.

## Notes / Edge Cases

- **The Pamela Anderson / CJ Parker conflation:** Same caveat as Rachel Green / Jennifer Aniston and Mikaela / Megan Fox. Much of the cultural reach for CJ is actually Anderson's career-wide reach (Playboy covers, Maxim placements) which extends well beyond Baywatch. For trait-stack purposes, the calc is on the character — the reach annotation conflates somewhat with the actress, but the cultural footprint of the character ON Baywatch alone (most-watched show in the world at peak) is sufficient on its own for "iconic male-target heartthrob of the 1990s" status.
- **Augmented-bust / canonical-natural projection:** Anderson's bust was famously augmented (multiple breast implant procedures, publicly discussed). The character CJ within the fictional frame is presented as having the body naturally — the male-fantasy projection is on the rare natural joint of slim BMI + DD+, which the joint base rate of 0.02 captures. A reviewer who insists on the literal-actress reading would say the character's body composition is achievable through cosmetic intervention, not a natural joint, which would change the philosophical framing but not the math (the male-fantasy projection is the relevant frame for the project, not the literal physical achievability route).
- **The "doesn't follow mainstream girl culture" call:** Borderline. CJ is new-age / spiritual / beach / unmaterialistic, which is one flavor of "not like other girls" (1990s new-age flavor). Mikaela's tomboy-mechanic flavor and CJ's new-age-beach flavor are both alternative-coded relative to mainstream-girl-culture (Cosmo, fashion celebrity, reality TV) — so I included the filter for CJ — but it's a softer fit than for tomboy-coded characters. A reviewer who excludes this filter would pull the joint to ~6e-6 (~1 in 170,000), still rare but less so.
- **High-SOI inclusion:** CJ has rotating romantic storylines but is not coded as promiscuous in the modern sense. The "open to romantic interest" framing of the show puts her in the high-SOI bracket as defined by the SOI-R top-quartile filter (open to short-term mating, not guarded). Borderline — a stricter reviewer might exclude this filter and pull the joint to ~6e-6. Included here on the grounds that the character's romantic-interest-of-the-week structure functionally requires high-SOI coding.
- **Lifeguard profession as scene-setting, not aligned-hobby:** Per ticket note ("Lifeguard profession is interest/scene-setting"). I excluded "plays the protagonist's hobbies" filter for CJ. This is consistent with the project's general convention of not double-counting show premises as character traits. A reviewer who reads "beach culture" as the audience-aligned hobby for the male target would add another 0.20–0.30 filter and pull the joint less rare to ~3–4e-7, which would actually make CJ rarer, not more common — a small effect, but flagging it.
- **CJ as the 1990s male-target counterpart to the standard pattern:** CJ's stack is structurally similar to Mikaela Banes' (one strong 0.02-tier scarcity factor on top of the standard "hot, slim, single, no kids, Cool Girl, low-N" base), but the rarity-driving trait differs: body composition joint for CJ vs. car expertise for Mikaela. The 1990s "buxom blonde lifeguard" template (CJ, Yasmine Bleeth, Carmen Electra-era Baywatch) and the 2000s "hot-girl-mechanic" template are different axes of male-fantasy stacking but produce close joint probabilities. The Man Stack hypothesis (that female-target stacks violate negative correlations more flagrantly than male-target stacks) is consistent with CJ — the violation of attractiveness ↔ low-girl-culture-engagement is mild here, and the magnitude is far smaller than the Christian Grey / Edward Cullen pattern of stacking 4–5 deeply negatively-correlated achievement traits.
