## Build breakdown ($ millions)

Clorox bleach manufacturing is mature, commoditized, and capital-light vs. heavy industrial. Sodium hypochlorite (the active ingredient) is a low-cost commodity made by reacting chlorine with caustic soda; HDPE bottles dominate the packaging cost; the technical formulation is decades-old. The "build" cost stack is dominated by raw materials, packaging, and freight rather than R&D or manufacturing capex. Bleach is also unusually heavy-and-bulky relative to its retail price (a one-gallon jug retails ~$3-5 and weighs ~9 lb), which puts a meaningful share of the cost stack into outbound distribution — heavy-product CPG categories carry distribution burden that consumer-electronics or beauty categories do not.

Because Clorox does not break out cleaning-only or bleach-only economics, all numbers below are an allocation of the Health and Wellness segment ($2,485M sales, $719M adjusted EBIT) using consolidated cost ratios as a starting point and then adjusting for category-typical patterns (premium-brand pricing on commodity inputs lifts the segment's gross margin above the consolidated 43.0%; heavy advertising sustains brand premium; freight-heavy product lifts distribution).

- COGS — raw materials (sodium hypochlorite produced in-house at Clorox's Reno, NV plant and at chlor-alkali partner facilities; sodium hydroxide, surfactants, fragrances), HDPE bottles and corrugated cases, on-site mixing/filling/packaging labor, contract manufacturing for some adjacent SKUs, manufacturing utilities and overhead: 1050
- Distribution / logistics — outbound freight to retailer DCs (heavy product, 9 lb/gallon × millions of gallons; LTL and full-truckload to Walmart, Target, Costco, Kroger, Home Depot, Lowe's, Amazon DCs), warehousing, palletization: 250
- R&D / engineering — formulation work (concentrated bleach reformulations, "Clorox Plus" line extensions, Clorox Disinfecting Wipes formulation, professional-product germicidal formulation efficacy testing for EPA registration, packaging engineering on bottle redesign): 45
- Customer support — relatively small for cleaning CPG; consumer hotline, retail-customer-service for trade partners, EPA-required adverse-event reporting, recall infrastructure: 15

**Build total: 1360**

## Sell breakdown ($ millions)

Bleach is a commodity active ingredient with a strong brand premium — Clorox commands meaningful pricing power vs. private-label and store-brand bleach (Sam's Choice, Great Value, etc.) despite chemically identical hypochlorite. That brand premium is sustained through continuous heavy advertising. Clorox is a top-50 US national advertiser by Kantar / Ad Age rankings, and management has explicitly framed FY24 as a year of stepped-up advertising-and-sales-promotion to "support efforts to rebuild market share" after the cyberattack disruption left some shelves empty in FY24 Q1. Total company advertising line of $832M in FY24 was up materially YoY; the Health and Wellness segment, anchored by the Clorox brand, gets the largest absolute share of that spend.

- Advertising / measured media + creative + digital + brand activation (TV, digital video, retail media networks, in-store, social, influencer where used; Clorox ran heavier "germ kill" / disinfection messaging through cold-and-flu seasons and back-to-school): 325
- Sales force compensation — Clorox national-account team (Walmart, Target, Costco, Kroger, Albertsons, Publix, HEB, Home Depot, Lowe's, Amazon, dollar-store channel), retail-execution merchandising, professional-channel sales reps for CloroxPro/Clorox Healthcare (healthcare facilities, education, foodservice, building services): 50
- Trade promotion / slotting / dealer incentives — coupon redemption, retailer feature/display ad allowances, end-cap programs, slotting for new-SKU introductions, DSD-like programs in select channels, "everyday low price" margin support to compete with private-label bleach: 80

**Sell total: 455**

## Methodology notes

**Segment-mapping nuance: there is no "Clorox Cleaning segment" anymore.** The ticket calls for the "Clorox Cleaning segment" but Clorox restructured segment reporting effective FY2021 — what used to be Cleaning is now part of the broader Health and Wellness reportable segment, which bundles three sub-businesses: (a) the Cleaning sub-business (the iconic Clorox bleach, Clorox Disinfecting Wipes, Pine-Sol, Liquid-Plumr, Formula 409, Tilex, Green Works — by far the largest sub-business inside H&W and what bleach belongs to), (b) Professional Products (CloroxPro and Clorox Healthcare brands serving hospitals, schools, foodservice, and building-services contractors with germicidal/disinfectant cleaners), and (c) Vitamins, Minerals & Supplements (NaturalVitality, RenewLife, Rainbow Light — a 2018 acquisition that has consistently underperformed and was, as of mid-FY25, in the process of being divested). The cleaning sub-business is meaningfully more profitable than VMS, so the 28.9% segment EBIT margin ($719M / $2,485M) likely understates pure-cleaning economics. For bleach specifically, the build/sell ratio is probably somewhat higher (more build-heavy) than the H&W blended numbers above suggest, because VMS carries heavy advertising/sales intensity relative to the simple commodity-cleaner economics of bleach. Used H&W as the disclosed proxy and noted the limitation.

**The cyberattack distortion — partly handled, partly residual.** The August 2023 cyberattack disrupted Clorox's order-management and manufacturing systems for several weeks, leading to ~$49M of direct remediation costs (forensic, third-party consulting, system rebuild) and a much larger ~$380M revenue impact through unfulfilled shipments and inventory damage. FY24 Q1 (July-September 2023) was the most distorted quarter; FY24 Q2-Q4 saw progressive recovery, partial inventory rebuild by retailers, and pricing/cost-savings actions that pushed gross margin back up 360bp YoY (43.0% vs. 39.4%). The FY24 net sales of $7,093M is down 4% YoY but absorbs both the cyberattack drag and underlying volume softness. The choice was between using FY24 (latest available, partially distorted) or FY23 (earlier, less distorted but reflects pre-pricing-action margin levels). Used FY24 because gross margin had largely normalized by year-end and the segment-level adjusted EBIT data is the most current. Cyberattack remediation costs of ~$50-60M sit mostly in cost of products sold and corporate G&A and don't materially shift the build/sell ratio at the segment level.

**Why H&W's gross margin is plausibly higher than consolidated 43%.** Three structural reasons. (1) Cleaning brands have stronger brand-pricing power than household categories like trash bags (Glad, in Household segment) and cat litter (Fresh Step, in Household), where private-label competition is fiercer per-dollar even though Glad is also a strong brand. (2) Health and Wellness includes Professional Products which, while smaller in absolute dollars, carries higher gross margin (specialized formulations, EPA registrations, healthcare-facility relationships, lower advertising intensity). (3) Pricing actions taken in FY23-FY24 (5-7% cumulative pricing on Clorox bleach and Disinfecting Wipes) flowed through to FY24 ahead of input-cost relief, expanding margin. Estimated H&W COGS ratio at ~50-52% (vs. consolidated 57%) which yields the $1,300M COGS / $1,050M COGS-ex-distribution figures used.

**Heavy-product distribution burden — the Coca-Cola problem in miniature.** Bleach is ~9 lb per gallon and retails at $3-5 per gallon. The freight cost per unit is meaningful relative to the gross sale price, similar to (though less extreme than) the Coca-Cola can issue noted in ticket 005. Clorox ships bleach via LTL and truckload to retailer DCs; outbound freight is in COGS but worth separating for the build/sell taxonomy. Used $250M of distribution within total H&W COGS — about 10% of segment sales, which is consistent with heavy-CPG distribution benchmarks (vs. 4-5% for lighter consumer goods like cosmetics or apparel). This pushes the build number up materially vs. categories with better dollar-per-pound economics.

**Advertising allocation — H&W gets the lion's share.** Total company FY24 advertising of $832M (11.7% of total net sales) is allocated across segments. The Clorox brand is the company's flagship and gets the heaviest measured-media support. Estimated H&W's share at ~$325M (39% of company advertising on 35% of company sales — slightly heavier than proportional, consistent with Clorox brand being the strategic priority). Total H&W "sell" intensity (advertising + sales force + trade promo) of ~$455M is about 18.3% of segment sales — well above the consolidated 16% sell intensity, reflecting the brand-pricing-premium model that defines the cleaning category.

**R&D is real but small.** Total company R&D of $126M (1.8% of net sales) is below CPG-peer median (~2-3%). Clorox does maintain meaningful R&D capacity — formulation chemistry, EPA-registration efficacy testing, packaging engineering, and product-extension development (concentrated bleaches, scented bleaches, splash-less formulations, the Clorox Disinfecting Wipes lineup) — but it is not technology-intensive R&D in the way that Apple's or Tesla's is. Allocated $45M of the $126M total to H&W (~36%, slightly above sales-mix share, since cleaning has the most active formulation/regulatory work).

**Operating profit calibration vs. reported adjusted segment EBIT.** Reported FY24 H&W adjusted EBIT was $719M (28.9% margin). Consolidated FY24 adjusted operating profit, after corporate G&A, was lower than the $1,354M segment-EBIT total by approximately $430M — that gap is the unallocated corporate function. Allocating ~$165M of that to H&W (35% of mix, with a slight finance/legal premium for the regulated segment) brings allocated H&W operating profit to ~$554M; further conservatively reduced to ~$505M for restructuring/cyberattack-residual / VMS-write-down allocation, consistent with FY24 being a transition year with several non-recurring items at the corporate level. The $505M figure is below the headline $719M adjusted segment EBIT but more representative of the segment's "fully-loaded" economics — which is what we want for a cross-product comparison.

**Build_pct sensitivity.** The headline 74.9% sits inside the ticket's prediction range (65-75%) and on its upper edge. Three plausible alternate scenarios:

- **More build-heavy** (heavier distribution, lighter advertising allocation — closer to industrial CPG): build $1,420M, sell $400M, build_pct = **78.0%**
- **As classified** (H&W blended with H&W-typical advertising intensity): build $1,360M, sell $455M, build_pct = **74.9%** (headline)
- **More sell-heavy** (cyberattack-driven advertising surge sustained, weaker distribution allocation): build $1,290M, sell $510M, build_pct = **71.7%**

All three scenarios cluster in the low-to-mid 70s, well above the consumer-DTC outliers (Liquid Death ~48%, athletic apparel 50-60%) and roughly in line with food-and-beverage CPG (Coca-Cola ~67%, Heinz ketchup ~70-75% expected) but below the heavy-physical-product B2B baseline. Bleach is a pure-CPG-build-heavy category but not extreme — the brand is too valuable for advertising to be trivial.

**Why this ratio is interesting as a chart point.** Bleach is one of the simplest possible products — water, sodium hypochlorite, a fragrance — that has been turned into a billion-dollar branded business through ~110 years of advertising and trust-building. The fact that the build/sell ratio is ~75% rather than ~95% (which a naive look at "it's just chemistry" might predict) is the chart's point: even in commodity-input categories, sustained brand-premium pricing requires ongoing sell investment, and the sell line is meaningful. Conversely, the fact that it's 75% rather than 50% (which a naive look at "it's all marketing" might predict) is also the point: the heaviness and bulk of the product, the manufacturing/regulatory/EPA infrastructure, and the distribution network represent real build cost that brand-led commentary tends to undercount. Bleach sits in the middle of the ratio distribution and reads like the central case for branded household consumables.

**Confidence: medium.** This isn't "high" because Clorox does not separately report cleaning or bleach economics — the H&W segment is a reasonable proxy but bundles in VMS (a meaningfully different sub-business) and Professional Products (also different from consumer cleaning). It also isn't "low" because Clorox is a US-listed company with detailed segment reporting, advertising broken out as a separate line, and the cleaning sub-business is the dominant share of H&W. The build_pct is robust to most alternate allocations within ±5pp; the directional answer (build-heavy in the 70-78% range) is the more confident claim than the precise headline number.

**What's surprising in the numbers.** Two things. First, total advertising of $832M ($325M allocated to H&W) is substantial in absolute terms — Clorox spends more on advertising than many entire industries do — but as a percentage of revenue (11.7%) it's lower than commodity-FMCG with weak brands often do, because the brand premium has been built across decades and current advertising is more "maintenance" than "creation." Second, distribution cost is unusually high as a share of revenue (~10%) compared to other CPG categories on this chart — a structural tax on heavy-bulk-cheap-retail products that doesn't show up in lightweight categories like cosmetics or wearables.
