## Methodology notes

**Segment vs. consolidated framing.** The ticket directs use of NA Retail segment financials with advertising pulled from consolidated. NA Retail is the segment that contains Cheerios; per CAGNY 2024, Cheerios is one of nine $1B+ retail brands at General Mills (alongside Betty Crocker, Pillsbury, Nature Valley, Old El Paso, Häagen-Dazs, Yoplait, Blue Buffalo, Totino's). Segment-level cost-line decomposition (COGS, advertising, R&D, residual SG&A by segment) is NOT disclosed in the 10-K — only segment net sales and segment operating profit are. The build/sell split below is therefore an allocation, not a direct disclosure. The principal allocation method: pro-rata to revenue (NA Retail 62.82% of consolidated) for cost lines, then back-solving residual SG&A to match the disclosed segment OP of $3,080.4M. This is a defensible approach but understates how unevenly advertising actually distributes across segments (NA Retail is the most ad-intensive segment because cereal/snacks/meals are heavy media spenders and the international/foodservice segments are not).

**Advertising — directly disclosed at consolidated level, not estimated.** $824.6M for FY2024 is taken straight from the 10-K Note "Certain Consolidated Statements of Earnings amounts are as follows" supplemental disclosure (line: "Advertising and media expense (including production and communication costs)"). FY2023 was $810.0M; FY2022 was $690.1M — meaningful step-up driven by the "Accelerate" strategy and a deliberate increase in brand investment. Per CAGNY 2024, media investment grew 41% from FY2018 to FY2023 and was up high-single digits in H1 FY2024. No Kantar triangulation needed for the consolidated figure. The NA Retail share is allocated proportionally at 62.82% ($518M); a more realistic NA-Retail share would likely be 70-75% given category mix, but using the 70% number ($577M) only moves NA build_pct from 91.0% to 90.7% — material to the dollar number, immaterial to the ratio.

**R&D — directly disclosed at $257.8M for FY2024 ($257.6M FY2023, $243.1M FY2022).** Per Note 2, "All expenditures for research and development (R&D) are charged against earnings in the period incurred." Classified as build per plan.md rubric. R&D is small relative to revenue (1.3%) — typical for CPG where innovation work is mostly recipe, packaging, and category extensions rather than capital engineering.

**Cost of sales includes shipping/distribution.** Note 2: "Shipping costs associated with the distribution of finished product to our customers are recorded as cost of sales and are recognized when the related finished product is shipped." General Mills self-distributes via warehouse delivery for shelf-stable products (cereal, baking mixes) and via DSD (direct-store delivery) for select fresh/refrigerated items. This means COGS captures both manufacturing AND outbound logistics, which is the right treatment per plan.md (distribution = build).

**Trade promotion is netted against revenue, not in any cost line.** Per Note 2: "Sales include shipping and handling charges billed to the customer and are reported net of variable consideration and consideration payable to our customers, including trade allowance and consumer coupons." This means $19,857.2M consolidated net sales is AFTER trade promotion deductions. Trade promo for CPG cereal/snacks is typically 10-20% of gross sales — so true gross sales were roughly $22-24B, and the trade promo bucket (which would be SELL per plan.md rubric) is ~$2-4B "missing" from the ratio. If we added trade promo back to both revenue and sell, the consolidated build_pct would drop from 87.3% to roughly 75-80%. Plan.md acknowledges this convention and instructs us to report on the as-disclosed basis. Note this caveat — it's the dominant interpretive issue. Comparing General Mills (high build%) vs. Coca-Cola Co (66.8% build%) is partly real (cereal mfg is more vertically integrated than concentrate mfg) and partly a netting artifact (KO's bottlers carry some of the same trade promo and KO's own consolidated reporting also nets trade promo, so the comparison is symmetric — but the structural integration difference is real).

**SG&A residual classification ($593.8M NA Retail, $2,176.6M consolidated).** Consolidated SG&A is $3,259.0M. Subtracting directly-disclosed advertising ($824.6M) and R&D ($257.8M) leaves $2,176.6M of "residual" SG&A on the consolidated income statement. After NA Retail proportional allocation ($1,367.0M) and back-solving to segment OP, only $593.8M of residual SG&A is attributable to NA Retail (the difference of $773M reflects unallocated corporate items + restructuring centrally managed + other segments' allocations). Plan.md fallback is a 50/50 sell-vs-overhead split when the split inside SG&A residual is not disclosed. Used 50/50 here — $297M each side. CPG-typical residual SG&A is sell-heavy because trade-marketing / category-management / large-account selling labor is large relative to corporate G&A in CPG. A 60/40 sell-heavy split would push build_pct from 91.0% to 90.5%; a 40/60 overhead-heavy split would push it to 91.4%. Trivial swing in the headline ratio, meaningful in the absolute dollar split.

**Why the build_pct is so high (91%).** This is a structural fact, not an artifact:
1. **Vertical integration.** General Mills owns its mills (Buffalo, Cedar Rapids, Albuquerque, Covington, Reed City, etc.), packaging lines, and warehouse-delivery distribution. Unlike Coca-Cola (where bottling is independently owned), General Mills' cost structure captures the full make-and-ship chain on its own books.
2. **Commodity-heavy COGS.** Oats, wheat, sweetener, dairy, oils, and packaging dominate COGS. The 10-K explicitly lists hedged commodities: "grains (oats, wheat, and corn), oils (principally soybean), dairy products, natural gas, and diesel fuel." Consolidated COGS is 65.1% of net sales — far higher than KO's 39%.
3. **Modest SG&A.** Consolidated SG&A is 16.4% of net sales, of which advertising is 4.2% and R&D is 1.3%. The remaining 11% residual SG&A is split between sell-side activities (sales force, brand-management, marketing programs) and overhead.
4. **Trade promo netting.** As noted above, ~$2-4B of trade-promo-equivalent sell spend is netted from revenue rather than appearing in costs, mechanically inflating the build_pct.

**Cheerios brand-specific note.** Brand-level economics for Cheerios are NOT disclosed. Cheerios is one of nine $1B+ retail-brands at GIS (per CAGNY 2024) — meaning Cheerios brand US retail sales exceed $1B, but the brand's contribution to General Mills' net sales is lower (after trade promo netting and excluding the retailer markup, the brand's net-sales contribution to GIS books is plausibly $700-900M). The Cheerios brand also has joint-venture exposure outside North America via Cereal Partners Worldwide (CPW), the GIS-Nestlé JV that competes in international ready-to-eat cereal. CPW is reported as an equity-method investment ($1,718.5M of CPW net sales in FY2024 with $145.2M earnings before income taxes), NOT consolidated, so it's not in the $12,473.4M NA Retail revenue. Cereal-specific within NA Retail is bundled with other categories in the "U.S. Morning Foods" operating unit (and similar Canada operating unit), which the 10-K notes had low-single-digit net sales declines in FY2024.

**Capex ($774.1M consolidated) and D&A ($552.7M).** Capex is the cash spend on PP&E this year (mills, packaging lines, IT, productivity capex). D&A is the period charge against earlier capex. Per plan.md rule, D&A is the in-year cost that flows through COGS (manufacturing depreciation) and a smaller share through SG&A (corporate D&A). Current-year capex itself is not double-counted in build. The D&A of $552.7M is consistent with a moderately asset-heavy fully-integrated CPG operation.

**Restructuring, impairment, and other exit costs ($241.4M consolidated).** Allocated to overhead per plan.md framing — neither build (no product cost contribution) nor sell (no customer-facing spend). These are centrally managed and not pushed to segment OP, so they DO NOT affect the NA Retail segment-level breakdown. They appear in the consolidated reconciliation only.

**Unallocated corporate items ($333.9M consolidated).** Net of mark-to-market commodity gains/losses ($39.1M loss for FY2024) and certain centrally-managed comp/benefits items. Per the 10-K: "We exclude these gains and losses from our segments to realize the economic effects of the derivative without experiencing any resulting mark-to-market volatility." Same treatment as restructuring — not pushed to NA Retail.

**Divestitures gain ($0 in FY2024).** No material divestitures in FY2024. FY2023 had $444.6M divestiture gain (Helper main meals + Suddenly Salad, sold to Eagle Family Foods Group). FY2022 had $194.1M (international yogurt). The absence of a FY2024 divestiture gain means the Y/Y operating profit comparison is roughly clean of one-time items at that level.

**Confidence: medium.** Consolidated income-statement line items, segment net sales, segment operating profit, advertising expense, R&D expense, D&A, and capex are all directly disclosed in the FY2024 10-K. The medium rating reflects that segment-level cost-line decomposition (NA Retail's COGS, NA Retail's advertising, NA Retail's residual SG&A) is NOT separately disclosed and required proportional-allocation estimation. The headline build_pct of 91.0% is robust to the SG&A residual split (88-92% range across reasonable sell/overhead assumptions) but the absolute dollar splits between sell and overhead are estimates. The dominant interpretive issue (trade promo netted from revenue) would push build_pct down by ~10-15 percentage points if added back symmetrically — but plan.md and other tickets in this project use the as-disclosed basis, so the comparison across products is consistent on this convention.
