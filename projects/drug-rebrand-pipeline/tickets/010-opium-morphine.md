# Ticket 010 — Opium → Morphine

## Demon identity

Opium. Recreational use demonized in waves: 19th-century opium dens in San Francisco / NYC Chinatowns (driving the 1875 SF Opium Den Ordinance and the 1909 Smoking Opium Exclusion Act), the international Opium Conferences leading up to the 1912 Hague Convention. The original "demon drug" of US federal drug policy — the foundational scare story.

## Medicine identity

**Morphine** (isolated from opium by Sertürner in 1804–1817). The original "miracle medicine" that came from the demon plant. Plus the broader morphinan family: codeine (also opium-derived), oxycodone (semi-synthetic), hydrocodone, hydromorphone. Morphine remains the gold-standard inpatient analgesic. Various brand forms (MS Contin, Roxanol, Avinza, Kadian).

## Arc type

`street_to_medicine`. This is the granddaddy of the project — the *first* clearly successful demon-to-medicine rebrand in modern pharma, and the template that later rebrands echo.

## Chemistry hypothesis

**Cousin** at most — morphine is one alkaloid extracted from opium (which contains many: morphine, codeine, thebaine, papaverine, etc.). Opium the substance is not the same as morphine the alkaloid; morphine is a *purified component*. The analogous case is "marijuana → THC purified." Document this carefully.

For the broader morphinan family (codeine, oxycodone): each is its own rebrand step. Keep this ticket focused on opium → morphine; oxycodone gets its own ticket (025).

## Source hierarchy

1. **Sertürner's morphine isolation papers** (1805–1817) — primary historical sources.
2. **Bayer's heroin history** — Bayer marketed heroin from 1898 as a "non-addictive" morphine alternative (covered in ticket 003 for the heroin arc, but worth context here).
3. **1914 Harrison Narcotics Tax Act** — US legal milestone defining the medical-vs-recreational split.
4. **WHO essential medicines list** for morphine's role in modern medicine.
5. **DEA ARCOS data** for current morphine prescription volumes.
6. **History-of-medicine peer-reviewed sources** — opium dens, 19th-century opium tinctures (laudanum, paregoric), the transition to morphine.

## Caveats

- Opium and morphine have been *both* medicine and demon throughout their history — laudanum was 19th-century medicine, opium dens were the demon image. The rebrand was less about new identity and more about *purifying out the "medical" part* and keeping the smokable form demonized.
- Awareness of "morphine comes from opium" is probably high — this is one of the better-known pharma genealogies. Find survey data.
- The opium-morphine arc set the cultural template ("derivative of demon plant = respectable medicine") that all later rebrands draw from.
