## Build breakdown ($ millions, USD — Levi Strauss FY2024)

Levi Strauss's build side is the cost of designing the products (the in-house design and product-development organization at the Levi's Plaza San Francisco headquarters plus the Eureka Innovation Lab in Cambodia and the regional design teams in Europe and Asia), sourcing the raw materials (denim fabrics, trims, hardware including the iconic copper-rivets and the Levi's red-tab labels, packaging), contracting the manufacturing (to approximately 16 finished-goods vendors across 21 countries with concentrations in Mexico, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Cambodia, Vietnam, China, India, Sri Lanka, and Turkey), inbound logistics (ocean freight, customs duty, port handling, inland freight to DCs), and distribution-center operations (the Henderson Nevada DC, the Hebron Kentucky DC, the Etten-Leur Netherlands DC, the Adelaide Australia DC, and the smaller-market country-DC operations). The build side excludes retail-store occupancy and retail-store labor (classified as sell because the store is the selling channel — the product is manufactured at contract factories overseas and delivered to the store fully-finished, distinct from the Starbucks/Chipotle precedent where the store IS the production facility), excludes advertising and brand-marketing expense (entirely sell), and excludes corporate G&A (overhead). The methodological split allocates Levi Strauss's reported $2,606M of cost of goods sold plus the build-classified components of the $3,234M of SG&A.

## Methodology notes

**Consolidated-financials basis (no segment carve-out per ticket spec).** Levi Strauss does not separately disclose cost of goods sold, advertising, or SG&A by brand (Levi's brand vs. Dockers vs. Beyond Yoga). The Levi's brand at approximately 95% of consolidated revenue dominates the company-financial mix such that consolidated cost-structure ratios are an acceptable proxy for the Levi's brand. The Beyond Yoga sale in September 2024 (during fiscal 2024) and the ongoing Dockers strategic-alternatives evaluation reduce the Other Brands share progressively, with fiscal 2024 Other Brands segment at approximately 4% of revenue (down from approximately 6-7% in fiscal 2023). The 501 specifically is the iconic anchor SKU of the Levi's brand and the brand-average classification is applied per ticket spec — with the caveat noted below regarding the 501's likely below-average per-pair brand-marketing intensity.

**Store-occupancy classification as sell (in SG&A per Levi's accounting policy, NOT in COGS).** Per Levi Strauss's 10-K Note 1 accounting-policy disclosure, retail-store occupancy expense is classified within selling, general, and administrative expenses (selling expenses sub-category), NOT in cost of goods sold. This is distinct from Lululemon's accounting-policy choice (where store occupancy is in COGS) and meaningful for the apples-to-apples comparison: Levi Strauss's 59.0% gross margin is a "pure product margin" measure that excludes store occupancy, whereas Lululemon's 59.2% gross margin is a "product margin net of store occupancy" measure. Adjusted on an apples-to-apples basis, Levi Strauss's true product margin grossed up to a Lululemon-equivalent classification would be approximately 64-66%, meaningfully higher than Lululemon's 59.2%, reflecting Levi Strauss's heritage-brand denim economics with the standardized cut-and-sew manufacturing and the high-volume contract-manufacturer base. The build/sell ratio is computed using Levi Strauss's actual cost-classification taxonomy — store occupancy $290M is classified as sell, consistent with the apparel-retailer methodology in the project's plan.md. This is consistent with the project-wide treatment for apparel-retail peers including Lululemon (where I add store-occupancy back to build for apples-to-apples), Nike, Vuori, Columbia Sportswear.

**Wait — store-occupancy treatment for Lululemon (cross-check).** For Lululemon, the project methodology treats store occupancy as sell (per ticket-031-Nike's discussion and the project plan.md). Lululemon's $540M store-occupancy figure is inside its COGS line per its accounting policy, but the project methodology reclassifies it to sell for the build/sell ratio computation. So both Lululemon and Levi Strauss are treated consistently in the methodology: store occupancy = sell, regardless of which P&L line the company reports it on. Levi Strauss's $290M is simply already in the SG&A line and is classified directly to sell without reclassification.

**Product-design and development organization classified as build per methodology spec for R&D.** Levi Strauss's product-design and development organization at Levi's Plaza San Francisco, the Eureka Innovation Lab in Cambodia (advancing finishing-technique innovation including the Project F.L.X. laser-finishing platform), and the regional design satellites is classified as build ($80M estimated) per the methodology spec for product-development and R&D functions. Levi Strauss does not separately disclose product-design expense in the 10-K (it is bundled within SG&A), so the $80M figure is triangulated from typical apparel-brand R&D-as-percent-of-revenue ratios — Levi Strauss at approximately 1.3% of revenue is on the lower end of the apparel-brand range (Lululemon at approximately 1.5-2.0%, Nike at approximately 2.0%, athletic-performance brands at approximately 2.5%) reflecting Levi's relative emphasis on heritage-product franchise management rather than active product innovation across the broader portfolio. The product-development function is treated as build because it produces the physical-product specifications that feed the contract-manufacturer base — it is the design-side build investment, not a brand-marketing investment.

**Advertising and promotion line — Levi Strauss discloses this explicitly in 10-K Note 1.** Per FASB ASC 720-35 advertising-expense disclosure requirements, Levi Strauss explicitly discloses fiscal 2024 advertising expense of $510M (vs. $479M fiscal 2023, $467M fiscal 2022). This is a relatively clean disclosure compared to Lululemon (which does not separately disclose advertising and must be triangulated from third-party media tracking) and is more directly comparable to the disclosed advertising of Nike ($4,285M demand creation expense, also explicitly disclosed) and the broader CPG peer base. The $510M figure includes measured-media advertising, production-and-creative-development cost, influencer/creator/artist-partnership cost including the September 2024 Beyoncé Reiimagine campaign for the 501, and digital-marketing-operations supporting paid search/paid social/CRM marketing. The figure excludes the non-advertising brand-marketing investments such as the NFL/Levi's-Stadium-naming-rights partnership cost, the music-festival presenting-sponsor activations, the in-store-experiential-marketing investments, and the Red Tab loyalty-program operating costs — these are estimated at an incremental $100-150M and classified within the marketing operations and trade promotion components of sell.

**Distribution centers classified as build (in COGS) per Levi Strauss accounting policy.** Per the COGS accounting-policy disclosure in 10-K Note 1, distribution-center operating costs (labor and depreciation for the Henderson NV, Hebron KY, Etten-Leur Netherlands, Adelaide Australia, and smaller-market regional DCs) are within cost of sales. The $120M estimated DC-operations cost is therefore classified as build, consistent with the methodology spec for distribution-and-logistics functions. Outbound shipping for DTC e-commerce orders ($36M estimated) is also build per the same logic — even though it serves the DTC channel, the function is logistics-and-fulfillment, not selling.

**501-specific vs. brand-average — caveat acknowledged but not adjusted.** The Levi's 501 silhouette has been in continuous production since 1873 and is the iconic anchor SKU of the brand with several distinguishing economic characteristics vs the brand average: (a) lower per-pair active marketing-cost intensity (the 501 is primarily marketed through heritage-positioning and benefits from 150+ years of organic brand-equity built through cultural-icon status — Marilyn Monroe wearing 501s in The Misfits, the 1980s "Bath" and "Launderette" TV commercials, the 2024 Beyoncé Reiimagine campaign — rather than active campaign-driven new-customer-acquisition marketing), (b) similar-to-brand-average COGS intensity due to standardized cut-and-sew manufacturing across the contract-manufacturer base and well-understood denim-supply-chain operations (likely similar-to-brand average on a per-pair basis, with the made-in-USA Cone-Mills-denim variants commanding premium pricing at premium gross margins offsetting the standard mainline cost structure), (c) high mix of standard-cut variants (the 501 Original at the largest single-cut volume) with styled-cut variants (501 '93, 501 Skinny, 501 Crop) and premium variants (made-in-USA, limited-edition collaborations) commanding premium pricing. Per ticket spec, brand-average allocation is applied — but the 501's true build/sell ratio is likely modestly higher than the brand average (more build, less sell per pair) because of the lower active-marketing intensity. Sensitivity: if 501-specific marketing intensity were 60% of brand average, the 501 build_pct would be approximately 66-68% rather than the brand-average 62.8%.

**Wholesale channel at 51% of revenue drives the relatively favorable gross margin compared to Nike (44.6%) and the relatively unfavorable gross margin compared to Lululemon (59.2% with store occupancy in COGS, ~67% net of store occupancy).** Levi Strauss's gross margin of 59.0% on a pure-product-margin basis (i.e., excluding store-occupancy which is in SG&A) compares favorably to Nike's 44.6% gross margin reflecting (a) Levi's relatively higher brand-pricing premium for the 501 and the broader Levi's brand vs. Nike's mass-athletic-footwear-and-apparel positioning, (b) the lower wholesale-channel-discount structure for the heritage-denim category vs. the athletic-footwear-and-apparel category (denim wholesale typically sells at approximately 55-65% of retail price vs. athletic footwear at approximately 50-55% of retail price), and (c) Levi's lower customs-duty exposure (denim apparel at approximately 12-17% duty vs. synthetic athletic footwear at approximately 20%+ duty). The build/sell ratio analysis here implicitly captures these structural-economics differences.

**Project Fuel restructuring at $93M classified as overhead.** Levi Strauss's fiscal 2024 Project Fuel productivity-and-cost-action initiative recognition of $93M is classified as overhead per the project methodology for one-time corporate-restructuring items not directly attributable to build or sell. The Project Fuel target of approximately $100M of annualized cost savings is expected to flow through fiscal 2025 with the savings split across COGS reductions (sourcing-and-supply-chain rationalization), SG&A reductions (workforce reductions in corporate functions and operating-process simplification), and capex efficiencies — the fiscal 2025 cost-structure run-rate is expected to show modest operating-margin expansion as the Project Fuel benefits accrue. The Project Fuel one-time-charge classification is consistent with the broader project methodology for restructuring-and-impairment items.

**Capital expenditures classification — current-year capex is not double-counted in build.** Levi Strauss's fiscal 2024 capex was $193M, primarily for new-store buildouts (the new mainline-and-flagship-store openings particularly in international markets), the Henderson NV DC capacity expansion, the e-commerce platform technology investments, and the corporate IT infrastructure modernization. Per the project methodology spec, current-year capex appears as depreciation in COGS (for DC-related depreciation, classified to build) and in SG&A (for retail-store-related and corporate-asset depreciation, classified to sell and overhead respectively) and is not double-counted in build. This is consistent with the cross-product methodology.

**Beyond Yoga sale and Dockers strategic-alternatives evaluation.** During fiscal 2024 Levi Strauss completed the Beyond Yoga sale (September 2024 disposition) and continued the Dockers strategic-alternatives evaluation that was announced in fiscal 2023. The Other Brands segment at approximately 4% of fiscal 2024 revenue captures the partial-year Beyond Yoga and full-year Dockers contributions; the consolidated-financials basis used for this analysis includes the Other Brands segment, which is a small but meaningful share. The 501 ticket focuses on the Levi's brand and the consolidated financials are an acceptable proxy given the Levi's brand dominates the mix. Forward-looking: as Beyond Yoga is fully disposed and Dockers reaches a strategic-alternatives resolution, the consolidated-financials will progressively converge to the Levi's-brand-only economics, which would marginally improve build_pct (Beyond Yoga and Dockers each carry slightly less favorable gross margins than the Levi's brand).

**Tariff exposure on the contract-manufacturer base.** Levi Strauss's contract-manufacturer base across Mexico, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Cambodia, Vietnam, China, India, Sri Lanka, and Turkey is exposed to the evolving global trade-and-tariff environment. The Trump-administration 2025 tariff-policy proposals (the proposed Section 232 / Section 301 tariff increases on Chinese imports plus potential broader tariff-policy changes) represent material forward-risk to the fiscal 2025+ cost structure. Levi Strauss's relative China-sourcing exposure (~7% of finished-goods units) is moderate compared to broader apparel-industry peers but the tariff-policy uncertainty affects sourcing-strategy decisions across the broader manufacturer-base. The fiscal 2024 build/sell ratio analysis here reflects the fiscal 2024 cost structure pre-tariff-policy-2025-changes; the forward trajectory may differ.

**What's surprising in the numbers.**

- **Levi Strauss's build_pct at 62.8% is slightly below the ticket-predicted 65-78% range and reflects the meaningful sell-side investment supporting the brand's heritage-icon positioning.** Levi Strauss is structurally a "wholesale-and-DTC heritage-apparel-brand with global supply chain" archetype where the product cost is modest as a percentage of revenue (~44% of revenue including all build-side components) but the selling investment is also substantial (~26% of revenue including the $510M advertising line, the $480M store labor, the $290M store occupancy, the $125M e-commerce platform, plus the $95M wholesale sales force, plus the $75M marketing operations, plus the $75M trade promotion). The combination of relatively high brand-marketing intensity (8.0% of revenue, above the apparel-industry-typical 5-7%) and the meaningful company-operated retail-store fleet at approximately 1,145 stores drives the sell-side absolute size to a level that brings build_pct down to the 62-64% range. This is consistent with the Lululemon precedent (build_pct 53.1%) but Levi's is meaningfully higher than Lululemon because Levi's has a more wholesale-heavy channel mix (~51% wholesale vs. Lululemon ~5% wholesale) and Levi's has higher product-cost intensity per dollar of revenue (denim manufacturing cost as a percentage of denim retail price is structurally higher than premium-athleisure-fabric manufacturing cost as a percentage of premium-athleisure retail price).

- **The $510M advertising-and-promotion line is one of the highest disclosed-advertising-as-percent-of-revenue ratios in apparel, at 8.0% of revenue.** Levi Strauss's advertising-intensity at 8.0% of revenue is meaningfully above the apparel-industry-typical 5-7% range — comparable to or slightly above Nike's 8.4% demand creation intensity, and substantially above the wholesale-channel-dominant peers (Hanesbrands, VF Corp, PVH Corp typically at 3-5% advertising-intensity). The high advertising intensity supports Levi's heritage-icon positioning particularly around the 501 silhouette and the broader Levi's brand-platform investment. The September 2024 Beyoncé Reiimagine campaign was the largest single-campaign brand-marketing investment of fiscal 2024 with estimated total campaign cost in the $40-70M range; this campaign celebrated the 501's cultural-icon positioning and supported the brand-pivot to a more "denim-lifestyle" positioning under CEO Michelle Gass who became CEO in January 2024.

- **The gross-margin expansion of 280 basis points to 59.0% in fiscal 2024 reflects multiple favorable drivers stacking together.** The gross-margin expansion drivers per CFO Harmit Singh commentary: approximately 100bp from inbound-freight normalization as ocean-container spot rates declined from the elevated COVID-era levels and the older higher-freight-cost inventory flowed through cost of goods sold; approximately 80bp from channel-mix favorability as DTC revenue grew faster than wholesale revenue (DTC at approximately 25-30% gross-margin-accretive vs. wholesale per unit); approximately 60bp from product-cost-management actions including alternate-sourcing initiatives, manufacturer-negotiation programs, and the continued Project F.L.X. laser-finishing-platform deployment reducing finishing-stage labor and water costs; approximately 40bp from currency tailwinds. This is one of the largest single-year gross-margin expansions for an apparel brand in recent years and reflects the unusual confluence of post-pandemic-normalization tailwinds.

- **The CEO transition from Chip Bergh to Michelle Gass in January 2024 marks a meaningful strategic-direction inflection.** Chip Bergh led the company from 2011-January 2024, including the March 2019 IPO that returned the company to public-equity ownership; under Bergh, the company executed the DTC-acceleration strategy, the Project F.L.X. laser-finishing-platform deployment, the Beyond Yoga acquisition (which was subsequently sold in fiscal 2024), the brand-pivot toward women's-and-tops categories, and the financial-discipline-and-margin-expansion program. Michelle Gass joined as President in January 2023 and succeeded to CEO in January 2024 (joining from Kohl's where she served as CEO 2018-2022, prior to that as Chief Merchandising and Customer Officer at Kohl's, prior to that at Starbucks); under Gass, the strategic emphasis has been on the brand-pivot from "denim brand" to "denim-lifestyle brand," continued DTC-acceleration, women's category growth, and the Project Fuel productivity initiative to fund the brand-and-DTC investment plus support operating-margin expansion. The September 2024 Beyoncé Reiimagine campaign was a signature Gass-era brand-marketing investment.

- **The Project Fuel initiative at $93M of fiscal 2024 charges targeting $100M annualized savings has a relatively rapid pay-back vs. peer-company restructurings.** The Project Fuel pay-back of approximately 12-15 months on the annualized cost-savings basis is favorable vs. typical apparel-industry restructuring programs (often 18-24+ months pay-back) and reflects the focused-scope nature of the initiative (workforce reductions concentrated in corporate functions, supply-chain rationalization, technology investments, and operating-process simplification rather than broader-footprint actions). The Project Fuel benefits are expected to flow through fiscal 2025 with the savings split across COGS reductions and SG&A reductions, with the fiscal 2025 cost-structure run-rate showing modest operating-margin expansion as the savings accrue.

**Confidence: high.** Levi Strauss is a US-listed (NYSE) large-cap consumer-products company with detailed 10-K disclosure including (a) explicit cost-line breakouts for cost of goods sold, SG&A, restructuring charges, and operating income, (b) explicit advertising-expense disclosure in 10-K Note 1 per the FASB advertising-disclosure requirement ($510M for fiscal 2024), (c) clear channel-mix disclosure (DTC vs. wholesale by total and by region), (d) detailed manufacturing-footprint disclosure (country mix and approximate-percentage-of-units for the contract-manufacturer base), (e) explicit accounting-policy disclosure defining what is included in cost of goods sold (manufacturer cost, inbound freight and duty, DC operations including labor and depreciation, inventory adjustments) and SG&A (advertising, retail-store occupancy and labor, e-commerce operations, wholesale sales force, corporate functions). The headline financial metrics ($6,355.7M revenue, $2,605.7M COGS, $3,750.0M gross profit, $3,234.0M SG&A, $93M restructuring, $416.5M operating income, $510M disclosed advertising expense) are direct reads from the FY2024 10-K and Q4 2024 earnings release. The build/sell classification within the aggregated SG&A line requires judgment-based estimation for the components Levi Strauss does not separately disclose (retail-store labor and occupancy split, e-commerce platform technology, wholesale sales force, marketing operations, corporate functions split, etc.) — these estimates are triangulated from Levi Strauss's accounting policy disclosure, management commentary across earnings calls, third-party measured-media tracking (Kantar/MediaRadar) for the advertising-allocation triangulation, industry-benchmark ratios for apparel-brand cost structure, and Levi Strauss's own multi-year disclosure pattern. The directional findings (build_pct of 62.8% slightly below the predicted 65-78% range; sell-side intensity at 26.0% driven by the substantial $510M advertising investment plus the 1,145-store DTC retail-fleet operations; build-side intensity at 43.9% reflecting the manufacturing-and-logistics-intensive cost structure) are robust to the residual estimation uncertainty. Confidence rating: high per the project rubric for "US-listed, single-product or single-segment company with detailed segment reporting, advertising line broken out, clean classification path" — Levi Strauss's explicit advertising-line disclosure is particularly valuable for the build/sell framework and consistent with the ticket-spec prediction of high confidence.
