## Overview

Vuori Inc. (Encinitas, CA) is a premium DTC-first athleisure brand founded in 2015 by Joe Kudla. As of November 2024, Vuori was valued at $5.5B following an $825M secondary investment led by General Atlantic and Stripes, confirming a revenue scale of approximately $1B — a figure consistent across multiple press sources for the fiscal 2024 period. The company has been profitable since 2017 and is growing at an estimated 40-50% annually. All figures below are estimates from press disclosures, funding-round context, and benchmarking against Lululemon (the closest public athleisure peer with disclosed cost structure). **No audited financials exist in the public record.**

## Methodology notes

**Revenue estimate and fiscal year.** Multiple press sources in late 2024 confirm revenue "approximately $1B" for Vuori. The November 2024 General Atlantic deal at $5.5B valuation — approximately 5.5× revenue, a typical high-growth DTC multiple — implicitly validates the $1B scale. The ticket noted "~$1B revenue 2023," but research suggests this milestone was reached in 2024 or as a late-2024 run rate rather than a 2023 calendar-year figure. FY2024 is used throughout.

**Cost structure derivation — benchmark-first approach.** With no public filings, the cost structure is triangulated from three inputs: (1) Lululemon FY2024 10-K as the closest disclosed athleisure peer (gross margin 59.2%, COGS 40.8% of revenue, SG&A 35.5% of revenue, operating margin 23.7%); (2) CEO Joe Kudla's publicly stated unit-economics philosophy ("never acquired a customer at a loss," profitable since 2017, capital-efficient growth); (3) Vuori's known operating parameters (100+ stores, ~75% DTC, ~25% selective wholesale, ~50% annual growth, valuation implying healthy profitability). Vuori is assumed to have slightly better gross margin than Lululemon (~61% vs. 59.2%) given its smaller store count relative to revenue and more efficient early-stage sourcing, offset by smaller-scale supply chain and faster growth requiring growth investment.

**Gross margin and COGS proxy.** Estimated gross margin of ~61% implies COGS of ~$390M. Per the Lululemon accounting policy precedent (Note 1, Lululemon FY2024 10-K), athletic-apparel COGS conventionally includes store occupancy/rent/depreciation alongside product and freight costs. Vuori likely follows similar accounting. The build/sell reclassification moves $65M of store occupancy from COGS into the sell category (stores are a selling channel, not a production facility), consistent with the treatment applied in the Lululemon result (ticket 030).

**Operating margin.** 15% operating margin ($150M on $1B revenue) is the working assumption, reflecting Vuori's "profitable since 2017" track record and capital-efficient DTC model. In growth mode with 100+ stores and 40-50% annual revenue growth, actual margin could range from 10% to 20% — 15% is the midpoint. EV/EBITDA at 15% margin and $5.5B valuation would be ~37×, which is high but not unusual for high-growth profitable consumer-brand companies at this stage.

**Marketing spend — the biggest estimation uncertainty.** Performance marketing at $120M (12% of revenue) is the central estimate for a growth DTC brand. Kudla's stated philosophy of "never acquire a customer at a loss" implies disciplined CAC management, not zero CAC spend. At a 4:1 LTV:CAC ratio and assumed average LTV of ~$300, implied CAC of ~$75 × 1.5M new customers/year ≈ $110M in paid acquisition — roughly consistent with the $120M estimate. Brand marketing at $40M (4% of revenue) is consistent with Lululemon's pre-2024 baseline (before the 'Wherever You Are' step-up). Sensitivity: if marketing spend is 20% of revenue instead of 16% total, sell total rises to ~$440M and build_pct falls to ~44% — still within the ticket's 40-55% expected range.

**Store cost estimation.** 100 stores × $650K average annual occupancy = $65M. Premium locations (SoHo, Beverly Hills, downtown Chicago) might run $800K-$1.5M/year; suburban lifestyle centers $400-600K. The $650K average reflects Vuori's mix of urban-premium and suburban locations. Store labor at $800K/store (approximately 15-20 FTE at $40-50K average including part-time staff and benefits) = $80M. Both figures are consistent with Lululemon's disclosed store-fleet cost on a per-store basis ($1,000M occupancy ÷ 767 stores = $1,300K/store for Lululemon; Vuori's lower average reflects newer, typically smaller stores — Vuori's target format is approximately 2,500-4,000 sq ft vs. Lululemon's ~4,400 sq ft average).

**Wholesale classification.** Vuori's ~25% wholesale channel (Nordstrom, REI, Equinox) contributes to sell-side cost via the wholesale account management team and trade-selling infrastructure ($15M estimate). Unlike grocery CPG, Vuori does not carry slotting fees or trade promotion at scale — the selective wholesale strategy means Nordstrom and REI pursue Vuori, not the other way around, so sell-side cost for the wholesale channel is relatively modest. Trade promotion is not netted against revenue for this type of brand relationship.

**Build_pct at 50.4% — in range, modestly above midpoint.** The ticket predicted 40-55% for a DTC growth brand in sell-heavy mode. The 50.4% result says build and sell costs are roughly equal at this stage — a sensible finding for a brand that spends heavily on physical retail and digital marketing ($200M combined) but also carries significant apparel manufacturing and supply chain costs ($290M combined manufacturing + freight + DC). The result is slightly higher than the low-40s range some pure-DTC-growth brands show (where there are no physical stores and nearly all SG&A is marketing), because Vuori's 100-store fleet creates a substantial "traditional retail" cost layer on the sell side that competes with the build cost numerically. Compared to Lululemon (53.1%), Vuori's marginally lower build_pct reflects its heavier marketing intensity as a growth brand.

**What's notable in the numbers.** Vuori's position near the 50/50 build-sell line reflects the tension between two modes: (1) a capital-efficient DTC-first founder model that resists spending on customer acquisition at a loss, and (2) an aggressive physical retail expansion (100+ stores, adding 20-25/year) that makes store operations a significant sell-side investment. The brand is not in the sell-heavy camp of pure performance-marketing DTC brands (where build_pct can fall to 30-35%) — it's increasingly a hybrid retail operator with a DTC heritage, structurally converging toward Lululemon's 53% build_pct as it matures and scales.
