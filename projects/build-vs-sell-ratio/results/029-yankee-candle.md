## Build breakdown ($ millions, USD — Yankee Candle brand-level FY2024, triangulated)

Yankee Candle's build side is the cost of designing, manufacturing, and distributing scented candles and home-fragrance products. The Yankee Candle business operates a vertically-integrated US-manufacturing model with the primary production facility at South Deerfield/Whately, Massachusetts (the world's largest candle manufacturing plant, originally built by founder Michael Kittredge in the 1980s-1990s and continuously expanded through the Madison Dearborn and Newell ownership periods), where paraffin and soy wax is melted, fragrance oils are blended into the wax, the wax is poured into glass jars or other vessels, wicks are inserted, and finished candles are cooled, labeled, and packed for shipment. The brand also offers diffusers, room sprays, car fragrances, wax melts (for use with electric wax warmers), and seasonal limited-edition fragrances developed by the in-house fragrance development team. The build side captures all production, distribution, and product-development costs but excludes the retail-channel selling costs (which sit in the Sell breakdown) and the brand-marketing costs (also Sell).

- **COGS — raw materials (paraffin and soy wax, fragrance oils, glass jars and vessels, wicks, labels, packaging)** (Yankee Candle's primary raw material costs include paraffin wax (sourced from petroleum refining byproduct suppliers, with significant exposure to crude oil prices and refining-margin dynamics), soy wax (a renewable alternative used in the brand's positioning of certain product lines, sourced from agricultural processors), the fragrance-oil blends developed by the brand's fragrance development team in collaboration with major fragrance houses (Firmenich, Givaudan, IFF, Symrise) which provide the proprietary scent compounds, the glass jar vessels (sourced from US and imported glass manufacturers, with the iconic Yankee Candle large jar being the brand's flagship vessel), the cotton/paper wicks, the labels and graphics, and the corrugated packaging for shipment. The raw-material cost has meaningful commodity-price exposure to paraffin (oil-derived) and to the various components of fragrance oils. Estimated raw-material cost at approximately 18-22% of brand-level revenue): **$160**

- **COGS — manufacturing labor and plant overhead (Whately/South Deerfield MA production)** (the South Deerfield/Whately Massachusetts manufacturing campus employs approximately 1,000-1,500 production-line and operations personnel including production-line workers, supervisors, maintenance and engineering staff, quality assurance personnel, and plant management. Manufacturing labor cost at average compensation approximately $50-60K including benefits and payroll taxes totals approximately $60-90M; plant overhead including utilities (the candle manufacturing process is energy-intensive due to the wax-melting and pouring operations), facility depreciation, equipment maintenance, environmental compliance, and plant management estimated at approximately $40-60M; combined manufacturing labor and plant overhead approximately 10-12% of revenue. The vertically-integrated US-manufacturing position is both a competitive moat (brand-authenticity, made-in-USA positioning, supply-chain control, fragrance-quality consistency) and a fixed-cost burden requiring volume support to maintain unit-economic competitiveness against lower-cost offshore competitors): **$85**

- **COGS — inbound and outbound freight, warehousing operations** (Yankee Candle ships finished candle inventory from the South Deerfield, MA manufacturing campus to regional distribution centers, then to retail-channel partner distribution centers (Walmart, Target, Kohl's, Bed Bath & Beyond/successor, supermarket chains, drugstore chains, specialty home-decor retailers), to the brand's own approximately 400 retail boutique stores, and to direct-to-consumer e-commerce customers via small-parcel shipping (UPS, FedEx, USPS). Outbound freight is meaningful given the bulk-and-weight characteristics of glass-jar candles (a Yankee Candle large jar weighs approximately 22 oz with the candle plus another 6-8 oz for the glass, and shipping breakage risk requires substantial protective packaging for individual-unit DTC shipments). Estimated inbound and outbound freight and warehousing operations approximately 5-6% of revenue): **$44**

- **Fragrance research and development, product development** (Yankee Candle's in-house fragrance development team in collaboration with the major fragrance houses develops the brand's approximately 200+ active fragrance SKUs including the year-round core fragrance library, the seasonal-collection releases (spring, summer, fall, Halloween, holiday/winter), the limited-edition collaborations, the YC Collection premium tier launched in 2024 as a brand-positioning extension, and the new-product-form innovation (the diffusers, wax melts, car fragrances, room sprays, signature-fragrance candles). The R&D investment includes fragrance-formulation work, consumer-testing and panel-based fragrance evaluation, packaging-design and brand-creative development, and the manufacturing-process engineering for new product forms. Estimated at approximately 1.5% of revenue, classified as build per the methodology spec for product-development functions): **$12**

- **Customer support attributable to product issues (quality complaints, returns, defect handling)** (Yankee Candle offers a satisfaction-guarantee return policy and handles customer-support inquiries including product-quality complaints (wick performance, scent throw consistency, glass-jar defects, fragrance-allergy concerns), shipping-damage claims for e-commerce orders, retail-store customer-service support, and the loyalty-program customer-service operations. Customer support for product-related issues estimated at approximately 0.5% of revenue, classified as build per the methodology spec for product-support functions): **$4**

## Sell breakdown ($ millions, USD — Yankee Candle brand-level FY2024, triangulated)

The brand operates across multiple channels including mass-merchant wholesale (Walmart, Target, Kohl's, supermarket chains, drugstore chains representing the dominant volume channel), specialty home-decor and gift-shop wholesale (Bed Bath & Beyond/successor, smaller specialty retailers, hardware-and-home-improvement channels), direct-to-consumer e-commerce via yankeecandle.com (approximately $140-150M e-commerce revenue per third-party tracking, ~18% of brand revenue), and the proprietary retail-boutique store fleet (estimated approximately 25-30% of brand revenue, declining as the fleet contracts).

- Yankee Candle brand-level operating margin implied: 168 / 800 = **21.0%** — consistent with historical Yankee Candle pre-acquisition disclosure (20-25% operating margin in the pre-2007 era), reflecting the high-margin scented-candle category economics moderated by the substantial retail-fleet operating cost
- Cross-check against Newell H&CS segment FY2024 normalized economics: H&CS segment revenue approximately $4,055M, normalized operating margin approximately 11-12% per quarterly disclosures (Q4 reported as 11.7%). The implied 21% operating margin for Yankee Candle brand-level is higher than the segment-blended 11-12% because Yankee Candle (high-margin scented candles) carries higher margins than the segment-blended mix that includes lower-margin commercial cleaning products (Rubbermaid Commercial) and kitchen appliances (Crock-Pot, Calphalon, Mr. Coffee, FoodSaver). This margin-mix divergence is the principal reason the brand-level cost structure cannot be modeled by simply applying segment-averaged cost ratios.

## Methodology notes

**Why Yankee Candle brand-level triangulation rather than H&CS segment-level numbers.** The ticket directs that Newell Home Fragrance category be used if reported, otherwise the H&CS segment. Newell does not disclose category-level or brand-level financials in any public filing — only the three operating segments (H&CS, L&D, O&R) are reported. Applying H&CS segment cost ratios to estimate Yankee Candle brand-level economics is mathematically straightforward but conceptually misleading because the H&CS segment is a blend of high-margin scented candles (Yankee Candle, WoodWick, Chesapeake Bay — collectively approximately $900M-$1.1B revenue per estimates), branded kitchen appliances (Crock-Pot, Calphalon, Mr. Coffee, FoodSaver, Oster, Sunbeam, Pyrex — collectively the largest category by revenue at approximately $1.6-2.0B), and B2B commercial cleaning products (Rubbermaid Commercial, Mapa, Spontex — approximately $1.0-1.4B). These three businesses have materially different cost structures: scented candles have gross margins of approximately 60% (COGS ~40% of revenue), branded kitchen appliances have gross margins of approximately 25-35% (COGS ~65-75% of revenue), and commercial cleaning has gross margins in the 30-40% range with B2B sell-side cost structures different from consumer-brand sell side. Applying the segment-blended 33.6% gross margin to Yankee Candle would substantially understate the brand's gross margin and overstate COGS. The brand-level triangulation here uses third-party trade-press revenue estimates and category-specific cost-structure benchmarks to model Yankee Candle's economics in a way that is conceptually correct even at the expense of being not-directly-disclosed.

**Revenue triangulation at approximately $800M.** Yankee Candle brand-level revenue is triangulated from multiple third-party sources clustering in the $750M-$850M range for FY2024. Specific data points: (a) the historical $844M revenue at the 2013 Newell acquisition, providing a reference point for the brand at independent-company scale; (b) e-commerce-only revenue tracked by ECDB at approximately $144M for 2024, which combined with the typical 18-20% e-commerce share for premium consumer brands implies total revenue of approximately $720-800M; (c) Statista's historical revenue series showing peak revenue of $844M with subsequent moderation through the post-pandemic period; (d) trade-press estimates and industry-analyst commentary consistent with the $750-850M range. The midpoint estimate of $800M is used here as the brand-level revenue for the build/sell ratio analysis. Sensitivity: at $750M revenue, the build_pct would still be approximately 60% with proportional scaling; at $850M, similarly 60%. The build_pct is fairly insensitive to the revenue assumption because all cost categories scale roughly proportionally with revenue.

**COGS structure for scented candles vs. consolidated H&CS COGS.** Scented candles are a high-gross-margin product category — typical industry gross margins for branded-premium scented candles are 55-65%, implying COGS of 35-45% of revenue. This contrasts sharply with the Newell H&CS consolidated gross margin of 33.6% (COGS of 66.4%) which is dragged down by the lower-margin commercial cleaning and kitchen appliances categories. For this analysis, Yankee Candle brand-level COGS is estimated at approximately 36% of revenue ($289M of $800M revenue is base COGS without the corporate-allocated and customer-support and R&D adjustments; including those build-classified SG&A items the total build is $352M or 44% of revenue). This is materially below the consolidated H&CS COGS ratio and reflects the category-specific scented-candle cost structure.

**Retail-boutique-store operating cost as a sell-side classification.** Yankee Candle operates approximately 400 retail boutique stores at fiscal year end 2024 (down from approximately 450 a year earlier following the 2024 closure of approximately 43 stores). The retail-fleet operating cost — rent, labor, utilities, store management — is classified as sell per the methodology rubric for corporate-owned retail (consistent with the project-wide treatment of Apple Retail, Nike DTC, Lululemon stores, Starbucks stores, Chipotle restaurants). The estimated $145M retail-fleet operating cost represents approximately 18% of brand revenue, which is high relative to consumer-brand peers without proprietary retail (typical 0-5% range) but consistent with brand-specialty-retailer peers like Bath & Body Works (the closest Yankee Candle category competitor with proprietary retail), which has retail-fleet-operating-cost ratios in the 25-30% range with a larger store fleet and higher retail-channel share. Alternative classification: if retail-store operations were reclassified as build (treating retail stores as a product-delivery channel rather than a selling channel), build_pct would rise to approximately 86% — the +25 percentage-point swing illustrates the sensitivity to this classification choice.

**Advertising and promotion estimate at $64M (8% of revenue).** Yankee Candle's brand-marketing investment is estimated at approximately 8% of brand revenue, in the upper portion of the typical premium-branded consumer-goods range (5-10% A&P-to-revenue). The estimate reflects (a) management commentary across the FY2024 quarterly releases emphasizing 'purposely increased A&P investment' for core brands including Yankee Candle, (b) third-party measured-media tracking (MediaRadar, Comperemedia) estimating Yankee Candle measured-media spend at approximately $30-60M, with the difference between measured-media and total A&P composed of digital-direct-marketing, brand-creative production, loyalty-program marketing, influencer marketing, and licensing/partnership marketing not captured by measured-media trackers, (c) the historical Yankee Candle pre-acquisition disclosure showing brand-marketing intensity of approximately 6-8% of revenue in the independent-company era. Sensitivity: at 6% A&P ratio, build_pct would rise to approximately 62%; at 10% A&P, build_pct would fall to approximately 58%.

**Sales force and trade promotion at $20M combined (2.5% of revenue).** The wholesale-channel selling and trade-promotion investment is modest in absolute terms because Yankee Candle's wholesale channel is concentrated on a small number of large retail customers (Walmart, Target, Kohl's, Bed Bath & Beyond/successor, Amazon, supermarket chains, drugstore chains, specialty home-decor retailers) with established account-management relationships rather than a fragmented small-customer base requiring a large sales force. Trade promotion at 1% of revenue is consistent with the moderate-promotional-intensity scented-candle category (less promotional than the heavy-trade-promotion food and beverage CPG categories, but more promotional than the premium-luxury home-fragrance category at the high end). Both lines are classified as sell.

**Corporate G&A overhead allocation at $48M (6% of revenue).** Newell's consolidated corporate overhead — executive office, HR, legal, finance, treasury, IT, corporate communications — is allocated across the brand portfolio. The 6% of revenue allocation to Yankee Candle is consistent with typical CPG conglomerate corporate-overhead allocations and is derived as the residual to reconcile the brand-level operating margin to the implied 21% level. This is treated as excluded overhead per the methodology spec — not as build or sell.

**Brand-level operating margin of 21% — high-margin scented-candle category economics.** The implied 21% operating margin for the Yankee Candle brand is high relative to the Newell H&CS segment-blended 11-12% normalized operating margin but is consistent with (a) the historical Yankee Candle pre-acquisition disclosure of 20-25% operating margin in the independent-company era, (b) the category-typical economics for branded-premium scented candles, and (c) the cross-check that the Newell H&CS segment-blended margin is depressed by the lower-margin commercial cleaning and kitchen appliances categories that average down the otherwise high-margin Home Fragrance contribution. The Bath & Body Works (Yankee Candle's closest category competitor) operating margin is approximately 18-22% at the brand level, providing a comparable reference point.

**What's surprising in the numbers.**

- **Yankee Candle brand-level build_pct at approximately 60% falls in the lower-middle of the ticket-predicted 55-70% range, reflecting the substantial retail-boutique-store fleet operating cost.** The brand's combination of low-COGS-but-retail-fleet-heavy economics is structurally different from upstream-only CPG brands in this panel (where build_pct typically runs 70-80% reflecting heavy COGS-and-distribution dominance) and from advertising-heavy DTC brands (where build_pct can fall to 40-50% reflecting unusually heavy marketing intensity). Yankee Candle's 60.3% build_pct positions the brand in the specialty-retailer cluster alongside other proprietary-retail consumer brands (Bath & Body Works, Williams-Sonoma at 65.6%, Apple Retail-blended brands, Starbucks at ~50-55%).

- **The retail-boutique-store fleet at approximately 400 stores represents both a meaningful sell-side cost burden ($145M, 18% of revenue) and a strategic-asset-vs-liability question for the brand.** Management has been rationalizing the fleet — approximately 43 stores closed in 2024 with further closures planned in fiscal 2025 — reflecting the post-pandemic decline in mall traffic, the rising share of e-commerce in the brand's channel mix, and the goal of focusing the retail-fleet investment on the strongest-productivity store locations. A fleet contraction over the next 3-5 years could plausibly reduce the brand's sell-side cost ratio by 3-5 percentage points (if the fleet contracts to ~250-300 stores from the current ~400), which would shift build_pct higher to approximately 64-66% — even without underlying changes in COGS or A&P intensity.

- **The Newell-acquisition history (2013, $1.75B from Madison Dearborn at ~$844M revenue) provides a useful baseline that Yankee Candle has roughly flatlined in absolute revenue terms over the subsequent decade, despite the pandemic-era category spike in 2020-2022.** This is a relatively rare 'no-growth-but-still-meaningful' brand within the Newell portfolio — generating substantial cash flow (~$168M operating income on $800M revenue at 21% margin) but not growing the top line meaningfully. The strategic implication is that Yankee Candle is a cash-generative legacy brand within Newell's portfolio rather than a growth engine — which informs both the marketing-investment posture (sustaining brand health rather than aggressive growth) and the retail-fleet rationalization posture (optimizing the existing footprint rather than expanding).

- **The competitive intensity in home fragrance has structurally increased over the past 5-10 years.** Bath & Body Works has aggressively expanded its multi-wick candle line as a direct Yankee Candle competitor, smaller premium brands (Diptyque, Jo Malone, Voluspa, Boy Smells, P.F. Candle Co., Brooklyn Candle Studio) have proliferated at the high end with strong digital-native brand-building, and mass-merchant private-label and dupe brands (Walmart Mainstays, Target Project 62, Aldi Specially Selected, the Studio McGee Target collaboration candles) compete at the low end with substantial gross-margin advantages from lower brand-marketing intensity. Yankee Candle's strategic response has been the YC Collection premium-tier launch (2024-2025) attempting to defend the upper-mid-tier price point and to maintain brand-relevance among younger consumers — the brand-marketing investment ratio of ~8% reflects this competitive defense posture.

- **The vertically-integrated US manufacturing at South Deerfield/Whately, MA is both a competitive moat and a strategic constraint.** The brand-authenticity benefit (founded in MA, manufactured in MA, the 'New England heritage candle' positioning) is real and supports brand premium. But the fixed-cost burden of the manufacturing campus requires volume support that has become harder to maintain as the post-pandemic category-revenue normalization has reduced production-volume utilization. The unit-cost competitiveness against lower-cost offshore-manufactured competitors (private label, dupe brands) is a structural challenge that management has been addressing through plant-productivity initiatives and SKU rationalization. The build-side cost structure of 44% of revenue reflects a higher-than-purely-offshore-manufactured cost basis but is moderated by the brand-premium pricing power that the US-manufactured positioning supports.

- **The H&CS segment-level normalized operating margin of approximately 11-12% understates the Yankee Candle brand-level profitability.** Applying segment-blended cost ratios to Yankee Candle would suggest brand-level operating margin of approximately 11-12%, but the brand-level estimate of 21% operating margin reflects (a) higher-margin scented-candle category economics vs. lower-margin commercial cleaning and kitchen appliances, (b) brand-pricing power that supports higher gross margins than the segment blend, (c) the offsetting drag from substantial retail-fleet operating cost (which segment-level data does not capture differentially by brand). The brand-level estimate of 21% is more consistent with both the historical pre-acquisition disclosure and the comparable scented-candle-brand-with-retail peer (Bath & Body Works at 18-22% operating margin).

**Confidence: low.** This is an unequivocally low-confidence triangulation. The headline Newell Brands consolidated financial metrics (net sales $7,582M, gross profit $2,548M, SG&A $2,083M, operating income $67M reported / $618M normalized) come directly from the FY2024 earnings release and 10-K filing — these are clean US-listed-company disclosures. But the brand-level Yankee Candle figures (revenue $800M, build $352M, sell $232M, overhead $48M, profit $168M, build_pct 60.3%) are triangulated estimates from third-party trade-press revenue tracking, e-commerce-database revenue inference, category-specific cost-structure benchmarks, historical pre-acquisition disclosure baselines, and management commentary on A&P investment and retail-fleet rationalization. Newell does not disclose brand-level or category-level financial detail in any public filing — only the three operating segments (H&CS, L&D, O&R). The low-confidence rating reflects (i) the absence of any direct brand-level financial disclosure from Newell requiring a constructive bottom-up triangulation, (ii) the necessity to estimate each major cost-structure component from category-specific benchmarks rather than direct line-item disclosure, (iii) the methodological choice to use brand-level triangulation rather than segment-averaged cost ratios (which would have produced an even more misleading result given the segment's mixed-margin composition), and (iv) the sensitivity to the retail-fleet-operating-cost classification (alternative classifications would shift build_pct by up to 25 percentage points as discussed in methodology notes). The directional finding (Yankee Candle brand-level build_pct of approximately 58-65%, consistent with the ticket-predicted 55-70% range and the specialty-retailer-with-brand-marketing cluster) is robust to the residual estimation uncertainty — the brand carries higher-than-typical-CPG sell-side intensity from its proprietary retail-boutique-store fleet, partially offset by the high-gross-margin scented-candle category economics.
