# Jack Dawson

## Character Summary
- **Source work:** Titanic (1997, film), as portrayed by Leonardo DiCaprio. Set April 10–15, 1912, on the RMS Titanic's maiden voyage.
- **Audience:** female-targeted
- **Decade slot:** 1990s
- **Cultural reach proxy:** Titanic grossed ~$1.84B worldwide on its initial 1997–98 run; cumulative worldwide gross with re-releases ~$2.26B (one of the all-time top-grossing films, held the #1 record for 12 years). 11 Academy Awards (tied for the most ever). 100M+ global theater admissions. Jack Dawson is a perennial fixture on "best movie boyfriends" lists in Cosmopolitan, Glamour, Marie Claire, BBC, Vulture, and Empire retrospectives nearly three decades later.
- **Canonical version used:** 1997 theatrical film. The 2012 3D re-release is the same character data; not differentiated.

## Joint Probability

- **Method:** threshold (female-target stack)
- **Naive product of marginals:**
  3e-4 × 0.05
  = 1.5e-5
  (≈ 1 in 67,000)
- **Correlation adjustment:** The two stacked traits (career-tier working artist; orphan/family-unencumbered) are approximately independent in modern US data — being orphaned by 20 doesn't materially predict working-artist status, and vice versa. Historically, drifter/itinerant-artist populations may have had a mild overrepresentation of family-displaced individuals (Depression-era hobo-artist stereotype, post-war wanderers), but this is not a measured joint and the modern marginals are roughly independent. No meaningful correlation adjustment needed; honest joint stays at ~1.5e-5.
- **Adjusted estimate:** ~1.5e-5 (≈ 1 in 67,000). Bracket: [5e-6, 5e-5].
- **Population implication:** Out of ~127M US adult men, roughly ~1,900 expected matches — a small but very real population. Out of the ~3.5B global adult-male population, on the order of ~50,000 matches. Jack Dawson's universal-desirable stack is **orders of magnitude more achievable** than billionaire-pianist-pilot stacks (Christian Grey ≈ 1 in 10^11; Edward Cullen ≈ similarly extreme). Jack lands much closer to the "this person actually exists in the world" end of the distribution. The fantasy element of his appeal is concentrated almost entirely in the personality/archetype/plot layer (the working-class artist who saves the rich girl from a gilded cage and dies for her), not in slider-maxing.

## Confidence Assessment
- **Overall:** medium-high
- **Trait identification:** high — Jack Dawson's traits are unambiguously established on screen and well-documented in 28 years of writing about the film. The stack is short and clean: artist, orphan, drifter, 20 years old. Easy to enumerate completely.
- **Base rates:** medium — family-unencumbered at 5% is a reasoned composite, not a single citation. Visual-artist career-tier rate (3e-4) is a derived BLS+gig-adjusted estimate, defensible but not measured precisely. A reviewer reading Jack as a talented amateur (rather than career-tier) could push the rate higher (1e-3 or even 5e-3); a reviewer reading him as merely a hobbyist sketcher could exclude the trait entirely.
- **Correlation adjustments:** high — with only two stacked traits and no strong joint signal between them, correlation handling is simple and the no-adjustment call is well-supported.

## Notes / Edge Cases

- **Reference-case validation:** Per ticket 015's notes, Jack was expected to land at relatively low improbability — "a character who passes characterization without slider-maxing." Result confirms this strongly: ~1 in 67,000 is six orders of magnitude more achievable than Christian Grey's ~1 in 10^11. The methodology cleanly distinguishes "Stack-rarity" heartthrobs (slider-maxing fantasy figures) from "personality/archetype" heartthrobs (Jack, Han Solo, Johnny Castle). This is exactly what the project hypothesizes the female-stack distribution should look like at its low-improbability end, and it validates that the metric isn't pre-rigged to make every female-target lead look absurd.
- **Personality dominates the appeal:** Jack's enduring "best movie boyfriend" status across nearly three decades is overwhelmingly driven by *personality, plot, and archetype* — the working-class free spirit who awakens the trapped heiress, paints her like one of his French girls, dances steerage with her, and ultimately dies clutching her hand on a piece of debris. None of that is slider-stacking. The universal-desirable rarity number alone undersells his cultural footprint by an enormous margin, in the same way it does for Han Solo and Johnny Castle. This is a real signal for the project: heartthrob appeal partitions cleanly into stack-rarity types and personality/archetype types, and Jack is among the strongest examples of the latter.
- **Artistic-skill threshold ambiguity:** Used 3e-4 (career-tier working visual artist). Jack is plausibly read at this tier — he earns francs sketching strangers in Paris, has a portfolio of completed works at displayable quality, and Rose explicitly remarks on his gift. He is NOT virtuoso/master tier (gallery-represented, internationally renowned); he's young and journeyman-level. A reviewer applying a strict virtuoso reading would exclude this trait and collapse the joint to 0.05 (just orphan status, ~1 in 20). A reviewer reading him as merely a talented amateur would push the rate to ~5e-3, lifting the joint to ~2.5e-4 (~1 in 4,000). Mid-estimate is the honest middle; uncertainty is reflected in the ~3x bracket.
- **Fitness exclusion:** Jack ice-skates, runs, climbs, and survives the freezing Atlantic for several minutes before hypothermia. By practical measures he's at least above-average fit. He was excluded from the stack under the strict "visible muscle, athletic" reading because DiCaprio at 22 was lanky-slim rather than muscular and the character is canonically poor and undernourished. A reviewer who reads "athletic capability" as the standard rather than "visible muscle" would include this, multiplying the joint by 0.20 → ~3e-6 (~1 in 333,000). Stated here for transparency; primary number leaves it out.
- **Age below window:** Jack at canonically 20 fails the 28–38 prime window — same situation as Johnny Castle (canonically 25). Defaulted to canonical age. The fact that two of the most enduring "best movie boyfriend" characters (Jack at 20, Johnny at 25) are below the assumed "powerful but still hot" window is itself a finding worth tracking — the female-target heartthrob distribution may include a meaningful "youthful golden boy" mode that the prime-window threshold misses. Don't reweight retrospectively, but flag for the final graph annotations.
- **Two-trait stack is real signal, not laziness:** Some characters genuinely just don't have many universal-desirable traits. The methodology must be willing to land them at "barely improbable" without inventing traits to pad the count. Jack's two-trait stack is the project's clearest example of this so far. If the female-target distribution turns out bimodal (Stack-rarity mode at 1e-10 to 1e-12, personality/archetype mode at 1e-4 to 1e-6), Jack will be near the median of the second mode.
