## Build breakdown ($ millions, USD; underlying CHF in parentheses)

The Nespresso system is structurally a coffee-CPG-meets-luxury-DTC-retailer hybrid. The build side encompasses (a) green coffee bean procurement and roasting through the AAA Sustainable Quality Program with farmer relationships in 18+ origin countries (Colombia, Brazil, Ethiopia, Costa Rica, Kenya, Indonesia, India, etc., with the AAA program operated jointly with the Rainforest Alliance), (b) high-volume automated capsule manufacturing at the three Swiss production sites (Avenches in canton Vaud, opened 2018 as the third and most modern site; Romont in canton Fribourg; and Orbe in canton Vaud which also houses the Nestlé Coffee R&D Centre and the Nespresso Centre of Excellence), (c) machine COGS paid to OEM partners (Krups/Groupe SEB, Magimix, De'Longhi, Breville/Sage, Eugster/Frismag, Koenig, Turmix) for machines that Nespresso ships through its own DTC channels — only this slice of machine economics is inside the Nespresso GMB, since machines sold through third-party retail are booked by the OEM not Nestlé, (d) distribution and last-mile fulfillment for the unusually high-DTC channel mix (Nespresso.com e-commerce and Nespresso Club phone-order channel together represent the bulk of pod sales in most mature markets, plus third-party retail through Vertuo Pop placement at Williams-Sonoma / Sur La Table / Costco / Amazon / major-market supermarkets), (e) R&D split across capsule innovation, machine system design (licensed to OEMs), and Vertuo platform development, (f) customer support through the Nespresso Club call centers and machine repair operations.

- **COGS — green coffee procurement and roasting** (green coffee beans purchased through the AAA Sustainable Quality Program from 140,000+ farmers across 18 origin countries; sustainability premium paid versus C-market arabica due to the AAA program's quality-tier-and-traceability requirements; roasting at the three Swiss sites with proprietary roast profiles and blend recipes; the major 2024 input-cost story is record-high arabica bean prices that lifted COGS materially versus 2023 — arabica futures hit multi-decade highs in Q4 2024, driven by Brazilian frost concerns, robusta market tightness, and continued post-pandemic demand recovery; Nespresso's high-end positioning means it sources predominantly arabica with a meaningful single-origin and reserve-grade share, so it bears more of the arabica spike than commodity-coffee competitors): **$1,569 (CHF 1,382)**

## Methodology notes

**Why machine + pods as a system rather than the standalone Nespresso machine SKU.** The ticket explicitly directs that this be measured as the system not the SKU because the standalone Nespresso machine is a razor-and-blades loss-leader / cross-subsidized device. Nestlé's Nespresso GMB economics make this concrete: machines themselves are not even manufactured by Nestlé — they are designed by Nespresso, licensed to OEM partners (Krups/Groupe SEB, Magimix, De'Longhi, Breville/Sage, Eugster/Frismag, Koenig, Turmix), and manufactured by those OEMs. When sold through Nespresso-owned channels (boutiques, Nespresso.com), Nespresso books the machine revenue and pays the OEM a transfer price; when sold through third-party appliance retail (Williams-Sonoma, Sur La Table, Costco, Amazon, John Lewis), the OEM books the revenue and Nestlé does not. In both cases the machine price is positioned to acquire a customer who will then purchase capsules through the proprietary closed-system over the 10+ year typical machine life. Looking at 'the Nespresso machine' as a standalone economic unit is therefore meaningless — there is no standalone machine economics; the entire system is co-engineered as a customer-acquisition-plus-recurring-consumption funnel. The Nespresso GMB as Nestlé reports it captures both halves of the funnel (the machine retail revenue Nespresso books plus the recurring capsule revenue) and is the correct unit of analysis.

**Why fiscal year 2024.** The FY2024 annual report (issued February 2025) is the most recent Nestlé filing with full Nespresso GMB disclosure. The Nespresso 2024 story is dominated by (a) the record-high arabica bean cost inflation that pressured the build side of the ratio, (b) the deliberate step-up in advertising and marketing investment (Vertuo World campaign called Nespresso's biggest ever) that pushed the sell side higher, and (c) the continued Vertuo rollout in the US where Keurig still has dominant pod-coffee share. The combination of cost pressure and increased marketing spend pushed Nespresso's UTOP margin down from 20.3% (2023) to ~20.0% (2024 estimate), consistent with the H1 2024 -20bps disclosure and a modest H2 recovery from pricing.

**CHF-to-USD conversion at 1.135.** Nestlé reports in Swiss francs (CHF). The 2024 annual average CHF/USD rate was approximately 1.135 USD per CHF (CHF was structurally strong versus USD through 2024 and into 2025). All USD figures in the breakdown are converted from underlying CHF estimates at 1.135. This conversion is consistent within ~2% — the daily CHF/USD ranged roughly 1.10-1.17 through 2024, and using the annual average produces representative USD figures while the underlying CHF figures are the authoritative inputs.

**Nespresso 2024 UTOP margin estimate of 20.0% — triangulated from disclosure.** Nestlé does not disclose Nespresso's full-year 2024 UTOP margin as a standalone number in the press release; the half-year 2024 disclosure noted Nespresso UTOP margin -20bps versus prior year on increased A&M investment. The 2023 anchor of 20.3% (disclosed) less ~30bps deterioration produces a 2024 estimate of ~20.0%. The full-year 2025 disclosure (which would confirm 2024) would refine this number but is not yet at the level of granularity needed at the GMB level. The ~20.0% UTOP estimate is on the conservative side of Nespresso's recent history — pre-2022 the UTOP margin ran 22-25% — and the 20bps-30bps deterioration story is internally consistent with the explicit ad-spend step-up and the coffee-input-cost narrative.

**Build/sell classification of boutique operating cost — 70/30 split toward sell.** Nespresso boutiques are dual-purpose facilities. They function both as brand-experience marketing venues (the Apple Store analogue — premium retail environment, trained product specialists, in-store demonstrations, tastings, customer onboarding, the new 'immersive coffee experience' flagship concept rolled out at NYC and Champs-Élysées) and as in-person pickup/fulfillment locations (customers can walk in and buy capsules across the counter; many regular customers use boutiques for capsule pickup rather than ordering online or via the Club). The 70/30 split toward sell-side reflects the economic reality that the primary value of operating a premium-rent NYC flagship store is brand experience and customer acquisition, not fulfillment efficiency — fulfillment could be done much more cheaply through warehouse logistics. The 30% build-side share captures the actual fulfillment value the boutiques provide. A different classification (e.g. 50/50) would shift ~$180M between build and sell and would move the build_pct from 70.8% to ~73.3%; a 90/10 split toward sell would move it to ~68.5%. The directional finding (build_pct in the 68-73% range) is robust to this classification choice.

**Boutique cost estimate of $600M total — derived from typical premium-retail unit economics.** 818 boutiques averaging ~$700K USD annual operating cost per store produces ~$575M total; rounded to ~$600M to account for the disproportionate cost of premium-flagship stores (NYC Fifth Avenue, Champs-Élysées Paris, Bond Street London — these flagship stores likely cost $5-10M+ each annually given the rent of those locations). This is consistent with publicly-disclosed premium-retail comparable economics (Apple Stores average ~$2-3M+ per year operating cost depending on format; Nespresso boutiques are smaller-format on average but premium-located). The total figure carries triangulation uncertainty of ±$100M but the directional contribution to the sell side is meaningful and well-justified.

**Advertising-and-marketing intensity for Nespresso ~9-10% of revenue, versus Nestlé corporate average of 8.1%.** Nestlé does not disclose Nespresso-specific A&M intensity, but several pieces of evidence indicate it runs above the corporate average: (a) Nespresso is the most retail-presence-heavy and premium-positioned brand in the Nestlé portfolio, (b) the long-running Clooney ambassador program plus the new Dua Lipa addition imply heavy talent fees on top of media spend, (c) Nestlé executives explicitly described the Vertuo World campaign as the largest in Nespresso's 40-year history, (d) the Nespresso brand has historically run higher A&M intensity than Nescafé instant coffee because of the premium positioning and the structural need to defend the closed-pod price point against compatible-pod competitors. The combined advertising + brand events / sponsorships estimate of ~$731M represents ~10% of revenue, which is plausible given these factors. This estimate carries triangulation uncertainty of ±$100M; a different intensity assumption (e.g. 8% or 12%) would shift build_pct by ~2 percentage points but the directional finding (build_pct in the high 60s to low 70s) is robust.

**Arabica coffee cost inflation was the dominant 2024 build-side story.** Arabica coffee bean prices hit multi-decade highs through 2024 — Brazilian frost concerns in mid-2024, robusta market tightness pushing arabica substitution demand, continued post-pandemic out-of-home coffee demand recovery, and weather-driven crop concerns in major arabica-producing regions all contributed. Nespresso's premium-arabica sourcing (the AAA program is predominantly arabica with meaningful single-origin and reserve-grade share) means it bears more of the arabica spike than commodity-coffee competitors who can substitute toward robusta. The COGS-green-coffee line of $1,569M is materially higher than would have been the case in 2022-2023 with normal-range arabica pricing. This is the primary reason the Coffee category UTOP margin compressed 40bps in 2024 despite Nestlé's pricing actions partially offsetting input costs.

**Why I do not count compatible-pod competitor pressure as a build/sell classification issue.** Compatible-pod competitors (Lavazza A Modo Mio compatibles, JDE Peet's L'Or compatibles, supermarket private-label Nespresso-compatible pods from many roasters since the original-line patents expired in 2012) capture an estimated 30-40% of the Nespresso Original-line installed-base pod consumption in many European markets. This is an economic pressure on Nespresso's pod revenue per installed machine — but it does not change the build/sell ratio computation since it manifests as lower revenue per Nespresso Original-line machine rather than as a build-or-sell cost line. The Vertuo system (barcode-reading, larger pod format) was designed in part to create a new technical platform that compatible-pod competitors have not yet meaningfully entered, and the Vertuo-rollout strategic priority in 2024 is partly a response to this. The build/sell ratio captures the cost structure Nespresso operates with given its current revenue base; compatible-pod competition affects the revenue base level but not the ratio composition.

**Machine OEM transfer pricing — explicit recognition of the razor-and-blades structure.** Nespresso does not manufacture machines; machines are made by OEM partners under license. When a Nespresso machine is sold through Nespresso.com or a boutique, Nespresso pays the OEM a transfer price and books the customer-facing retail price; the machine COGS line in the Nespresso GMB is this transfer price. The retail margin on machines is typically thin per the razor-and-blades strategy — machines are positioned as a customer-acquisition vehicle for the pod-revenue stream. When a Nespresso machine is sold through third-party appliance retail (Williams-Sonoma, Sur La Table, Costco, Amazon, John Lewis, Selfridges), the OEM books the revenue and pays Nespresso a small license fee (which is in the Nespresso GMB top line but is small). This means the Nespresso GMB revenue is heavily dominated by pod sales — likely 80-85% of revenue is pods/capsules, 10-15% is machines sold through Nespresso-owned channels, and the remainder is accessories and the Nespresso Professional out-of-home business. The 10-15% machine revenue share is consistent with the ~$453M machine COGS line.

**Nespresso Professional (out-of-home) — a meaningful sub-business inside the GMB.** Nespresso Professional sells to offices, hotels, restaurants, airline lounges (Emirates, Lufthansa, Singapore Airlines), and other commercial customers. Q4 2024 growth in out-of-home channels was specifically called out by Nestlé as a contributor to Nespresso's full-year performance. This sub-business operates more like a B2B sales-force-driven business than a DTC-retail business, so it has a different sell-side cost structure (sales-force compensation rather than boutique rent). The blended Nespresso GMB sell-side ratio is the consumer-DTC-dominated number; if Nespresso Professional were broken out separately it would likely show somewhat lower sell-side intensity and higher build-side intensity than the consumer business. The blended ratio used here is the GMB-level disclosure and reflects the consumer-business weight.

**What's surprising in the numbers.**

- **Nespresso build_pct of 70.8% is at the upper end of the ticket-spec predicted range (55-70%).** The arabica-cost inflation in 2024 pushed the build side higher than would have been the case in 2022-2023 — at normal-range arabica pricing, the green-coffee COGS line would be ~$1,200-1,300M rather than ~$1,570M, and the build_pct would settle closer to 67-68%. The 70.8% datapoint reflects 2024's commodity-cost environment rather than a structural shift in Nespresso's cost composition.

- **Sell-side intensity of 21.8% (sell / revenue) is among the highest in the consumer-products panel.** This is driven by the combination of (a) the 818-boutique premium-retail footprint (~$420M sell-side share of boutique opex), (b) the heavy A&M intensity around the Vertuo rollout (~$663M advertising / measured media), and (c) the machine-subsidy and trade-promotion intensity inherent to the razor-and-blades structure (~$249M). For comparison, the KitchenAid stand mixer (a brand-led premium gifting product) ran sell-side intensity of ~10.3% — Nespresso is more than double that, reflecting the DTC retail model. Among the panel comparables, Nespresso's sell-side intensity is closer to Starbucks-like or premium beauty-like territory than to typical CPG.

- **The razor-and-blades structure operates as designed.** The thin/negative-margin machine economics are real (machines sold by Nespresso at near-cost transfer price plus thin retail markup), and the high gross margin on capsules (~70%+ gross margin on capsules per the residual computation) more than offsets to produce the ~20% UTOP margin at the GMB level. The strategic genius of the closed-pod system is on full display in the cost structure — the customer-acquisition cost is paid upfront as machine subsidy + boutique rent + Clooney media, and amortized over the 10+ year pod-purchase tail.

- **Boutique strategy is unusually retail-investment-intensive for a beverage CPG.** Most beverage CPG companies do not operate any owned-retail footprint at all (Coca-Cola Co., Mondelez, AB InBev all sell through third-party retail with zero owned-retail). The 818-boutique Nespresso network is closer to Apple's or LVMH's owned-retail strategy than to typical CPG distribution. This is a deliberate brand-equity choice — Nespresso has consistently said that the boutique network is core to the premium positioning, the customer-onboarding experience, and the defensive moat against compatible-pod competitors.

- **Switzerland manufacturing footprint is a structural cost commitment.** All Nespresso capsules globally are manufactured at the three Swiss plants (Avenches, Romont, Orbe). Swiss labor and operating costs are materially higher than would be the case if Nespresso manufactured in lower-cost European countries (Poland, Romania) or in Asia. This is similar to the Rolex 'Swiss Made' commitment — the manufacturing geography is part of the brand-equity positioning and is a deliberate cost commitment. The CHF appreciation versus USD/EUR through 2023-2024 amplified this cost pressure in customer-currency terms.

- **The Vertuo World campaign as the biggest in 40 years signals the strategic priority of US market penetration.** Keurig still has dominant US pod-coffee market share, and Vertuo (with the larger pod format closer to American drip-coffee consumption preferences) is Nespresso's primary attack vector in the US. The campaign step-up in 2024-2025 is the public face of this strategic priority. The sell-side increase in the 2024 datapoint is therefore partly a strategic investment in future growth rather than steady-state operating cost — to the extent the Vertuo penetration succeeds, the future Nespresso sell-side intensity could compress back toward 18-19% rather than the 21.8% observed in 2024.

**Confidence: medium.** Major inputs (Nespresso GMB sales, Nestlé corporate UTOP margin and A&M intensity, 2023 Nespresso UTOP margin) come from Nestlé's own audited annual report disclosures. The major triangulations (Nespresso 2024 UTOP margin specifically, boutique operating cost total, Nespresso-specific A&M intensity, green-coffee COGS line at 2024 arabica pricing, machine COGS line, the 70/30 boutique build/sell classification) are estimated within reasonable bands and the directional finding (build_pct in the 68-73% range) is robust to those classification choices. The medium-confidence rating reflects the necessity of estimating Nespresso-specific A&M intensity, the boutique cost triangulation, and the 2024 Nespresso UTOP margin from the half-year directional disclosure rather than reading it directly from a single line item. The fact that Nestlé reports Nespresso as a standalone GMB with revenue and (historically) margin disclosure makes this a more confident analysis than would otherwise be possible for a brand-level question inside a multi-segment parent, but the residual estimation required keeps it below the 'high' bar consistent with the ticket spec.
