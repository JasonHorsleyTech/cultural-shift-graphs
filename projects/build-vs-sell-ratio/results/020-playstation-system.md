## Build breakdown ($ millions)

The PlayStation system economics are a hardware-software-services bundle where the hardware platform is a near-zero-margin enabling layer for the software and services profit pool. Build is dominated by (a) third-party software royalties paid back to publishers when third-party games sell through PSN store (these are gross-up costs that scale with revenue), (b) hardware cost of goods sold for PS5 base + Pro consoles, (c) first-party game studio costs (development + content amortization, mostly classified as R&D and content amortization), and (d) PlayStation Network operational costs. The "marketing-led" stereotype of console gaming is real but the absolute marketing dollars are modest relative to the COGS firehose, as has been consistent across other consumer electronics tickets.

- **Cost of goods sold (G&NS)** — third-party software royalty payments (when a third-party game sells digitally through PSN store, Sony books gross revenue at retail price and pays ~70% of net to the publisher, recorded as COGS — this is the largest single line in G&NS COGS at ~$8-9B annually); PS5 base console hardware cost (custom AMD Zen 2 + RDNA 2 APU manufactured at TSMC 7nm process, GDDR6 memory from Micron and Samsung, custom SSD from Sony in-house design with Kioxia/Samsung NAND, optical drive sub-module on disc SKU, DualSense controller hardware, packaging, ocean freight from contract assembly in China to regional distribution); PS5 Pro console hardware (higher ASP, larger custom APU with upgraded RDNA 2/3 hybrid GPU and PSSR upscaler, more GDDR6, larger SSD); first-party software content amortization (capitalized portion of first-party game development is amortized to COGS over the post-launch revenue tail of each title); third-party physical software wholesale COGS; warranty reserves and refurbishment for PS5 returns; PlayStation Network and cloud streaming infrastructure operating costs (AWS spend, in-house data center electricity and depreciation, content delivery network for PSN store and game-update distribution); distribution and logistics. Estimated 70-72% of G&NS revenue: **22,000**

- **R&D allocated to G&NS** — first-party game studio development (PlayStation Studios — Naughty Dog Santa Monica, Insomniac Burbank, Insomniac North Carolina via Firesprite/Pixelopus heritage, Sucker Punch Bellevue, Santa Monica Studio LA, Bend Studio Oregon, Bungie Bellevue, Bluepoint Austin, Housemarque Helsinki, Guerrilla Amsterdam, Polyphony Digital Tokyo, London Studio, Media Molecule Guildford, Firesprite Liverpool, Pixelopus, Haven Studios Montreal, Nixxes PC porting studio Utrecht — ~17 studios with ~4,000+ developers); console silicon and system architecture R&D (custom AMD APU semi-custom partnership; system board, memory subsystem, custom SSD I/O architecture; thermal engineering for PS5 base and Pro; DualSense controller engineering with adaptive triggers and haptics; PSVR2 sustaining engineering); Tempest Engine 3D audio; system software (PS5 OS, PS Store backend, account/identity systems, PS+ subscription stack, PSN messaging and party-chat infrastructure); cloud streaming engineering for PS+ Premium streaming tier; remote play and PS App development. Sony allocated ~¥273 billion (~$1.80B) to G&NS R&D in FY2024: **1,800**

- **Customer support and network operations (allocated from G&A)** — PlayStation Support call centers in EN/FR/DE/ES/JA/KO/ZH/PT covering hardware warranty claims (panel issues, controller drift, fan failure, disc drive failure, SSD issues), PSN account support (account recovery, charge disputes, parental controls, two-factor authentication assistance), PS+ subscription support (billing, plan changes, refunds for incorrectly purchased content), online chat support, security operations (PSN has not had a major public breach since 2011 but security operations are continuous), trust and safety operations (player conduct moderation, child safety, transactions safety). Customer support estimated at 1.3% of segment revenue: **400**

**Build total: 23,800**

## Methodology notes

**Why we used the segment, not the SKU.** The ticket explicitly mandated this restructuring; this notes section documents why. The PS5 console as a standalone unit is sold near cost — Sony reportedly entered hardware-profit-per-unit territory during late FY2023 and has been at modest positive per-unit margin during FY2024 (~$20-40 net per unit on PS5 base; somewhat higher on PS5 Pro at $699+ ASP). If we measured PS5-the-SKU we'd capture a near-zero-margin loss-leader and miss where the system economics actually live: third-party software royalties (the largest single revenue line at ~45% of G&NS), first-party software (~10%), and PlayStation Plus / network services subscription revenue. The system is the unit because the hardware is engineered to enable a software/services economy, not because consumers buy hardware in isolation. Methodology choice: G&NS segment financials, gross of intercompany royalty flows but net of inter-segment Sony Group flows (G&NS pays Sony Music for music licensing in games, Sony Pictures for trailer placements — these are immaterial to the ratio).

**The third-party royalty gross-up effect on the ratio.** Sony's largest single revenue line within G&NS is Network Services (~45% of segment sales), which is dominated by digital game sales through the PlayStation Store. When a third-party publisher's game sells digitally on PSN at $69.99, Sony books $69.99 in gross revenue and pays roughly $48.99 (70%) back to the publisher as COGS. The $48.99 royalty payment is a real cost to Sony but it inflates both the revenue denominator and the build numerator by an equal amount. **If we measured Sony's net take instead, both revenue and build would shrink by ~$5-7B.** Stress test: at net-take treatment, G&NS revenue would be ~$24B, build would be ~$17.5B, sell unchanged at $2.9B, build_pct would shift to ~85.8% (down from 89.1% in gross-treatment). The directional finding (system is build-dominated) is robust either way. I used gross treatment for consistency with how Sony reports the segment and how peer companies (Microsoft Gaming, Nintendo) treat their digital marketplaces.

**Why R&D was allocated at 36% of consolidated rather than revenue-share 33%.** Sony's R&D firehose is concentrated in three segments — G&NS (game studios + console silicon), I&SS (CMOS image sensors — Sony's profitable mobile-camera-sensor business and a meaningful R&D consumer), and ET&S (Bravia panels, Alpha cameras, audio). G&NS gets a slightly over-revenue-share R&D allocation because PlayStation Studios first-party game development is structurally R&D-heavy (4,000+ developers across 17 studios with multi-year AAA development cycles), and console silicon engineering (custom AMD APU partnership, system architecture, Tempest Engine, DualSense haptics, PSVR2 sustaining) requires meaningful semiconductor design work even though the manufacturing is outsourced to AMD/TSMC. The ¥273B G&NS R&D figure is from Sony's Annual Report segment R&D disclosure.

**First-party game development capitalization vs expensing — where does it land in build/sell?** Sony first-party AAA game development is partially capitalized (the portion clearly identifiable as creating future-revenue-generating intangible assets) and partially expensed. Capitalized development is then amortized to COGS over the post-launch revenue tail (typically 3-5 years for AAA narrative titles, longer for live-service like Helldivers 2). Both treatments land in build per the rubric — capitalized portion flows through COGS as content amortization, expensed portion flows through R&D. In FY2024, first-party content amortization is estimated at $1.5-2.0B (running through COGS); first-party current-year R&D expense is ~$1.0B (running through R&D); plus third-party contract development (e.g., partnerships with FromSoftware, Square Enix, Capcom for timed exclusives) where the cost-revenue-recognition pattern depends on the deal structure. All flows to build either way.

**The Concord write-down is in FY2024 OI.** Sony's prominent live-service failure of FY2024 was Concord (released August 2024, pulled from market September 2024 within 12 days of launch). Reported development cost over the multi-year cycle exceeded $200M; Sony has not separately disclosed the FY2024 write-down but it absorbed into G&NS operating income for the year. This is part of why Q3 FY2024 OI didn't accelerate as strongly as Q4 — Concord absorbed Q2-Q3 losses, then Q4 had the upward year-end adjustment swing. Helldivers 2 (released February 2024 with first-mover-success live-service economics) was the partial offset — a $40M-budget title that drove ~$1B+ in revenue with positive contribution margin from launch. Both effects are in the FY2024 numbers as-reported.

**PS5 hardware ASP and per-unit margin.** PS5 base console ASP wholesale to retailers ~$425 (retail $499 less typical retailer margin); PS5 Pro ~$580 wholesale ($699 retail); PS5 Slim base SKU ~$425 wholesale ($499 retail). Sony's commentary indicates per-unit hardware contribution margin is positive but modest — Mat Piscatella at Circana and Bernstein analyst estimates put it at ~$20-40 net per unit on PS5 base, somewhat higher on PS5 Pro. On 18.5M units shipped FY2024, total hardware contribution to G&NS OI is ~$500-700M — a meaningful but not majority piece of the $2.73B segment OI. The bulk of the OI comes from PS+ subscription margins and third-party royalty take (where Sony's marginal cost of supporting one more digital sale through PSN is near zero).

**Currency and reporting period.** Sony reports in JPY with a consolidated FY ending March 31 each year. FY2024 = April 2024 to March 2025. Used average FY2024 USDJPY of ~152 for translation; this is consistent with Sony's own reference rate published in the FY2024 Annual Report. JPY weakened from ~149 at start of FY24 to ~150 at end of FY24, with intra-year volatility around 145-160. Using the average rate produces stable USD comparisons.

**Marketing intensity is genuinely lower than other CE peers.** Sony G&NS spends ~$1.8B globally on marketing — meaningful in absolute terms (more than the entire revenue of mid-tier publishers like THQ Nordic or Embracer Group) but as a percentage of revenue (~5.9%) it's lower than Samsung VD (~8% of TV revenue), lower than Apple consolidated (~7%), lower than Nintendo (which is more marketing-intensive per dollar of revenue because Nintendo doesn't have third-party royalty gross-up inflating its denominator). The Microsoft/Xbox comparison would put Sony in the middle — Microsoft Gaming has higher marketing intensity per revenue dollar because Game Pass conversion is the strategic priority.

**Sales force compensation classification per Sony's books.** Sony reports SG&A as a single consolidated line at the segment level without breaking out selling vs general/administrative. I used a bottom-up estimate of advertising spend ($1.8B from third-party media measurement) plus sales force ($400M from headcount estimates of ~3,000-3,500 commercial-org employees globally at ~$120K loaded comp) plus trade promotion ($700M from analyst estimates) for total sell of $2.9B. The residual SG&A of ~$1,270M lands in overhead. This bottom-up approach is more defensible than a top-down 60/40 split given the disclosure gap.

**Confidence: medium.** Sony Group is publicly listed on TSE (6758) and NYSE (SONY) as ADRs, files detailed Japanese Securities Reports and English 20-Fs, and discloses segment-level revenue, operating income, R&D, and capex by segment. The segment-level reporting is materially better than Samsung's within-DX sub-segment reporting (Samsung tickets came in at low-medium confidence) but lower-resolution than US-listed pure-plays like Apple or Microsoft (where segment SG&A and gross margin can be partially reverse-engineered). The G&NS segment R&D figure is disclosed; advertising allocation is estimated from third-party media measurement. The third-party royalty gross-up treatment is the meaningful methodology choice; I documented sensitivity above. Confidence sits at medium — the build_pct ratio is robust to ±2pp under all plausible alternative classifications, but absolute revenue/profit figures depend on the FX rate and segment-allocation assumptions.

**What's surprising in the numbers.**

- **Build_pct of 89.1% is at the upper end of the ticket's anticipated 75-85% range, but consistent with the broader project finding.** Apple iPhone/Mac/AirPods came in at 93-95%; Samsung VD at 86.9%; PlayStation system lands between them at 89.1%. The "marketing-led" stereotype of console gaming holds in directional terms (Sony G&NS is more marketing-intensive than Apple per revenue dollar) but the magnitude is small and the system is still build-dominated. The third-party royalty gross-up is a meaningful contributor to this — without it the ratio would land at ~85.8%, still build-dominated but closer to the middle of the ticket's range.
- **Hardware is genuinely a small piece of segment economics now.** Hardware revenue is ~25% of G&NS sales (~$7.5B on ~$30.7B); hardware contribution to OI is ~$500-700M of $2.73B (~20-25% of OI). The system is now genuinely a software-and-services business with hardware as the enabling layer. PS5 reaching hardware profitability per unit during FY2023 was a structural shift — for most of the PS5 cycle prior, hardware was a contribution loss. The ratio reflects the post-2023 economics.
- **R&D over-indexes vs revenue share.** G&NS gets ~36% of Sony's R&D budget despite being ~33% of consolidated revenue. First-party game studios (4,000+ developers across 17 studios) and console silicon engineering are genuinely R&D-heavy. This is a build-skewing effect that Apple's hardware businesses also exhibit.
- **The Concord write-down + Helldivers 2 success roughly cancel in FY2024 OI.** Sony's most prominent live-service launch failure (Concord, $200M+ over the cycle, pulled within 12 days) and most prominent live-service success (Helldivers 2, $40M budget, $1B+ revenue) both materially impacted FY2024 G&NS results. The net effect is roughly a wash for the year-on-year segment OI improvement (which is driven mostly by hardware mix-shift and PS+ subscriber growth, not by these two titles individually).
- **PlayStation Studios scale.** ~17 first-party studios, ~4,000+ developers, total operating cost of $2.0-2.5B annually flowing through R&D and content amortization. This is comparable in absolute scale to Microsoft Gaming Studios (with ZeniMax/Bethesda + Activision Blizzard now combined post-2023 acquisition close) but smaller than Tencent-scale gaming portfolios. Growing first-party multi-platform release strategy (Helldivers 2 launched simultaneously on PS5 + PC; Horizon Zero Dawn Remastered; God of War Ragnarok released on PC October 2024) is a structural shift from the historical "PlayStation exclusive" model — it expands the addressable revenue base for first-party content but doesn't change the cost structure ratios materially.
- **PS+ subscribers at 123M — a structurally important recurring revenue base.** Sony has ~123M PS+ subscribers paying an average ~$80-90/year blended across Essential, Extra, and Premium tiers — implies $10-11B annualized PS+ revenue (some of which is captured in the Network Services revenue category, some in add-on content). Mix shifting toward higher tiers (Premium 17M tier with cloud streaming, PS3 streaming, classics catalog) is structurally margin-accretive.
