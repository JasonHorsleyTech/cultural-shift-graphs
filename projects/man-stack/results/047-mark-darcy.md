# Mark Darcy

## Character Summary
- **Source work:** *Bridget Jones's Diary* (2001 film), Miramax / Universal / StudioCanal, dir. Sharon Maguire, screenplay Helen Fielding / Andrew Davies / Richard Curtis, with Colin Firth as Mark Darcy. Underlying novel: Helen Fielding, *Bridget Jones's Diary* (1996), originally serialized in *The Independent* (1995) as a Pride-and-Prejudice-riffing modern-London weekly column. The Mark Darcy character is an explicit Mr.-Darcy-of-1813 transplant — Fielding has confirmed in interviews that she lifted the surname, the haughty-introduction beat, the "noble man initially seen as cold" arc, the Wickham-Cleaver parallel, and the eventual humbled-confession structure directly from Austen, with the modern transposition (London barrister, Christmas-jumper Reindeer-print mortification, etc.) layered over the inherited Regency skeleton. The 2001 casting of Colin Firth — who had played the Regency Mr. Darcy in the 1995 BBC adaptation, ticket 005 — is the explicit meta-joke of the film.
- **Audience:** female-targeted
- **Decade slot:** 2000s
- **Cultural reach proxy:** Worldwide gross ~$281.9M on a ~$25M budget (Box Office Mojo) — one of the highest-grossing romantic comedies of the early 2000s. Helen Fielding's source novel sold 15M+ copies globally and was translated into 40+ languages. The franchise produced three sequels — *The Edge of Reason* (2004), *Bridget Jones's Baby* (2016), and *Mad About the Boy* (2025) — with Mark Darcy returning in the first two. Mark Darcy has appeared on virtually every "best fictional boyfriend" / "best rom-com leading man" list of the 2000s–2020s (Cosmopolitan, Glamour, Marie Claire, Vulture, Empire, Buzzfeed, Time Out) and is consistently cited alongside Mr. Darcy 1995 as the cultural touchstone for the "reserved-but-secretly-devoted English gentleman" archetype. Colin Firth's two Darcys — 1995 Fitzwilliam Darcy and 2001 Mark Darcy — have effectively defined the female-target English-leading-man template for the post-1990s romance genre.
- **Canonical version used:** 2001 film (Colin Firth's portrayal). The novel and the screenplay align closely on the universal-desirable trait stack — top human rights barrister, Cambridge-educated, tall, reserved, divorced from Natasha — so adaptation choice doesn't materially shift the calc. Where the 2001 film adds material the novel is silent on (the Christmas-jumper meet-cute, the snowy-streets climax, the Daniel Cleaver fistfight), those additions are treated as canonical to *this* version. The 2004 sequel (*Edge of Reason*) is non-load-bearing for the trait stack — it adds plot complications but doesn't alter Mark's underlying universal-desirable profile.

Traits considered and excluded:

- **Income ≥ $10M/yr**: BORDERLINE — flagged. Mark Darcy is canonically wealthy: lives in a substantial house in Holland Park (London property of that scale ≈ £5–15M), drives premium cars, dresses in bespoke tailoring, has a country house (per *Edge of Reason*), and his parents (Sir Malcolm and Lady Darcy) carry knighthood-tier social standing. However, his depicted profession is "top human rights barrister" — a specialism where even silk-tier QCs typically earn £400K–£1.5M/yr in the UK market (cf. UK Bar Council fee surveys; commercial silks reach £2–5M but human-rights specialists do not). Combined with plausible inherited family income (Sir Malcolm's wealth) the realistic total annual income is roughly $1–4M, which is top ~0.5% of UK earners but does NOT clearly clear the project's $10M default threshold (top 0.02% of US tax filers, base rate 2e-4). Excluded for conservatism — counting the income tier here would be a stretch beyond the underlying canonical signaling. The analogous decision was made for Mr. Darcy 1995 in reverse direction (his £10K/year mapped *clearly above* $10M tier per Hume / MeasuringWorth — the conservative call there was to count it; the conservative call here is the reverse, to NOT count it). The expert-barrister trait already captures the professional-achievement source of his wealth, and double-counting would inflate the rarity. If a reviewer reads Mark as clearing $10M income (e.g. via aggressive inheritance assumption), the joint shifts from ~3.8e-6 to ~7.6e-10 (~5,000x rarer), placing him in Mr. Darcy 1995's neighborhood. See Notes for the wealth-tier sensitivity.
- **Intelligence ≥ 130 IQ**: BORDERLINE — flagged. Mark is Cambridge-educated, top-of-bar at QC-equivalent rank, demonstrates rapid courtroom thinking and complex international-legal reasoning on screen, recovers Bridget's necklace through investigative cleverness, and his profession itself selects on top-2% verbal/analytic cognitive ability. The case for IQ ≥ 130 is significantly STRONGER than for Mr. Darcy 1995 (whose Cambridge education was excluded for conservatism due to lack of on-screen demonstration). However, including the trait here would partially DOUBLE-COUNT with the expert-barrister trait — top-tier legal advocacy implicitly selects on cognitive ability (per the joint-distribution note in the new base-rate entry: "for joint with IQ ≥ 130, naive product OVERSTATES rarity by ~2–3x"). Excluded under the same Mr. Darcy 1995 precedent (conservative reading; Cambridge alone is not enough), and to avoid the double-count. If a reviewer includes IQ ≥ 130 with the appropriate ~2.5x correlation discount, the joint shifts from ~3.8e-6 to ~3e-8 — moving Mark into Edward Lewis's neighborhood (~1e-8) rather than the Romeo / Jack Dawson achievable-archetype band.
- **Physical fitness (top quintile)**: NO. Mark engages Daniel Cleaver in the famous 2001 street-fistfight sequence, but the choreography is deliberately COMIC and bumbling — both men are middle-aged white-collar professionals in suits, the punches are awkward, the fight is intercut with shopkeepers shouting at them for property damage. This is the OPPOSITE of an athletic-competence beat; it's a Whedon-style "two desk-bound men try to fight over a woman" comedy set-piece. Mark is depicted throughout the film as fit-but-sedentary — slim build, no visible muscle, no athletic activity, no sport, no on-screen physical training. Excluded.
- **Musical / artistic skill**: NO. Not depicted playing instruments, painting, composing, or performing at any tier in either film. Excluded.
- **Languages beyond English**: NO direct on-screen evidence in the 2001 film. Per top-tier human rights litigation practice, fluency in at least one non-English language (French / German for European Court of Human Rights work) would be plausible but is NOT canonically established. Excluded under strict reading; would add 0.20 if a single non-native language were counted at marginal rate.
- **Family unencumbered**: EXPLICITLY FAILS. Sir Malcolm and Lady Darcy (Mark's parents) are alive and intimately involved in the plot — they are friends of Bridget's parents Pam and Colin, host the recurring Christmas-turkey-curry parties, and Mark's mother engineers the meet-cute by inviting Bridget to the Christmas party (in the novel, the famous reindeer-jumper introduction). This is the structural OPPOSITE of family-unencumbered — Mark's family is the social mechanism by which he meets Bridget. NOT counted. The same call as Romeo Montague (ticket 045) — canonical inverse architecture, family-encumbered as plot premise.
- **Currently single / divorced**: Mark is at the start of the film very recently divorced from his Japanese ex-wife Natasha (or in *Edge of Reason* canon, more recently separated). This is plot-relevant but is NOT a universal-desirable trait on the FEMALE-target stack (the methodology assigns the "currently single" filter to the MALE-target stack, not the female-target stack — being single is a dating-availability filter applied to women only in this methodology, see plan.md). Excluded as not-on-this-stack (not as "trait fails").

## Personality / Characterization (Excluded from Calc)

- **Reserved / awkward / emotionally restrained**: The defining beat. Mark Darcy as written by Fielding and played by Firth is the platonic ideal of "English emotional repression as romantic appeal" — cannot easily express affection, struggles in social situations, blushes and stutters in moments of vulnerability. Polarizing in modern reception (some find emotional reserve attractive, some find it frustrating).
- **Secretly devoted / quietly principled**: Defends Bridget against Daniel Cleaver's slut-shaming at a literary launch, helps Bridget's mother out of the fraudster Julio scheme, recovers her stolen necklace, ghostwrites her career-saving TV interview research. The "secretly does enormous favors without taking credit" beat is the load-bearing romantic-fantasy hook of the character — and is the explicit modern-update of Mr. Darcy 1813's behind-the-scenes Wickham/Lydia rescue. Personality, not a slider.
- **The reindeer-jumper humiliation / Christmas-party meet-cute**: Iconic comedic beat — the screenplay's ironic visual joke that the dignified barrister was once dressed by his mother in the reindeer Christmas jumper. Polarizing register (some find the wry self-deprecation attractive, some find it twee).
- **Wronged-by-Cleaver backstory**: Mark's first wife Natasha cheated on him with Daniel Cleaver — the explicit Wickham-parallel from the Austen template. This is the "history of being wronged" beat that establishes Cleaver as the antagonist and Mark as the maligned-but-honorable man. Plot-architecture rather than universal desirable trait.
- **Honor-bound social manner / "decent chap" coding**: The British-establishment-decency aesthetic. Polarizing — some readers find it deeply attractive (chivalric, reliable), some find it stuffy.
- **The "I like you very much, just as you are" speech**: The single most-quoted line of the franchise — Mark's confession to Bridget that he loves her without wanting her to change. Personality / character-arc beat.
- **Awkward physical comedy register**: Firth plays Mark with deliberate physical-stiffness comedy — the formal handshake, the upright posture, the inability to dance to pop music, the Christmas-jumper humiliation. Polarizing.
- **Cambridge-educated establishment register**: Defines a specific upper-middle-class British register that splits by audience preference. Personality, not slider.

## Joint Probability

- **Method:** threshold (female-target stack)
- **Naive product of marginals:**
  0.04 × 0.094 × 1e-3
  = (0.04 × 0.094) = 3.76e-3
  × 1e-3 = **3.76e-6**
  (≈ 1 in 266,000)
- **Correlation adjustment:**
  - **Expert-barrister × age 28–38: NEGATIVE correlation** (load-bearing). Per the new base-rates.md entry: silk-tier / partner-rank legal practice typically requires 12–18 years post-bar practice, so the modal expert-tier advocate is 40+. P(expert-tier lawyer | age 28–38) ≈ 0.3–0.5x the marginal rate. Naive product UNDERSTATES rarity for this pair — Mark at canonical age ~37 making top-tier QC equivalent is precocious by real-world UK bar timelines (typical QC silk-call is ~mid-40s). Pulls joint **~2–3x rarer than naive**.
  - **Height × age**: weakly positive (taller men slightly skew younger in modern cohort data via Flynn-effect / nutritional shifts; mild). Pulls joint slightly less rare. ~1.1x adjustment.
  - **Height × expert-barrister**: approximately independent. Height does correlate weakly with income / professional success in labor economics (Persico-Postlewaite-Silverman 2004, Case-Paxson 2008), but the effect at the top-tier-professional level is small relative to the order-of-magnitude bracket already in play. Negligible adjustment.
  - **Net direction:** The expert-barrister × age negative correlation dominates. Per the project methodology note: female-side stacks tend to be MORE rare than naive product because traits trade against each other in time-budget terms. Mark's stack exhibits a clean instance of this — making top human rights barrister by age 37 is meaningfully rarer than the naive product of (expert lawyer marginal) × (age 28–38 marginal) suggests. Estimated **~2x rarer than naive**.
- **Adjusted estimate:** ~**2e-6** (≈ 1 in 500,000). Bracket: [1e-6, 4e-6].
- **Population implication:** Out of ~127M US adult men (Census 2024), expected matches ≈ **~250 individuals** (range ~125–500 across the bracket). Out of ~3.5B global adult men, expected matches ≈ ~7,000. Mark Darcy's universal-desirable stack is **eminently real-world achievable** — there are several hundred US men, and several thousand globally, who would clear his strict trait stack (tall, in prime age window, top-tier legal advocate). His position in the female-target distribution: **moderate-rare band** — sitting between the achievable-archetype cluster (Romeo ~4e-5, Jack Dawson ~1.5e-5, Westley ~1e-6, Johnny Castle ~3e-7) and the deeper-rare cluster anchored by Mr. Darcy 1995 (~2.5e-9). His trait stack at the conservative reading is roughly **1,000x LESS RARE than Mr. Darcy 1995**, primarily because Mark doesn't clear the income $10M tier or the family-unencumbered trait, where Mr. Darcy 1995 clears both. This is the load-bearing finding for this character — in the conservative reading, the *modern* Darcy is significantly more achievable than the *Regency* Darcy, contrary to the franchise's deliberate conceit that they are equivalent. The fantasy element of Mark's appeal is concentrated in **personality and arc** (the reserved-but-secretly-devoted register, the Christmas-jumper humiliation, the "just as you are" speech, the reindeer-print-to-romantic-hero arc) rather than slider-stacking — a pattern consistent with the project's broader observation that 1990s/2000s heartthrobs lean on archetype where 2010s–2020s heartthrobs lean on slider-maxing.

## Confidence Assessment
- **Overall:** medium-high — trait identification is clean, the expert-barrister trait is well-grounded in canonical evidence, and the headline number sits in a regime (~10⁻⁶) where uncertainty is bounded by ~1 order of magnitude.
- **Trait identification:** high — Mark's traits are well-established across novel, screenplay, and broader franchise canon. Height (Firth at 6'2"), age (~37 canonical), top human rights barrister status (explicit dialogue, multiple scenes, plot function) are all unambiguous. The two borderline calls (income $10M, IQ ≥ 130) are flagged transparently.
- **Base rates:** medium-high — height (NHANES) and age (Census) are well-grounded existing entries; the new expert-barrister entry is calibrated to BLS Lawyers OEW data and is consistent with the project's existing 1e-3 expert-tier calibration for chef / pilot / surgeon / smith / builder / combatant. The expert-barrister rate is the largest novel-calibration source of uncertainty, but the value is anchored to real BLS occupational data and matches the cross-project tier convention.
- **Correlation adjustments:** medium-high — the expert-lawyer × age negative correlation is well-supported in real-world UK bar timelines (silk-call age data is publicly tracked by the Bar Standards Board) and the magnitude (~2x) is conservative. The remaining adjustments (height × age, height × profession) are small and don't load-bear on the headline number.

## Notes / Edge Cases

- **The "modern Darcy" parallel — load-bearing methodological observation.** Mark Darcy is the only character in the dataset where the canonical text explicitly invites comparison with another already-counted character (Mr. Darcy 1995, ticket 005, same actor, same surname, same character template). Helen Fielding's deliberate transposition from Regency Pemberley to 21st-century Holland Park gives a clean apples-to-apples test of the project's central thesis: are slider-stacks consistent across decades, or do they intensify? The result for THIS pair is *non-monotonic* — Mark Darcy at the conservative reading (~2e-6) is roughly **1,000x LESS RARE than Mr. Darcy 1995** (~2.5e-9). The reason is structural: Mark loses three traits Mr. Darcy 1995 had (income at $10M tier, family-unencumbered orphan status, fitness top quintile via on-screen riding/fencing/swimming) and gains only one (the expert-barrister trait that Mr. Darcy 1995 didn't have because Regency aristocrats didn't work). The Austen-template skeleton is preserved (tall, reserved, eventually-revealed-devoted) but the stack-rarity calculation comes out very different. This is itself a finding: writers who *deliberately preserve archetype across eras* still produce trait stacks of meaningfully different rarity, because the era's social structure (inherited aristocracy → working professional) shifts which traits are credible to portray. Worth flagging in the final graph annotations as the project's cleanest within-archetype era-comparison data point.

- **Wealth-tier sensitivity is the dominant calibration uncertainty.** The single largest swing in Mark's joint probability is whether you read his wealth as clearing the $10M income tier:
  - Conservative read (used in headline): top human rights barrister + family wealth ≈ $1–4M/yr income. Below $10M threshold. EXCLUDE income. Joint = ~2e-6.
  - Aggressive read: aggregate household wealth via Sir Malcolm Darcy inheritance + barrister income ≈ $10M+ effective annual position. INCLUDE income at 2e-4. Joint = ~4e-10 (~5,000x rarer, putting Mark into Mr. Darcy 1995's neighborhood).
  - Headline uses conservative for two reasons: (1) the methodology threshold is INCOME not WEALTH; Mark's plausible income is below the threshold even with optimistic assumptions; (2) double-counting income with the expert-barrister trait would inflate rarity since most of the income comes from the profession. Stated for transparency; a reviewer who reads the wealth aggressively would land in a meaningfully different regime.

- **Intelligence is the second-largest sensitivity.** The case for IQ ≥ 130 is significantly stronger for Mark than for Mr. Darcy 1995 — he demonstrates ongoing professional cognitive mastery (top human rights litigation, courtroom thinking, complex international-legal procedure) rather than just educational pedigree. Excluded under the same Mr. Darcy 1995 precedent (Cambridge alone insufficient without explicit on-screen IQ-130-coded feats) AND to avoid double-counting with the expert-barrister trait (the new base-rate entry notes "for joint with IQ ≥ 130, naive product OVERSTATES rarity by ~2–3x" — top legal practice implicitly selects on cognitive ability). If a reviewer includes IQ ≥ 130 with the appropriate ~2.5x correlation discount, joint shifts from ~2e-6 to ~1.6e-8 (~125x rarer) — putting Mark into Edward Lewis's neighborhood (~1e-8) rather than the Romeo / Westley achievable-archetype band.

- **Fitness exclusion and the Cleaver fistfight scene.** The 2001 film's iconic Daniel-Cleaver fistfight is sometimes cited as evidence of Mark's physicality, but the choreography is deliberately COMIC — both men are middle-aged white-collar Londoners in business suits, the punches are awkward and ineffective, the fight is intercut with shopkeepers shouting at them for breaking a window. This is structurally OPPOSITE to athletic-competence framing; it's a "two desk-bound rivals brawl over a woman" set-piece. Counted as fitness-fail. A reviewer reading "engages in a fistfight, ergo fit" would push the trait, adding 0.20 and dropping joint to ~4e-7 (~5x rarer); the conservative reading is to exclude.

- **The deliberate inversion of Mr. Darcy 1995's family-unencumbered trait.** Mr. Darcy 1813/1995 is canonically an orphan (both parents dead before the novel begins); Mark Darcy 2001 is canonically the OPPOSITE — both parents alive, intimately involved in the plot, and structurally the meeting mechanism between Mark and Bridget. Helen Fielding made this inversion deliberately — the modern Darcy's parents are part of the narrative warmth (the Christmas-turkey-curry running gag, the friendship with Pam and Colin Jones), where Mr. Darcy 1813's orphanage was part of his Regency-aristocratic isolation. Counted accurately as a non-clear (no 0.05 contribution to the stack rarity) — a meaningful structural difference between the two Darcys.

- **No mysterious dark past, by deliberate design.** Mr. Darcy 1813/1995 has the Wickham-Ramsgate backstory — the Georgiana-near-elopement that becomes the load-bearing personality-mystery beat. Mark Darcy 2001's "dark past" is canonically OPEN — Natasha cheated on him with Daniel Cleaver, the divorce is matter-of-fact rather than concealed, Bridget learns it relatively early in the film. Per plan, mysterious-past is a personality trait flagged but not quantified; Mark's past is *not* mysterious in the relevant sense (it's openly disclosed and discussed), so even the personality-flag doesn't fully apply. Same conclusion: not counted.

- **Comparison to other female-target leads in this dataset:**
  - **Mr. Darcy 1995 (~2.5e-9):** Mark is ~1,000x LESS rare. Same actor, same surname, same template — but Mark loses 3 traits (income tier, fitness, family-unencumbered) and gains 1 (expert-barrister). This is the clean within-archetype era-comparison data point of the dataset.
  - **Edward Lewis (~1e-8):** Mark is ~200x LESS rare. Edward stacks billionaire-tier wealth (3e-6) which alone dominates his joint. Mark's expert-barrister (1e-3) is much less rare than billionaire-tier (3e-6).
  - **Romeo Montague (~4e-5):** Mark is ~20x MORE rare. Romeo's 2-trait stack (inherited wealth $10M + fitness) is structurally simpler; Mark's 3-trait stack with expert-skill rarity 1e-3 is meaningfully deeper.
  - **Westley (~1e-6):** Mark is in the same neighborhood (~2x more rare). Both are 3-trait stacks with one expert-skill trait. Comparable rarity.
  - **Jack Dawson (~1.5e-5):** Mark is ~10x MORE rare. Jack's 2-trait stack (visual artist + orphan) is shallower.
  - **Christian Grey (~2e-24) / Edward Cullen (~1e-18) / Aaron Warner (~1e-18):** Mark is ~10¹²–10¹⁸ less rare. These slider-maxed-fantasy heartthrobs are the project's high-water marks; Mark is in a fundamentally different register — established-professional-archetype rather than slider-stacked-fantasy.

- **2000s decade-slot pattern.** Mark Darcy joins Edward Cullen (~1e-18) and Christian Grey (~2e-24) in the 2000s female-target slot. Note the EXTREME bimodality in this decade: Mark at ~2e-6 is on the achievable-archetype end while Cullen and Grey are at the project's furthest extreme. The 2000s decade produced both the most-grounded-in-realism and the most-slider-maxed female-target heartthrobs in the dataset — a clear sign that the slider-stacking trend was crystallizing during this decade rather than being decade-uniform. Mark Darcy specifically anchors the "established-professional Englishman" sub-archetype that has a long pedigree (Mr. Darcy 1813/1995, Heathcliff, etc.) and continues into the 2010s/2020s in less prominent forms.

- **The minimal-stack pattern continues.** Mark Darcy joins Romeo Montague, Jack Dawson, Westley, Johnny Castle, and Will Turner as another female-target heartthrob whose universal-desirable stack contains 3 traits or fewer. The pattern is now clearly established across the dataset: the most enduring romantic male leads (Mark, Mr. Darcy 1995, Romeo, Jack, Westley, Johnny, Will) consistently lean on archetype + personality + cultural footprint rather than slider-stacking, where 2010s–2020s heartthrobs (Edward, Rhysand, Cardan, Aaron Warner) heavily slider-stack. The decade-stratification appears to be doing real work in the dataset — the slider-stacking trend genuinely intensified in 2010s–2020s.

- **One new base-rate entry added by this ticket** — *Expert/professional barrister / advocate-tier lawyer (US adult)* at ~1e-3. This entry was needed because Mark is the first dataset character whose primary universal-desirable contribution is "top legal advocate" rather than "top corporate-finance figure" / "top doctor" / "top combatant" / etc. The entry is calibrated to BLS Lawyers OEW data and matches the cross-project 1e-3 expert-tier convention. The new entry's joint-distribution note flags the expert-lawyer × age 28–38 NEGATIVE correlation that loads on Mark's joint here, the IQ × expert-lawyer POSITIVE correlation (relevant for any future character claiming both traits), and the time-budget NEGATIVE correlation with multi-skill stacking (relevant for any future character claiming expert-lawyer plus other expert-skills).
