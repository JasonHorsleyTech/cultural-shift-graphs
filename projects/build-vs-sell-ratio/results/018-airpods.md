## Build breakdown ($ millions)

AirPods are a hardware product where component cost is the dominant line — small components, but multi-million-unit volumes. The H2 chip, custom drivers, MEMS microphones, lithium-ion battery cells, and the precision-injection-molded plastic housings drive the BOM. The marketing footprint is iconic (the silhouette dancing ads, the launch keynote moments) but modest in dollar terms — AirPods don't get an annual launch cadence the way iPhone does, so per-year ad spend is materially lower as a percentage of revenue. The Apple Retail footprint dedicated to AirPods is small (a try-on station and a peg display, vs the demo tables Macs and iPads occupy and the central display arc iPhone gets). Customer support per active AirPods user is very low because AirPods are functionally unrepairable — Apple replaces them rather than servicing them.

- **Cost of sales (AirPods-attributed COGS)** — H2 silicon (AirPods Pro 2 and AirPods 4 ANC; designed in-house by Apple, fabricated by TSMC), H1 silicon (older AirPods 3 still in lineup), W2 silicon (AirPods Max); custom 11mm dynamic drivers (or planar-magnetic for AirPods Max); high-excursion neodymium magnet assemblies; lithium-ion battery cells (CATL, Sunwoda, Varta) — both for the earbuds (~50mAh per bud) and for the charging cases (~500mAh); MEMS microphones (multiple per ear — 3+ on Pro models for ANC, transparency mode, voice pickup); MagSafe charging case mechanism with U1 ultra-wideband chip integration for precise Find My; medical-grade silicone ear tips (Pro models); polycarbonate housings (precision-injection-molded with tight optical-quality surface finish); machined aluminum stem accents; gold-plated charging contacts; antenna assemblies; final assembly via Luxshare (primary, primarily in Vietnam since 2020) and Goertek (secondary, China); inbound logistics; freight; quality assurance; warranty cost reserves: 13,943

- **R&D allocated to AirPods (5.68% of total Apple R&D $31,370M)** — H-series chip design (multiple generations of in-house silicon for AirPods; the H2 chip launched with AirPods Pro 2 in 2022 and propagated to AirPods 4 with ANC in 2024 — multi-year, multi-hundred-million-dollar silicon design effort); spatial audio engineering (Dolby Atmos integration, the head-tracking algorithms that compute audio-source position relative to user head orientation in real time using on-device ML, the binaural rendering pipeline); Adaptive EQ tuning (per-individual ear canal response measurement and equalization); Active Noise Cancellation algorithms (the AirPods Pro 2 launched at 2x ANC effectiveness vs original Pro — pure algorithmic and silicon engineering); Adaptive Audio and Conversational Awareness features (AirPods 4 introduced contextual transparency mode, requires ML); the FDA-cleared hearing-aid functionality on AirPods Pro 2 (Sept 2024 — significant FDA submission and software validation work); Find My integration with U1 ultra-wideband; firmware OTA update infrastructure; MagSafe charging case engineering; long-term R&D on bone-conduction microphones and lossless wireless audio (multi-year exploratory). Some R&D shared with Apple Watch (sensor and wireless work) and HomePod (acoustic algorithms). Allocated by AirPods' total-revenue share (5.68%); per consistent methodology, overstates AirPods-specific R&D because Apple's $31.4B includes Apple Car wreckage, Vision Pro, AR glasses, and platform R&D not directly benefiting AirPods. Per ticket guidance, accept overstatement: 1,782

- **AppleCare and customer support (AirPods share, non-retail)** — call center operations and online chat support for AirPods (pairing issues, firmware troubleshooting, warranty claims); AppleCare+ for AirPods claim handling (covers accidental damage including loss-of-bud-with-fee replacement); replacement-unit logistics (AirPods are nearly never repaired — the standard remedy is whole-unit swap, which means inventory of replacement units flows through support cost); warranty reserves administration; iFixit gives AirPods a 0/10 repairability score (sealed glued construction, non-replaceable batteries) so Apple's customer-support cost per active AirPods user is materially lower than per-active-iPhone or per-active-Mac. Excludes Genius Bar staff in Apple Stores (those are in Apple Retail SG&A, classified as Sell). AirPods share of Apple customer support spend ~5% (well below 5.68% revenue share, reflecting the low service intensity of unrepairable replacement-cycle hardware): 111

**Build total: 15,836**

The launch cadence is 18-24 months between major refreshes (vs the annual iPhone cycle), so per-year advertising spend is materially below iPhone. In-store, AirPods occupy a try-on station and pegged display row near the checkout — minimal floor space relative to Mac demo tables or the central iPhone display arc. A substantial fraction of AirPods purchases are online or through the Apple Store app, further reducing physical-retail-attributable cost.

## Methodology notes

**The central ratio is robust; the operating-margin number is not.** Build_pct of 94.6% is structurally driven by the dominant scale of AirPods COGS (~$13.9B) and the genuinely modest AirPods marketing footprint (~$400M ad spend, ~$500M retail allocation). Stress test: doubling AirPods advertising to $800M and AirPods retail allocation to $1,000M (extreme high-end stress test that would put AirPods sell at $1,800M, double the central estimate), build_pct only drops to ~89.8%. Conversely, applying a higher Wearables-specific GM of 45% (which lowers AirPods COGS to $12,212M), build_pct drops to ~94.0%. **Even at the most aggressive marketing assumption combined with the highest plausible Wearables GM, build_pct stays above 88%.** AirPods is build-heavy in ratio terms despite the cultural perception of AirPods as a marketing-driven phenomenon.

**Why the ratio came in higher than the ticket-anticipated 80-88% range.** Same scale dynamic as iPhone and Mac: once COGS gets to $14B, even Apple's well-funded global marketing operation can't move the ratio below 88% without an implausibly large per-product ad-spend assumption. The ticket's anticipated 80-88% appears to have implicitly assumed (a) a higher Wearables-specific GM significantly compressing COGS, AND (b) a higher AirPods-specific marketing intensity. Even granting both, the highest-marketing+lowest-COGS stress case lands around 87-88% — not the 80% lower bound. The actual answer at central allocation is 94.6%, with full stress range 88-95%. Disclosed.

**Within-category allocation is the genuinely low-confidence layer.** Apple discloses Wearables/Home/Accessories revenue ($37,005M) but does NOT break out AirPods separately. AirPods revenue is estimated at 60% of category ($22,203M) based on Counterpoint Research and Bernstein analyst triangulation — within the ticket's 60-70% guidance band but at the conservative low end given Apple Watch's recent growth has continued while AirPods category has matured. Stress test across the 60-70% range:
- *60% (central):* AirPods rev $22,203M, build_pct 94.6%
- *65%:* AirPods rev $24,053M, build_pct 94.8% (R&D and overhead allocations scale up proportionally; sell scales up only partially)
- *70%:* AirPods rev $25,904M, build_pct 95.2%

The **headline ratio is robust to the AirPods-share assumption** within the ticket's stated 60-70% band — it moves only ~0.6pp across the full range. Confidence: low not because the ratio is uncertain but because the absolute revenue and dollar allocations to AirPods are inherently estimated rather than disclosed.

**Why I used products-GM (37.2%) instead of a Wearables-specific GM estimate.** Per ticket guidance: 'apply products-segment gross margin to AirPods revenue, even though Wearables-category gross margin is reportedly higher than iPhone — note.' Apple's CFO Luca Maestri repeatedly described Wearables gross margin as 'very strong' on FY2024 earnings calls without giving a specific figure. Bernstein and Counterpoint analyst estimates of Wearables-category GM cluster in the 40-45% range — meaningfully above the products-segment average of 37.2%. Using products-GM is conservative for AirPods (overstates COGS, raises build_pct, lowers operating margin). Stress test: at Wearables-implied GM of 45%, AirPods COGS = $12,212M (down $1,731M), AirPods build = $14,105M, AirPods sell unchanged, build_pct = 94.0%. The ratio is not materially sensitive to the GM assumption.

**Why I allocated R&D by total revenue share (5.68%) rather than products revenue share (7.53%).** Same rationale as iPhone and Mac tickets: services has its own R&D and SG&A footprint. Allocating by total revenue share is conservative for AirPods — it allocates a smaller R&D share, which lowers AirPods build and slightly raises build_pct. Stress test: at 7.53% products-revenue allocation, AirPods R&D = $2,362M (up $580M), AirPods build = $16,416M, AirPods sell unchanged, build_pct = 94.8%. Negligible move.

**Why R&D classification stays as build despite the Vision Pro / cancelled car overhang.** Per plan.md guidance. AirPods-allocated R&D of $1,782M overstates AirPods-specific R&D because Apple's $31.4B R&D includes the now-cancelled Apple Car project, Vision Pro R&D, AR glasses R&D, and platform R&D. AirPods-specific R&D is probably $700M-1B (H-series silicon, spatial audio, ANC algorithms, hearing-aid software, MagSafe case engineering, firmware). If AirPods-specific R&D is $800M instead of $1,782M, AirPods build drops to $14,854M, build_pct drops to ~94.3%. Headline ratio not materially sensitive.

**The Apple Stores classification is the most consequential single methodology call** — same as iPhone and Mac. Per ticket: Apple Stores → sell. AirPods retail allocation of $500M sits in the Sell column.
- *If Apple Stores were classified as build* (the production/distribution view), AirPods build_pct would rise to ~97%.
- *If Apple Stores were classified as overhead* (the corporate-real-estate view), Apple Retail would not show in Build or Sell, and AirPods build_pct would rise to ~97.5%.
- *Sticking with Sell* (ticket guidance) gives AirPods build_pct = 94.6%. Chosen answer.

Consistent with iPhone and Mac methodology — Apple's choice to operate its own stores doesn't change the underlying nature of the activity (a non-vertically-integrated retailer like Best Buy stocking AirPods is unambiguously a sell channel).

**AirPods advertising calibration vs. iPhone and Mac.** The ticket explicitly flagged: 'lower advertising spend per dollar than iPhone.' I gave AirPods 5% of Apple's $8B ad spend (= $400M) on a 5.68% revenue share — a 0.88x under-index. iPhone got 70% on 51.45% revenue share (1.36x over-index); Mac got 7% on 7.67% revenue share (0.91x under-index). AirPods slots between iPhone (heavily over-indexed marketing) and Mac (slightly under-indexed marketing) in absolute terms but is the lightest per-dollar of revenue across the three Apple product tickets. This reflects the AirPods launch cadence (18-24 months between major refreshes vs annual iPhone cycle) and the lower per-launch ad spend.

**AirPods customer support intensity is genuinely the lowest in the Apple lineup.** AirPods are functionally unrepairable (iFixit 0/10 repairability — sealed glued construction with non-replaceable batteries; Apple does not offer Self Service Repair for AirPods). The standard remedy for failure (battery degradation after 2-3 years is the dominant failure mode) is whole-unit replacement under warranty/AppleCare, not service. This means the customer-support cost per active AirPods user is materially lower than per active iPhone user or per active Mac user. Allocated 5% (vs 5.68% revenue share, vs iPhone 70% and Mac 10% of customer-support spend), and even that may overstate. Disclosed.

**Confidence: low.** Apple is US-listed with detailed financial disclosure at the consolidated level and category level, BUT does NOT break out AirPods revenue separately — it's estimated within the broader Wearables/Home/Accessories category. This adds an extra allocation step beyond what was required for iPhone and Mac (where the category-level revenue was directly disclosed). The 60% AirPods-share-of-category assumption is anchored to ticket guidance (60-70%) and Counterpoint analyst data (55-60%), but is genuinely not derivable from any Apple filing. Calling this 'medium' (as I did for iPhone and Mac) would overstate the disclosure path; calling this 'low' is consistent with the rubric's 'SKU economics obscured by parent' qualifier. Note: even at low confidence, the headline build_pct ratio is robust to ±1pp under all plausible allocation alternatives across the full 60-70% AirPods-share range — the answer is genuinely build-heavy regardless of the input assumption.

**What's surprising in the numbers.**

- **AirPods build_pct of 94.6% is meaningfully higher than the ticket's 80-88% estimate.** Same lesson as iPhone (93.2%, vs ticket-anticipated 75-85%) and Mac (94.0%, vs ticket-anticipated 80-90%): once a hardware product is at multi-billion-dollar revenue scale, the COGS-driven build column dwarfs any plausible marketing footprint, and the ratio lands in the 90s. AirPods is structurally indistinguishable from iPhone and Mac in build_pct terms despite the cultural perception of AirPods as a marketing-driven cultural phenomenon.
- **AirPods is more build-heavy than iPhone in ratio terms (94.6% vs 93.2%).** The reason: AirPods has materially lower marketing intensity per dollar of revenue (~4.0% of revenue, vs iPhone's 5.2% and Mac's 4.6%) AND lower customer-support intensity (because AirPods are unrepairable replacement-cycle hardware). Both push the build_pct ratio higher.
- **AirPods is a >$20B product line — bigger than the entire Mac category.** AirPods at $22B (estimated) is roughly the same size as iPad ($26.7B) and meaningfully larger than the entire Mac category ($30B includes MacBook Air, MacBook Pro, iMac, Mac mini, Mac Studio, Mac Pro). For a product that started shipping in 2016 and was widely mocked at launch, this is a remarkable revenue trajectory. AirPods alone are larger than the entire revenue of most public consumer-electronics companies.
- **The Wearables-category higher GM doesn't materially change the ratio — but it does change the operating margin story.** If Wearables GM is 45% (the upper end of analyst estimates) instead of the 37.2% products average I applied, AirPods operating margin rises from 22.8% to ~30.6% — closer to Apple's consolidated 31.5%. The build_pct moves only 0.6pp under this revision, but the implied operating margin moves materially. AirPods may be one of the most profitable consumer hardware lines in the Apple portfolio on a per-dollar basis, even as the build_pct ratio shows it's build-heavy in dollar terms.
- **AirPods pioneered the 'true wireless' category but is now a 25-30% market-share player.** Apple's TWS-earbud share peaked above 50% in 2020 and has compressed steadily as Samsung, Sony, Bose, and Chinese OEMs (Xiaomi, Huawei, Anker/Soundcore) scaled. AirPods retains a premium-tier dominance and ecosystem-lock-in advantage with iPhone users, but the category has matured into a competitive market — which may explain the modest marketing intensity vs iPhone (less need to defend a category Apple invented and now dominates the high end of, vs the constant iPhone competitive marketing pressure).
