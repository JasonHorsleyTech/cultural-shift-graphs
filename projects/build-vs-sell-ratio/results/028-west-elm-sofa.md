## Build breakdown ($ millions, USD — Williams-Sonoma consolidated FY2024, applied to West Elm brand-revenue weighting)

The WSM build side is the cost of designing, sourcing, importing, distributing, and fulfilling the multi-brand product range. Williams-Sonoma operates a vertically-integrated specialty-retail model that owns its brands and contracts manufacturing primarily with Asian (Vietnam, India, China) and a small share of domestic suppliers, then ships inventory to its in-house East Coast (Olive Branch, MS) and West Coast (Tracy, CA) distribution centers, and fulfills both retail-store replenishment and e-commerce direct-to-consumer orders through that owned supply chain. For West Elm specifically, the brand's dominant product category is upholstered furniture (sofas, sectionals, chairs) which is heavily Vietnam-sourced, freight-intensive (a sofa is bulky and high-volume relative to dollar value), and carries a meaningful furniture-grade tariff exposure under the Section 301 China tariffs (now partially mitigated by the Vietnam-sourcing shift but reintroduced as a risk by the 2025 tariff posture). The brand also maintains an in-house design and merchandising team in San Francisco (the West Elm design studio operates within the broader WSM design organization with brand-specific creative leadership), which represents real R&D-equivalent intellectual-property investment in build per the methodology spec.

- **COGS — merchandise cost (raw materials, finished-goods purchase from contract manufacturers, inbound freight, tariffs)** (Williams-Sonoma sources approximately 60% of merchandise from foreign vendors, concentrated in Vietnam, India, and China; for the upholstered-furniture category that dominates the West Elm range, the primary sourcing countries are Vietnam (which has emerged as the dominant US upholstered-furniture sourcing market following the 2018-2019 Section 301 China tariffs and the 2022-2024 supply chain diversification trend), India (growing share particularly for occasional furniture and tabletop), China (declining share but still material for hard-furniture and some upholstered-furniture categories), and a small domestic US share (the West Elm Workspace US-manufactured line and various other made-in-USA-positioned product lines). Inputs include the kiln-dried hardwood and engineered-wood frames typically sourced from Vietnamese and US frame suppliers, the foam cushions sourced primarily from US-based foam manufacturers (Polymeric, etc.), the down/feather fills for premium-tier upholstered furniture, the fabric upholstery and leather sourced from a mix of Italian, Chinese, and US textile mills, the metal hardware (legs, brackets, mechanisms for sleeper-sofa models) sourced from Asian metalworks, and the packaging materials. Inbound ocean freight from Vietnam, India, and China to West Coast and East Coast US ports — freight rates moderated significantly from the 2021-2022 spike levels through 2023-2024, contributing meaningfully to the FY2024 gross margin expansion of 320 basis points year-over-year. Section 301 tariffs on Chinese-origin furniture remain at 25% on most furniture HTS codes, with Vietnam and India tariffs at standard MFN rates of typically 0-5% for furniture categories (creating a structural sourcing-shift incentive away from China). This line represents the largest single component of the build side at approximately 37.6% of consolidated revenue): **$2,900**

- **COGS — distribution and warehousing operations (DC labor and ops, outbound freight)** (Williams-Sonoma operates two major in-house distribution centers — Olive Branch, MS (East Coast) and Tracy, CA (West Coast) — plus regional satellite DCs for furniture-specific delivery. The DC operations include receiving inbound containers from Asian sourcing partners, picking and packing both small-parcel e-commerce orders and larger furniture-delivery shipments, retail-store replenishment fulfillment, and outbound logistics including last-mile white-glove furniture delivery for the larger-format items like sofas. The DC labor (warehouse workers, forklift operators, pickers, packers) and DC operating costs (utilities, equipment, packaging supplies) flow through COGS. Outbound freight (small-parcel via UPS/FedEx, LTL trucking for medium-size items, white-glove specialty delivery for furniture) is also classified within COGS. For West Elm specifically, the brand maintains a dedicated design studio with brand-specific creative leadership focused on contemporary/modern styling, color and material trend forecasting, exclusive-collection development (West Elm Modern, West Elm Workspace, West Elm Kids, the various designer-collaboration capsule collections with industry-recognized designers and artists), and the merchandising-driven product-assortment management. ## Methodology notes

**Why Williams-Sonoma consolidated (with brand-revenue weighting) rather than West Elm brand standalone.** The ticket explicitly directs this approach because Williams-Sonoma does not separately disclose brand-level cost-of-goods-sold, brand-level advertising spend, brand-level store operating cost, brand-level e-commerce operating cost, or brand-level operating income. The only brand-level disclosed metric is net revenue (per the segment-revenue footnote in the 10-K). Using consolidated expense ratios applied to brand-level revenue produces a defensible analytical-entity treatment: the resulting build_pct is a property of the consolidated WSM business model (vertically-integrated multi-brand specialty home-furnishings retail) and reflects the brand at the consolidated economics rather than at a hypothetical brand-isolated economics. The ticket notes explicitly: 'Brand-level differentiation is hard since most cost lines are consolidated. Build_pct will be the WSM consolidated number — note this honestly.'

**The store-occupancy classification choice and its impact on build_pct.** Williams-Sonoma reports cost of goods sold and occupancy expense as a combined line on the income statement, totaling approximately $4,126M for fiscal 2024. The total occupancy expense within this line is approximately $793M, of which approximately $605M is retail-store occupancy and approximately $180M is DC/warehouse occupancy. The ticket directs that 'Stores → sell per rubric.' Applying this strictly: store occupancy ($605M) is reclassified from COGS+occupancy to sell; DC occupancy ($180M) remains in build; merchandise COGS net of all occupancy (approximately $3,333M) plus DC operations sits in build. Combined with the SG&A reclassifications (product design, sourcing, customer support, catalog-photo-build portion to build; store labor, advertising, e-commerce, B2B to sell; corporate G&A residual to overhead), this produces the frontmatter values that reconcile to within 0.02% of total revenue.

**Sensitivity to the store-occupancy classification.** If store occupancy were classified as build instead of sell (treating retail stores as a product-delivery channel rather than a sell-side activity), build_pct would rise to approximately 73-74% (build of $4,386M; sell of $1,375M). If catalog production-and-mailing of approximately $150-200M were also classified as build instead of sell (treating catalogs as product-content rather than channel activation), build_pct would rise further to approximately 76-77%. The chosen 65.6% reflects strict application of the ticket's classification rules; the +/-8 percentage-point sensitivity to occupancy and catalog classification is the largest methodological lever in this analysis.

**Why the result falls within the ticket-predicted 60-75% range.** The Williams-Sonoma cost structure has (a) substantial build-side product cost — merchandise COGS at approximately 43% of revenue, DC operations and product-design and customer-support functions adding another 6-7%, totaling approximately 49% of revenue as build; and (b) substantial sell-side cost — store occupancy at approximately 7.8% of revenue, store labor at approximately 10%, advertising at approximately 6%, e-commerce operations at approximately 1.5%, B2B sales force at approximately 0.3%, totaling approximately 25.7% of revenue as sell. Build/sell ratio = 49% / (49% + 25.7%) = approximately 65.6%, in the lower-middle of the ticket-predicted range.

**Where West Elm specifically might differ from the WSM consolidated.** Three structural reasons West Elm's brand-specific build_pct could plausibly deviate from the WSM consolidated 65.6% number: (a) **Product mix.** West Elm is more heavily upholstered-furniture-weighted than the WSM consolidated mix (Pottery Barn is also upholstery-heavy but Williams Sonoma is kitchen-and-tabletop and Pottery Barn Kids/Teen is bedding-and-decor-mix); upholstered furniture is the most freight-intensive and the most foreign-sourced (predominantly Vietnam) category, so West Elm's COGS-share of revenue may be 1-2 percentage points higher than consolidated, pushing build_pct moderately higher. (b) **Marketing intensity.** West Elm targets a younger digital-native urban demographic with heavier digital-marketing investment than the catalog-heritage Pottery Barn and Williams Sonoma brands; this skews the West Elm advertising mix toward digital (paid social, paid search, influencer marketing) which has different unit economics than catalog. West Elm advertising-as-percent-of-revenue may be in the same 5.8-6.2% range as consolidated but the composition shifts. (c) **Store productivity.** West Elm stores are typically smaller-format urban-neighborhood locations rather than the larger-format mall-and-lifestyle-center Pottery Barn footprint; West Elm store occupancy per dollar of revenue may be slightly higher (urban-flagship rent intensity) but West Elm store labor per dollar of revenue may be similar to consolidated. Net effect of these three factors: West Elm brand-specific build_pct is plausibly within ±2 percentage points of the consolidated 65.6%, centered at approximately 64-67%.

**Build/sell classification of the $793M occupancy line.** The single largest methodological choice in this analysis is the disposition of the $793M occupancy expense, which combines retail-store occupancy (approximately $605M) and DC/warehouse occupancy (approximately $180M). Per the ticket's "Stores → sell" rule, store occupancy is sell; DC occupancy is build. This treatment is consistent with the broader project convention for vertically-integrated retailers (the Apple, Nike, Lululemon, Starbucks, Chipotle results all face a similar question for company-owned retail and have applied similar logic).

**Build/sell classification of in-house design and merchandising at $80M.** Williams-Sonoma's in-house product design and merchandising function is the primary intellectual-property differentiator versus competitors who resell commodity-catalog product, and the function operates conceptually as an R&D-equivalent investment in product development. Estimated at approximately 1% of revenue ($80M consolidated, $19M West Elm brand-weighted), classified as build per the methodology spec for R&D and product-development functions.

**Build/sell classification of customer support at $115M.** Customer support for hardware/product issues (warranty claims, delivery scheduling, damage and defect handling, return-and-exchange processing) is classified as build per the methodology spec for hardware-equivalent post-purchase product support. Estimated at approximately 1.5% of revenue ($115M consolidated). A different classification (lumping customer support into G&A overhead) would shift the build_pct down by approximately 1.0-1.5 percentage points.

**Build/sell classification of catalog production and direct mail.** Williams-Sonoma mails over 300 million catalogs annually across the brand portfolio, representing approximately $150-200M of the consolidated advertising line. The catalog is conceptually a marketing channel (sell-side advertising), but the underlying product photography studio operation and product-content asset creation also support the digital-channel merchandising and would exist regardless of whether the catalog channel persisted. This analysis splits catalog/photo investment 70% sell / 30% build (the build portion being the product-content-asset creation that supports multi-channel use), with approximately $50M of the catalog-photo investment classified as build and $130M as sell within the consolidated advertising line. Sensitivity: alternative splits of 100% sell or 50/50 sell-build would shift build_pct by approximately ±0.5 percentage points.

**Advertising expense triangulation at $450-480M consolidated.** Williams-Sonoma does not separately disclose advertising expense as an income-statement line item; the figure is disclosed within the SG&A footnote in the 10-K (Note 1 or Note 2 depending on the year). The fiscal 2024 figure is triangulated at approximately $450-480M from (a) the explicit earnings-release language citing higher advertising expenses as a driver of the SG&A rate increase, (b) the prior-year (FY2023) disclosed advertising of approximately $410-440M, and (c) the third-party measured-media tracking (MediaRadar, Comperemedia, Winmo) showing approximately $200-250M of measured-media spending which excludes catalog production-and-mailing (approximately $150-200M of spend per industry benchmarks for a 300-million-catalog program) and digital-direct-marketing (approximately $50-100M not captured by measured-media trackers). The triangulated $450-480M figure is consistent with the 5.8-6.2% advertising-to-revenue ratio that is typical for specialty home-furnishings retailers with substantial catalog programs (RH, Wayfair, Crate & Barrel run similar 5-7% advertising-to-revenue ratios).

**Store labor estimation at $770M.** Williams-Sonoma does not separately disclose store-level labor cost. The estimate of $770M (approximately 10% of revenue) is triangulated from (a) the approximately 8,500 store-level personnel at fiscal year end (per 10-K Item 1 disclosure of total employee headcount of approximately 19,000 minus the estimated 10,500 non-store personnel), (b) the average compensation per store-level employee of approximately $45,000-50,000 including benefits and payroll taxes (consistent with specialty-retail wage benchmarks for a mix of full-time and part-time staff), and (c) the typical specialty-retailer store-labor-to-revenue ratio of 9-11% (Apple Retail runs lower at approximately 7-8% reflecting higher per-employee productivity; mass-retail runs higher at 12-15%). The $770M estimate falls in the middle of the plausible range.

**Sensitivity to brand-specific cost structure.** As discussed above, West Elm's brand-specific cost structure may differ from the WSM consolidated by ±2-3 percentage points in either direction. The headline build_pct of 65.6% should be interpreted as a brand-revenue-weighted application of the consolidated WSM cost structure, with the West-Elm-specific true build_pct most likely in the 64-68% range. A more granular brand-specific cost-structure analysis is beyond the scope of this ticket given the absence of brand-level disclosure from WSM.

**What's surprising in the numbers.**

- **The Williams-Sonoma consolidated build_pct of 65.6% places the company in the middle of the specialty-retailer cluster in the consumer panel.** For comparison, other corporate-owned-retail consumer brands in this panel are running: Chipotle ~70%, Starbucks ~50-55%, Apple iPhone ~70-75%, Lululemon ~55-60%, Nike Brand ~65-70%. WSM at 65.6% is consistent with the specialty-retailer cluster and reflects the moderately high COGS-as-percent-of-revenue (driven by imported physical-goods inventory plus store occupancy and freight) offset by moderate sell-side intensity (store labor and advertising consuming approximately 25.7% of revenue).

- **The fiscal 2024 gross margin expansion of 320 basis points year-over-year is exceptionally strong for a specialty-retailer in a category-downturn period.** The 46.5% FY2024 gross margin was up from 43.3% in FY2023, driven by (a) supply chain efficiency improvements as ocean freight rates normalized from the 2021-2022 spike levels (a multi-year structural tailwind), (b) merchandise margin discipline as the company avoided heavy promotional activity even during the category-soft FY2024 period, and (c) occupancy leverage from the modest store-fleet rationalization and lease renewals at favorable terms. The structural cost-discipline story is what enabled the record operating margin of 17.9% (or 18.6% GAAP including the Q1 freight-adjustment benefit) despite revenue declining slightly year-over-year — operating profit was up 18% year-over-year on revenue down 0.5%.

- **West Elm's sequential comp progression through fiscal 2024 (−4.8% Q1, −3.7% Q2, −3.5% Q3, +4.2% Q4) shows the brand emerging from category-downturn ahead of Pottery Barn (which finished Q4 at −0.5% comp).** Management commentary attributes the West Elm relative improvement to (a) product newness and contemporary-styling refresh, (b) the brand's stronger digital-channel position appealing to the younger urban demographic that recovered furniture-purchasing intent ahead of the older suburban Pottery Barn demographic still affected by the housing-formation slowdown, and (c) the brand-extensions and partnerships (West Elm Workspace contract business, West Elm Kids extension into kids' furniture, the designer-collaboration capsules). This is a brand-level insight not directly visible in the build_pct number but contextually important for understanding West Elm's strategic positioning within the WSM portfolio.

- **The 60% foreign-vendor merchandise sourcing concentration in Vietnam, India, and China creates a material 2025 tariff exposure that is not yet reflected in the FY2024 cost structure.** The 10-K Risk Factors discussion of potential additional tariffs on Vietnam, India, and remaining China imports under the 2025 Trump administration tariff posture represents a material near-term cost-structure risk to fiscal 2025. For upholstered furniture specifically (the dominant West Elm category), Vietnam is the largest single sourcing country and tariff increases would directly hit the build-side cost. The fiscal 2024 build/sell ratio analyzed here reflects the pre-2025-tariff cost structure; the fiscal 2025 ratio could see build_pct increase by 2-4 percentage points if the tariff scenarios materialize, with offsetting sell-side intensity reduction as advertising and store-labor optimization respond.

- **The WSM e-commerce channel share of approximately 65-66% of revenue is the highest among major specialty home-furnishings retailers.** This long-tenured strategic emphasis on digital-channel investment (dating from the founder-era leadership and continuing under CEO Laura Alber) has produced the highest e-commerce penetration in the category and contributes to the company's structural margin advantage versus peers (Williams-Sonoma operating margins of 17-19% compare favorably to Wayfair operating margins typically negative or low-single-digit, RH operating margins of 8-12%, Crate & Barrel private but reportedly 5-8% operating margin). The high e-commerce channel share also means that store-occupancy intensity (occupancy-as-percent-of-revenue) is lower than would be expected for an equivalent revenue base sold through a higher-store-share channel mix — partially offsetting the urban-flagship-store-rent intensity that characterizes West Elm and Pottery Barn footprints.

- **The ~$450-480M total advertising expense (5.8-6.2% of revenue) is composed of approximately 33-42% catalog production-and-mailing, 33-42% digital direct marketing, 10-20% broadcast and CTV, and 5-10% production cost.** This composition reflects WSM's transitional position between its catalog-heritage marketing model and a fully-digital-direct customer-acquisition model. The catalog continues to drive brand activation particularly for the older-demographic Pottery Barn and Williams Sonoma brands, but the catalog share of total advertising has been declining over the past decade as the digital direct marketing share has grown. The composition shift continues to produce gradual sell-mix efficiency gains (digital channels are more measurable and provide better-attributed customer acquisition than catalog) even as the total advertising-to-revenue ratio remains relatively stable in the 5.8-6.2% range.

**Confidence: medium.** The headline Williams-Sonoma consolidated financial metrics (net revenues $7,712M, gross profit $3,586M, SG&A $2,152M, operating income $1,434M, occupancy expense $793M, brand-level revenue breakdown) come directly from the FY2024 earnings release and 10-K filing — these are clean US-listed-company disclosures. The major triangulations are (a) the consolidated advertising-expense estimate of $450-480M (disclosed in the SG&A footnote but the precise figure is approximate; triangulated from prior-year disclosure, earnings-release commentary, and third-party measured-media trackers), (b) the store-labor estimate of $770M (not separately disclosed; triangulated from total employee headcount, average compensation benchmarks, and specialty-retailer ratios), (c) the store-occupancy versus DC-occupancy split of the $793M total occupancy line (approximately $605M store / $180M DC, estimated from typical specialty-retailer DC-occupancy ratios), and (d) the application of consolidated cost ratios to West Elm brand-revenue weighting (the ticket-mandated approach given the absence of brand-level cost disclosure). The medium-confidence rating reflects (i) the necessity to triangulate SG&A sub-components from the SG&A footnote and industry benchmarks rather than direct line-item disclosure, (ii) the inability to compute brand-specific cost economics that would require disclosure WSM does not provide, and (iii) the methodological choice around the store-occupancy classification (the build_pct of 65.6% reflects strict application of the ticket's "Stores → sell" rule; alternative classifications would shift the result by up to 8 percentage points as discussed in methodology notes). The directional finding (WSM consolidated and West Elm brand-weighted build_pct of approximately 65-68%, consistent with the specialty-retailer cluster, with sell-side intensity of approximately 25-26% of revenue driven by store occupancy, store labor, and advertising) is robust to the residual estimation uncertainty.
