# Ticket 045 — Boeing 737

## Product

The Boeing 737 family (currently the 737 MAX). Iconic narrowbody commercial aircraft. Anchor for the B2B / structural panel — heavy capital, heavy R&D, near-zero advertising-as-percent-of-revenue.

## Parent company

The Boeing Company (NYSE: BA).

## Panel

b2b

## Segment to use

**Boeing Commercial Airplanes (BCA) segment.** Boeing reports three segments: Commercial Airplanes, Defense Space & Security, Global Services. The 737 sits squarely in BCA. Use BCA segment revenue and operating income/loss as the basis. Note that BCA has been loss-making in recent years (737 MAX grounding aftermath, 2024 door-plug incident, production halts).

## Source hierarchy

1. **Boeing FY2024 10-K** (or most recent — Boeing's results have been volatile post-MAX). Look for: BCA segment revenue and operating loss (Note 22 or equivalent segment footnote), R&D expense (Boeing breaks out R&D on the income statement), advertising expense (Boeing's advertising is famously tiny — typically not even a separate line, lumped into G&A).
2. **Boeing 10-Q** filings for any updated segment color.
3. **Boeing earnings call transcripts** for context on production rates, charges, and inventory writedowns affecting BCA cost structure.
4. **Aviation Week / FlightGlobal trade press** for unit economics context (per-frame margins, list price vs realized price).

## Specific caveats for this product

- **BCA has been loss-making.** Don't be surprised if profit_millions is negative for the most recent year. Report it honestly. The build_pct calculation is unaffected by profitability — it's build / (build + sell).
- **Advertising is minimal.** Aerospace OEMs sell to airlines via direct sales force, not consumer advertising. Sell line will be dominated by sales force comp, customer support, dealer/airline incentives, and trade-show / airshow spend (Paris, Farnborough). The sales force is small in headcount but each transaction is enormous.
- **R&D is a huge build line.** Boeing R&D is multi-billion annually, partly funded by customer advance payments (program accounting). Default: count all BCA-allocated R&D as build per the rubric.
- **Deferred production cost / program accounting** is a Boeing-specific GAAP wrinkle. Costs are spread over an accounting block of frames. This means COGS in any single year doesn't equal the cash cost of the planes shipped that year. For our purposes, use reported segment cost of revenue — it's the closest thing to "what it cost to produce the planes."
- **Customer support** for Boeing is partly through Global Services segment (separately reported). Don't double-count: keep BCA-only for the 737 entry. Note that this likely understates true "build" by excluding ongoing support that's billed via Global Services.
- **Sales force comp:** Boeing doesn't break this out. Estimate as a small fraction (1-3%) of BCA revenue based on industry typical.
- **Trade promotion/incentives:** When airlines order, list prices are heavily discounted — the discount IS the sales incentive. But discounts are netted against revenue per ASC 606, so they don't appear as a sell expense. This understates measured sell, but consistently. Note this.

## Output format

Write result to `results/045-boeing-737.md` per the YAML frontmatter spec. Methodology must:
1. Confirm BCA segment was used and reference the specific 10-K segment footnote.
2. Address program accounting — explain that segment cost of revenue is the build proxy.
3. Acknowledge the sell-line minimization caveat (price discounts netted from revenue).
4. Honestly report the loss if BCA was loss-making in the chosen year.

## What "good data" looks like

- BCA segment revenue, segment cost of revenue, segment R&D, and a small estimated sell line — all cited from the 10-K.
- Build_pct probably 92-98% — Boeing is structurally one of the most build-heavy companies in the panel. This is the "structural baseline" the panel is meant to establish.
- Confidence: **high** — Boeing's segment reporting is detailed.
- Methodology note: program accounting and price-discounts-netted explained briefly.
