## Build breakdown ($ millions, USD — NIKE Brand FY2024)

NIKE Brand's build side is the cost of designing the products (the in-house design and product-creation organization at the Beaverton Oregon headquarters plus the global design satellites and the innovation-research network), sourcing the raw materials (leather, rubber, foam, textiles, components), contracting the manufacturing (to approximately 121 footwear-factory contractors and 287 apparel-factory contractors across the Vietnam-Indonesia-China-Cambodia-Thailand-India base), inbound logistics (ocean freight, air freight for fast-replenishment SKUs, customs duty, port handling), and distribution-center operations (warehousing, picking, packing, shipping including outbound shipping for direct-to-consumer e-commerce orders). The build side excludes retail-store occupancy and retail-store labor (classified as sell since for an athletic-apparel-and-footwear brand the retail store is purely the selling channel — the product is manufactured at contract factories overseas, distinct from the Starbucks/Chipotle precedent where the store is the factory), excludes demand-creation expense (entirely sell), and excludes corporate G&A (overhead). The methodological split allocates NIKE Brand's allocated $27,344M of cost of sales and the build-classified components of NIKE Brand's allocated $11,803M of operating overhead across build categories.

- **Cost of products from contract manufacturers (footwear, apparel, equipment finished-goods cost including raw materials, contract manufacturer labor, and contract manufacturer gross margin)** — the largest single component of NIKE Brand build cost, representing the FOB-factory cost of the footwear and apparel products purchased by Nike from the contract-manufacturer base in Vietnam, Indonesia, China, Cambodia, Thailand, India and the smaller-share manufacturer countries. Footwear FOB-factory cost for a typical Nike model is approximately $20-25 per pair (with low-end models like Air Force 1 standard colorways at approximately $15-20 per pair due to the standardized leather-and-rubber construction and the high-volume manufacturing efficiencies, and high-end performance models or limited-edition collaborations substantially higher); apparel FOB-factory cost is approximately $8-15 per unit for the typical t-shirt/short/legging item. The Air Force 1 specifically benefits from extreme manufacturing-volume efficiency (the silhouette has been in continuous production since 1982 with a stable bill of materials and well-understood manufacturing process), supporting an FOB-factory cost in the lower end of the Nike footwear range. ## Methodology notes

**Consolidated-ratio allocation to NIKE Brand per ticket instruction.** Nike does not separately disclose cost of sales, demand creation, or operating overhead for NIKE Brand vs Converse — the 10-K reports cost lines at the consolidated NIKE Inc. level and at the NIKE Brand geographic-segment level (with segment EBIT but not the underlying cost-line components). Per the ticket spec, consolidated expense ratios are applied to NIKE Brand revenue: NIKE Brand $49,322M ÷ NIKE Inc. $51,362M = 96.03% allocation, applied to consolidated COGS $28,475M / demand creation $4,285M / operating overhead $12,291M to derive the NIKE Brand allocations. Sensitivity: Nike's segment-reported NIKE Brand EBIT (after Global Brand Divisions allocation, before corporate items at the Corporate-Other line) is approximately $8,309M, materially higher than the consolidated-ratio-allocated EBIT of $6,060M — the gap is the corporate items ($2,276M at consolidated) that include foreign-currency hedge results, restructuring charges accelerated above the segment level, and unallocated corporate-Other costs. The consolidated-ratio approach therefore overstates NIKE Brand cost intensity by approximately 4.6pp of revenue (the corporate items spread to NIKE Brand and Converse pro-rata), but the build/sell ratio structure is approximately preserved because the corporate items flow predominantly into overhead rather than into build or sell directly. An alternative segment-EBIT approach would push the overhead bucket down toward $2,700M (segment-direct overhead only) and the profit bucket up to ~$8,309M, leaving build and sell approximately unchanged — the headline build_pct of 76.2% would remain approximately the same.

**Store-occupancy and store-labor classification as sell (consistent with apparel-retailer methodology).** For NIKE Brand, the NIKE-Direct retail stores are purely the selling channel — the product (footwear, apparel, equipment) is manufactured in contract factories overseas, shipped to Nike-controlled distribution centers, then shipped to retail stores for display and sale. This contrasts with the Starbucks/Chipotle precedent where the retail store IS the production facility. Nike-Direct store occupancy ($1,500M) and store labor ($2,200M) are therefore classified as sell. This is consistent with the project-wide treatment for apparel-retail peers including Lululemon, Levi's, Vuori, Columbia Sportswear.

**Product-creation/design/innovation classified as build per methodology spec for R&D.** Nike's product-creation organization at the Beaverton HQ campus and the global design satellites is classified as build ($1,000M estimated) per the methodology spec for product-development and R&D functions. Nike does not separately disclose product-creation expense in the 10-K (it is bundled within operating overhead), so the $1,000M figure is triangulated from typical athletic-apparel-brand R&D-as-percent-of-revenue ratios (Nike at approximately 2.0% of revenue is in the middle of the range; Adidas reports approximately 2.5%; On Holding reports approximately 2.5%; the smaller premium-running brands report similar levels). The product-creation function is treated as build because it produces the physical-product specifications that feed the contract-manufacturer base — it is the design-side build investment, not a brand-marketing investment.

**Demand creation classified entirely as sell — Nike's term for advertising plus endorsements plus brand events.** Per Nike's accounting policy disclosure, demand creation expense consists of "advertising and promotion expenses, including costs of endorsement contracts, complimentary products, television, digital and print advertising and media costs, brand events and retail brand presentation." All four components are demand-generation/customer-acquisition investments classified as sell per the methodology spec. The athlete-and-team endorsement contracts (LeBron James, Cristiano Ronaldo, NFL on-field, Premier League partnerships, Olympic federation partnerships, Jordan Brand active athletes) are within this $4,285M consolidated total (allocated $4,115M to NIKE Brand), representing approximately 20-30% of demand creation per third-party triangulation. The Air Force 1 specifically does not have product-specific athlete endorsement allocation since the silhouette is primarily marketed through lifestyle/streetwear positioning rather than athlete-driven performance positioning — but the brand-average allocation is applied per ticket spec.

**Distribution centers classified as build (in COGS) per Nike accounting policy.** Per the COGS accounting-policy disclosure, distribution-center operations (warehousing, assembly, packaging, shipping including outbound shipping for direct-to-consumer e-commerce orders) are within cost of sales. The $1,800M estimated DC operations cost is therefore classified as build, consistent with the methodology spec for distribution-and-logistics functions. Outbound shipping for DTC e-commerce orders ($744M residual including other supply-chain costs) is also build per the same logic — even though it serves the DTC channel, the function is logistics-and-fulfillment, not selling.

**Air Force 1 vs brand-average — caveat acknowledged but not adjusted.** The Air Force 1 silhouette has been in continuous production since 1982 and is an evergreen volume seller with several distinguishing economic characteristics vs the brand average: (a) lower per-pair marketing-cost intensity (the AF1 is primarily marketed through lifestyle/streetwear positioning and benefits from organic brand-equity built over four decades rather than active athlete-endorsement-driven marketing), (b) similar-to-brand-average COGS intensity due to standardized leather-and-rubber construction and high-volume manufacturing efficiencies (likely slightly below brand average on a per-pair basis but at the same gross-margin percentage as the wholesale-and-DTC channel mix), (c) high mix of standard-colorway variants (the white-on-white AF1 representing the highest single-SKU volume) with limited-edition and collaboration variants commanding premium pricing. Per ticket spec, brand-average allocation is applied — but the AF1's true build/sell ratio is likely modestly higher than the brand average (more build, less sell per pair) because of the lower marketing intensity. Sensitivity: if AF1-specific marketing intensity were 50% of brand average, the AF1 build_pct would be approximately 79-81% rather than the brand-average 76.2%.

**Wholesale channel at 56% of NIKE Brand revenue drives the relatively low gross margin (44.6%).** Nike sells to wholesale partners at approximately 50-55% of retail price (the wholesale-channel-discount-and-margin-haircut). Wholesale revenue is therefore approximately half of the equivalent retail revenue and COGS is similar, compressing gross margin without compressing operating margin proportionally. The DTC channel at 44% of revenue captures the full retail-price margin. The blended gross margin of 44.6% reflects the channel-mix structure. The build/sell ratio analysis here implicitly captures this channel-mix effect by computing build/sell on Nike's actual cost structure. Nike's lower gross margin vs Lululemon (44.6% vs 59.2%) is principally driven by this channel-mix difference (wholesale-heavy vs DTC-dominant), more than by any underlying product-cost or brand-premium difference.

**Capital expenditures classification — current-year capex is not double-counted in build.** Nike's fiscal 2024 capex was $763M, primarily for DC-technology investments (automated DC capacity in North America), digital-platform-technology investments (Nike Digital, Nike app, SNKRS), and the retail-store buildout program (Nike Live and House of Innovation flagships). Per the project methodology spec, current-year capex appears as depreciation in COGS (for DC-and-tech depreciation, classified to build) and in operating overhead (for retail-store and corporate-asset depreciation, classified to sell and overhead respectively) and is not double-counted in build. This is consistent with the cross-product methodology.

**Trade promotion / co-op marketing — netted against revenue.** Nike's trade-promotion and co-op-marketing payments to wholesale partners (the Nike-store-within-a-store program at Foot Locker and Dick's, the in-store-fixturing-and-marketing-collateral support, the joint promotional-marketing programs for major-product-launch windows) are typically netted against revenue per ASC 606 revenue-recognition treatment rather than reported as a separate selling expense. This is consistent with broader CPG and athletic-apparel-industry practice. The headline revenue is therefore net-of-trade-promotion, and the demand creation expense line excludes the trade-promotion component (which would otherwise be partially classified as sell under the methodology spec). The ratio is not materially affected — both numerator (revenue) and denominator-implicit components are net of trade promotion.

**What's surprising in the numbers.**

- **NIKE Brand build_pct at 76.2% is in the upper end of the ticket-predicted 65-80% range and reflects the wholesale-heavy channel mix and the manufacturing-and-logistics-intensive cost structure of physically-produced sneakers and athletic apparel shipped from Asia.** Nike is structurally a "wholesale-and-DTC athletic-brand with global supply chain" archetype where the product cost is substantial (~59% of revenue including all build-side components) and the selling investment is meaningful but proportionally smaller (~18.5% of revenue including demand creation and DTC retail operations). This positions Nike in the cluster with other manufactured-product-with-meaningful-DTC consumer brands like Apple (similar wholesale/DTC mix), Samsung (different category but similar structure), and the major athletic-apparel peers (Adidas, Puma, On Holding), and structurally distinct from the DTC-dominant high-gross-margin brands like Lululemon (build_pct 53%).

- **The wholesale-channel-mix effect is the dominant driver of the gross-margin gap vs DTC-dominant athletic peers.** Nike at 44.6% gross margin vs Lululemon at 59.2% — the 14.6pp gap is principally a channel-mix effect (Nike 56% wholesale at ~50% of retail-price vs Lululemon ~95% DTC at full retail-price) and not principally a brand-premium effect. If Nike's channel mix were 95% DTC like Lululemon, the implied gross margin would be approximately 57-60% (similar to Lululemon). The build/sell ratio analysis correctly captures this channel-mix dynamic — Nike's higher build_pct (76.2% vs 53.1%) reflects the wholesale-channel revenue-compression more than any underlying product-or-brand-cost difference.

- **Demand creation at $4.3B consolidated is one of the largest absolute advertising-and-promotion budgets in consumer products globally.** Nike's demand creation expense is among the top-10 largest US-listed-company advertising-and-promotion budgets globally — comparable to or larger than the major beverage advertisers (Coca-Cola, PepsiCo), the major CPG advertisers (P&G, Unilever, L'Oréal), and the major tech advertisers (Apple, Amazon, Google). The 8.4%-of-revenue demand-creation-intensity is in the upper range for consumer-goods brands and supports the brand's flagship-marketing positioning with the athlete-endorsement portfolio (LeBron lifetime contract, the NBA/NFL/Premier League partnerships, the Paris 2024 Olympic Games activation, the Jordan Brand culture-event programming).

- **The 6% YoY increase in demand creation in fiscal 2024 (to $4,285M from $4,060M) reflects the Paris 2024 Olympic Games activation timing.** The Olympic-cycle marketing windows (Olympics every 2 years, alternating Summer and Winter) drive periodic step-ups in demand creation, with fiscal 2024 (Nike's fiscal year ending May 31, 2024) capturing the pre-Paris-Olympics activation campaigns; the trailing fiscal 2025 will capture the Paris-Olympics-during and post-activation. The athlete-endorsement-contract amortization is the largest non-measured-media component, with active contracts including the LeBron James lifetime contract, Cristiano Ronaldo's long-running partnership, Tiger Woods's continuing partnership through TaylorMade-acquisition (Nike-equipment but apparel through Nike), and the broader player roster across the major-sports footprint.

- **Restructuring charges of $379M in fiscal 2024 from the December 2023 cost-action program reflect the strategic-reset under the now-replaced CEO John Donahoe (CEO 2020-October 2024).** The December 2023 cost-action program targeted approximately $2B in three-year cumulative cost savings through workforce reductions (approximately 2% of total Nike workforce, completed early calendar 2024), product-portfolio simplification (reduced SKU count and emphasis on iconic-silhouette franchises like Air Force 1, Air Max 1/95/97, Jordan retro), technology-and-automation leverage, and AI/ML integration in operations. The strategic context: Nike's lifestyle/sportswear-footwear performance had deteriorated in fiscal 2024 with comparable revenue declines in several major-market wholesale doors and DTC underperformance vs internal targets, prompting the leadership change in October 2024 when Elliott Hill (a long-time Nike executive who had retired in 2020) returned as President and CEO. Under Hill, the company is rebalancing the product portfolio toward performance-sport categories (running, basketball, training) and rebuilding wholesale-partnerships that had been deprioritized during the DTC-acceleration era of fiscal 2020-2023.

- **The NIKE-Direct channel mix at 44% of NIKE Brand revenue is a meaningful structural shift from the pre-COVID baseline.** Nike's NIKE-Direct channel grew from approximately 30% of revenue in fiscal 2017 to approximately 44% in fiscal 2024, reflecting the multi-year DTC-acceleration strategy under the Donahoe-era Consumer Direct Offense / Consumer Direct Acceleration playbook (the strategy of growing direct-to-consumer revenue faster than wholesale, rationalizing the wholesale-partner base, and investing in the Nike Digital e-commerce platform and the Nike-owned-store fleet). The strategy reached an inflection in fiscal 2024 with wholesale-channel performance underperforming and management commentary acknowledging the need to rebalance the channel-mix back toward wholesale-partner growth — the Hill-era strategy under the new CEO is rebuilding wholesale-partnerships with Foot Locker, Dick's, and the regional-and-country wholesale-account base.

**Confidence: high.** Nike is a US-listed (NYSE) large-cap consumer-products company with detailed 10-K disclosure including (a) explicit cost-line breakouts for cost of sales, demand creation, and operating overhead — a structurally cleaner disclosure than many CPG peers who bundle advertising within SG&A, (b) detailed segment-reporting at the NIKE-Brand-region level with segment EBIT disclosed, (c) clear channel-mix disclosure (NIKE Direct vs Wholesale by total and by region), (d) detailed manufacturing-footprint disclosure (country mix for footwear and apparel contract factories), (e) explicit accounting-policy disclosure defining what is included in cost of sales, demand creation, and operating overhead. The headline financial metrics ($51,362M revenue, $28,475M COGS, $22,887M gross profit, $4,285M demand creation, $12,291M operating overhead, $6,311M operating income, $5,700M net income) are direct reads from the FY2024 10-K and Q4 2024 earnings release. The build/sell classification within these aggregated lines requires judgment-based estimation for components that Nike does not separately disclose (DC operations within COGS, product creation/design within operating overhead, retail-store occupancy and labor within operating overhead) — these estimates are triangulated from Nike's accounting policy disclosure, management commentary across earnings calls, third-party measured-media tracking (Kantar/MediaRadar) for the advertising-vs-endorsement-and-events split within demand creation, industry-benchmark ratios for athletic-apparel-brand cost structure, and Nike's own multi-year disclosure pattern. The directional findings (build_pct of 76.2% in the upper end of the predicted 65-80% range; sell-side intensity at 18.5% driven by the substantial demand-creation investment and the NIKE-Direct retail-fleet operations; build-side intensity at 59.1% reflecting the wholesale-heavy channel mix and the manufacturing-and-logistics-intensive cost structure) are robust to the residual estimation uncertainty. Confidence rating: high per the project rubric for "US-listed, single-product or single-segment company with detailed segment reporting, advertising line broken out, clean classification path" — Nike's explicit demand-creation-line disclosure is particularly valuable for the build/sell framework.
