## Build breakdown ($ millions, USD — Lululemon consolidated FY2024)

The build side excludes store-occupancy cost (classified as sell since the store is the selling channel, not the production facility — distinct from the Starbucks/Chipotle case where the store IS the factory) and excludes brand-marketing, store-labor, and corporate G&A costs. ## Methodology notes

**Store-occupancy classification as sell, not build — distinct from the Starbucks/Chipotle precedent.** For Lululemon, the retail store is purely the selling channel — the product (technical athletic apparel) is manufactured in contract factories in Vietnam, Cambodia, Sri Lanka, Bangladesh, and Indonesia, then shipped to North American and international distribution centers, then to retail stores for display and sale. This contrasts with the Starbucks/Chipotle precedent where the retail store IS the production facility (a barista pulls espresso shots and steams milk in the customer's eyeline; a Chipotle line worker assembles the burrito in front of the customer). The Lululemon retail store does not manufacture, finish, or transform the product — it is purely a brand-activation, customer-acquisition, fitting-room, and transaction-completion environment. Per the project methodology rubric, store-occupancy is therefore classified as sell. This is consistent with the project-wide treatment for apparel-retail peers (Nike DTC stores, future tickets for Levi's, Vuori, Columbia Sportswear) where the brand operates retail-store fleets for selling but not for production.

**Lululemon's COGS accounting policy includes store occupancy and depreciation — gross margin headline understates pure product margin.** Per the Note 1 accounting policy disclosure, Lululemon's cost of goods sold line includes 'occupancy costs of our company-operated stores including rent, depreciation, common area maintenance fees, real estate taxes, and utilities' and 'depreciation related to our distribution centers and store technology' and 'the costs related to our product development team' — components that other retailers may report below the gross-profit line. The reported 59.2% gross margin is therefore not directly comparable to apparel peers that report store occupancy as a separate selling expense (where their gross margin headline would be higher). For purposes of the build/sell ratio analysis, the store-occupancy component of COGS is reclassified to sell ($1,000M of the $4,317M COGS), the product-development team cost is kept in build ($250M, which would normally be in SG&A as R&D), and DC depreciation is kept in build. This reclassification is conceptually correct for the build/sell framework but produces a build-side number ($3,917M) that is lower than the headline COGS figure ($4,317M) — readers should not naively equate COGS with build for Lululemon.

**Store-labor classification as sell at $1,500M.** Lululemon's store labor (Educators, store managers, district managers, community coordinators) is classified as sell per the methodology spec for retail-store selling functions. The estimated $1,500M is approximately 14.2% of revenue, derived from approximately 30,000-35,000 store-level employees at average annual compensation including benefits and payroll taxes of approximately $42-50K. Alternative classification: if store labor were reclassified as overhead (treating retail-store operations as a holistic operating function rather than as a selling function), build_pct would not change but sell would drop to ~$2,000M and overhead would rise to ~$2,200M — directionally the same ratio result but with different overhead inflation. The chosen classification follows the project precedent for retail-store labor as a sell-side investment for selling-channel-focused brands.

**Brand-marketing investment estimate at $550M (5.2% of revenue) — material step-up from historical baseline.** Lululemon does not disclose advertising expense in the 10-K (unlike companies with measured-media expense above the FASB disclosure threshold), so the $550M figure is triangulated from (a) third-party measured-media tracking (Kantar/MediaRadar) estimating Lululemon US measured-media spend at $200-300M for fiscal 2024, with the difference composed of brand-creative production, ambassador-program operations, community-event activation, sponsorships (FC Bayern, NHL, IRONMAN), influencer/creator partnerships beyond the ambassador roster, paid social and paid search digital-direct marketing, and loyalty-program marketing, (b) management commentary across the Q3 2024 and Q4 2024 earnings calls explicitly emphasizing stepped-up brand-marketing investment with the 'Wherever You Are' global brand campaign as the largest brand-marketing investment in company history, and the expectation that brand-marketing as a percentage of revenue would remain at the elevated fiscal 2024 level through fiscal 2025, (c) historical Lululemon marketing-investment trajectory showing the brand has moved from a 'brand-community-driven low-marketing-spend' positioning (estimated 1-2% of revenue in the 2010-2015 era) to a more conventional brand-marketing posture as the brand has matured and competitive intensity has increased (Nike, On, Hoka, Vuori, Athleta, Alo, the proliferating brand competition particularly in women's athleisure). Sensitivity: at $450M brand-marketing (4.3% of revenue), build_pct would rise to 54.3%; at $650M (6.1% of revenue), build_pct would fall to 51.9%. The headline 53.1% is moderately sensitive to the marketing-estimate.

**Product-development team in build rather than overhead — per Lululemon's COGS accounting policy.** Lululemon's accounting policy classifies the product-development team cost within cost of goods sold rather than within SG&A. For the build/sell framework, product-development is classified as build (the team designs the proprietary fabrics, develops the seasonal collections, and engineers the technical-design and fit specifications — all build-side functions). The $250M estimate is approximately 2.4% of revenue, consistent with vertically-integrated technical-apparel-brand R&D ratios (Lululemon discloses the Science of Feel innovation platform supporting fabric and product innovation; the Air Support, SmoothCover, Glow Up, and other recent fabric platforms reflect the ongoing R&D pipeline). Alternative classification: if product-development were classified as overhead (treating R&D as a corporate-level investment rather than a build-side product function), build_pct would drop to ~51% — a 2pp swing. The chosen build-classification follows the project precedent for product-development functions across other tickets.

**Wholesale channel is minor — Lululemon is approximately 95%+ DTC.** Lululemon's wholesale channel (sweat collective fitness studios, premium hotels, yoga-studio partnerships, the Showrooms specialty-retailer network) represents less than 5% of total revenue. This is a structural difference from Nike, Levi's, Columbia Sportswear, and other major-apparel-brand peers that derive 50-70% of revenue from wholesale channels. The DTC-dominant model is the principal driver of Lululemon's high gross margin (59.2%) relative to wholesale-heavy apparel peers (Nike at ~44%, Levi's at ~58%, Columbia at ~48%) — by avoiding the wholesale-channel-discount-and-margin-haircut, Lululemon captures the full retail-price margin on the majority of revenue. The build/sell ratio analysis here implicitly captures this DTC-channel-mix benefit by computing build/sell on Lululemon's actual cost structure rather than on a hypothetical wholesale-channel-adjusted structure.

**Capital expenditures classification — current-year capex is not double-counted in build.** Lululemon's fiscal 2024 capital expenditures were approximately $700-800M (slightly above fiscal 2023's $670M), primarily for new store buildouts (56 net new stores opened), the new Bedford, Pennsylvania distribution center coming fully online, store-renovation programs, and technology investments. Per the project methodology spec, current-year capex appears as depreciation in COGS (the store-related portion classified to sell, the DC-and-tech portion to build) and is not double-counted in build. This is consistent with the cross-product methodology.

**What's surprising in the numbers.**

- **Lululemon's build_pct at 53.1% falls in the middle of the ticket-predicted 45-60% range, reflecting the high-gross-margin DTC-apparel cost structure offset by substantial sell-side investment in retail-stores, brand-marketing, and community-program operations.** Lululemon is structurally a 'premium-DTC-with-retail-presence' archetype where the product cost is relatively modest (~37% of revenue including all build-side components) and the selling investment is substantial (~33% of revenue including retail-fleet operations and the stepped-up brand-marketing investment). This positions Lululemon in the same general cluster as other premium-DTC consumer brands (Starbucks at ~97% build-pct is in a completely different cluster because the store IS the factory; Yankee Candle at 60% build-pct is in a similar cluster but with higher COGS intensity due to physical-manufactured-product economics; future Nike Air Force 1s and Vuori tickets are expected to be in a similar 50-60% range).

- **The stepped-up brand-marketing investment in fiscal 2024 with the 'Wherever You Are' campaign is a strategic inflection.** Lululemon historically positioned itself as a 'community-and-brand-ambassador-driven low-traditional-marketing-spend' brand — the company famously avoided traditional broadcast advertising for years, relying instead on the in-store community programming, the ambassador network, and word-of-mouth brand-building. The fiscal 2024 step-up in brand-marketing reflects (a) the brand reaching a scale where conventional brand-marketing investment is necessary to defend share against the proliferating competition (Nike, On, Hoka, Vuori, Athleta, Alo Yoga, the smaller brands), (b) the need for international-market awareness-building in the new-market entries (the China Mainland flywheel is one of the highest-priority strategic investments), (c) the men's-category awareness-building to grow what is now ~25% of revenue with significant headroom, (d) the response to the Americas comparable-sales decline of 1% in fiscal 2024 which management addressed in part through brand-marketing investment alongside the product-newness refresh. The expected continuation at the elevated level through fiscal 2025 implies the brand-marketing-investment-ratio of approximately 5-6% of revenue is a structural commitment rather than a one-time push.

- **The Americas comparable-sales decline of 1% in fiscal 2024 is the first negative annual Americas comp since the immediate-COVID period, signaling the maturing-brand challenge.** Lululemon's Americas business is the dominant scale-channel (approximately 74% of revenue, approximately $7.8B), and the women's category within Americas had been the historical growth engine. The fiscal 2024 -1% Americas comp combined with the +22% international comp signals the brand's growth-pivot from Americas-women's-driven to international-and-men's-driven. The implication for the build/sell ratio is that the brand-marketing-investment ratio is likely to stay elevated as the international and men's growth investments require continued brand-awareness-building.

- **The 767-store retail fleet is a substantial sell-side cost burden but a distinctive brand-activation asset.** The retail-fleet operating cost (store occupancy $1,000M + store labor $1,500M = $2,500M, approximately 23.6% of revenue) is the dominant component of Lululemon's sell-side cost stack. The store-fleet expansion pace has moderated from the peak rate (the fiscal 2024 +56 net new stores is below the fiscal 2023 pace) as management focuses on store-productivity improvement and on selectively expanding into the strongest-opportunity new markets. The store-fleet operating cost as a percentage of revenue is structurally similar to other proprietary-retail consumer brands (Starbucks at ~58% if we counted store operations as sell rather than build, Bath & Body Works at ~25-30%, Apple Retail blended at ~10-15%) — Lululemon's 23.6% is in the typical-specialty-retailer range.

- **The high-gross-margin (59.2%) DTC-apparel cost structure is structurally different from the lower-gross-margin (~44%) wholesale-apparel peers and reflects the channel-mix benefit, not just brand-premium pricing.** Nike's lower gross margin reflects 60%+ of revenue flowing through wholesale (where Nike sells to retailers at approximately 50% of retail price, so the wholesale revenue is half of the equivalent DTC revenue and the COGS is similar — the wholesale-channel-haircut compresses gross margin without compressing operating margin proportionally). Lululemon's DTC-dominant model captures the full retail-price margin on approximately 95% of revenue. This is the principal explanation for the gross margin gap between Lululemon and Nike, more than the brand-premium-positioning explanation (which is a smaller component). The build/sell ratio analysis captures the channel-mix benefit accurately by computing on Lululemon's actual cost structure.
