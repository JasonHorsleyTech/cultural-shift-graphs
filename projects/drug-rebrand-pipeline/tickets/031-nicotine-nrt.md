# Ticket 031 — Tobacco/nicotine → Nicotine Replacement Therapy (NRT)

## Demon identity

Tobacco (and its core active ingredient, nicotine). Demonization is *the* template public-health success of the 20th century: 1964 Surgeon General's report, 1971 broadcast advertising ban, 1998 Master Settlement Agreement, indoor smoking bans (Calabasas CA 2006 onward), graphic warning labels. Cultural understanding of "tobacco kills" is near-universal.

## Medicine identity

**Nicotine Replacement Therapy** — Nicorette gum (FDA approved 1984), nicotine patches (Habitrol, Nicoderm CQ, others, 1991–1992), nicotine inhalers and nasal spray (1996–1997), lozenges (2002). All OTC since 1996–1999 depending on form. Plus prescription smoking-cessation drugs (Chantix / varenicline, Zyban / bupropion — see ticket 018) which are *not* nicotine but compete in the same space.

## Arc type

`street_to_medicine` for the nicotine-specific arc. Nicotine the molecule has been rebranded from "the addictive bit of the demon plant" into a legitimate OTC medicine. The rebrand is one of the more *successful* and uncontroversial cases in the project.

## Chemistry hypothesis

**Identical.** Nicotine is nicotine — in cigarettes, in gum, in patches, in vapes. The active molecule doesn't change. The rebrand mechanism is entirely about route of administration (slow-release patch vs. fast-spike cigarette) and removing the combustion byproducts that cause most tobacco harm.

## Source hierarchy

1. **FDA NRT approval history** — Nicorette 1984, patches 1991–1992, OTC switch dates 1996–1999.
2. **Surgeon General reports** for the demonization timeline.
3. **CDC tobacco cessation guidelines** showing NRT's role.
4. **WHO Framework Convention on Tobacco Control** (2003).
5. **IQVIA / OTC sales data** for NRT product volumes.
6. **Vaping / e-cigarette** literature for context — e-cigs are a parallel nicotine-rebrand path with separate regulatory status.

## Caveats

- Vaping / e-cigarettes are an *adjacent* rebrand path that this ticket should mention but not deeply cover — they're consumer products with shifting regulatory framing, not a clean medicine identity.
- The "nicotine itself isn't very harmful; combustion byproducts are" framing is the load-bearing argument for the rebrand. Honesty section should acknowledge this is genuinely true *and* that nicotine still has cardiovascular effects.
- Public awareness that "Nicorette is the same drug as cigarettes" is probably moderate — the framing has been "patches contain nicotine but it's safe!" rather than "this is the same drug."
- One of the highest rebrand-success scores in the project — NRT is mainstream, OTC, recommended by virtually every doctor.
