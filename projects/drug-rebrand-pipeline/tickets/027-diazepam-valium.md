# Ticket 027 — Diazepam / Valium

## Demon identity

**Valium** (diazepam). Demonization peaked in the 1970s — "Mother's Little Helper" (Rolling Stones, 1966), Betty Ford's public addiction, congressional hearings on benzodiazepine dependence. By 1978 Valium was the most-prescribed drug in the US; by 1980 public concern about dependence had peaked and prescriptions began falling.

## Medicine identity

**Valium** (diazepam). FDA approved 1963 (Hoffmann–La Roche). Indications: anxiety, seizures, alcohol withdrawal, muscle spasms, preoperative sedation. Continues to be prescribed today as generic diazepam — brand "Valium" itself has receded.

## Arc type

`failed_medicine_rebranded` in spirit, but unusual — Valium was *demonized while remaining a medicine*. The "rebrand" was the shift in clinical guidelines: short-course prescribing only, hard limits on chronic use, replaced as first-line anxiety treatment by SSRIs. Same molecule, same medicine identity, but very different cultural and clinical positioning over 60 years.

## Chemistry hypothesis

**Identical.** Diazepam is diazepam. The molecule hasn't changed.
