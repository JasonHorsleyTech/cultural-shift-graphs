## Methodology notes

**Segment selection and Ford Credit carve-out.** Used the Ford Blue segment as the F-150 ICE proxy per the ticket. Ford reports four operating segments: Ford Blue (ICE/hybrid Ford and Lincoln brand vehicles), Ford Model e (EVs), Ford Pro (commercial), and Ford Credit (financing). The ticket's nameplate mix slide confirms F-150 (ICE and Hybrid) is >80% Ford Blue and <20% Ford Pro; F-150 Lightning is >80% Ford Model e. Ford Blue is used as the representative segment for the ICE F-150. Ford Motor Credit is reported as a separate segment with FY2024 revenue of $12.3B and EBT of $1.7B — it is excluded from this analysis per plan.md's carve-out for automaker financing subsidiaries. Ford Blue segment financials are clean of Ford Credit by definition.

**Revenue and EBIT are primary-source numbers.** Ford Blue FY2024 revenue of $101,900M and EBIT of $5,284M (5.2% margin) are taken directly from Ford's official Q4 2024 earnings presentation (slide 14, slide 27). These are adjusted EBIT figures — Ford uses adjusted EBIT to exclude special items (restructuring, pension remeasurement) — consistent with measuring underlying operating economics. The quarterly progression (Q1: $905M, Q2: $1,171M, Q3: $1,627M, Q4: $1,581M = $5,284M) is internally verified.

**COGS allocation.** Ford does not publish a Ford Blue-specific income statement showing COGS, R&D, and SG&A separately; it reports only segment revenue and EBIT. The consolidated income statement (FY2024) shows total COGS of $158,434M on $184,992M revenues (85.6% COGS ratio). Ford Credit has an ~$12.3B revenue line with associated operating costs (~$10.6B); subtracting these yields automotive COGS of ~$147.8B on automotive revenues of $172.7B (85.6% ratio maintained). Ford Blue's share of automotive revenues is $101.9B / $172.7B = 59.0%, implying Ford Blue COGS ≈ $87,000M (85.4% of Blue revenue). This COGS figure includes manufacturing, materials, assembly labor, plant depreciation, tooling amortization, warranty expense, and distribution/logistics to dealers — all classified as build.

**R&D allocation.** Ford's total R&D was approximately $8.0B in FY2024 per 10-K MD&A disclosure. Ford Blue (mature ICE/hybrid platforms) is allocated a lower share than Ford Model e (which absorbs heavy EV battery chemistry, software-defined vehicle, and charging-ecosystem R&D investment). Estimated Ford Blue share: 35% × $8.0B = $2,800M (2.7% of Blue revenue). This is a judgment call — Ford does not publicly disclose per-segment R&D.

**SG&A allocation and split.** Consolidated SG&A is $10,287M (5.6% of total revenue). Ford Blue's implied SG&A is $96,616M (total costs) - $87,000M (COGS) - $2,800M (R&D) = $6,816M. This is slightly above a pure revenue-share allocation (55% × $10,287M = $5,658M), which is consistent with Ford Blue being the largest segment absorbing more overhead. Within Blue's $6,816M SG&A: advertising ($1,540M) is classified sell; an estimated 40% of the remaining $5,276M ($2,110M) is classified sell (dealer support programs, field sales, co-op advertising); 60% ($3,166M) is classified overhead (corporate G&A, executive comp, finance, legal, IR). The 40/60 split is a judgment call; alternative splits in the range of 30/70 to 50/50 would move build_pct by roughly ±0.5 percentage points.

**Advertising.** Ford's disclosed advertising costs were approximately $2.8B in FY2024 per 10-K MD&A (down from ~$3.0B in 2023, with EV-specific advertising cut ~45%). F-150 is Ford's flagship and most-advertised product; Ford Blue covers all Ford and Lincoln ICE/hybrid vehicles. Blue's share estimated at 55% of total Ford advertising (slightly below 59% revenue share, since Ford Pro brands also carry heavy advertising for commercial customers). Result: ~$1,540M allocated advertising, or about 1.5% of Ford Blue revenue. This is very low compared to CPG — automakers spend modestly on advertising relative to the scale of revenue, because most "selling" costs are embedded in dealer incentives that are netted against revenue (see below).

**The dealer incentive netting issue — key methodological caveat.** The build_pct of 96.1% is computed on Ford Blue's NET reported revenue, which is already net of dealer cash incentives, rebate programs, and marketing support payments to dealers. Ford calls these out explicitly in its EBIT bridge as "Net Pricing" variance. Industry data suggests Ford spends approximately $3,500–4,000 per vehicle on dealer incentives; for Ford Blue's ~2,400K consumer units (F-150 ICE share of 2,862K total Blue wholesales), this implies ~$8.4–9.6B in incentives that are NOT visible as an expense — they reduce the $101.9B revenue figure rather than appearing in COGS or SG&A. If these were treated as sell costs and gross revenue were used (consistent with how CPG trade promotion is disclosed in some analyses), the gross revenue would be ~$111B, dealer incentives would add ~$9.0B to sell, and build_pct would fall to approximately 88–91%. Plan.md notes that automotive dealer incentives are the equivalent of CPG trade promotion and carry the same netting caveat. At the scale of automotive incentives (8–9% of gross revenue vs. 2–5% for CPG), this methodology choice has a meaningful effect on the ratio — unlike CPG where "it doesn't change the ratio much."

**Surprisingly low marketing intensity.** Ford Blue's advertising-to-revenue ratio (~1.5%) is dramatically lower than P&G's Fabric & Home Care (~11%) or Nike (~10%). This reflects the capital intensity of automotive manufacturing: Ford Blue's $87B of vehicle COGS dwarfs any conceivable marketing spend. The "buy vs. sell" ratio for an automaker is structurally driven by the enormous cost of building a physical vehicle. F-150 is the best-selling vehicle in America for 47 consecutive years, which reduces the need for aggressive advertising to build brand awareness.

**Ford Blue EBIT decline context.** Blue EBIT fell $2.2B from $7.5B (2023) to $5.3B (2024), driven by adverse exchange ($(1.2)B), volume/mix ($(1.1)B), and higher warranty/material costs ($(0.9)B) — partially offset by net pricing gains (+$0.7B). The 2023 figure would produce a higher build_pct (revenue flat, lower EBIT means same cost base divided into same revenue → similar ratio). The FY2024 snapshot is a year of cost pressure for Ford Blue.
