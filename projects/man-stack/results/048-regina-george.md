# Regina George

## Character Summary
- **Source work:** Mean Girls (2004), Paramount Pictures teen comedy directed by Mark Waters, written by Tina Fey (loosely based on Rosalind Wiseman's nonfiction book *Queen Bees and Wannabes*, 2002). Regina George is the queen-bee antagonist of North Shore High, leader of the Plastics (the school's popular mean-girls clique consisting of Regina, Gretchen Wieners, and Karen Smith), and the central object of new-girl protagonist Cady Heron's social-warfare arc. Played by Rachel McAdams (then 25, two months prior to The Notebook's release that same summer). Regina is canonically the most popular and most attractive girl at North Shore — the film's narrative engine is "what if a fundamentally good outsider tried to take down the Queen Bee from inside the Plastics?", and Regina's social, sexual, and aesthetic dominance is the load-bearing premise of the conflict. The character functions narratively as: (1) the apex of the high-school social hierarchy, (2) the archetype of the wealthy-popular-mean-girl as a *2000s-specific male-target fantasy figure* (the "hot popular bitch you can't have" projection), and (3) the cultural reference point for the mean-girl trope itself in post-2004 American teen-media discourse. She is reformed after the bus accident in the third act and joins the lacrosse team, but the canonical version of the character — the version the film is *about* — is the mid-film alpha-female apex.
- **Audience:** male-targeted
- **Decade slot:** 2000s
- **Cultural reach proxy:** Mean Girls grossed approximately **$130M worldwide** on a ~$17M budget (Box Office Mojo / The Numbers — the second-highest-grossing teen film of 2004 after The Princess Diaries 2). The film has a 84% Rotten Tomatoes critics score, an A− CinemaScore, and is widely regarded as one of the most quotable American films of its decade. The stage musical adaptation (Tina Fey, Jeff Richmond, Nell Benjamin) opened on Broadway in 2018 and ran for 833 performances; a 2024 musical-feature-film remake (Reneé Rapp as Regina) grossed ~$105M worldwide. "Mean Girls Day" (October 3rd, the date Aaron Samuels asked Cady what day it was) is an annual internet-culture observance with major retail and brand-marketing engagement (Burger King, Target, Walmart, etc., 2010s–2020s). The phrases "fetch," "she doesn't even go here," "you can't sit with us," "on Wednesdays we wear pink," and "the limit does not exist" are post-2004 cultural-meme commonplaces. Rachel McAdams' Regina has had recurring placement on "best movie villains," "iconic teen-film characters," and "best 2000s film characters" lists across Empire, Time, BBC, IGN, Rotten Tomatoes, etc., from 2004 to present. The Mean Girls visual grammar (pink wardrobe coordination, the cafeteria seating chart, the "Burn Book") is one of the most-recognized teen-film visual signatures of the 21st century. The character occupies the *iconic-female-antagonist-as-reluctant-male-fantasy* slot specifically — the "hot popular bitch" archetype — and is the canonical reference point for that archetype in post-2004 cultural memory. Per the heartthrobs.md note: chosen over Allie Hamilton (The Notebook, also 2004, also Rachel McAdams) for stronger male-target cultural footprint, on the grounds that Allie is fundamentally a female-target romance lead while Regina is the canonical *male-coded* fantasy projection of the same actress's 2004 vintage.
- **Canonical version used:** The 2004 Mark Waters / Tina Fey film, Rachel McAdams in the role. The 2018 Broadway musical and 2024 film-musical remake (Reneé Rapp) are noted as supplementary cultural evidence of the character's enduring footprint, but the trait profile is the original 2004 film characterization. Regina is canonically a high-school senior aged 17–18 in the source — BELOW the project's standard 20–28 base-population window. This is the same age-edge case handled for Linda Barrett (ticket 024, 17–18), Lisa from Weird Science (ticket 014, 18-coded but synthesized), and Mikaela Banes (ticket 008, 16–17 in source). Following the established convention: traits are applied AS IF the character ages into the 20–28 male-fantasy projection window, with the underage-source caveat flagged in Notes.

## Joint Probability

- **Method:** filter (male-target stack)
- **Naive product of marginals:**
  0.20 × 0.22 × 0.34 × 0.70 × 0.25 × 0.25
  = (0.20 × 0.22) = 0.044
  × 0.34 = 0.01496
  × 0.70 = 0.01047
  × 0.25 = 2.618e-3
  × 0.25 = **6.545e-4**
  (≈ 1 in 1,530, applied within a base population of US women aged 20–28)
  Note: Regina's stack is SHORT — six filters total, NOT seven or eight. The Cool Girl Agreeableness filter and the doesn't-follow-girl-culture filter are BOTH excluded cleanly (Regina is the canonical inverse of both). The body-composition factor is the BMI marginal (0.22), NOT the BMI+DD+ joint (0.02). The result is that Regina's naive product is ~10x larger (less rare) than naturalistic-stacked female-fantasy projections like Daisy Duke (~6.5e-5 naive) or Linda Barrett, and ~100× less rare than buxom-stacked characters like CJ Parker, Jessica Rabbit, or 2B.
- **Correlation adjustment:** Mixed-direction adjustments. Pairs:
  - Top-quintile attractiveness ↔ BMI 18.5–22: positive halo (slim figures rate higher in male-rated attractiveness studies; r ≈ 0.20–0.30). Naive product OVERSTATES rarity by ~1.2–1.5x for this pair.
  - Top-quintile attractiveness ↔ low neuroticism: positive halo (r ≈ 0.10–0.15, Langlois et al. 2000 "what is beautiful is good" effect). OVERSTATES by ~1.1–1.2x.
  - Top-quintile attractiveness ↔ high SOI: positive halo in the mate-market literature (more attractive women report more short-term mating opportunities and slightly higher SOI-R; r ≈ 0.10–0.20). OVERSTATES by ~1.2–1.3x.
  - Top-quintile attractiveness ↔ currently single: NEGATIVE in real world (more attractive women more likely partnered). UNDERSTATES rarity by ~1.2–1.4x.
  - No-kids ↔ currently single: positive (single women more likely childless). OVERSTATES by ~1.2x.
  - High SOI ↔ low neuroticism: approximately independent in published norms; no adjustment.
  - High SOI ↔ low Agreeableness: in Big 5 norms, high SOI is mildly NEGATIVELY correlated with high Agreeableness (the Cool Girl proxy), which means high SOI is mildly POSITIVELY correlated with the LOW-Agreeableness profile Regina actually has — but since Agreeableness is excluded from the filter set rather than included, this correlation does not enter the calc directly. (Internal-coherence note: Regina's high-SOI + low-Agreeableness combination is consistent with real-world Big 5 patterns rather than the "Cool Girl who is also high-SOI" combination that violates norms. Her stack is internally consistent with real human distribution shape.)
  - **Net direction:** Three positive-halo pairs (attractiveness↔BMI, attractiveness↔low-N, attractiveness↔high-SOI) and one mild positive (no-kids↔single) vs. one mild negative (attractiveness↔single). Positive halos dominate, particularly the attractiveness-cluster halos that load on the "what is beautiful is good" effect. Net adjustment: roughly **1.4–1.7x LESS rare than naive product**. Same direction as Daisy Duke's adjustment but slightly stronger because Regina has the high-SOI halo with attractiveness (which Daisy excludes) and lacks the strong negative attractiveness↔not-girly correlation (which Daisy has, but mildly).
- **Adjusted estimate:** **~4.4e-4** (≈ 1 in 2,300 women aged 20–28). Bracket: [2.5e-4, 8e-4].
- **Population implication:** Out of ~14M US women aged 20–28, roughly **6,100** match Regina George's universal-desirable trait stack (range ~17,500 down to ~3,500 across the bracket midpoint and bracket endpoints). **Regina lands in the LEAST-RARE end of the male-target distribution** — comparable in numerical rarity to Linda Barrett (ticket 024, ~5,800 matching), Mr. Big-class characters, and Rachel Green (ticket 006). She is meaningfully LESS rare than naturalistic-stacked Daisy Duke (~620 matching, ticket 034) by ~10x, and ~100x less rare than buxom-stacked Jessica Rabbit (~560 matching, ticket 044), CJ Parker (~15 matching, ticket 016), or Mikaela Banes (~24 matching, ticket 008). **The structural reason: Regina's male-fantasy projection is OVERWHELMINGLY characterization-driven (alpha-mean-girl, designer-fashion-wealth, social-power-fantasy) rather than universal-desirable-stack-driven.** She lacks the Cool Girl multiplier that brings naturalistic male-target characters into deeper rarity, lacks the not-girly multiplier (she is its inverse), lacks the buxom body-composition joint, and lacks any deep-skill or aligned-interest filter. Her stack is the SHORTEST and SIMPLEST in the male-target distribution to date — six common-rather-than-rare filters that combine to ~1 in 2,300. This is itself a project-level finding for the male-target side: **the alpha-bitch / mean-girl-as-male-fantasy archetype derives its appeal from CHARACTER TYPE rather than from universal-desirable trait stacking.** The male audience's fantasy projection onto Regina is "the hot popular bitch I want even though she would never want me" — a projection that is structurally about social-status-asymmetry and unattainability rather than about extreme trait stacking. A reviewer who included the wealth/social-power filter (asymmetrically, against project methodology) would push Regina toward deeper rarity, but per project rules wealth is not a male-target filter and the chosen calc reflects this honestly.

## Confidence Assessment
- **Overall:** medium
- **Trait identification:** high — Regina George's traits across the 2004 film are well-documented (Wikipedia, Mean Girls fan wiki / Fandom, TV Tropes, two decades of cultural-criticism coverage of the film, recurring "best movie villains" / "iconic teen film characters" list placement). The character is canonically slim-blonde-tall-attractive, broken-up-with-by-end-of-film, childless, low-neuroticism (cold-aggressive style), high-SOI (canonically sexually-strategic), and emphatically NOT Cool Girl (she's the antagonist) and emphatically NOT not-girly (she IS mainstream girl culture). All five trait identifications are unambiguous from the source.
- **Base rates:** high — all six base rates used (top-quintile attractiveness, BMI 18.5–22 marginal, currently single, no kids, low neuroticism, high SOI) are already in `base-rates.md` from prior tickets, with strong NHANES / Pew / Census / Big-5 / OkCupid-anthropometrics / SOI-R-norms provenance. No new entries required for this ticket.
- **Correlation adjustments:** medium — the directional claim that the positive halos dominate (because Regina's stack lacks the strong negative-correlation pairs that drive Mikaela / CJ Parker / Daisy toward rarer-than-naive) is well-supported in mate-market and Big-5 literature, but the exact magnitude (1.4–1.7x net less rare) is judgment, not a measured joint distribution. Bracketing of [2.5e-4, 8e-4] is wide for this reason.
- **Methodological-asymmetry caveat:** medium-low. The project methodology does NOT include wealth as a male-target filter (only as a female-target filter), which is why Regina's canonical wealth, designer-fashion presentation, and social-power dominance do not enter the calc. A reviewer who finds the gender-asymmetry of the methodology objectionable could argue Regina's stack should include a wealth filter (~0.05–0.10 for "lives in a large suburban-Chicago McMansion / wealthy family" tier), which would pull her joint toward ~3e-5 and into the mid-rare band. The project plan deliberately retains the asymmetry for principled reasons (the two stacks reflect *what each genre actually stacks*, not symmetric trait lists) — the calc here follows the project methodology faithfully, with the asymmetry caveat flagged.

## Notes / Edge Cases

- **Regina is a male-target heartthrob whose appeal is overwhelmingly characterization-driven.** This is the dominant project-level finding for this character. The universal-desirable-trait calc lands at ~1 in 2,300 — squarely in the LEAST-RARE end of the male-target distribution — because the calc captures none of what actually makes Regina a male-fantasy figure. The "alpha-bitch / queen-bee / hot-popular-rich-girl" archetype derives its appeal from CHARACTER TYPE (social-status-asymmetry, unattainability, cruelty-as-power-display, designer-fashion wealth-fantasy, Machiavellian charisma) rather than from extreme stacking of universal-desirable sliders. The project's framework is *correct* to put her in the least-rare end of the male-target distribution by trait-stack rarity — and *incomplete* in capturing why she nonetheless functions as a male-target heartthrob at all. This is parallel to the Mr. Big finding on the female-target side (charm/charisma-driven appeal that the calc misses) and parallel to the broader Daisy / Linda / Rachel Green finding on the male-target side (cultural footprint exceeds trait-stack-rarity for naturalistic-iconic characters).
- **Sharp inverse to Cool Girl + not-girly.** Regina's exclusion calls on Cool Girl Agreeableness and Doesn't-follow-girl-culture are both *clean and emphatic*, in the sense that she is the canonical inverse of both. This is structurally distinct from the "ambiguous exclusion" calls handled for Linda Barrett (excluded on Cool Girl because the source film treats her as a non-character object of fantasy, not because she is anti-Cool-Girl) or Daisy Duke (Cool Girl included, not-girly included via country-coding). Regina is *anti-Cool-Girl* (she is high-disagreeable, conflict-seeking, partner-undermining) and *the central exemplar of mainstream-girl-culture* (she IS the Mean Girls / popular-girl framing the not-girly base rate explicitly references). The two clean exclusions are the dominant arithmetic move that places her stack in the least-rare end of the male-target distribution.
- **The wealth / social-power asymmetry is the single biggest methodological caveat.** Regina is canonically wealthy (suburban Chicago McMansion, mom-buying-Lexus, designer-label wardrobe) and canonically the apex of school-social-power. The project methodology, by design, does NOT include wealth or social-status as a male-target filter — only as a female-target filter (per the plan rationale: "men's media doesn't stack income or languages onto female love interests"). This asymmetry is principled — the two stacks reflect *what each genre actually stacks*, not a symmetric trait list. But the asymmetry has the calc-level consequence that male-target heartthrobs whose appeal IS specifically social-status-and-wealth-coded (Regina here, plausibly arguments for others in the dataset like Wonder Woman / Diana of Themyscira's princess-status, or Daenerys's queen-status) end up under-counted by the calc. A reviewer who wished to apply a wealth filter symmetrically (≤0.10 for "wealthy family / private-jet-tier / Lexus-as-graduation-gift" coding) could push Regina to ~4.4e-5 (~1 in 23,000), bringing her into the same calc-band as Daisy Duke or Linda Barrett. The principled methodological choice is to follow the project plan as written and flag the caveat. **Regina's stack is the dataset's strongest single argument for revisiting the gender-asymmetry of the methodology — flagged for Jason's review.**
- **Underage-source caveat.** Regina is canonically a 17–18-year-old high-school senior in the source film. BELOW the project's 20–28 base-population window. Per the established convention for Linda Barrett (ticket 024), Mikaela Banes (ticket 008), and Lisa from Weird Science (ticket 014), traits are applied AS IF the character ages into the 20–28 male-fantasy projection window. The 2024 musical-feature remake (Reneé Rapp as Regina) ages the character implicitly older (Rapp was 23 at filming) but maintains the high-school-senior framing in the diegesis. The trait stack is identical for the 2004 and 2024 versions — the underage-source caveat applies to both.
- **Body-composition exclusion of DD+ proxy was clean.** Regina's body presentation is long-and-lean / fit-blonde / popular-girl rather than buxom-blonde. The film's visual emphasis is on the pink-dress / leg-and-waist silhouette and the tall-and-imposing framing — NOT on bust. Same exclusion call as Daisy Duke (legs, not bust) and Linda Barrett (slim-athletic, not buxom). Sharp distinction from CJ Parker, Jessica Rabbit, Lara Croft, Tifa Lockhart, 2B — all of whom carry the rare BMI+DD+ joint at 0.02. This single methodological move (BMI marginal at 0.22 vs. BMI+DD+ joint at 0.02) is an 11x difference and is one of the biggest single-factor calc differences across the male-target dataset.
- **High-SOI inclusion was a clean call (NOT a borderline case).** Distinct from the Daisy Duke (ticket 034) and Jessica Rabbit (ticket 044) exclusions. Regina's flirting is canonically NOT wholesome and is canonically NOT subverted by loyalty — it is canonically calculating, instrumental, and openly used as a power tool. The kissing-Aaron-in-front-of-Cady beat is the textbook canonical example of the high-SOI strategic-mating-display behavior. The cheating-with-Shane-Oman beat is canonical short-term-mating behavior. Regina is the *clean inclusion case* on high-SOI in a way Daisy and Jessica are *clean exclusion cases*. This is itself a useful calibration data point for the methodology.
- **Low-neuroticism inclusion is the most-interesting calc call.** Most male-target heartthrobs who clear low-N are calm-and-serene characters (Daisy Duke's down-to-earth Southern-girl, Princess Peach's relentlessly-cheerful coding, Yuna's quiet-resilience). Regina clears low-N from the OPPOSITE direction — she is calm-and-serene NOT because she is unflappable in a soft way but because she is *cold-aggressive* in a way that maps onto low neuroticism in Big 5 norms. Cold-aggressive style (calculated, controlled, holds-grudges-and-executes-on-them) is anti-correlated with high neuroticism (anxious, dysregulated, panic-coded). Regina's emotional stability is the *Machiavellian-strategist* form of low-N, which is rarer in the male-target dataset and is itself a project-level data point about the diversity of low-N profiles across heartthrob archetypes.
- **Comparison to the female-target alpha-bitch / love-interest-as-antagonist cluster.** There is no clean female-target equivalent of Regina in the dataset — the female-target heartthrobs are uniformly designed to be desirable-as-PARTNERS (Mr. Darcy, Christian Grey, Edward Cullen, Augustus Waters, Aragorn, etc.), not desirable-as-antagonists. The closest male-target-equivalent on the female-target side is Cardan Greenbriar (ticket 031, antagonist-becomes-love-interest), but Cardan is canonically a romantic lead by the end of the trilogy whereas Regina is canonically NOT a romantic lead (she is reformed-but-unattached at film's end). The asymmetry is itself a finding: male-target media accommodates the "antagonist as fantasy projection" archetype more readily than female-target media does. Regina is the canonical reference point for that asymmetry in the dataset.
- **Conservative reading vs. permissive reading.** The chosen filter set (top-quintile attractiveness + BMI marginal + single + no-kids + low-N + high-SOI; NO Cool Girl, NO not-girly, NO buxom joint, NO aligned-interest, NO wealth) reflects the most-honest reading of the canonical character per project methodology. A maximally-permissive reviewer who included a wealth filter (~0.10 for "Lexus-as-graduation-gift / suburban-Chicago-McMansion tier") would pull the joint to ~4.4e-5 (~1 in 23,000), bringing Regina into the same calc-band as Daisy Duke or Linda Barrett. A maximally-conservative reviewer who excluded the high-SOI filter (treating Regina as a 17-year-old whose canonical sexual behavior is debatable) would pull the joint the opposite direction toward ~1.8e-3 (~1 in 555), placing her even further toward the least-rare end. The 30x range between conservative and permissive readings is itself a signal that Regina is a structurally-borderline character — her cultural footprint as a male-fantasy figure is enormous, but her canonical-trait-bound stack is moderate-to-common. **The most-honest summary**: Regina George is a male-target heartthrob whose universal-desirable trait stack is comparatively common (~1 in 2,300), and whose appeal as a male-fantasy figure derives almost entirely from characterization (alpha-bitch + designer-fashion-wealth + social-power-asymmetry + cruelty-as-power-display) that the methodology deliberately excludes from the calc. This is consistent with — and arguably strengthens — the project's recurring finding that male-target heartthrobs derive substantial cultural weight from CHARACTERIZATION and ICONIC IMAGERY rather than from maxing universal sliders.
