# Simon Basset, Duke of Hastings

## Character Summary
- **Source work:** Bridgerton, Netflix series (Season 1, 2020), based on *The Duke and I* (Julia Quinn, 2000), Book 1 of the Bridgerton series.
- **Audience:** female-targeted
- **Decade slot:** 2020s
- **Cultural reach proxy:** Bridgerton Season 1 was viewed by 82M households in its first 28 days, becoming Netflix's largest series launch at the time and the platform's most-watched series ever in that window. Sustained on Netflix Top 10 globally for months. Julia Quinn's *Bridgerton* novels jumped from solid backlist sellers to NYT bestsellers post-show — combined sales reportedly exceeded 20M copies across the series; *The Duke and I* topped USA Today and NYT lists in early 2021. "I burn for you" became a meme. Regé-Jean Page received a SAG Award nomination and was widely speculated as the next James Bond after his single-season run. The "Duke of Hastings" is the canonical 2020s Regency-romance heartthrob and the named example most cited in 2020–2022 "best Netflix love interests" rankings.
- **Canonical version used:** Netflix series Season 1 (2020) Bridgerton canon — Regé-Jean Page's portrayal — as primary, supplemented by Julia Quinn's novel *The Duke and I* (2000) where the show defers to or extends book canon. Where show and book diverge (e.g. Simon's reconciliation with his father, the duel circumstances), the show takes precedence as the version cultural recognition is anchored to.

## Why Simon over Anthony

Per ticket 011 ("pick the lead with stronger validation signal"): Simon Basset is the Season 1 breakout that drove the show's cultural saturation — the SAG nomination, the "I burn for you" meme, the Bond-rumor afterglow, and the recurring placement in 2020s "best romance leads" rankings (Cosmopolitan, Glamour, Vulture, BuzzFeed, Refinery29, Marie Claire) all attach to Simon specifically rather than Anthony. Anthony Bridgerton (Season 2 lead, 2022) is also widely loved but consistently rates as the secondary Bridgerton heartthrob in aggregated rankings; Season 2 came after the cultural-moment window had cooled and did not produce comparable single-actor breakout. Simon's higher rank (Duke vs. Viscount), more dramatic family-unencumbered backstory (orphan plus father-vow), and stronger reach signal make him the canonical Bridgerton entry for this slot.

Traits considered and excluded:
- **Height ≥ 6'2":** Not explicitly canonical. Julia Quinn's novel describes Simon as "tall" without numerical specification. Regé-Jean Page is variously listed at 6'0"–6'1" — clearly tall on screen but does not robustly clear the 6'2" threshold. Excluded from the count to avoid relying on inflation. If counted at the marginal 0.04, the joint would shift ~25x toward more rare; flagged in Notes/Edge Cases.
- **Languages, professional musical skill, expert pilot/chef/etc.:** None canonical. Simon is well-educated and speaks French in the standard British-noble way of his era (Oxford-class male would have Latin and likely French), but no language is robustly displayed as a fluency-level skill in the show or book in a way that meets the "additional language counted" threshold. Music and other expert skills are not part of his characterization.
- **Boxing as a separate expert skill:** Folded into "physical fitness top quintile" rather than counted separately. Simon's boxing is gentleman-amateur level (Gentleman Jackson's was the elite hobby boxing club for nobles, not a professional competitive league); counting it as a separate "expert skill" would inflate his stack inconsistent with how Han Solo's piloting or Christian Grey's kickboxing were treated.
- **"Duke" as a separate status trait:** The status is monetized via the wealth trait. Counting "noble title" separately from "billionaire-tier wealth" would double-count.
- **Mysterious dark past (father-vow, stutter trauma):** Per plan, mysterious-past is personality/characterization, not universal-desirable. Excluded from the calc, listed under Personality.

## Personality / Characterization (Excluded from Calc)

- **Tortured/brooding rake:** The defining personality, polarizing — many readers/viewers find brooding-rake romantic, others find it tiresome. Preference splits.
- **Childhood stutter and the trauma framing:** Defining angsty backstory hook; characterization, not slider. (Note: the *act* of overcoming the stutter is folded into the intelligence/conscientiousness signal, but the trauma-framing of the stutter is personality.)
- **Father-rejection / "vow to never have an heir":** The single load-bearing personality conflict. Polarizing — many readers find the redemption arc romantic, others find the controlling deception of Daphne troubling.
- **Reformed-rake arc / sleeping his way through London before settling:** Standard Regency-romance archetype, characterization not slider.
- **Emotional avoidance / refuses-to-let-anyone-in framing:** Characterization. Some readers find it romantic, some grating.
- **Specific deep voice / charismatic presence:** Performer-attribute that drove Page-specific cultural fascination — but voice and charisma are not on the universal-desirable trait list (preferences split, and the trait is hard to operationalize).
- **Need-to-be-fixed-by-the-heroine arc:** Caretaker fantasy hook for the reader; characterization, not slider.
- **Honor-coded duel willingness:** Plot mechanic and character framing; not a universal-desirable trait.

## Joint Probability

- **Method:** threshold (female-target stack)
- **Naive product of marginals:**
  3e-6 × 0.02 × 0.20 × 0.094 × 0.05
  = 3e-6 × 0.02 = 6e-8
  × 0.20 = 1.2e-8
  × 0.094 = 1.128e-9
  × 0.05 = 5.64e-11
  ≈ **5.6e-11 (≈ 1 in 1.8 × 10¹⁰)**
- **Correlation adjustment:**
  - **Inherited-wealth × young-age (positive correlation, pulls less rare).** This is the dominant correction for Simon and an important contrast with Christian Grey. The base-rates.md billionaire entry flags that "billionaire AND age 28–38" is naively overstated as more common than it is — but that flag is calibrated to *self-made* billionaires, who are rare among the young because business empires take time to build. Simon is *inherited*: a young duke inherited a major dukedom from his father, which is the canonical pathway to young-billionaire-tier wealth historically and still today (Walton heirs, Pritzker heirs, European nobility heirs). For inherited wealth, the conditional rate of "billionaire-tier AND age 28–38" is roughly *equal to or slightly higher than* the marginal billionaire rate because the inheritance event peaks when the heir is in early-to-middle adulthood. Net for Simon: the naive product slightly OVERSTATES rarity (treats wealth and age as independent when in inherited-noble framing they are weakly positively coupled). Adjustment: **~2–3x less rare** than naive.
  - **No skill-stacking time-budget cost.** Simon does not stack the multi-domain mastery traits (virtuoso piano + pilot + chef + multiple languages + ...) that drive the time-budget penalty for Christian Grey, Edward Cullen, Mr. Darcy on the wealth-as-multi-skill side, etc. He has effectively three "achievement" traits in the count (wealth, intelligence, fitness), and only intelligence requires any deliberate-practice budget. The time-budget penalty does not bite. **No adjustment.**
  - **Cognitive halo (intelligence ↔ noble education, positive correlation, pulls less rare).** Oxford education and high-IQ are positively correlated via shared g-loading and selection; Regency-era nobility had access to the best tutoring and schooling for their sons. The joint of "noble + intelligent" is naively overstated as more rare than it is. Adjustment: **~1.3–1.5x less rare** than naive (small, since intelligence is one trait of five).
  - **Fitness × age 28–38 (positive correlation, pulls less rare).** Peak male athletic fitness years overlap the prime age window. Adjustment: **~1.2x less rare** than naive.
  - **Family-unencumbered ↔ all others:** approximately independent to first order. Slight conditional correlation with wealth in the noble-inheritance framing (a young duke whose father just died is more likely to be the sole surviving heir if the line was already thin) — but small and second-order. **No adjustment.**
  - **Net direction:** All adjustments pull the same direction (less rare than naive product). This is the OPPOSITE direction from most female-target heartthrob stacks (which tend to pull rarer than naive due to time-budget). The reason is that Simon's stack is "inherited status + relatively standard noble traits" rather than "self-made stacked masteries." The math correctly captures that Simon is rare because *his class is rare* (top of the British peerage), not because he is doing something cognitively or physically impossible. Net adjustment: **~3–5x less rare than naive product.**
- **Adjusted estimate:** ~**1.5e-10** (≈ 1 in 7 × 10⁹). Bracket: [5e-11, 3e-10].
- **Population implication:** Out of ~8 billion humans alive today, expected matches ≈ **1.2** — i.e. roughly a *handful* of people alive on Earth right now match Simon Basset's full universal-desirable trait stack. Out of ~80M US adult men specifically (smaller relevant base), expected matches ≈ **0.012** — fewer than 1, which is the right order of magnitude for "a real-world Duke of Hastings doesn't exist in America." Compared to the female-target dataset processed so far, Simon is meaningfully *less* rare than Christian Grey (~14 orders of magnitude less rare), Edward Cullen (~8 orders less rare), or Mr. Darcy (~3–4 orders less rare). His stack is closer to Han Solo / Aragorn-tier rarity (high but not absurd) — driven by inherited status rather than skill-stacking.

## Confidence Assessment
- **Overall:** medium-high — trait identification is clean, base rates are all from existing entries with established calibration, and the correlation reasoning runs in a consistent direction.
- **Trait identification:** high — every trait counted (wealth/duke status, Oxford-education-implied intelligence, boxing-implied fitness, age, family-unencumbered) is robustly canonical in Bridgerton Season 1 and Quinn's *The Duke and I*. The decision to exclude height (Page is 6'0"–6'1", canon doesn't specify ≥6'2") is conservative and explicit.
- **Base rates:** medium-high — wealth (Forbes/Bloomberg-anchored), age (Census), fitness (NHANES), family-unencumbered (composite), and IQ (definitional) are all from established base-rates.md entries. Mapping a Regency duke to "billionaire-tier" carries calibration uncertainty — historical-to-modern wealth conversion is contested — but the modern equivalent for the major Regency dukedoms is robustly billionaire-tier across multiple historical-economics references (Piketty 2014, Allen "British Industrial Revolution in Global Perspective"), so the 3e-6 choice is defensible.
- **Correlation adjustments:** medium — directionality is well-grounded (inherited-wealth/age positive correlation is the canonical noble-inheritance pattern; cognitive halo is documented). Magnitudes (3–5x less rare net) are judgment-anchored to plan methodology guidance and the contrast with Christian Grey's far more severe time-budget penalty.

## Notes / Edge Cases

- **Anthony vs. Simon coin-toss.** The ticket explicitly invited picking either Anthony Bridgerton or Simon Basset. Both are dukes/viscounts with similar Regency-extreme-wealth profiles, similar fitness, similar age windows. The decisive factor was reach signal: Simon's Season 1 cultural moment is consistently the Bridgerton breakout in aggregated rankings, and "I burn for you" plus the Bond-rumor afterglow gave Page a bigger single-actor footprint than Bailey's. Anthony's stack would be slightly less rare on family-unencumbered (Anthony has many living siblings and a large supportive family — fails the orphan/no-obligations criterion). Picking Simon is also the right call for stack-rarity purposes within the Bridgerton ensemble: the orphan + father-vow combo is more demographically extreme than Anthony's "burdened patriarch with seven siblings" archetype.
- **Height exclusion is conservative.** If Simon were counted at ≥6'2" (the threshold), the joint would shift from ~1.5e-10 to ~6e-12 — about 25x rarer. The exclusion is defensible (Page is shorter than 6'2" and Quinn's novel doesn't specify), but a charitable reading would include it. Bracketing Simon's adjusted estimate at [5e-11, 6e-12] for "with vs. without height" gives a sense of the calibration sensitivity.
- **Wealth-tier choice (billionaire vs. $10M income).** Mapping a Regency duke to a modern wealth tier is the largest single calibration choice in this profile. Using $10M income (2e-4) instead of billionaire-tier (3e-6) would make the joint roughly 70x less rare, putting Simon at ~1e-8 — a meaningful change. The billionaire-tier choice is correct because (1) major Regency dukedoms had real-modern-equivalent net worth in the multi-billion range when capitalized, (2) the show frames Hastings as one of the senior English peerages, and (3) consistency with Mr. Darcy and Edward Cullen, who were placed at billionaire tier on similar reasoning. But a charitable reading at $10M income would put him in line with Han Solo / Aragorn rarity, which feels slightly under-stacked given the canonical "Duke of Hastings" framing.
- **Comparison to Mr. Darcy.** Both are Regency-era ultra-wealthy English gentleman heartthrobs. Darcy is a *commoner with extreme wealth* (Pemberley estate, £10K/year). Simon is a *duke* (highest noble rank below royal). On wealth, Simon outranks Darcy. On stack, Simon is roughly comparable: similar wealth tier, similar education, similar fitness, similar age window. Darcy is roughly 0–1 orders of magnitude rarer on the trait stack alone (slightly older average, slightly more reserved/intellectual framing pushes the IQ trait harder, slightly more family-encumbered with Pemberley estate management and sister Georgiana). The two are within calibration noise of each other — they are the same archetype across two centuries of Regency-romance canon, which is itself a methodologically interesting finding.
- **Comparison to Christian Grey.** Simon is roughly 14 orders of magnitude *less* rare than Christian Grey (~1.5e-10 vs ~2e-24). The gap is the entire methodological story: Christian stacks seven mastery skills (billionaire + virtuoso pianist + pilot + chef + kickboxer + multilingual + Harvard) into 27 modern years; Simon stacks inherited status + standard noble traits with no time-budget violation. Both are female-target Regency-coded heartthrobs (Christian's Anastasia romance retains many Regency-romance beats — billionaire, mysterious past, broody, dominant), but Christian inherits the Regency *aesthetic* while inflating the *trait stack* far beyond what Quinn's actual-Regency canon does. This contrast is one of the strongest internal findings the dataset produces so far: the modern descendants of the duke-romance archetype have inflated the trait stack by ~14 orders of magnitude over the originals.
- **Personality dominance.** Like most female-target heartthrobs, Simon's cultural pull is heavily personality-driven — the brooding-rake-with-trauma archetype, the father-vow plot, the "I burn for you" chemistry, Page's specific charisma and voice. The trait stack alone underrepresents the cultural lightning rod the character became. Recognized.
- **Show vs. book canon precedence.** The show altered some plot beats from the novel (most notably the deception-conception scene, which is more violently coded in the book than the show; the show softened the moral framing). For trait counts (height, age, wealth, family, fitness, intelligence) the show and book agree, so the precedence question doesn't affect the calculation.
- **Race and the "Regency colorblind casting" historical question.** The show's choice to cast Page (a Black actor) as the Duke of Hastings is part of its "alternate-Regency" framing where racial integration of the peerage occurred under Queen Charlotte. This is a creative choice for the show and does not affect the universal-desirable trait stack as defined in the project. Flagged for completeness — the project counts canonical traits regardless of casting choices.
