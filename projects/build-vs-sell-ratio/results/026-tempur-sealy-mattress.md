## Build breakdown ($ millions, USD — Tempur Sealy North America segment FY2024)

The Tempur Sealy North America build side is the cost of producing premium memory-foam mattresses (Tempur-Pedic line, manufactured at the Albuquerque NM and Crawfordsville IN foam plants), traditional innerspring mattresses (Sealy and Stearns & Foster brands, assembled at approximately 30 Sealy regional plant locations across the US), adjustable bed bases (the Ergo line for Tempur-Pedic and various Sealy/Stearns & Foster bases), and bedding accessories (Tempur pillows, mattress protectors, sheets), plus the inbound logistics for foam chemistry inputs and outbound logistics for the unusually-bulky finished-mattress shipments. Mattresses are physically heavy and high-volume relative to dollar value (a queen-size Tempur mattress is ~80 lbs and ~80×60×11 inches packaged), so freight is a structurally-large cost item. The FY2024 build side is also influenced by the continued ramp of the Crawfordsville IN foam pour plant which opened 2023 — depreciation on the new facility began flowing through COGS in 2024, contributing to the gross-margin profile.

- **COGS — raw materials (memory foam chemistry, polyurethane foam, innerspring components, fabric/ticking, packaging)** (the primary input is proprietary TEMPUR memory-foam chemistry — polyurethane foam with viscoelastic additives, poured at the Albuquerque and Crawfordsville foam plants using process technology developed in-house and protected by trade secrets and patents; for the Sealy and Stearns & Foster lines the inputs are pocketed coil innerspring assemblies sourced from Leggett & Platt and other coil suppliers, polyurethane foam and gel-foam comfort layers, fabric ticking sourced from US and import textile suppliers, and packaging materials including the increasingly-common compressed/rolled mattress packaging that has become standard for shipping efficiency; foam chemistry input prices were elevated through 2024 reflecting petrochemical feedstock costs and the structural pricing power of the major MDI/TDI suppliers (BASF, Covestro, Huntsman, Dow); the AAA-equivalent quality-tier sourcing for Tempur-Pedic premium products carries a sustainability and quality premium versus commodity foam suppliers): **$1,420 (62.9% of NA COGS)**

- **COGS — direct manufacturing labor** (foam pour plant operators at Albuquerque NM and Crawfordsville IN, mattress assembly labor across the approximately 30 Sealy/Stearns & Foster regional assembly plants, quality control inspectors, shift supervisors at production sites; US manufacturing labor costs reflect the structural decision to keep mattress production largely domestic rather than offshore — mattress manufacturing has not significantly offshored to Asia in the way that consumer electronics or apparel has, partly because of the high freight cost of shipping finished mattresses internationally and partly because regional production-and-distribution networks are more cost-effective for the bulky-finished-good economics; Tempur Sealy's labor base in the US benefits from the regional-plant footprint reducing labor cost relative to high-cost-coastal markets): **$285 (12.6% of NA COGS)**

- **COGS — manufacturing plant overhead, utilities, and indirect labor** (plant-level overhead at the foam pour facilities including utilities for the energy-intensive foam pour process, the regional mattress assembly plants' overhead, plant management and engineering staff not directly assigned to production lines, plant-level quality systems, environmental compliance for the chemistry-intensive foam pour operations including VOC monitoring and waste handling, plant-level safety programs and OSHA compliance): **$135 (6.0% of NA COGS)**

- **COGS — manufacturing equipment depreciation** (depreciation share for the Albuquerque NM and Crawfordsville IN foam pour plants, including the major 2023-2024 ramp of Crawfordsville which began contributing meaningfully to depreciation expense in 2024; depreciation on the regional mattress assembly plant equipment, the proprietary foam pour systems, the encasement and finishing equipment; FY2024 capital expenditures were $97.3M consolidated, modest relative to the existing fixed-asset base; the Crawfordsville plant capex from 2022-2023 of approximately $90M is now flowing through depreciation): **$140 (6.2% of NA COGS)**

- **COGS — outbound freight and distribution** (mattress freight is a structurally-large cost item — a queen-size Tempur mattress in retail packaging is approximately 80 lbs and 80×60×11 inches in dimensional shipping volume, so freight cost per unit is meaningfully higher than for typical consumer durables; LTL trucking from regional plants to retail-distribution-center destinations (Mattress Firm distribution centers in major regions, other specialty-bedding-retailer distribution networks, mass-retail distribution centers); white-glove delivery to consumers for the DTC e-commerce channel and for some retail-store-final-mile arrangements; the recent shift toward boxed-in-a-box compressed mattress packaging for some product lines reduces dimensional freight cost meaningfully but Tempur-Pedic premium products are typically shipped in traditional uncompressed packaging to preserve product integrity): **$215 (9.5% of NA COGS)**

- **COGS — warranty reserves and customer-service operations attributable to product issues** (the Tempur-Pedic 10-year limited warranty plus the Sealy 10-year warranty creates a significant warranty reserve obligation — historically Tempur Sealy reserves approximately 0.5-1.0% of net sales for warranty exposure; the customer service operations associated with warranty claims, comfort-guarantee returns within the trial period (typically 90-120 nights), and product replacements; mattress disposal logistics for warranty-replaced units): **$63 (2.8% of NA COGS)**

**COGS subtotal: $2,258 (matches segment gross profit calculation: NA net sales $3,789M − NA gross profit $1,530.8M = $2,258.1M)**

- **R&D — product innovation, foam chemistry, mattress engineering, smart-bedding development** (Tempur Sealy operates a centralized R&D function supporting the Tempur-Pedic, Sealy, and Stearns & Foster product lines plus the Tempur ActiveBreeze cooling-system platform launched 2023; Tempur foam chemistry R&D continues to develop new viscoelastic formulations, cooling-gel composites, and multi-density layered foam constructions; engineering R&D for adjustable-base mechanisms (the Ergo platform smart-base controls, the Sealy adjustable-base electronics); smart-bedding R&D including the partnership exploration of sensor-enabled mattresses and connected-base health-tracking applications; sustainability R&D including bio-based foam chemistry exploration and recyclable-mattress design initiatives; the consolidated R&D spend is not separately disclosed in the income statement and is allocated within G&A per the company's accounting policy — the ~$98M consolidated estimate is triangulated from public commentary about R&D investment intensity (~2% of consolidated net sales is the typical management description) and the North America segment share is approximately $75M based on the segment-revenue weighting): **$75 (estimated)**

**Build total: $2,333**

## Sell breakdown ($ millions, USD — Tempur Sealy North America segment FY2024)

Tempur Sealy's sell-side intensity is among the highest in the consumer-products panel — the mattress category has structurally heavy advertising and trade-promotion intensity reflecting (a) the long replacement cycle (consumers buy a mattress every 8-10 years on average, so brand recall must be sustained continuously rather than driven by recurring-purchase cadence), (b) the considered-purchase nature of the category (consumers research before buying, so brand-credibility marketing investment matters more than impulse-purchase activation), (c) the heavy reliance on third-party specialty retail (Mattress Firm and other dealers) which requires substantial trade-promotion, floor-model, and sales-rep field-force investment to defend shelf space, and (d) the structural cost of the holiday-sales-driven consumer-purchase pattern (Memorial Day, Labor Day, Black Friday, President's Day are the four major mattress-buying windows and require coordinated advertising-plus-promotion activation). The FY2024 NA segment sell-side breakdown is estimated at approximately $688M, derived from the $918.7M segment-attributable SG&A less the estimated $230M segment-attributable G&A allocation.

- **Advertising and measured media** (national TV including the long-running Tempur-Pedic 'Sleep is Power' campaign and successor brand creative, the Sealy 'America's #1 Mattress Brand' positioning campaign, plus Stearns & Foster premium-tier brand campaigns; CTV and streaming advertising including substantial investment in Hulu, Netflix ad-tier, Roku, and Amazon Prime Video for the cord-cutting demographic that aligns with the considered-purchase mattress buyer; digital video and YouTube including the Tempur-Pedic-branded sleep-content channel; paid search defending the brand terms (Tempur, Tempur-Pedic, Sealy, Stearns & Foster) against competitor bidding (Casper, Purple, Sleep Number, DreamCloud, Saatva, Helix, Nectar, etc.); display and programmatic with sophisticated audience segmentation around mattress-purchase-intent windows, the major-life-event triggers (new home, marriage, divorce, new baby, college dorm), and the demographic-and-income-tier segmentation supporting the brand-tier strategy across Tempur (premium $2,000+), Sealy (mid $700-1,500), and Stearns & Foster (upper-mid $1,500-2,500); celebrity endorsements and athlete partnerships including the Tempur-Pedic NBA partnership and athlete-sponsorship platform; OOH/billboards in major markets particularly during the four major mattress-buying-window holiday weekends; print advertising including Sleep Magazine, Bedtimes, and consumer publications; podcast advertising particularly in sleep-and-wellness-focused podcast inventory; production cost for the campaign creative including the multi-talent talent fees for the brand campaigns): **$310 (estimated NA share of ~$410M consolidated advertising spend)**

- **Trade promotion, dealer co-op, floor models, and retailer incentives** (the mattress industry's structural reliance on third-party specialty retailers (Mattress Firm, Bob's Discount Furniture, Raymour & Flanigan, Big Lots, regional bedding chains) requires substantial trade-promotion intensity — co-op advertising contributions where Tempur Sealy reimburses retailers for portions of locally-funded advertising that features the brand, dealer-rebate programs tied to volume tiers and brand-mix targets, holiday-window promotional pricing support; floor models — physical mattresses given to retailers for showroom display use, technically a COGS-classified inventory shipment but functionally a sell-side investment because the mattresses are essentially permanent floor-loan units that the retailer is given at near-zero cost in exchange for shelf space and category exclusivity commitments; mass-retail trade-promotion to Costco, Sam's Club, and warehouse-club channels for the value-tier Sealy and Stearns & Foster product placements; mass-retail trade-promotion to big-box (Macy's, JCPenney, Big Lots) for category placement; retailer-training programs for the trained-mattress-salesperson role that drives third-party-retail conversion): **$155**

- **Sales force and trade-marketing organization** (the mattress-industry sales-rep model — Tempur Sealy maintains a substantial field sales force calling on third-party specialty retail accounts (Mattress Firm corporate buyers and regional managers, the major specialty-bedding-chain accounts, the warehouse-club category buyers), the merchandising and category-management team supporting retailer assortment decisions, the trade-show and industry-event presence (the Las Vegas Furniture Market, the High Point Furniture Market, the World Market Center events), the in-store training programs that develop trained-mattress-salesperson capability at retailer floor level — historically Tempur-Pedic invested heavily in retail-staff certification programs as a brand-quality differentiator); the e-commerce channel team supporting Tempur.com and Sealy.com DTC operations, plus the marketplace presence on Amazon, Wayfair, and other online retail destinations): **$118**

- **DTC e-commerce operations and small owned-retail footprint** (Tempur.com and Sealy.com e-commerce platform operations including digital storefront management, online merchandising, customer-service operations supporting the online channel, online-channel marketing and conversion optimization; the small Tempur-Pedic owned-store footprint — Tempur Sealy operates approximately 100+ Tempur-Pedic owned stores across the US, primarily in major-metro markets, functioning as both brand-experience venues and DTC sales channels; in-store mattress demonstration equipment and showroom design; the omnichannel coordination between owned-store, e-commerce, and third-party-retail channels; this line is meaningfully smaller than would be the case for a primarily-DTC brand because Tempur Sealy remains primarily a wholesale-to-retail business): **$70**

## Methodology notes

**Why North America segment rather than consolidated.** The ticket explicitly directs use of the North America segment for cleanest single-product proxy. North America is approximately 77% of consolidated revenue and is the canonical US mattress-OEM cost structure (third-party specialty retail dependence, Memorial-Day-Labor-Day-Black-Friday-President's-Day promotional cycle, US manufacturing footprint with high-freight outbound distribution, the brand-tier portfolio of Tempur premium / Stearns & Foster upper-mid / Sealy mid-market). The International segment runs differently — higher gross margin reflecting less promotional intensity in European retail, more direct-to-retailer commercial structure, growth driven by new-product launches rather than promotional-window activation, and a meaningful share of the segment in higher-margin Asia-Pacific markets (Japan, South Korea, Australia). Using NA only produces a clean US-focused datapoint comparable to other US-focused brands in the panel (Lululemon NA, KitchenAid NA, Whirlpool NA all use North America segment focus for the same reason).

**Why fiscal year 2024 — and the critical timing of the Mattress Firm acquisition.** The Mattress Firm acquisition closed February 5, 2025 — twenty-four business days after the close of FY2024. The FY2024 numbers therefore reflect the standalone Tempur Sealy mattress-OEM business with zero Mattress Firm contribution; this is the cleanest comparable to other consumer-product brands in this panel that don't own their downstream retail. The FY2025 results (reported February 18, 2026) include approximately eleven months of Mattress Firm operations and reflect a vertically-integrated mattress-OEM-plus-retail business that is structurally not comparable to the FY2024 datapoint or to the other panel comparables. The corporate name change to Somnigroup International (effective February 18, 2025) marks the structural transition from the legacy Tempur Sealy business analyzed here to the new vertically-integrated combined entity. For the purposes of this build-vs-sell-ratio analysis, FY2024 is the correct snapshot and the analysis is explicitly restricted to the mattress-OEM cost structure.

**Implied Mattress Firm acquisition impact on build/sell ratio.** If Mattress Firm had been included for FY2024, the consolidated revenue would have been approximately $4.93B (Tempur Sealy) + $4.4-4.6B (Mattress Firm 2024 estimated revenue based on 2,300+ store count and ~$2M avg per-store revenue) = approximately $9.3-9.5B combined revenue. The cost structure shift would be substantial: Mattress Firm's cost structure as a pure-retail business is approximately 50-55% COGS (cost of goods sold to consumers — primarily the wholesale acquisition cost of mattresses from Tempur Sealy, Serta Simmons Bedding, Sleep Number, and other suppliers), 35-40% SG&A (heavy in retail labor for mattress-store sales associates, store rent for the typical 8,000-12,000 sq ft retail footprint, in-store advertising and yellow-page-equivalent local marketing, regional management overhead), and 5-10% operating margin. Mattress Firm's cost structure on inclusion would shift the combined entity build_pct toward sell-side because retail labor and store rent are sell-side classifications, partially offset by the COGS/wholesale-acquisition cost being a build-side line. The net direction would be a meaningful sell-side shift — the combined entity build_pct would likely be ~62-67% rather than the legacy ~72% consolidated or ~77% NA-segment standalone. This is why the legacy FY2024 mattress-OEM datapoint is the cleaner comparable.

**Build/sell classification of R&D — moved from G&A to build per the methodology spec.** Tempur Sealy does not separately disclose R&D as an income-statement line item; the R&D activity is allocated within G&A per the company's accounting policy. The estimated $75M NA-segment-attributable R&D (derived from a triangulated $98M consolidated estimate at ~2% of consolidated net sales) is moved from G&A to build for purposes of this analysis, consistent with the methodology spec's classification of R&D as a build-side cost. This shift moves $75M from overhead to build and slightly raises the build_pct. The triangulated R&D estimate carries uncertainty of ±$25M; a different R&D estimate would shift build_pct by less than 0.5 percentage points and does not materially change the directional finding.

**Build/sell classification of trade promotion — left in sell.** Trade promotion in the mattress category includes co-op advertising contributions to retailers, dealer-rebate programs tied to volume tiers, holiday-window promotional pricing support, and the floor-model program where physical mattresses are essentially given to retailers for showroom display use. The floor-model program in particular sits ambiguously between build (the mattresses are physical inventory shipped from manufacturing) and sell (the economic function is shelf-space acquisition rather than revenue-generating product sale). The accounting treatment varies — some floor models are recognized as inventory shipments at zero or near-zero revenue (effectively reducing reported revenue from the corresponding retail account), some are recognized as sample-and-promotional expense within selling and marketing. For consistency with the segment SG&A reporting, the floor-model and trade-promotion intensity is left in the sell line at approximately $155M for the NA segment. A more aggressive sell-classification (moving an additional $50-100M of floor-model COGS to sell) would shift build_pct down by 1-2 percentage points; the directional finding (build_pct in the mid-70s for NA segment) is robust.

**Advertising estimate of $310M for NA segment is triangulated, not disclosed.** Tempur Sealy does not separately disclose advertising expense in the income statement. The $310M NA-segment advertising estimate is triangulated from (a) Winmo/Comperemedia third-party measured-media tracking that places consolidated Tempur Sealy 2024 US advertising at approximately $410M, (b) the assumption that approximately 75-80% of consolidated advertising is NA-attributable since most measured media in the Winmo data is US-market-focused, and (c) the assumption that advertising is the largest single line within the segment selling and marketing expense, consistent with the company's published guidance that brand investment is the primary structural strategy. The estimate carries uncertainty of ±$50M; a different advertising estimate would not change the build/sell classification but would shift the within-sell composition. The 2025 guidance of ~$730M total advertising for the post-Mattress-Firm combined entity is consistent with approximately $410-450M for the legacy Tempur Sealy portion of the combined entity, supporting the FY2024 estimate.

**Sales force compensation estimate.** Tempur Sealy does not separately disclose sales force compensation; the field sales force calling on third-party specialty retailers, the merchandising and category-management team, and the trade-show/industry-event presence is a structurally significant cost in the mattress-OEM business. The estimate of $118M for NA-segment sales force and trade-marketing organization is derived from typical industry-comparable headcount (approximately 250-350 sales reps and trade-marketing personnel) at industry-comparable fully-loaded compensation (approximately $300-400K per FTE including base, variable comp, benefits, and travel/expense). The estimate carries uncertainty of ±$30M.

**Consolidated-vs-NA-segment sensitivity.** The headline NA-segment build_pct of 77.2% is meaningfully higher than the consolidated build_pct of 72.3%. The difference reflects the structural distinction between US mattress-OEM cost structure (heavy COGS share due to US manufacturing labor, freight, and the Sealy/Stearns & Foster spring-mattress assembly footprint) and International mattress-OEM cost structure (higher gross margin reflecting product mix toward Tempur-Pedic premium across the international markets, more efficient European/Asian distribution networks, and lower promotional intensity outside the US specialty-bedding-retail model). Either datapoint is defensible as the headline; this analysis uses NA-segment per the ticket guidance.

**The build_pct of 77.2% is higher than the ticket-spec predicted range of 50-65%.** The ticket prediction implicitly assumed mattresses are very sell-heavy because the category is famously marketing-intensive. The actual datapoint shows that while the sell-side is high in absolute terms ($688M, 18.2% of NA revenue, among the highest in the consumer panel), the build side is also large because mattress manufacturing is materially cost-intensive — foam chemistry inputs, US manufacturing labor across approximately 30 plant locations, structural outbound freight cost for the bulky finished good, plus Crawfordsville plant depreciation flowing through 2024 numbers. Mattresses are not a low-COGS-high-marketing product like a beverage or a fragrance; they are a high-COGS-and-high-marketing product. The 77.2% build_pct datapoint is a meaningful empirical correction to the marketing-driven category narrative.

**What's surprising in the numbers.**

- **Tempur Sealy build_pct of 77.2% is at the upper end of the consumer-products panel range and meaningfully exceeds the ticket-spec predicted range of 50-65%.** The mattress category's reputation for marketing intensity (60-day return guarantees, financing, heavy TV/radio) is justified — sell-side intensity of 18.2% of NA revenue is genuinely high — but the COGS share is also large because mattress manufacturing is materially capital-and-labor-intensive in a way that beverage CPG or apparel is not. The category combines high build AND high sell, producing a build_pct in the mid-to-upper-70s rather than a lower number.

- **Sell-side intensity of 18.2% of revenue is comparable to premium DTC brands.** For comparison, Lululemon ran sell-side intensity of approximately 15-16%, Nike Brand approximately 12-13%, Apple iPhone approximately 6-7%. The Tempur Sealy NA sell-side intensity is closer to Starbucks territory (~16-18% sell intensity) than to typical CPG (~8-12%). This is structurally driven by (a) the long replacement cycle requiring sustained brand-recall investment, (b) the third-party-specialty-retail channel structure requiring substantial trade-promotion and field-sales investment, and (c) the four-major-holiday-window promotional cadence requiring coordinated activation.

- **Gross margin of 40.4% in NA is meaningfully lower than International's 56.9%.** The 16.5-percentage-point gap between segment gross margins is the largest segment-margin spread observed across the multi-segment companies in this panel. The gap reflects (a) US manufacturing labor cost structure vs. lower-cost European/Asian production, (b) the US product mix skew toward Sealy and Stearns & Foster mid-tier brands with lower gross margin vs. International being heavily Tempur premium-brand weighted, (c) US promotional intensity that compresses net-realized pricing more than International, and (d) US outbound freight cost intensity for the bulky-finished-good economics being structurally higher than European/Asian freight cost given US distance economics.

- **Capital expenditure intensity of $97.3M consolidated for FY2024 is modest at approximately 2% of revenue.** This is meaningfully lower than the 2022-2023 capex levels (approximately $200M per year) when the Crawfordsville IN plant was being built. The 2024 capex pullback reflects the completion of the major capacity-expansion cycle and a return to maintenance-level capex; the 2025 guidance returned to approximately $200M reflecting both ongoing Tempur Sealy capex and the layered Mattress Firm refurbishment capex of approximately $25M.

- **The Mattress Firm acquisition fundamentally changes the cost-structure analysis going forward.** The legacy Tempur Sealy mattress-OEM business analyzed here will not be comparable to FY2025-and-forward Somnigroup financials. Anyone comparing this datapoint to FY2025-and-forward Somnigroup datapoints needs to control for the vertical-integration shift — the combined entity's cost structure is fundamentally different from either standalone Tempur Sealy mattress-OEM or standalone Mattress Firm retail. The FY2024 NA-segment datapoint here is the last clean snapshot of the mattress-OEM cost structure before vertical integration.

- **The FTC challenge timeline (May 2023 announce → November 2024 vote-to-challenge → late January 2025 consent decree → February 5, 2025 close) created over twenty months of regulatory uncertainty during which Tempur Sealy operated as a standalone business.** This long uncertainty period had operational consequences — Tempur Sealy delayed certain investments and Mattress Firm operated under integration uncertainty — but the FY2024 financial snapshot reflects the operating performance of the legacy business through this period. The consent decree required divestiture of approximately 73 overlapping Mattress Firm and Tempur-Pedic stores; this is small relative to Mattress Firm's 2,300+ store base and does not materially affect the cost-structure analysis.

- **Brand-tier product portfolio creates a within-segment ratio dispersion that this analysis blends.** The Tempur-Pedic premium brand ($2,000+ retail price points, ~30-35% of NA segment revenue, premium-positioned advertising-heavy strategy) runs a different build/sell ratio than the Sealy mid-market brand ($700-1,500 retail, ~50-55% of NA revenue, more value-positioned with lower advertising intensity per dollar of revenue) and the Stearns & Foster upper-mid brand ($1,500-2,500, ~10-15% of NA revenue, premium-positioned). The blended NA-segment ratio of 77.2% averages across these three brand-tier sub-portfolios; if each were broken out separately, Tempur-Pedic would likely show a slightly lower build_pct (~72-74%) reflecting its higher advertising intensity per dollar of revenue, and Sealy would likely show a higher build_pct (~80-82%) reflecting lower advertising intensity per dollar of revenue.

**Confidence: medium.** Major top-line and segment numbers (consolidated and NA segment net sales, gross profit, gross margin, operating income, segment operating income, consolidated SG&A breakdown into selling/marketing and G&A, EBITDA and adjusted EBITDA) come directly from the audited Somnigroup FY2024 earnings release with full segment disclosure. The major triangulations (NA-segment SG&A breakdown into sell and overhead, the within-sell allocation across advertising/trade promotion/sales force/DTC/brand events, the R&D estimate of $98M consolidated and $75M NA-attributable, the advertising estimate of $410M consolidated and $310M NA-attributable from Winmo/Comperemedia third-party tracking) are estimated within reasonable bands. The medium-confidence rating reflects (a) the necessity to triangulate advertising spend from third-party tracking rather than direct disclosure, (b) the segment-level SG&A breakdown not being disclosed below the consolidated level, requiring revenue-weighted apportionment, and (c) the R&D estimate being entirely triangulated rather than disclosed. The directional finding (NA-segment build_pct in the mid-to-upper-70s, sell-side intensity in the high-teens-percent of revenue, mattress category being structurally both high-build-and-high-sell rather than predominantly one or the other) is robust to the residual estimation uncertainty.
