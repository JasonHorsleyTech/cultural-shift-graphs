## Build breakdown ($ millions, USD — Inter IKEA Group consolidated FY24, converted from EUR at 1.085)

The Inter IKEA Group build side captures the upstream economics of designing, sourcing, manufacturing, and distributing the IKEA product range to franchisees (predominantly Ingka Group, plus the small number of independent franchisees). Inter IKEA Group is structurally an upstream entity — design, supply, brand, and franchise IP — and does not operate IKEA retail stores (except the experimental IKEA Delft store in the Netherlands). The build composition therefore looks more like a wholesale-and-manufacturing business than a consumer-retail business, with very heavy COGS share relative to revenue. The BILLY bookcase is one of approximately 9,500 product articles in the IKEA range; the BILLY-specific cost structure inherits the Inter IKEA Group cost structure because BILLY is produced in the IKEA Industry-owned Swedish/Slovakian plants (Kattilstorp, Hultsfred, and the new 35,000 sqm Slovakia plant under construction), with the new BILLY production capacity in Slovakia announced as a major FY24 capex investment.

- **COGS — raw materials, contract manufacturing, IKEA Industry plant operations, and global sourcing from ~800 external suppliers** (Inter IKEA Group sources approximately 90% of the IKEA product range from approximately 800 external suppliers worldwide and produces approximately 10% internally via IKEA Industry's approximately 40 wholly-owned manufacturing plants concentrated in Sweden, Lithuania, Poland, Slovakia, Russia (historical, divested 2022), and the United States. The COGS line includes raw material costs (particleboard, MDF, polypropylene, fabrics, glass, metal, foam, electronics for connected products) which declined sharply in FY24 — raw material and commodity prices fell -11.4% year-on-year, contributing the bulk of the gross margin improvement; direct manufacturing labor at IKEA Industry plants; payments to external contract manufacturers for the 90% of range sourced externally; manufacturing overhead at IKEA Industry plants; depreciation on IKEA Industry production equipment and facilities; and inbound and outbound logistics costs of moving raw materials to manufacturing plants and finished goods from plants/suppliers to franchisee distribution centers. The COGS share of revenue at 79.4% reflects Inter IKEA Group's positioning as effectively a manufacturer-wholesaler in its relationship with the IKEA retail entities — Inter IKEA marks up at low single-digit operating margin on its wholesale-to-franchisee book of business, then earns its overall operating profit through the 3% franchise fee on top of wholesale): **$22,837 (79.4% of revenue)**

- **Operating expense allocation — R&D, product development, and range management (IKEA of Sweden function)** (Inter IKEA Group operates the IKEA of Sweden range development and design function based primarily in Älmhult, Sweden. This is the function that designs the entire IKEA product range from concept through prototyping, materials selection, supplier qualification, and range launch. The FY24 range development activity included the continuation of the BILLY range sustainability redesign (switch to starch-based glue at the Kazlų Rūda Lithuania plant, recycled wood content increases, design-for-disassembly initiatives, packaging optimization), continued PAX wardrobe range refresh ahead of the Hultsfred capacity expansion, ongoing investment in the broader sustainability range targets including the FY30 climate-neutral commitment, smart-home range development (the Inter IKEA partnership ecosystem for the Dirigera hub and the broader IKEA Home Smart range), and the multi-year affordability/cost-out program that engineered out cost from the existing range while maintaining quality. The IKEA of Sweden function plus the IKEA Industry plant-level engineering teams represent the R&D-equivalent activity; estimated at approximately EUR 400M of the EUR 3,151M total operating expenses, classified as build per the methodology spec which moves R&D from G&A to build): **$434**

- **Operating expense allocation — supply chain management, sourcing, and category management functions** (Inter IKEA Group's central supply chain function manages the relationships with approximately 800 external suppliers, the IKEA Industry internal manufacturing capacity planning, the global distribution network of approximately 30+ distribution centers (many owned by Inter IKEA, some operated by Ingka), inbound logistics planning, supplier sustainability and code-of-conduct enforcement, the IKEA Way (IWAY) supplier standards program, and the category management function that determines what to manufacture internally versus source externally. These supply chain HQ functions are conceptually build-side because they directly support the production-and-distribution economics; the physical distribution and logistics expenses are largely in COGS but the planning, sourcing, and supplier management functions sit in operating expenses. Estimated at approximately EUR 200M of total operating expenses): **$217**

**Build total: $23,488** (build_millions in frontmatter; consists of $22,837M COGS, $434M R&D/range development moved from G&A to build per methodology, and $217M supply chain/sourcing management functions)

## Sell breakdown ($ millions, USD — Inter IKEA Group consolidated FY24)

Inter IKEA Group's sell-side function is structurally light relative to typical consumer-products companies in this panel because Inter IKEA Group does NOT operate the IKEA retail stores and does NOT directly sell to consumers (except via the small IKEA Delft store). Inter IKEA Group's customers are the IKEA franchisees (Ingka Group and the small number of independent franchisees), and the wholesale-to-franchisee channel is structurally close to an administered transfer between related parties rather than a competitive sell-in requiring traditional sales-force investment. The 'selling' that Inter IKEA Group does is therefore primarily brand-investment selling — building and maintaining the IKEA brand globally so that Ingka Group and other franchisees can sell to consumers in their local markets. The retail-level advertising and store-marketing activity that most consumer brands consider their primary 'sell' function lives at Ingka Group, not Inter IKEA Group.

- **Marketing, brand investment, and global communications** (Inter IKEA Group operates the central IKEA brand function including the IKEA Communications business which produces the global IKEA brand creative, range books and product imagery used by all franchisees in their local marketing, the historical IKEA catalog (discontinued for the global print version after 2020 but continued in some markets in reduced form), digital brand content and global social media presence, sustainability communications and the corporate-affairs brand-narrative function, and the central marketing systems and tools provided to franchisees. The FY24 operating expense growth was driven by 'a significant one-off investment in marketing to support IKEA retailers in communicating the lower prices' to consumers globally — Inter IKEA Group disclosed this explicitly as the year-over-year opex increase driver. The estimated EUR 1,000M of total marketing/brand investment in FY24 includes both the normalized brand-and-communications run-rate (estimated EUR 600-700M) and the FY24 one-off price-communication marketing investment (estimated EUR 200-400M lift). This is approximately 3.8% of Inter IKEA Group revenue and approximately 2.2% of the consumer-facing IKEA system retail sales of EUR 45.1B — a meaningful absolute spend but a small proportion of the consumer-facing economics because most consumer-facing advertising and marketing is funded and executed at the franchisee (Ingka and other) level. Wholesale-to-franchisee sales force activity is minimal because the franchisee relationships are administered through long-term franchise agreements rather than transactional account management): **$1,085 (estimated EUR 1,000M converted at 1.085)**

## Methodology notes

**Why Inter IKEA Group as the 'parent' rather than full IKEA value chain.** The ticket explicitly directs Inter IKEA Group as the default analytical entity, with the alternative 'full IKEA value chain' (combining Inter IKEA + Ingka, netting intercompany) as a sensitivity. Inter IKEA Group captures the IKEA brand ownership, the IKEA Concept franchisor function, the IKEA range development and design function (IKEA of Sweden), the global supply chain and sourcing function, and the wholly-owned IKEA Industry manufacturing operations (~40 plants producing ~10% of the range, including the BILLY bookcase plants in Sweden and the new Slovakia facility). This is the closest analog to a 'parent company' for the BILLY bookcase because the BILLY product economics (design, materials, manufacturing, sourcing, distribution to retailers) sit primarily at Inter IKEA Group; the BILLY-to-consumer retail economics (store labor, store rent, local advertising, in-store experience) sit at Ingka Group. Using Inter IKEA Group as the headline produces a build_pct that reflects the upstream supply-chain economics of the BILLY bookcase, which is methodologically defensible but produces a higher build_pct than panel-comparable consumer companies because Inter IKEA Group structurally lacks the retail/sell function.

**Why the build_pct of 95.6% is materially higher than the ticket-predicted range of 70-85%.** The ticket prediction implicitly anchored on consumer-products-comparable benchmarks (other consumer brands in the panel run 65-90% build_pct), but Inter IKEA Group's structure is genuinely different — Inter IKEA Group is upstream of retail and does not have a traditional 'sell' function. Its only meaningful sell-side cost is the brand-investment marketing spend (EUR ~1B in FY24, elevated by the price-communication one-off), while the consumer-facing advertising and retail-store operations that would normally sit in 'sell' for a consumer brand are at Ingka Group instead. The 95.6% build_pct therefore reflects the structural reality of Inter IKEA Group as a manufacturer-and-IP-licensor, not a marketing artifact. The build_pct prediction range in the ticket was reasonable for a hypothetical 'IKEA whose retail and supply were integrated under one entity' but did not anticipate how light the residual Inter IKEA Group sell-side is when retail operations are excised.

**Full IKEA value chain sensitivity (combined Inter IKEA + Ingka view).** If we combine Inter IKEA Group and Ingka Group into a 'full IKEA value chain' view (eliminating intercompany wholesale revenue and franchise fees), the picture changes materially. Approximate consolidated economics: total external revenue approximately EUR 47,000M (predominantly Ingka retail to consumers at EUR 41,800M plus Inter IKEA's external revenue to non-Ingka franchisees, IKEA Industry external sales, and IKEA Delft retail; intercompany wholesale and franchise fees between Inter IKEA and Ingka of approximately EUR 22,000M+ are eliminated); combined operating profit approximately EUR 3,600M (Inter IKEA EUR 2,339M plus Ingka EUR 1,300M, with intercompany franchise fees netting to zero); combined operating costs approximately EUR 43,400M. The combined cost structure splits very differently because Ingka adds approximately EUR 13-15B of retail-side sell costs (retail store labor for 166,732 co-workers, store real estate depreciation across approximately 380 owned IKEA stores, local-market advertising and store-level marketing, distribution-to-consumer logistics) plus approximately EUR 1-2B of incremental overhead. Estimated combined build_pct: approximately 60-65% (build of approximately EUR 24-25B comprising Inter IKEA COGS plus build-side opex; sell of approximately EUR 14-16B comprising combined marketing and retail-channel sell-side functions). The 95.6% Inter IKEA standalone build_pct and the estimated ~62% combined-IKEA build_pct differ by approximately 33 percentage points, reflecting the structural choice of where to draw the entity boundary.

**Why the FY24 operating profit (EUR 2,339M) is essentially flat versus FY23 (EUR 2.2B operating profit on EUR 29.1B revenue) despite the EUR 2.6B revenue decline.** The wholesale price reduction of approximately 10% (15% full-year-effect) reduced revenue by approximately EUR 3B at the same wholesale volume level. This was partly offset by volume growth (+4.5% store visitation, +21% online visitation translating into modest physical-unit volume growth despite the cost-of-living squeeze on consumers). The raw material and commodity price decline of -11.4% drove a parallel COGS reduction that allowed gross margin to expand by approximately 2 percentage points (FY24 gross margin 20.7% vs. FY23 ~18.0%), which roughly offset the revenue decline at the gross profit line. Below the gross profit line, the marketing one-off investment lifted opex by an estimated EUR 200-400M, partially offset by good-cost-control reductions elsewhere in opex. Net effect: operating profit roughly flat year-on-year despite the deliberate price reduction strategy. The FY24 results validate the management strategic case that the price reductions improved consumer traffic and volumes enough to defend operating profit while transferring real value to consumers.

**Build/sell classification of marketing spend — left in sell at EUR 1,000M.** Inter IKEA Group does not separately disclose marketing expense as a line item. The marketing/brand spend is triangulated from (a) the explicit FY24 disclosure that 'a significant one-off investment in marketing' was the driver of the operating expense growth — this suggests an incremental EUR 200-400M one-off on top of normalized marketing, (b) the structural role of Inter IKEA Group in funding global brand investment for the IKEA brand while franchisees fund local-market activation, and (c) industry benchmarks for global brand investment as a percentage of revenue at consumer-brand companies (typically 2-6% of revenue, anchored toward the lower end for IKEA which historically benefited from organic brand awareness via the catalog and store experience). The EUR 1,000M estimate carries uncertainty of approximately ±EUR 250M; a different marketing estimate would shift build_pct by 1-2 percentage points but would not change the directional finding that Inter IKEA Group standalone runs structurally high build_pct in the mid-90s.

**Build/sell classification of R&D and range development — moved from operating expenses to build at EUR 400M.** Inter IKEA Group does not separately disclose R&D expense as a line item. The IKEA of Sweden range development and design function, the IKEA Industry plant-level engineering teams, and the central sustainability-range development activity collectively represent the R&D-equivalent cost. Estimated at EUR 400M for FY24 based on the range scope (~9,500 product articles, multiple major range refreshes per year including the BILLY range sustainability redesign and the PAX wardrobe refresh), the IKEA of Sweden headcount and Älmhult facility footprint, and the typical R&D intensity of a furniture/home-furnishings brand (1-2% of revenue is typical; at EUR 26.5B revenue this implies EUR 265-530M, with EUR 400M as the midpoint estimate). Per the methodology spec which classifies R&D as build-side, this EUR 400M is moved from operating expenses to the build line.

**Build/sell classification of supply chain and sourcing management functions — moved from operating expenses to build at EUR 200M.** The central supply chain HQ function managing approximately 800 external suppliers, IKEA Industry capacity planning, distribution center network management, and the IWAY supplier-standards program is conceptually build-side because it directly supports the production-and-distribution economics rather than the sell-to-customer economics. The estimated EUR 200M is approximately 6.3% of total operating expenses, consistent with the importance of supply chain HQ functions for a consumer-goods business of this scale.

**The IKEA franchise fee structure as a structural component of the analysis.** Inter IKEA Systems B.V. (a subsidiary of Inter IKEA Group) collects a 3% franchise fee on net retail sales from all IKEA franchisees including Ingka Group. For FY24, total IKEA franchisee retail sales of EUR 45,100M imply franchise fees of approximately EUR 1,353M paid to Inter IKEA Systems. This franchise fee is a component of Inter IKEA Group's revenue (the remainder being wholesale revenue from selling IKEA products to franchisees, IKEA Industry external B2B sales, and IKEA Delft retail revenue). In the standalone Inter IKEA Group view used here, the franchise fee revenue is included in the EUR 26,500M total revenue without distortion. In the combined IKEA value chain sensitivity, the franchise fee from Ingka to Inter IKEA is an intercompany transaction that nets to zero on consolidation, neither affecting consolidated revenue nor consolidated profit.

**Tangible fixed assets growth from EUR 3,800M to EUR 4,600M reflects approximately EUR 1.1B of capex in FY24 against approximately EUR 300M of depreciation.** The capex investments include the new BILLY bookcase production plant in Slovakia at 35,000 sqm (announced FY24, under construction), the PAX wardrobe production expansion in Hultsfred Sweden, distribution center expansions in multiple geographies, and the pending acquisition of the Baltic States retail operations (currently in regulatory review). The Slovakia BILLY plant is particularly relevant to the BILLY bookcase specifically as it adds dedicated capacity for one of the most volume-significant SKUs in the IKEA range (BILLY production at approximately 6 million units per year globally). Capex appears as depreciation flowing through COGS over time; current-year capex itself is not double-counted in build.

**Sensitivity to the EUR/USD conversion rate.** All dollar figures use 1.085 as the FY24 (September 2023 - August 2024) average EUR/USD rate. A 5% rate variation would shift all dollar values by 5% proportionally and would not change the build_pct percentage. The frontmatter dollar figures should be interpreted with this conversion convention.

**What's surprising in the numbers.**

- **The Inter IKEA Group standalone build_pct of 95.6% is the highest in the consumer panel by a meaningful margin.** This is structural rather than a marketing artifact — Inter IKEA Group does not operate retail stores and does not directly sell to consumers, so it lacks the retail-channel sell-side cost structure that most consumer brands carry. The mismatch with the ticket-predicted 70-85% range reflects that the ticket was anchored on integrated-consumer-brand comparables rather than on the upstream-supply-chain-and-franchisor entity that Inter IKEA Group actually is.

- **The sell-side intensity of 3.8% of revenue is by far the lowest in the consumer panel.** For comparison, Tempur Sealy ran 18.2%, Lululemon approximately 15-16%, Nike Brand 12-13%, Apple iPhone 6-7%. Inter IKEA Group's 3.8% reflects (a) the absence of a retail-channel sell function (which lives at Ingka), and (b) the wholesale-to-franchisee transactions being administered transfers rather than competitive sells requiring traditional sales force investment. The structural lesson: build/sell ratio depends heavily on where in the value chain the analytical entity sits — an upstream supplier and a downstream retailer have fundamentally different cost structures even within the same value chain.

- **Inter IKEA Group's gross margin of 20.7% (FY24) is consistent with a wholesale-distributor business model, not a typical consumer-brand model.** Comparable consumer brands in this panel run gross margins of 30-60% (Tempur Sealy NA 40%, Nike approximately 45%, Apple approximately 45%, Coca-Cola approximately 60%). The 20.7% Inter IKEA gross margin reflects that Inter IKEA Group is essentially a manufacturer and wholesale distributor of IKEA products to the Ingka retail entity (and small franchisees), priced at low single-digit wholesale margin with the overall Inter IKEA Group profitability driven by the 3% franchise fee on top of the wholesale book.

- **The FY24 'significant one-off investment in marketing' was the explicit driver of operating expense growth, despite raw material cost relief.** Inter IKEA Group disclosed this clearly: 'Operating expenses were up in FY24 following a significant investment in marketing to inform customers of the lower prices.' Excluding this one-off, operating expenses would have declined slightly due to cost control. The marketing investment was the strategic complement to the wholesale price reductions — Inter IKEA Group needed to communicate the lower prices to consumers globally to drive the visitation increases (+4.5% physical, +21% online) that translated price cuts into volume growth. The marketing investment effectively converted Inter IKEA Group profit into Ingka traffic, which is consistent with Inter IKEA Group's role as the brand-and-supply-chain entity that benefits from system-wide volume growth even at the cost of near-term Inter IKEA Group margin.

- **The FY24 net profit of EUR 2.2B (up materially from FY23) is driven by a funding-structure change, not by operating improvement.** Inter IKEA Group disclosed that net profit improved 'following a change in the funding structure' which reduced interest payments below the operating line. Operating profit was essentially flat year-on-year. Analysts using the FY24 net profit as a proxy for operating performance would misinterpret the year-on-year trend; this analysis uses operating profit (EUR 2,339M ~ $2,538M) rather than net profit for the profit_millions field in the frontmatter to capture the operating-business performance accurately.

- **The IKEA value chain structurally allocates economic profit between Inter IKEA Group and Ingka Group via the franchise fee and wholesale pricing decisions.** This is a related-party-pricing question with significant historical scrutiny — the European Commission and various tax authorities have examined the Inter IKEA-to-Ingka transfer pricing in past investigations. From a build/sell-ratio standpoint, the analyst's choice of which entity to use as the analytical 'parent' for IKEA has a structurally larger effect than for most other consumer brands in the panel because IKEA's vertical structure is unusually fragmented (most consumer brands either own their retail and consolidate it, like Apple, or franchise/license retail without owning the brand-and-supply entity, like McDonald's). The methodology note above on the full-IKEA value chain sensitivity makes this explicit.

- **The BILLY bookcase as a specific SKU within the IKEA range is approximately 0.5-1.0% of IKEA range revenue.** BILLY produces approximately 6 million units annually globally at an average retail price of approximately $100-150 (varies by variant), implying global BILLY retail revenue of approximately $600-900M (~EUR 550-825M). On the Ingka retail side this is approximately 1.2-1.8% of Ingka revenue; on the Inter IKEA Group side, the wholesale revenue from BILLY is approximately 0.5-0.7% of Inter IKEA total revenue. The BILLY-specific cost structure would inherit the Inter IKEA Group standalone cost structure analyzed here, with BILLY being a heavy-build product (particleboard manufacturing, freight-intensive bulky finished good) that probably runs build_pct above even the Inter IKEA Group average of 95.6% — BILLY-specific build_pct is probably in the 96-97% range, reflecting that this is essentially a manufacturing-and-distribution operation with very modest BILLY-specific marketing (BILLY is mostly a passive seller, driven by the IKEA store experience rather than dedicated BILLY advertising).

FY24 annual report. The major triangulations are (a) the marketing/sell-side estimate of EUR 1,000M (not separately disclosed; estimated from the operating expense composition disclosure and the explicit FY24 one-off marketing investment language), (b) the R&D and supply chain functional cost estimates of EUR 400M and EUR 200M respectively (not separately disclosed; estimated from typical industry intensities and the IKEA of Sweden and Inter IKEA Group functional structure), and (c) the EUR/USD conversion at 1.085 (estimated FY24 average). The medium-confidence rating reflects (i) the necessity to triangulate the opex sub-category breakdown from third-party benchmarks and Inter IKEA Group functional descriptions rather than direct line-item disclosure, (ii) the structural complexity of IKEA's Inter IKEA vs. Ingka split that makes the entity-boundary choice methodologically consequential, and (iii) the entity being privately held (no SEC filings; Dutch GAAP disclosure standards differ from US public-company practice in several respects). The directional finding (Inter IKEA Group standalone build_pct in the mid-90s, sell-side intensity in the low single-digits of revenue, structural anomaly relative to integrated-consumer-brand comparables, combined IKEA value chain sensitivity in the low-to-mid 60s) is robust to the residual estimation uncertainty.
