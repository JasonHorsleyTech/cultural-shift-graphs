# Yuna

## Character Summary
- **Source work:** Final Fantasy X (2001), Square / Square Enix PlayStation 2 JRPG; reprised in Final Fantasy X-2 (2003) and the FFX/X-2 HD Remaster (2013/2014). Yuna is the central female protagonist and love interest of Tidus, the player-character. She is a summoner on a sacrificial pilgrimage to defeat Sin, the kaiju-tier monster terrorizing the world of Spira.
- **Audience:** male-targeted
- **Decade slot:** 2000s
- **Cultural reach proxy:** Final Fantasy X sold ~8.5 million copies on PS2 (one of the best-selling PS2 JRPGs of all time, fastest-selling Final Fantasy entry up to that point per Square Enix sales reports). The FFX/X-2 HD Remaster has shipped a further ~8.5+ million units across PS3/PS4/Vita/PC/Switch/Xbox per Square Enix annual reports. Yuna is consistently top-tier in "best video game heroines" / "best Final Fantasy characters" / "top JRPG waifu" rankings — IGN, GameSpot, Polygon, Famitsu, Den of Geek, and similar outlets place her in the top 5–10 of their respective lists across multiple decades. She has been a recurring playable / featured character in Kingdom Hearts II, Dissidia Final Fantasy, World of Final Fantasy, and as a Theatrhythm representative; also a heavy cosplay subject and the canonical "summoner-priestess" template for subsequent JRPGs.
- **Canonical version used:** Final Fantasy X (2001) Yuna at age 17. The FFX-2 (2003) Yuna at age 19 wears a more revealing gunner outfit and is coded as more upbeat / playful — but the iconic version, the cultural-touchstone version, and the version that anchors Yuna's male-target heartthrob status is the FFX summoner Yuna in the white-and-purple kimono with her staff. The age-mismatch with the project's default 20–28 base population is flagged below.

## Personality / Characterization (Excluded from Calc)

- **Summoner / religious-priestess profession:** Fantasy-exclusive role with no clean real-world universal-desirable equivalent in the male-target trait list. Some male audiences find religious/spiritual women attractive; some find them alienating. Preferences split — characterization, not slider trait. Distinct from the project's "expert in X" achievement-axis traits (which apply to the female-target stack, not male-target).
- **Self-sacrificing / willing-to-die-for-the-mission:** Personality-coded — load-bearing for the male-fantasy projection of "she would do anything for the mission and, by adjacent extension, for me," but not a universal-desirable slider trait. Male audiences split on whether self-sacrificial coding is attractive (some read it as devotion, some read it as self-erasure).
- **Heterochromia / mixed-heritage exoticism (half human / half Al Bhed):** Pure design-aesthetic personality. Some find heterochromia and ethnic-coded heritage attractive; some are neutral. Not a slider trait.
- **Vulnerability / damsel coding:** Mixed — Yuna is rescued at points, but she is also the active protagonist of her own quest and resolves the climactic fight with her own summoning. Per plan, vulnerability flagged as personality and excluded from calc.
- **Devoted-to-protagonist / first-love coding:** The romantic arc with Tidus is the single most cited element of Yuna's male-fantasy appeal. This overlaps with high-Agreeableness and low-conflict coding but is more specifically "she would build her remaining life around the protagonist." Personality-coded at a level beyond the universal-desirable filters. Noted here.
- **Modesty / restraint / quiet voice:** 1990s/2000s JRPG female-lead modesty coding. Personality, not slider — preferences split (some male audiences prefer demure, some prefer assertive).
- **Quasi-Japanese / pan-Asian / hybrid-fantasy aesthetic:** The Spira setting blends Japanese, Okinawan, Polynesian, and Southeast Asian visual elements; Yuna's costume design and ceremonial movements draw from Okinawan / Japanese performance traditions. Aesthetic coding rather than universal-desirable trait.

## Joint Probability

- **Method:** filter (male-target stack)
- **Naive product of marginals:**
  0.20 × 0.22 × 0.34 × 0.70 × 0.10 × 0.25 × 0.25
  = (0.20 × 0.22) = 0.044
  × 0.34 = 0.01496
  × 0.70 = 0.01047
  × 0.10 = 0.001047
  × 0.25 = 2.618e-4
  × 0.25 = **6.55e-5**
  (≈ 1 in 15,300, applied within a base population of US women aged 20–28)
  Note: the body-composition factor (0.22) is the BMI marginal alone, NOT the BMI+DD+ joint rate (0.02) that buxom-coded characters trigger. Yuna's design is petite-modest, not buxom — this is the dominant arithmetic difference between Yuna's stack and CJ Parker's / Lara Croft's stacks.
- **Correlation adjustment:** Mixed-direction adjustments. Pairs:
  - Top-quintile attractiveness ↔ low neuroticism: positive halo (r ≈ 0.10–0.15). Naive product overstates rarity.
  - Top-quintile attractiveness ↔ Cool Girl Agreeableness: positive halo (r ≈ 0.10–0.15). Overstates.
  - Cool Girl Agreeableness ↔ low neuroticism: positive (both load on stability factor; r ≈ 0.20–0.25). Overstates.
  - No-kids ↔ currently single: positive (~1.2x overstatement).
  - Top-quintile attractiveness ↔ currently single: NEGATIVE in real world (more attractive women more likely partnered). Understates by ~1.2–1.4x.
  - Top-quintile attractiveness ↔ doesn't-follow-girl-culture: NEGATIVE in real world, but MILD for Yuna — the spiritual-modest "not girl culture" coding is closer to conventional-femininity-positive than tomboy-coded archetypes are, so the real-world correlation violation is small. Understates by ~1.1–1.2x.
  - **Net direction:** Several positive halos (attractiveness↔low-N, attractiveness↔Agreeableness, low-N↔Agreeableness, no-kids↔single) collectively meaningful. The negative correlations (attractiveness↔single, attractiveness↔not-girly) are fewer and milder for this character because Yuna does NOT violate the negative attractiveness↔girl-culture correlation as flagrantly as a tomboy-mechanic character. Net adjustment: roughly 1.2–1.5x LESS rare than naive product. Notably WITHOUT the high-SOI factor that several other male-target characters carry, Yuna's stack is one of the smaller in this dataset — fewer factors AND milder correlation violations.
- **Adjusted estimate:** ~4.7e-5 (≈ 1 in 21,000 women aged 20–28). Bracket: [2e-5, 1e-4].
- **Population implication:** Out of ~14M US women aged 20–28, roughly **660** match Yuna's universal-desirable stack (range ~140–1,400 across the bracket). Yuna lands at the LESS-rare end of the male-target distribution — substantially less stacked than Mikaela Banes (~24 matching) or CJ Parker (~15 matching) because she lacks the 0.02-tier rarity factor that those characters ride (Mikaela's car expertise, CJ's BMI+DD+ joint). Yuna's stack is "wholesome demure devoted first-love" rather than "hot-girl-with-rare-skill" or "buxom-blonde-fantasy" — the male fantasy here is calibrated on agreeableness, modesty, and emotional commitment more than on stacked rare desirable traits. This is a genre-coherent finding: JRPG female leads in the early-2000s lineage are commonly stacked LESS than Western live-action heartthrobs of the same era, and Yuna anchors that less-stacked end.

## Notes / Edge Cases

- **Age mismatch with base population:** Yuna is canonically 17 in FFX and 19 in FFX-2, both BELOW the project's default 20–28 male-target base-population window. The male-fantasy projection treats her as a young adult heartthrob — and the in-game society treats her as a fully-functioning adult professional (high summoner, deferred-to-by-elders, marriage-eligible) — but the literal age is below the base-population window. For methodological consistency I retained the 20–28 base population (the project default) and flagged this as a confidence caveat. A reviewer who insisted on a younger base population (e.g. 16–22) would slightly adjust the population implication but not the joint-probability ratio. The age discrepancy is real but does not change the rank-order finding.
- **Canonical version choice (FFX vs FFX-2):** I selected FFX summoner Yuna (the cultural-touchstone version) over FFX-2 gunner Yuna. The FFX-2 version is more revealing in costume and more upbeat in personality, which would slightly modulate some traits (low-neuroticism less load-bearing; "doesn't follow girl culture" reading less clean given FFX-2's pop-idol J-Pop scenes). The joint probability would not move substantially across versions; the ranking is robust.
- **Yuna as the LOW-stack anchor of the 2000s male-target distribution:** Yuna's adjusted joint of ~5e-5 places her substantially less rare than Mikaela Banes (~1.5e-6) and CJ Parker (~1.1e-6) — by roughly 1.5 orders of magnitude. This is not a flaw in the analysis; it is the finding. Yuna's male-fantasy projection rides on agreeableness, devotion, modesty, emotional stability, and first-love commitment, NOT on stacked rare-skill or extreme-body-composition traits. JRPG / anime-adjacent female leads of this era frequently stack LESS aggressively on universal-desirable axes than Western live-action contemporaries. Within the project's symmetric framework, this means Yuna is less of a "stacked fantasy" character on universal desirables — but the SAME male audience may project as much fantasy onto Yuna as onto a more-stacked character via the personality / characterization axis (devotion, sacrifice, first-love coding) which the project deliberately excludes from quantification. Flagged for the final graph annotations: Yuna should NOT be read as "less of a fantasy" — she is differently-coded, with the fantasy load-bearing on excluded personality coding. This is one of the cleanest examples in the dataset of the universal-vs-personality split being load-bearing for cross-character comparison.
- **The "cool girl" reading in 2001 Japanese context:** "Top-decile Agreeableness" is a within-population statistical descriptor, and Yuna's dutiful-summoner archetype in 2001 Japanese cultural-coding context maps cleanly to it within the gender-and-culture-norming the Big 5 literature uses. A reviewer who argues that Japanese cultural-norms shift the Agreeableness baseline (yamato nadeshiko archetype norms) and therefore Yuna's coding is closer to median than top-decile in-context could pull this filter from 0.10 to ~0.20 — which would relax the joint estimate by ~2x to ~1e-4. I retained 0.10 because the project measures in-genre stacking against US Big 5 norms (the project's stated methodology), not within-Japanese-cultural-context norms. Flagged.
- **Tidus's blitzball hobby NOT included:** Per the methodology I excluded the "plays protagonist's hobbies" filter because the alignment direction in FFX runs Tidus-aligns-with-Yuna's-pilgrimage rather than Yuna-aligns-with-Tidus's-blitzball-career. A reviewer who reads "shares the protagonist's adventure" generically as the aligned-interest filter would add a 0.20–0.30 multiplier and pull the joint to ~1.4e-5, still less rare than CJ or Mikaela. Excluded here on the grounds of avoiding double-counting the show premise (consistent with how CJ Parker's lifeguarding was treated).
- **Body-composition decision (BMI marginal only):** Yuna's design is petite-modest, NOT buxom-with-slim-waist. The summoner kimono in FFX is high-coverage; the character is not staged for bust emphasis. This is the single most consequential arithmetic decision separating Yuna's stack from CJ Parker's / Lara Croft's stacks (0.22 marginal vs. 0.02 joint = ~10x difference in the body-composition factor alone). I am confident in the marginal-only call; a reviewer arguing for the joint would need to introduce a DD+ implication that the design does not support.
