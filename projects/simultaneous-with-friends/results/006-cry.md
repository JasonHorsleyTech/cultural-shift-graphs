## Setup

Crying is one of the most private human acts. Adults rarely cry in public, which means nearly all episodes cluster into the same narrow window: evenings at home, late nights, quiet moments after the day's defenses come down. That clustering is both intuitively obvious and mathematically significant — it compresses the effective window where overlaps can happen, making simultaneous crying with a friend rarer than raw frequency alone would suggest.

## Inputs

**Frequency:**
- Women: 30–64 times/year (mid-estimate ~47/year → ~3.9/month → ~0.129/day)
- Men: 5–17 times/year (mid-estimate ~11/year → ~0.92/month → ~0.030/day)
- Weighted average (50:50 gender split): (0.129 + 0.030) / 2 ≈ **0.080/day**

**Duration:**
- Average episode: ~8 minutes (research reports ~7.7 min women, ~3.9 min men; the cited study mean is 8 min)
- Used: **480 seconds**

**Clustering:**
- Strong evening/night bias — emotional processing clusters when daytime distractions cease
- Effective active window: **6 hours** (roughly 6pm–midnight)
- The `(24/6) = 4×` correction scales active-window math back to wall-clock time

**Caveats:**
- Massive gender variance (women cry ~4× more than men by most estimates)
- Cultural variance is large: collectivist cultures suppress more; some Western samples show higher rates
- Age variance: young adults and older adults cry more than middle-aged; teenagers peak
- Self-report bias: men likely underreport; women may overreport in some social contexts
- The 50:50 gender split assumption is a simplification — real friend groups vary

## Math

```
r = f_day / (h × 3600)
r = 0.080 / (6 × 3600) = 3.704 × 10⁻⁶ per second

t_overlap = (24 / h) × 1 / (2 × r² × d × N)

t_50 = (24/6) / (2 × (3.704e-6)² × 480 × 50)
     = 4 / (2 × 1.372e-11 × 24000)
     = 4 / 6.585e-7
     ≈ 6.075 × 10⁶ seconds  (~70 days)

t_1  = (24/6) / (2 × (3.704e-6)² × 480 × 1)
     = 4 / 1.317e-8
     ≈ 3.038 × 10⁸ seconds  (~9.6 years)
```

## Result

- **With any of your 50 close friends:** simultaneous crying expected roughly **every 70 days** (~10 times a year)
- **With your single best friend:** simultaneous crying expected roughly **every 9.6 years**

## Surprise check

The 70-day figure for 50 friends feels about right intuitively — you have 50 people, crying is uncommon but not rare, and the narrow evening window concentrates the overlap opportunity. The best-friend number (~10 years) is the striking one: even with someone you're emotionally close to, both of you being in tears at the same moment happens barely a handful of times across a long friendship. That feels emotionally true. You may *talk* about having cried. You almost never cry simultaneously.
