## Methodology notes

**Retailer-vs-manufacturer view (critical caveat).** This result measures Costco-as-retailer, not the economics of whoever manufactures Kirkland items. Costco's co-manufacturing model means Kirkland products are produced by third-party manufacturers — often the same factories making national brands (e.g., Kirkland vodka co-distilled by Cognac Ferrand, the same distillery behind Grey Goose; Kirkland olive oil from major Mediterranean producers). Those manufacturer costs are invisible in Costco's 10-K. What appears on Costco's books is the transfer price Costco pays to the co-packer, and the co-packer's own COGS, gross margin, and (minimal) marketing costs are embedded in that transfer price. Costco's build_pct of 98.3% reflects Costco-as-retailer spending nearly everything on goods procurement and essentially nothing on advertising. The co-packer's own cost structure — which might resemble a typical CPG company (build_pct 70–80%) — is opaque from this vantage point.

**Segment selection.** Costco operates a single reportable segment (warehouse club operations worldwide), so the consolidated income statement is the only available proxy. Kirkland Signature represents approximately 30–34% of Costco merchandise sales — trade press estimates for FY2024 put Kirkland at ~$86B in sales out of $249.6B total net sales. Kirkland is not separately broken out in any segment reporting; per the ticket instructions, consolidated Costco is the best available proxy.

**COGS sub-allocation.** Costco's 10-K includes "buying and occupancy costs" within COGS alongside merchandise costs but does not separately disclose the buying/occupancy split. The total buying-and-occupancy component is estimated at ~$5,358M, derived as: if average Costco merchandise markup is ~13% (consistent with their known policy of capping national brands at 14% and Kirkland at 15%), implied supplier cost is ~$217B, meaning residual buying + occupancy is ~$5B. This is split into: (a) depot distribution + buying costs (~$4,858M, BUILD — Costco's supply-chain and procurement operating costs); and (b) warehouse real estate occupancy (~$3,500M, SELL per rubric). Costco does not disclose this sub-split; the ~$3,500M occupancy estimate is derived from first-principles (~890 warehouses averaging $3–4M/year in rent, building D&A, and net occupancy costs). Plausible range: $2,000–5,000M depending on owned/leased mix assumptions.

**Warehouse real estate as "sell."** The ticket instructs that warehouses default to "sell" per the project rubric (storefronts). This is a gray zone: Costco's warehouses are also the distribution channel and are deliberately utilitarian (bare concrete, bulk racking, no retail ambiance). However, they are storefronts visited by members, and the real estate footprint is a strategic asset enabling the business model. Per rubric, warehouse occupancy is classified sell.

**Advertising — confirmed near-zero.** Costco's 10-K confirms advertising expense is immaterial. MediaRadar estimates Costco's total measured advertising at $100–200M annually — roughly 0.04–0.08% of revenue. For context, a typical CPG company spends 8–15% of revenue on advertising; Costco's ratio is 100–200× lower. The membership model substitutes: 73M cardholders as of FY2024 are a captive audience reachable via the Costco Connection magazine, weekly mailer booklets, and app notifications. Those member-communication costs (~$200M estimated) are classified as sell, not overhead, since they directly substitute for external advertising.

**Membership fees and the Costco operating model.** Total operating income of $9,296M is heavily subsidized by membership fees of $4,828M, which flow almost entirely to profit (operating costs of the membership program are negligible). Merchandise operations themselves generate only ~$4,500M in operating income on $249.6B in net merchandise sales — a 1.8% merchandise operating margin. This is the structural logic of the Costco model: sell goods at near-cost to drive member value and renewal, then clip the annual fee. The 98.3% build_pct reflects this directly — Costco spends almost all its money buying and moving goods, not marketing them.

**SG&A classification.** Costco's SG&A of $22,810M is primarily warehouse employee wages and benefits. Costco employs ~310,000 people globally, the vast majority in warehouses, at among the highest retail wages in the US (~$25–30/hr average). Estimated total warehouse + corporate labor: ~$18B. These are classified as overhead: they are operational staff executing the retail service, not a sales force in the plan.md sense (no door-to-door selling, no trade promotion negotiation with retailers, no dealer incentive programs). Non-advertising corporate expenses (~$4.5B) are also overhead.

**Kirkland margin structure (for context).** Trade press and earnings call commentary note that Kirkland items carry slightly higher gross margins for Costco than national brands (~15% Kirkland cap vs. ~14% national brand cap), and that growing Kirkland penetration creates a "tailwind in overall margins" (CFO commentary, Q4 FY2024 earnings call). The Kirkland label also functions as a loyalty and membership-renewal driver — members come for Kirkland quality and stay for the membership value. This is the marketing function that the brand serves, without any advertising spend.

**Confidence: medium.** Costco is US-listed with clean, audited public financials — the consolidated income statement and operating income are directly reported and reliable. The build/sell classification is straightforward for a near-zero advertising company. Confidence is capped at medium because: (1) the sub-allocation within COGS (merchandise vs. buying vs. occupancy) requires estimation since Costco does not publish the breakdown; (2) the result measures consolidated Costco-as-retailer, not Kirkland product economics; and (3) the co-packer side of the Kirkland cost structure is invisible.
