## Methodology notes

**Segment selection.** Used P&G's Fabric & Home Care reportable segment as the cleanest proxy for Tide's economics. The segment is dominated by Tide (the largest single brand in the segment by a wide margin) plus Downy, Gain, Dawn, Cascade, Mr. Clean, Febreze, Swiffer. Segment net sales of $29,495M and segment earnings before income taxes of $7,339M are reported directly in Note 2 Segment Information of the FY2024 10-K. Segment EBT for F&H rose from $6,303M (FY23) to $7,339M (FY24); the FY24 indefinite-lived intangible impairment charge of $1,341M was booked in Corporate (Corporate EBT swung from $(1,147)M to $(2,422)M, roughly the size of the impairment), not in the F&H segment, so segment profit is clean of one-time write-downs.

**Parent-level cost allocation.** P&G discloses advertising ($9.6B), R&D ($2.0B), COGS ($40,848M), and SG&A ($23,305M) at the consolidated level only. Allocated to the F&H segment via segment revenue share (29,495 / 84,039 = 35.1%). This is the standard plan.md fallback when brand-level COGS is not disclosed.

**Advertising allocation (key uncertainty).** P&G's $9.6B FY24 ad spend was up from $8.0B in FY23 — a +20% step-up across the company. Tide and other F&H brands are heavy advertisers (Tide consistently ranks in the top 20 US measured-media advertisers per Kantar/Ad Age tables), so pro-rata likely **understates** F&H's true ad share. Did not pull Kantar's FY24 brand-level table directly; if Kantar's Tide-only number plus reasonable estimates for sibling brands (Downy, Dawn, Cascade, Gain) materially exceeds $3,370M, the sell side is understated and the build_pct number reported here is too high. Flagging this rather than guessing further.

**SG&A residual classification.** After subtracting the disclosed advertising ($9.6B) and R&D ($2.0B) from total SG&A ($23,305M), $11,705M remains. Per P&G's 10-K language, this residual contains: non-advertising marketing, selling expenses, other administrative expenses, D&A on non-manufacturing assets, and misc operating items. Split 50/50 between "sell-like" (selling, trade promo, non-advertising marketing) and "overhead-like" (admin, finance, legal, IR, non-mfg D&A) — a deliberately middle-of-the-road choice. CPG analysts typically peg the sell-like share between 40% and 60%, and a different split inside that band would shift build_pct by ±2 percentage points.

**Trade promotion netting.** Per CPG accounting standards, trade promotion and slotting fees are typically netted against revenue rather than expensed in SG&A. So both segment revenue ($29,495M) and the sell-side total here understate gross-of-promo figures consistently — does not move the ratio meaningfully.

**Distribution classification.** Per the FY2024 10-K language ("Cost of products sold also includes the cost to distribute products to customers"), distribution costs are inside COGS — counted as build, consistent with the plan.md rubric.

**Customer support.** A CPG product like Tide has minimal customer-support cost (basic 800-number, online help, occasional sample). Set to ~0 / folded into other COGS items.

**Confidence: medium.** Segment revenue and segment EBT are direct from a US 10-K (high confidence). The build/sell split inside the segment requires (a) pro-rating consolidated advertising, R&D, COGS, and SG&A by segment revenue share, and (b) splitting the SG&A residual into sell vs. overhead. Both steps are defensible and conventional, but they are estimates not disclosures. Calling this "high" would be overclaiming.
