# Jamie Fraser

## Character Summary
- **Source work:** Outlander novel series by Diana Gabaldon, *Outlander* (1991, originally titled *Cross Stitch* in the UK), and the subsequent eight novels (1991–2021). Adapted as the Starz/Sony Pictures Television series *Outlander* (2014–2026, eight seasons), with Sam Heughan as Jamie Fraser ("James Alexander Malcolm MacKenzie Fraser," Laird Broch Tuarach). Canonical text is the Gabaldon novels; the Starz adaptation supplies the dominant 2010s visual portrayal.
- **Audience:** female-targeted
- **Decade slot:** 2010s (the TV adaptation, beginning 2014, is the cultural reach driver and the locked decade slot — even though the source novel is 1991, Jamie's mass cultural footprint is firmly the 2014+ Starz era).
- **Cultural reach proxy:** Gabaldon's Outlander novels have sold approximately 50M copies worldwide across the nine main books and translations into 38 languages (Penguin Random House publisher data + Wikipedia compilation). The Starz TV series is one of the top-three drama franchises in the network's history; eight seasons spanning 2014–2026, with consistent multi-million-viewer linear ratings and very high streaming engagement (Variety, Deadline reports). Sam Heughan's Jamie Fraser appears on essentially every "best book boyfriend" / "best TV romance lead" list of the 2010s and 2020s (Goodreads, Cosmopolitan, BuzzFeed, Glamour, Vulture, Marie Claire), frequently in the top five and often #1 in book-romance-specific rankings.
- **Canonical version used:** Gabaldon novels primary canon (height, languages, education, lineage, age, family). Starz TV adaptation as visual canon (Sam Heughan portrayal). Where novels and TV agree (warrior, polyglot, laird, university-educated, family-unencumbered orphan), the trait is treated as fully canonical. The "canonical romantic-lead moment" is taken as Jamie's age in book 1 / season 1 (1743 timeline, Jamie aged ~22–23) rather than the older Jamie of later books — this is the iconic version, and it is the version that drove the cultural-reach numbers.

Traits considered and excluded:

- **Wealth ≥ $10M/yr or billionaire-tier:** Excluded. Jamie is "Laird Broch Tuarach" — owner of Lallybroch, a working Highland estate of approximately 60 tenant families. In 1740s Scottish-Highland economic terms this is upper-tier landed gentry / minor laird, comparable to a small American family farm-and-estate operation in modern terms (~$5–20M asset value, modest annual income). This does NOT clear the $10M/yr threshold and is structurally below the Bridgerton-Simon (Duke), Mr. Darcy (£10K/yr ≈ multi-billion modern), or Edward Cullen (Cullen-family $34B) wealth tiers used elsewhere in this dataset. Jamie's romance is decisively NOT built on wealth-stacking. The "laird" status is folded into characterization (he has an estate, he's a community leader) rather than counted as a slider-maxing trait. See Notes / Edge Cases.

- **Age in prime window (28–38):** Excluded at the canonical romantic-lead moment. Jamie is born 1 May 1721; book 1 / season 1 starts 1743, making him approximately 22–23 at the canonical romance moment. He does enter the 28–38 window in the middle books (Voyager / Drums of Autumn), but the iconic romantic-lead version is the young Highland warrior, not the older laird. Counting age would require choosing the older version, which would lower the cultural-reach justification for the locked decade slot. Conservative honest call: exclude age. (Note: this is the OPPOSITE direction from Aragorn's age exclusion — Aragorn is *too old* for the prime window, Jamie is *too young*. Both miss the strict 28–38 band.)

- **Musical/artistic skill:** Jamie is musically illiterate ("tone-deaf" is canon — multiple book references to Claire teasing him about his inability to carry a tune). Hard exclusion. The character explicitly does NOT have this trait.

- **Pilot, chef, surgeon, mechanic, virtuoso musician, professional dancer, professional artist, professional smith:** None apply. Jamie is a printer (in the Voyager/Edinburgh-printshop arc, post-canonical-romantic-moment), but printing is a tradesman skill, not "expert tier" in the surgeon/pilot/virtuoso-musician sense the project uses. He runs an estate (managerial), hunts (Highland-survival default), and farms (laird's-tenant default) — these are folded into his Highland-warrior / laird default skill profile, not stacked as separate mastery traits.

- **Faith / Catholicism:** Quiet but present; characterization, not slider.

## Joint Probability

- **Method:** threshold (female-target stack)
- **Naive product of marginals:**
  0.04 × 0.02 × 0.20 × 1e-3 × 5e-3 × 0.05
  = 0.04 × 0.02 = 8e-4
  × 0.20 = 1.6e-4
  × 1e-3 = 1.6e-7
  × 5e-3 = 8e-10
  × 0.05 = 4e-11
  ≈ **4e-11 (≈ 1 in 25 billion)**
- **Correlation adjustment:**
  - **Combat × physical-fitness top quintile (positively correlated, pulls less rare).** P(top-quintile fitness | special operations / master combatant) ≈ 0.95 per base-rates.md. Naive product OVERSTATES rarity by ~4–5x for this pair. Adjustment: **~4x less rare** on this pair.
  - **Cognitive halo (intelligence × multilingualism, positively correlated, pulls less rare).** Multilingualism and high-IQ load on overlapping general-cognitive-ability factors (r ≈ 0.20–0.30 per base-rates.md and Schmidt-Hunter literature). Jamie's University-of-Paris-educated polyglot scholar profile sits in the dense end of this halo cluster. Adjustment: **~2–3x less rare** than naive on the IQ × languages joint.
  - **Time-budget penalty for skill-stacking (combat-expert + multilingual scholar, mildly NEGATIVELY correlated, pulls slightly rarer).** Jamie has only ONE expert-tier skill (combat) plus the multilingual + scholar cluster. Per base-rates.md, the time-budget penalty bites hard at "stacks with 3+ expert-skill traits"; Jamie's stack has only 1, so the penalty is mild rather than dominant. A multilingual-scholar profile alongside a master-warrior life is plausible for an upper-class 18th-century male (university education + martial training were both expected), so the penalty is much weaker than it would be for, say, a billionaire-virtuoso-pianist-pilot stack. Adjustment: **~1.5–2x rarer than naive** on the combat × scholarship pair.
  - **Family-unencumbered ↔ all others:** approximately independent to first order. **No adjustment.**
  - **Net direction:** The two positive-correlation adjustments (combat × fitness, IQ × languages) collectively pull ~8–12x less rare than naive. The single mild negative-correlation adjustment (time-budget penalty for combat-scholar) pulls ~1.5–2x rarer. Net: **~5–6x less rare than naive product** — i.e. positive halos dominate the time-budget penalty because Jamie does NOT have the 3+ expert-skill stack that triggers severe time-budget bite. This is a meaningful structural difference from the Aragorn / Edward Cullen / Christian Grey tier.
- **Adjusted estimate:** ~**2e-10** (≈ 1 in 5 billion). Bracket: [5e-11, 1e-9].
- **Population implication:** Out of ~8 billion humans alive on Earth today, expected matches ≈ **1–2** — i.e. somewhere between zero and a handful of men currently alive worldwide match Jamie's full universal-desirable trait stack on the trait list (height + IQ + fitness + master combat + 3+ non-native languages + orphan/unencumbered). Restricted to the US adult-male population (~127M), expected matches ≈ **0.025**, i.e. roughly 1 in 40 Americas would contain one. Compared to other female-target heartthrobs in this dataset: Jamie is **rarer than Mr. Darcy** (~1e-5) by ~5 orders of magnitude (Darcy concentrates on wealth + status; Jamie distributes across height + skill + multilingual scholar). Jamie is **comparable in rarity to Han Solo** (~1e-10) — both are competent-badass profiles built on warrior skill + intelligence + physical attributes without billionaire-tier wealth-stacking. Jamie is **dramatically less rare than Aragorn** (~4e-20) by ~10 orders of magnitude — Aragorn adds billionaire-tier wealth + healing-expert + intelligence + multiple languages + everything Jamie has. Jamie is **dramatically less rare than Edward Cullen, Christian Grey, and Rhysand** for the same reason: he doesn't slider-max on wealth, doesn't have a virtuoso-music or supernatural-immortal stack, and isn't a 3+ expert-skill polymath. Jamie's trait stack is the most "physically achievable" of the female-target high-romance leads in this dataset.

## Confidence Assessment
- **Overall:** medium-high — trait identification is well-supported by Gabaldon's nine novels and the Starz adaptation, with little ambiguity on the universal-desirable axis. The trait stack is shorter than the project benchmarks (Edward, Christian Grey, Aragorn) and the headline number is correspondingly less extreme.
- **Trait identification:** high — every counted trait is robustly canonical in Gabaldon's text (height per multiple Claire POV references, languages per the Voyager / Drums-of-Autumn educated-laird scenes, combat per the entire series, family per book 1 backstory, intelligence per the University of Paris arc and ongoing strategic competence). The Starz adaptation preserves all of these without alteration, and Sam Heughan's casting (6'3", solidly built, multilingual-trained for the role) closely matches book canon. The closest call is age-window (excluded as too young at the canonical romantic-lead moment) — a reviewer who chose the older Jamie of later books could include age and shift the joint by ~10x rarer to ~2e-11.
- **Base rates:** high — height (NHANES), languages (Census ACS with joint-distribution adjustment), fitness (NHANES), family-unencumbered (composite Pew + Cornell + CDC), combat-expert (1e-3, established in this project's base-rates.md), and IQ (standard normal) are all from established base-rates.md entries with strong provenance. No new base-rate entries were required for this character.
- **Correlation adjustments:** medium-high — directional reasoning is well-supported by base-rates.md notes (combat × fitness positive ~4–5x, IQ × languages positive ~2–3x, time-budget penalty for skill stacking only mild at 1 expert skill). The net 5–6x adjustment toward "less rare than naive product" is anchored to the equivalent calculations for Han Solo and is structurally consistent with the project's calibration of "warrior + scholar but not slider-maxed" profiles.

## Notes / Edge Cases

- **Decade-slot reasoning.** The Outlander novel was published in 1991, but Jamie's mass-cultural footprint is firmly the 2014+ Starz era — that is when "Jamie Fraser" became a household name and a recurring presence on "best book boyfriend" lists, not when the book first appeared. The locked 2010s slot reflects cultural reach at peak, which is the right framing for this dataset's stratification logic. (A reviewer who slotted Jamie to the 1990s based on novel publication date would be technically defensible but would lose the "actual cultural moment" framing that the validation methodology is built on.)

- **Wealth: Jamie is NOT a billionaire-tier love interest, and that is the most important calibration call for this character.** Lallybroch is a working Highland estate of ~60 tenant families. In 1740s Scotland-Highland economic terms this is upper-tier landed gentry — comfortable, not poor, but nowhere near peer-of-the-realm or Lowland-aristocrat wealth. Translated to modern terms via standard land-and-livestock comparators, this is roughly $5–20M in asset value with modest annual income. It does NOT clear the $10M/yr threshold used elsewhere in this project (Bridgerton-Simon: Duke = billionaire-tier; Mr. Darcy: £10K/yr = multi-billion modern; Edward Cullen: Cullen family $34B). Counting Jamie at billionaire-tier (3e-6) would lower the joint by ~7 orders of magnitude to ~3e-17 and put him near Edward Cullen / Aragorn — which would be a meaningful misrepresentation of how the romance is actually constructed. Jamie's appeal is built on warrior + scholar + devotion + body, NOT on slider-maxed wealth. The honest exclusion of wealth is the load-bearing methodology call for this character. (A reviewer who insisted on counting "laird with an estate" at, say, the $10M/yr tier would shift the joint to ~8e-15 — meaningful but still 2 orders of magnitude milder than treating him as billionaire-tier.)

- **Age at canonical romantic moment.** Jamie is ~22–23 in book 1 / season 1 (1743 timeline, born 1 May 1721). The plan's "prime window" is 28–38 — Jamie misses by 5–6 years on the young side. He does enter the window mid-series (Voyager onward, post-1764), but the iconic romantic-lead version is the young Highland warrior the cultural-reach numbers are built on. Excluding age is the conservative honest call. Counting age would require either (a) using the older Jamie at the cost of the cultural-reach justification, or (b) using the young Jamie at the cost of the strict threshold definition. Same structural issue Aragorn faces in the opposite direction (too old).

- **Languages — softness and joint-distribution treatment.** Jamie is solidly 3+ non-native (French + Latin + Greek). Generous counts add German and Italian. The 5e-3 rate from base-rates.md applies to "fluent in 2 non-native languages"; per the joint-distribution note in that entry, 3+ should use ~5e-3 conservatively (the multilingual-correlation halo means each additional language adds less rarity than naive multiplication suggests). A stricter reading at 4+ languages would drop the rate to ~1e-3 and shift the joint by 5x rarer; the conservative 3+ rate is used to match the convention applied to Han Solo, Edward Cullen, and Aragorn.

- **Tone-deafness as a genuine NON-trait.** Most of the female-target leads in this dataset either have a virtuoso-musician trait stacked or have music as a non-counted personality beat (e.g. Aragorn singing in Elvish). Jamie is one of the few canonically-explicit cases of a love interest who is *bad* at music — Claire teases him about his inability to carry a tune across multiple books. This is structurally meaningful: it's evidence that Gabaldon was deliberately NOT slider-maxing on the artistic-virtuoso axis. The character is built to a different romantic logic (Highland-warrior + scholar + devotion) rather than to the every-skill-maxed Christian-Grey pattern. Worth flagging in the eventual graph annotations as a counterexample to the "all female-target heartthrobs slider-max everything" reading.

- **Combat-expertise calibration.** I set this at 1e-3 to match the pilot/mechanic/chef expert-tier convention used elsewhere in this project. A stricter "renowned/legendary combatant" tier (1e-4) is defensible — Jamie is canonically a top-tier Highland swordsman, served as a Jacobite officer at all three major battles (Prestonpans, Falkirk, Culloden), and is often described in the books as the best swordsman in the room. But his combat profile is "best of the local Highland warrior cohort" rather than "world-historical legend" — closer to Han Solo's "best pilot in the galaxy softened to 1e-3" calibration than to a true 1e-4 legendary tier. Conservative 1e-3 is the right call for parity.

- **Personality dominance — the Jamie-Fraser case in particular.** Jamie's cultural pull is overwhelmingly personality-driven: the "I will find you" devotion oath, the Sassenach loyalty, the Catholic-virgin-with-a-warrior-heart paradox, the wounded-warrior post-Wentworth arc, the whole "honor + tenderness + ferocity" alloy. The trait stack alone substantially underrepresents the character's romantic effectiveness. Survey data and fan-discourse coding would put Jamie at the very top of the female-target attraction distribution, but his joint-probability number (~2e-10) places him in the same tier as Han Solo — comparatively *moderate* among this project's leads. This is a meaningful methodology finding: when the calc puts a famously beloved heartthrob in a mid-tier rarity bucket, it's evidence that the *personality / characterization* axis (excluded by design) is doing more work for that character than the *universal-desirable* axis. Jamie is the cleanest illustration of this in the dataset so far. Worth flagging in the final graph annotations.

- **Comparison to other female-target leads in this dataset:**
  - **Mr. Darcy (~1e-5):** Jamie is ~5 orders of magnitude rarer in the universal-desirable calc. Both are landed-gentry warrior-scholar profiles; the gap comes from Jamie being TALLER (height trait), more PHYSICALLY ELITE (fitness trait), an EXPERT WARRIOR (combat trait), and SOLIDLY MULTILINGUAL (Darcy is implied educated but not multilingual in canon). Darcy's stack is concentrated in wealth/status; Jamie's is distributed across body + skill + scholarship.
  - **Han Solo (~1e-10):** Jamie is roughly comparable in joint rarity. Both are competent-badass profiles: pilot/mechanic skill expert (Han) vs. master combatant (Jamie); strategist (both); multilingual (both); physically fit (both); family-unencumbered (both). The match is structural rather than thematic — Han is a sci-fi rogue, Jamie is a historical Highland warrior, but the trait-stack architecture is nearly identical.
  - **Aragorn (~4e-20):** Jamie is ~10 orders of magnitude LESS rare. Aragorn adds billionaire-tier inherited wealth, healing-expert skill, and Numenorean-longevity-enabled time-budget headroom on top of essentially Jamie's stack. The gap is almost entirely on the wealth + healing-skill axes plus the longevity framing.
  - **Edward Cullen (~1e-18):** Jamie is ~8 orders of magnitude LESS rare. Edward stacks billionaire family wealth + virtuoso pianist + 5+ languages + multiple academic degrees + supernatural perfection on top of comparable physical/scholarly traits. Jamie is decidedly NOT in this slider-maxed tier.
  - **Christian Grey (~1e-24, project benchmark):** Jamie is ~14 orders of magnitude LESS rare. Christian's seven-mastery-skill stack is the project's high-water mark; Jamie has one expert skill (combat) and a multilingual-scholar overlay.
  - **Rhysand (Court of Thorns and Roses, ticket 021):** Jamie is dramatically less rare; Rhysand stacks fae-immortal supernatural traits on top of ruler-of-realm wealth + multiple skill mastery.
  - **Within the 2010s decade specifically:** Jamie is the moderate-rarity entry compared to the supernatural / billionaire / fae / immortal stacks (Rhysand, Bridgerton-Simon, Christian Grey if also slotted to 2010s). This is a useful within-decade signal: the 2010s female-target heartthrob field has a long tail of supernatural-or-billionaire slider-maxers AND a "physically achievable" anchor in Jamie. Both can be commercially huge; the trait-stack architecture differs.

- **Potential gendered axe-grind risk flag.** Jamie's number (~2e-10) is moderate by this project's female-target distribution. Including him in the dataset is important specifically *because* his stack is moderate — it provides a counterweight to the "all female-target heartthrobs are billion-times-more-improbable than male-target heartthrobs" reading the more extreme stacks (Aragorn, Edward, Christian) might encourage in isolation. The honest framing for the eventual graph: Jamie shows that a female-target romantic lead can be wildly successful commercially without requiring billionaire wealth or supernatural framing — the appeal can be carried by warrior + scholar + body + devotion alone. This is structurally similar to what Han Solo demonstrates on the male-target side. Both characters anchor the "competent-badass without slider-maxing" mid-zone of their respective distributions.
