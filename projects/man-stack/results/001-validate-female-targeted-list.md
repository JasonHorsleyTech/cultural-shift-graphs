# Validation: Female-Targeted Heartthrob List

**Ticket:** 001
**Type:** Validation (not a character profile)
**Audience:** female-targeted (male leads in female-targeted media)
**Scope:** the 25 locked characters in `heartthrobs.md`

## Methodology

Aggregated mentions and qualitative evidence from accessible English-language sources via web search:

1. Goodreads "Best Book Boyfriends" master list (10,803 books, 29,142 votes) and topic threads under the BookTok / Just BookTok Stuff group
2. Goodreads "All round best book boyfriends" community list and shelves (`best-book-boyfriends`, `best-boyfriend`)
3. Empire magazine's "100 Greatest Movie Characters" reader poll
4. Empire's "60 Best Romantic Movies" feature (with romantic-lead callouts)
5. WatchMojo "Top 10 Male Movie Characters You Want to Date" / "Top 10 Unforgettable Movie Couples of the 2000s"
6. Collider "10 Best Teen Heartthrobs of the 2000s, Ranked" and related "rom-com kings" features
7. HuffPost "21 Best Movie Boyfriends in Romantic Comedy History"
8. MovieWeb "15 Best Leading Men in Rom-Coms, Ranked"
9. Brit + Co "9 Movie Boyfriends Who Gave Us Impossible Standards"
10. Grazia "Literature's Great Romantic Heroes Ranked"
11. Daily Beast "Is Christian Grey Our New Mr. Darcy?" (and surrounding cultural-critique pieces)
12. Bridgerton couple/book ranking aggregations (Maude's Book Club, Hunch, Every Girl, Lit Bitch, Reflections by Krashid)
13. BookTok-specific: lemon8, Ruby Dark Rose's "most popular BookTok book boyfriends"
14. Cosmo / Brit+Co / Odyssey "movie boyfriends better than your significant other" type listicles
15. Wikipedia / fandom wikis used for sales, viewership, and box-office cross-validation only (not for fame ranking)

**Aggregation rule:** "appears in multiple aggregated rankings as a top-N entrant or is cited as the canonical example of its genre/decade." Counts are coarse (low / medium / high / very high) rather than numeric — published list scoring varies widely in scope (top-10 vs. top-100), so a strict frequency count would be false precision. Reach proxies (book/movie/streaming numbers) are pulled from Wikipedia and the Variety/Hollywood Reporter ecosystem.

**Cap:** one ticket's worth of work. Sources behind paywalls (full Vulture and Cosmopolitan archives, Empire print archive, complete RWA RITA/Vivian winner database) were not exhaustively crawled. Where a character had clear top-tier signal across 3+ free sources, additional confirmation was deemed unnecessary.

## Swap Considerations (recommendations to Jason; do NOT execute)

### 1. Bridgerton 2020s slot — Anthony Bridgerton vs. Simon Basset

The locked list flags this as a known judgment call ("pick whichever has stronger reach signal in the validation pass"). Validation finding:

- **Anthony Bridgerton** wins among book readers (76% top-4 placement in fan rankings; Bridgerton 2 / *The Viscount Who Loved Me* is consistently higher-rated than *The Duke and I*).
- **Simon Basset** wins on raw mainstream-show reach (Bridgerton S1's record-breaking debut put Regé-Jean Page on global front pages in a way Jonathan Bailey didn't experience until later).

**Recommendation:** lean **Anthony Bridgerton**, because (a) the Man Stack project is about *trait stacking* and Anthony's character carries more universal-desirable traits in the books (Viscount, fortune, eldest brother, athlete, fiercely protective), and (b) Anthony ages on, gets a full multi-season arc; Simon was largely a single-season phenomenon. But it's close — Simon would also be defensible. Jason should pick.

### 2. 1990s slot — Mr. Big vs. Patrick Verona (Heath Ledger, 10 Things I Hate About You)

Mr. Big has enormous cultural footprint but shows up in the modern discourse as much as a *cautionary tale* as a heartthrob. Patrick Verona is consistently named in iconic-90s-heartthrob lists, the role is what made Heath Ledger a global star, and the character is a cleaner stack-test target (mysterious bad-boy archetype).

**Recommendation:** keep **Mr. Big** unless Jason prefers the trait-stacking analysis to be cleaner. The locked list already flagged this as a known potential swap. Both are defensible; Mr. Big has higher *recognition* but Verona is a cleaner *romantic-lead* archetype.

### 3. 2020s slot — Nick Nelson (Heartstopper) — weakest validation signal on the list

Nick Nelson is the only locked character who **does not appear** in mainstream "best book/movie boyfriend" rankings outside the LGBTQ+ YA niche. He's hugely beloved inside that niche and his cultural significance for queer adolescence is genuinely large, but for the Man Stack analysis specifically — which is comparing universal-desirable trait stacking — Nick is a relatively *flat* target (he's deliberately written as a normal-good-boyfriend, not a slider-max). 

**Two options:**
- **Keep Nick** as the 2020s slot if Jason wants the F-target list to include LGBTQ+ representation explicitly. The trait profile will likely come out *low* on improbability (similar to Jack Dawson's role on the list — a "passes characterization without slider-maxing" reference case for the 2020s). This is a legitimate finding.
- **Swap Nick** for a stronger 2020s mainstream signal. Plausible alternates: **Xaden Riorson** from Rebecca Yarros's *Fourth Wing* (2023+, 4M+ copies, dominating BookTok 2023–2025), or **Hudson Vega** from *My Life with the Walter Boys* (Netflix 2023), or **Peter Kavinsky** from *To All the Boys I've Loved Before* (already a 2010s-late phenomenon — so doesn't fit a clean 2020s slot).

**Recommendation:** **Strongly consider Xaden Riorson** as a 2020s swap. He matches the romantasy-stack pattern better than Nick (ancient lineage, lethal warrior, throne-adjacent, "morally grey" — classic Man Stack territory), and is the most commercially dominant 2020s romance-lead emergence we found in validation. Either decision is defensible, but if the project goal is to test the Man Stack hypothesis, Xaden is a stronger test case than Nick.

### 4. Aragorn — borderline case, no swap recommended

Aragorn appears more often in fantasy / "characters you'd date" lists than in romance-lead rankings specifically. The Aragorn-Arwen romance is a B-plot. He's defensible for a 2000s slot because of LOTR's enormous reach and durable cultural presence, but he's the only F-target where the romance is genuinely secondary to the source work. **Recommendation:** keep him. The trait-stack analysis will be interesting precisely because LOTR isn't a romance, yet Aragorn still trait-stacks (king-in-exile, ranger, swordmaster, multilingual, immortal-beloved-of, etc.).

## High-Scoring Characters Missing From the Locked List

Worth flagging for completeness. None of these are recommended swaps unless paired with one of the swap considerations above:

- **Beautiful Disaster's Travis Maddox** — top of Goodreads BBB master list, but his cultural moment is squarely 2010s and the slot is full. Not recommended.
- **Hopeless's Holder / It Ends With Us's Atlas Corrigan / Ryle Kincaid** (Colleen Hoover) — Hoover is the dominant BookTok romance author by sales, but no individual Hoover hero has the same iconographic stickiness as Rhysand or Aaron Warner. Skip.
- **Rowan Whitethorn (Throne of Glass, SJM)** — cited frequently on BookTok lists. Same author as Rhysand; would be redundant.
- **Kaz Brekker (Six of Crows)** — strong YA fantasy showing. Niche compared to ACOTAR or Folk of the Air.
- **Peter Kavinsky (To All The Boys)** — strong 2010s teen-rom signal. Slot would crowd with Augustus Waters and Hardin Scott.
- **Xaden Riorson (Fourth Wing)** — see swap consideration #3 above.
- **Henry DeTamble (The Time Traveler's Wife)** — durable mid-2000s romance lead. Crowded out by Aragorn / Will Turner / Noah Calhoun for the 2000s slot.

## Aggregation Notes / Caveats

- Source ecosystem is heavily English-language and heavily BookTok-skewed for 2020s content. The 2020s slot is therefore disproportionately romantasy-coded.
- Empire/Vulture/Cosmo print archives are partly paywalled; mention counts for older characters (Han Solo, Westley, Maverick) are necessarily based on cultural-recognition signal rather than strict frequency.
- The "Best Book Boyfriends" Goodreads list is community-voted and skews recent (active voters are 2015–2025 readers); historical heroes (Mr. Darcy 1995) appear less often there but absolutely dominate cultural-critique writing about romantic heroes.
- "Book boyfriend" lists generally exclude TV-only and film-only characters, so cross-medium aggregation requires combining different source pools — a coarse, qualitative match was used rather than numerical aggregation.

## Summary

**23 of 25 confirmed cleanly.** Two slots have meaningful judgment calls:
- **Bridgerton 2020s** — Anthony vs. Simon (locked list pre-flagged this; recommend Anthony).
- **Heartstopper 2020s** — Nick Nelson is the weakest validation hit on the entire list; recommend serious consideration of Xaden Riorson (Fourth Wing) as a swap, OR explicit acknowledgment in graph annotations that Nick is a "characterization, not slider-max" 2020s reference case (analogous to Jack Dawson for the 1990s).

The locked list's 1990s flag (Mr. Big vs. Patrick Verona) is real but lower priority — Mr. Big's cultural footprint justifies keeping him.

No structural problems with the list. Generational stratification holds. Cultural-peak decade assignments are defensible across all 25.
