# Linda Barrett

## Character Summary
- **Source work:** Fast Times at Ridgemont High (1982), Universal Pictures, dir. Amy Heckerling, screenplay by Cameron Crowe (adapted from his 1981 nonfiction book of the same name). Linda Barrett is portrayed by Phoebe Cates. Supporting role — the worldly older friend / sexual mentor to protagonist Stacy Hamilton (Jennifer Jason Leigh). Linda is a high school senior at Ridgemont High working at Perry's Pizza in the mall food court. The character's narrative function is twofold: (1) she gives Stacy explicit sex/dating advice across the film (the carrot scene at the food court, the lecturing about giving oral, etc.), and (2) she is the object of Stacy's older brother Brad Hamilton's (Judge Reinhold) iconic masturbation fantasy in the bathroom — the "pool scene" — which is the single image the character is culturally remembered for.
- **Audience:** male-targeted
- **Decade slot:** 1980s
- **Cultural reach proxy:** Fast Times at Ridgemont High grossed ~$27M on a $4.5M budget at the 1982 US box office (per Box Office Mojo / The Numbers retrospective data) — modest in raw terms but a defining cultural object for the early-1980s teen comedy genre, repeatedly cited as one of the most influential teen films ever made (AFI, Rolling Stone, Empire retrospectives). The Linda Barrett pool scene is the load-bearing male-fantasy moment of the film and one of the most-referenced topless scenes in mainstream-cinema history — placed at or near the top of "iconic movie scenes" / "best male-fantasy moments" / "most memorable pool scenes" lists across Maxim, FHM, Empire, Esquire, GQ, and similar male-target media for the four decades since release. Phoebe Cates immediately became one of the defining sex symbols of the 1980s on the strength of this scene alone (subsequent roles in Private School, Gremlins, Gremlins 2, and recurring 1980s magazine "sexiest" placements). The red Cates-bikini look is one of the most-imitated swimsuit images in pop-culture iconography.
- **Canonical version used:** Per ticket instruction, the 1982 film version (the only adaptation), with the Brad-fantasy pool scene as the load-bearing male-fantasy reference. The film is set in suburban Southern California in the early 1980s. Linda is canonically a high-school senior — age coded as 17–18 in-source. This creates a significant methodological wrinkle for the base-population window (see Notes / Edge Cases for the honest age-handling discussion). For probability calculation purposes, the male-fantasy projection is treated as a young-adult woman within the 20–28 base-population window, consistent with how the rest of the male-target dataset has handled high-school-aged characters when the male-fantasy frame is the relevant projection (same convention as Mikaela Banes, ticket 008). The projection is honest; the literal in-fiction age is not.

## Joint Probability

- **Method:** filter (male-target stack)
- **Naive product of marginals:**
  0.20 × 0.22 × 0.34 × 0.70 × 0.25 × 0.25
  = (0.20 × 0.22) = 0.044
  × 0.34 = 0.01496
  × 0.70 = 0.01047
  × 0.25 = 2.618e-3
  × 0.25 = **6.545e-4**
  (≈ 1 in 1,530, applied within a base population of US women aged 20–28)
- **Correlation adjustment:** Mixed-direction adjustments. Linda's stack is a SIX-filter stack that misses the buxom-body-composition joint, the Cool Girl filter, and the doesn't-follow-girl-culture filter — making it one of the LESS deeply-stacked male-target characters in this dataset (closer to Wanda Maximoff / Ciri territory than to CJ Parker / Mikaela / Christian-Grey-tier extreme stacks).
  - Top-quintile attractiveness ↔ BMI 18.5–22: positive halo (slim figures rate higher in male-rated attractiveness studies; r ≈ 0.20–0.30). Naive product OVERSTATES rarity by ~1.2–1.5x for this pair.
  - Top-quintile attractiveness ↔ low neuroticism: positive halo (~1.1–1.2x overstates).
  - Top-quintile attractiveness ↔ high SOI: positive (more attractive women report more short-term mating opportunities; r ≈ 0.10–0.20). Naive product OVERSTATES rarity by ~1.2–1.3x for this pair.
  - Low-N ↔ high SOI: weak / approximately independent. Negligible adjustment.
  - No-kids ↔ currently single: positive (~1.2x OVERSTATES).
  - Top-quintile attractiveness ↔ currently single: NEGATIVE in real world (more attractive women more likely partnered). Naive product UNDERSTATES rarity by ~1.2–1.4x.
  - **Net direction:** Without the deep negative-correlation violations that characterize the buxom-and-tomboy-and-Cool-Girl stacked characters, Linda's adjustment is dominated by the positive halos (attractiveness↔BMI, attractiveness↔low-N, attractiveness↔SOI, no-kids↔single). The single negative-correlation pair (attractiveness↔single) is mild and only partially offsets. Net adjustment: roughly **1.4–1.8x LESS rare** than naive product (positive halo dominates clearly, because Linda's stack does not include the deep-violation filters).
- **Adjusted estimate:** **~4.1e-4** (≈ 1 in 2,400 women aged 20–28). Bracket: [2.5e-4, 7e-4].
- **Population implication:** Out of ~14M US women aged 20–28, roughly **5,800** match Linda Barrett's universal-desirable stack (range ~3,500–10,000 across the bracket). Linda lands in the LESS-rare end of the male-target distribution — significantly more common than CJ Parker (~15 matching), Mikaela Banes (~24), or Wanda Maximoff (~5,400 was Wanda's bracket; Linda is similar order of magnitude). The Linda stack is structurally a GROUNDED-FANTASY stack: hot, slim, single, no kids, calm, sexually experienced — but NO buxom proxy, NO Cool Girl, NO not-like-other-girls coding. The naturalistic teen-comedy genre delivers a less slider-maxed fantasy than the fantasy-genre alternatives. This is itself an interesting finding for the project: the most-iconic male-fantasy SCENE of the 1980s (Brad's pool fantasy) is built on a relatively naturalistic stack — the iconicity is in the staging and timing, not in the trait stacking. Compare to Christian Grey (~ 1 in 10^9+ on the female stack) or even Mikaela / CJ on the male side: Linda is several orders of magnitude less rare. The male-fantasy axis includes both extreme-stack characters AND naturalistic-fantasy characters, and Linda is one of the canonical naturalistic-fantasy reference points.

## Notes / Edge Cases

- **The age problem (most important edge case):** Linda Barrett is canonically a high-school senior, age 17–18. The base population for the male-target stack is US women aged 20–28. Three honest framings:
  (1) **Strict-canonical reading:** The character is below the base-population window. The probability calc would be technically inapplicable, OR would require treating the base population as "US women aged 17–28" (~17M instead of ~14M) with a correspondingly different denominator on some traits (e.g., childless rate would be slightly higher — ~75% — and single rate slightly higher too).
  (2) **Male-fantasy-projection reading:** The Brad pool fantasy projects on a sexually-mature young-adult woman; the cultural memory of the character is a sex symbol of the 1980s, not a child. The male-fantasy iconicity extends well beyond the literal in-fiction age. For projection purposes, treat as in-window. This is the convention used here and consistent with how Mikaela Banes (Transformers 2007, character age 16–17) was handled in ticket 008.
  (3) **Actress-projection reading:** Phoebe Cates was 18–19 during filming and the cultural sex-symbol status attaches to Cates herself across her early-1980s career, much of which was as a young adult. By the time of Private School (1983) and Gremlins (1984) Cates was clearly an adult sex symbol; the Linda Barrett iconicity carried forward into that adult-sex-symbol status.
  This ticket uses framings (2) and (3) jointly — the male-fantasy projection treats Linda as in-window, with the literal in-fiction age flagged honestly here. The math does not change under this convention. A reviewer who insists on strict-canonical reading should treat the result file as flagging the age incompatibility rather than computing a literal joint probability for an in-fiction 17-year-old.
- **Body composition is naturalistic, not buxom:** Sharp distinction from CJ Parker (ticket 016), who carries the BMI 18.5–22 AND DD+ joint at the rare 0.02 rate. Linda's pool-scene topless reveal shows proportionate, naturalistic bust — the DD+ proxy does not apply. This is the single biggest reason Linda's stack lands two orders of magnitude LESS rare than CJ's: the body-composition factor is 0.22 (marginal BMI alone) rather than 0.02 (joint BMI + bust). The Linda fantasy is "pretty slim brunette," not "buxom blonde bombshell."
- **Naturalistic-genre fantasy vs. fantasy-genre stacking:** Linda is one of the canonical naturalistic-fantasy reference points in the dataset (alongside Rachel Green, ticket 006). The character isn't slider-maxed across multiple rare-trait dimensions — she's a hot, sexually-experienced, cool, single young woman, and the male-fantasy iconicity comes from staging and timing (the pool scene), not from rarity-stacking. This contrasts with the slider-maxed male-target characters (CJ Parker, Mikaela Banes, Lisa from Weird Science) and provides a useful within-male-target spread for the final distribution. Project-level finding implication: the male-target distribution should have substantial spread between naturalistic-fantasy characters (Linda, Rachel) and fantasy-genre-stacked characters (CJ, Mikaela, Lisa) — not a tight cluster.
- **Cool Girl call — close exclusion:** I excluded Cool Girl Agreeableness on the grounds that Linda's characterization includes the catty / status-jockeying / fabricated-fiancé beats that don't fit the Cool Girl archetype (which is about low-conflict, low-maintenance, agreeableness). A reviewer who reads the broader pool-scene male-fantasy projection as Cool-Girl-coded would include the filter and pull the joint to ~6.5e-5 (~1 in 15,000), still in the same "naturalistic-fantasy" tier but more comparable to the standard male-target stack. The exclusion call here is a flagged judgment call; either way Linda lands in the less-rare end of the male-target distribution.
- **Doesn't-follow-girl-culture exclusion:** Excluded cleanly. Linda is mall-suburban-naturalistic-coded, not alternative-coded. Definitively distinct from Mikaela's tomboy-mechanic flavor and CJ's new-age-beach flavor. The exclusion makes Linda's stack ~4x less rare than it would be with the filter included; this reflects the genuinely-naturalistic framing rather than a methodological choice.
- **Low-N inclusion was borderline:** The pool-scene male-fantasy projection is overwhelmingly low-neuroticism-coded (calm, serene, eye-contact-direct), but the broader film characterization (the fabricated fiancé suggesting underlying status-insecurity) is more mixed. Counted YES on the strength of the cultural-memory dominance of the pool-scene projection, with the caveat flagged. A reviewer who excludes the low-N filter would pull the joint to ~2.6e-3 (~1 in 380), making Linda one of the LEAST-rare male-target characters in the dataset.
- **High-SOI is the cleanest filter call in the entire stack:** Linda is the most explicitly high-SOI-coded character in the male-target dataset to date — the carrot scene, the oral-sex-advice scene, and the matter-of-fact "I've slept with so many guys" framing are the entire characterization. No other male-target character's characterization is THIS load-bearingly built around explicit sexual-openness. If anything the high-SOI filter understates the character's coding — a stricter reviewer might apply a deeper filter (e.g., "publicly-explicit-sexual-mentor coded" at ~10% or less of US women aged 20–28), which would pull the joint a further ~2.5x rarer.
- **Cates / Linda conflation:** Some of the character's cultural reach is actually Phoebe Cates's career-wide sex-symbol footprint (Private School, Gremlins, magazine appearances), not Linda Barrett the fictional character per se. For trait-stack purposes, the calc is on the character; the reach annotation conflates somewhat with the actress, but the cultural footprint of the character ALONE (the Brad pool fantasy is one of the most-canonized individual male-fantasy scenes in mainstream cinema) is sufficient on its own for "iconic male-target heartthrob of the 1980s" status.
- **Conservative result vs. bracket midpoint:** I used the conservative end of the body-composition call (BMI 18.5–22 only, NOT joint with DD+), the borderline-include call on low-N, and the borderline-exclude calls on Cool Girl and doesn't-follow-girl-culture. A maximally-permissive reviewer who included Cool Girl (×0.10) and doesn't-follow-girl-culture (×0.25) would pull the joint to ~2e-5, still well within naturalistic-fantasy territory but closer to the rest of the male-target dataset. A maximally-conservative reviewer who excluded low-N would pull the joint the opposite direction to ~2.6e-3. The chosen filter set (top attractiveness + BMI + single + no-kids + low-N + high-SOI, no Cool Girl, no not-girly, no buxom) reflects the most-honest reading of the character as a naturalistic-fantasy reference point.
