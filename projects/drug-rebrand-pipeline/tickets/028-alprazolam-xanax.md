# Ticket 028 — Alprazolam / Xanax

## Demon identity

**Xanax** (alprazolam). Cultural demonization arrived in waves: 1990s "Mother's Little Helper" descendant framing; 2010s "Xans" / "bars" recreational use among teens and rappers (Lil Peep's 2017 fentanyl-alprazolam overdose, post-Mac Miller cultural moment); 2020s street counterfeit-Xanax-with-fentanyl crisis.

## Medicine identity

**Xanax** (alprazolam). FDA approved 1981 (Upjohn, now Pfizer). Indications: panic disorder, generalized anxiety disorder, anxiety-with-depression. Continues to be prescribed today, mostly as generic alprazolam. Brand has substantial cultural footprint despite recession of brand sales.

## Arc type

`failed_medicine_rebranded` in unusual configuration (like Valium ticket 027) — medicine → demonized-while-still-medicine → current. No rename, no new indication, just shifting cultural identity. Plus a growing street-counterfeit problem where "Xanax" pills are often fentanyl-contaminated.

## Chemistry hypothesis

**Identical** for genuine alprazolam. Street counterfeit "Xanax" is frequently *not* alprazolam (contains other benzos, fentanyl, or no active drug). The cultural-rebrand axis is muddied by counterfeit supply.

2. **Upjohn / Pfizer historical materials.**

4. **CDC overdose data** for alprazolam-involved deaths.

## Caveats

- Like Valium (ticket 027), this is a medicine-to-cultural-demon arc with no proper rebrand. Edge case for the project — flag in result.
- The counterfeit-Xanax-with-fentanyl phenomenon is recent and important — the "demon" street form isn't even the same molecule as the medicine.
- Cultural recognition of "Xanax" is essentially universal under-50 in the US.
