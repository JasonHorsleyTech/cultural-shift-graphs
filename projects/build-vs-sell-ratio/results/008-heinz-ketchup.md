## Build breakdown ($ millions)

Allocations follow the FY2024 10-K consolidated income-statement structure. Cost of products sold for KHC is the dominant build line; per the 10-K accounting policies, it includes raw materials (tomatoes for ketchup, dairy for cheese, wheat/grain for Mac & Cheese, packaging glass/plastic/metal, etc.), direct manufacturing labor and overhead, plant depreciation, and outbound distribution and shipping costs to customer warehouses. Co-packers / contract manufacturers are used in some condiment subcategories; their fees flow through cost of products sold as well.

- Cost of products sold (raw materials including tomatoes/dairy/grains/packaging, manufacturing labor and plant overhead, plant depreciation, warehouse-delivery distribution, contract-manufacturing fees): 16,878

- Distribution / logistics: already inside cost of products sold (warehouse-delivery dominant for KHC; some DSD activity in foodservice and refrigerated lines)
- Customer support: 0 (CPG; minimal customer-support function; Heinz consumer relations bundled into corporate G&A)

Adjusted vs. GAAP cross-check: Sum of Segment Adjusted Operating Income for FY2024 was $5,111M (NA) + $537M (Intl Developed) + $321M (Emerging) = **$5,969M**, vs. GAAP consolidated operating income of **$1,683M**. Using Adjusted Operating Income as the "profit" line — which arguably better represents underlying economics — would shift $3,669M from overhead to profit, but the build/sell numerator/denominator and the headline build_pct of 88.3% are unchanged either way.

## Methodology notes

**Consolidated vs. North America framing.** The ticket allowed either KHC consolidated or the North America segment as the anchor, noting Heinz is a flagship brand and segment ratio is a reasonable proxy. Used consolidated as the headline because the Heinz brand specifically is global — the UK is one of the largest single Heinz markets in the world (Heinz Baked Beans, Heinz Tomato Soup, and Heinz Tomato Ketchup are British staples), continental Europe is material, and Brazil and the Middle East are growing emerging-market footprints. Using NA-only would understate the global Heinz reach. The headline build_pct is fairly stable across the choice of anchor: NA segment Adjusted Operating Income margin (26.2%) is much higher than International Developed (15.2%) and Emerging Markets (11.6%), so an NA-only frame would push more of the dollars into profit and less into overhead, but the build/sell ratio is dominated by COGS (which has similar gross-margin behavior across geographies for KHC) and consolidated advertising-to-revenue (4.0%, stable across regions per public commentary). Estimated NA-only build_pct lands within 1-2 percentage points of consolidated.

**Advertising and marketing — disclosed as a combined line, not separately broken into measured media.** KHC reports "Advertising and Marketing" as a single line in earnings call commentary and investor disclosures, approximately $1,031M in FY2024 (~4.0% of net sales), down from approximately $1,071M in FY2023. This is structurally low compared to consumer-staples peers — General Mills runs around 4.5-5%, Mondelez approximately 5.6-5.8%, Coca-Cola Co. around 8-9%, P&G mid-to-high single digits depending on segment. The under-spend reflects 3G Capital legacy management at KHC: when 3G took over Heinz in 2013 and merged with Kraft in 2015, they imposed a zero-based-budgeting cost-discipline regime that aggressively cut marketing as well as other discretionary expenses. By FY2024-2025, multiple investors and analysts (and KHC's own management commentary) had publicly acknowledged that the marketing under-investment had eroded brand equity. In February 2025 KHC announced a $2B cost-cut program that would specifically increase marketing spend by approximately 30%, which would push advertising/marketing toward $1.3-1.4B over the medium term — but the FY2024 baseline used here reflects the under-invested state. This is the dominant reason the build_pct comes in higher than typical CPG peers (88.3% vs. Cheerios at ~91% and Mondelez/Oreos at ~83%). KHC sits between General Mills and Mondelez in marketing intensity, and the high build% is consistent with the ticket's prediction range while exceeding the upper-bound estimate.

**Heinz brand-specific economics not disclosed.** KHC does not break out brand-level COGS, advertising, or operating profit. The Heinz brand specifically (across ketchup, beans, soups, sauces, mayo, salad cream, etc.) is estimated at approximately $7-8B in global net sales — roughly 27-31% of consolidated KHC revenue. Heinz is one of KHC's two flagship brand families (Heinz and Kraft are the two banner brand systems); the company uses a "Brand Growth System" to concentrate marketing investment on top-tier brands including Heinz, Kraft, Oscar Mayer, Philadelphia, Lunchables, Velveeta, Ore-Ida, and Maxwell House. Per management commentary, brand-specific marketing on flagship "Brand Growth" brands runs above the parent average — suggesting Heinz brand-specific advertising-to-revenue may be 5-7% rather than the parent's 4.0%. If we ran a brand-specific Heinz allocation at $7,500M revenue, $375M brand advertising (5%), proportional COGS of $4,898M, proportional R&D of $42M, and proportional residual SG&A allocated 50/50, the brand-specific build_pct lands at approximately 86% — modestly lower than consolidated 88.3% because the elevated brand-ad intensity shifts mix toward sell. Reported the consolidated number for consistency with how Cheerios and Oreos were handled (parent/segment level, with brand-specific caveat noted).

**Cost of products sold composition.** FY2024 COGS of $16,878M is 65.3% of net sales — gross margin of 34.7%. This is significantly higher COGS-to-revenue than power-brand peers (Mondelez 60.9%, Coca-Cola Co. ~39%), reflecting KHC's positioning in lower-margin categories: condiments and sauces (Heinz ketchup, mustard, mayo) have decent margins but are commodity-intensive (tomato paste, vinegar, sugar); meat and cheese (Oscar Mayer, Kraft Singles, Philadelphia, Velveeta) carry significant raw-protein/dairy commodity exposure; and frozen and shelf-stable meals (Lunchables, Smart Ones, Ore-Ida) are even more commodity-heavy. This commodity-heavy cost structure mechanically inflates the build_pct.

**Trade promotion is netted against revenue.** KHC consolidated net sales of $25,846M are after-trade-promotion. Trade promo for shelf-stable CPG categories typically runs 12-20% of gross sales; the missing trade-promo bucket is conceptually $4-6B. If we added trade promo back symmetrically to both revenue (as gross sales) and to sell (as a trade-spend line), the consolidated build_pct would drop from 88.3% to roughly 74-76% — closer to the ticket's 65-80% expected range. Following the as-disclosed convention (consistent with Cheerios, Oreos, and Coca-Cola treatment) for cross-product comparability. The structural caveat applies symmetrically to all CPG entries in this panel, so the relative ordering across CPG products remains directionally meaningful.

**R&D — not separately disclosed in the income statement; estimated from public proxies.** KHC bundles R&D within SG&A and does not break it out as a separate income-statement line. Triangulation from MacroTrends LTM Q3 2024 indicates approximately $147M; the FY2024 estimate of $145M reflects this. R&D is small relative to revenue (0.56%) — typical for CPG food where innovation is recipe and packaging rather than capital engineering. The R&D classification is "build" per plan.md rubric. Even if R&D were materially higher (e.g., $250M), the build_pct moves by less than 0.5 percentage points.

**SG&A residual classification ($2,440M).** SG&A excluding impairments is $3,616M. Subtracting estimated advertising/marketing ($1,031M) and R&D ($145M) leaves $2,440M of "residual" SG&A. Per plan.md fallback, this is split 50/50 between sell and overhead — $1,220M each. Sensitivity: a 60/40 sell-heavy split would yield build_pct of 87.0%; a 40/60 overhead-heavy split would yield 89.5%. CPG-typical residual SG&A is sell-heavy (trade-marketing, key-account selling, category-management, foodservice sales) but KHC also has a meaningful corporate footprint (Pittsburgh/Chicago dual HQ, regional offices in São Paulo, Amsterdam) that adds genuine overhead weight. 50/50 is the defensible default.

**Impairment losses ($3,669M) — material to GAAP profit but not to build_pct.** FY2024 included $1,638M of goodwill impairment and $2,031M of intangible-asset impairment, totaling $3,669M. These are non-cash charges related to KHC's continuing revaluation of legacy goodwill and brand intangibles from the 2015 Kraft + Heinz merger, where 3G Capital paid full price for assets that have declined in market value. KHC has been periodically writing down these assets since 2019. Classified as overhead per plan.md framing (neither product cost contribution nor customer-facing spend, and not part of normal operating expenses). The impairments are massive relative to GAAP operating income (depressing it from ~$5,352M underlying to $1,683M reported), but they do not enter the build/sell numerator or denominator at all — the build_pct is unchanged whether impairments are treated as overhead or excluded entirely.

**Why the build_pct is so high (88.3%).** Three reinforcing factors:
1. **Structural marketing under-investment.** KHC's 4.0% advertising/marketing-to-revenue ratio is well below CPG peers; per public reporting and management commentary, this is a conscious legacy of 3G Capital cost discipline that is now being explicitly reversed via a $2B cost-cut/marketing-reinvestment program. This is the single largest factor pushing build_pct above the ticket's 65-80% expected range.
2. **Commodity-heavy COGS.** 65.3% COGS-to-revenue reflects KHC's category mix in condiments, meat, dairy, and frozen meals — categories with significant raw-input and packaging exposure. Vertically integrated manufacturing across major US plants (Mason, OH; Holland, MI; Fremont, OH; Pocatello, ID; etc.) and global operations places the entire make-and-ship chain on KHC's books.
3. **Trade promo netting.** Approximately $4-6B of trade-promo-equivalent sell spend is netted from revenue, mechanically inflating the build_pct by approximately 10-12 percentage points (similar to General Mills, Mondelez, and other warehouse-delivery CPG peers).

**Heinz ketchup specifically — near-monopoly category share reduces promotional pressure.** Heinz ketchup holds approximately 70% US market share in branded retail tomato ketchup (vs. Hunt's at ~17% and private label at ~10-12%), and dominates foodservice (the iconic Heinz packets). This near-monopoly position reduces the marginal need for promotional spending — Heinz doesn't need to fight for shelf the way a #2 or #3 brand would. It also means Heinz can sustain modest price increases without losing volume, which supports operating margins. This category-specific dynamic reinforces the parent-level under-spend on marketing for the Heinz brand specifically (less promotional intensity needed) and pushes the brand-specific build_pct to be modestly elevated relative to a more competitive category.

**Confidence: medium.** Consolidated income-statement line items (net sales, cost of products sold, SG&A, impairment losses, operating income), segment-level net sales and Segment Adjusted Operating Income, capital expenditures, and depreciation/amortization are all directly disclosed in the FY2024 10-K and Q4/FY earnings release. The medium rating reflects three layers of estimation: (1) FY2024 advertising/marketing of $1,031M is a combined figure, not separately broken into measured-media advertising and other marketing — consistent with KHC's reporting convention; (2) R&D is not separately disclosed in the income statement and is estimated at $145M from MacroTrends LTM proxy; (3) SG&A residual sell-vs-overhead split is the plan.md 50/50 default rather than a directly disclosed allocation. The headline build_pct of 88.3% is robust to these assumptions individually (range 86-90% across reasonable advertising/R&D/split assumptions). The dominant interpretive issue is trade-promo netting — adding it back symmetrically would push build_pct down to 74-76%, in line with the ticket's 65-80% expected range. Following the as-disclosed convention for cross-product comparability with Cheerios, Oreos, Coca-Cola, and the rest of the consumer panel.
