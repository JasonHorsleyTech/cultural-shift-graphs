# Hardin Scott

## Character Summary
- **Source work:** *After* series by Anna Todd (Wattpad 2013, Gallery Books print 2014; *After* film adaptation 2019, played by Hero Fiennes Tiffin). Originally One Direction / Harry Styles fan fiction reworked into an original series.
- **Audience:** female-targeted
- **Decade slot:** 2010s
- **Cultural reach proxy:** Wattpad reads ≈ 1.5 billion across the series (cited in Forbes / Variety coverage of the 2014 Gallery deal); print sales > 15 million copies worldwide across the five-book series; *After* film grossed ≈ $70M worldwide on a ~$14M budget; the franchise extended to five theatrical/streaming films through 2023.
- **Canonical version used:** Novel character (Anna Todd, *After* through *After Ever Happy*, 2014–2015) cross-referenced with the film canon as portrayed by Hero Fiennes Tiffin (where the novel and film diverge, the novel takes precedence — but the visual canon for height/build matters because the films cemented the character's physical archetype). Hardin is a 19–21-year-old British English-literature student at Washington Central University throughout the main series.

## Personality / Characterization (Excluded from Calc)

These define Hardin as a character but vary by individual taste — they are not universal-desirables.

- **Brooding bad-boy persona:** Tattoos, swearing, drinks, smokes, picks fights. A specific aesthetic that polarizes readers, not a slider every reader pushes up.
- **Mysterious dark past:** Witnessed his mother's assault as a child; reads as gothic-romance-coded trauma. Plot fuel, not a universal desirable.
- **Possessive/jealous streak:** Romance-genre coded; preference-dependent (some readers find this attractive, many find it red-flag).
- **Bookish/literary aesthetic:** Loves classic novels, quotes Austen and Brontë. Personality color, not a universal desirable.
- **British accent / nationality:** Specific cultural fetish, not universal.
- **Redemption-arc availability:** "I can fix him" appeal — preference-dependent and explicitly gendered to romance-genre conventions.
- **Wealthy father / connected family:** Adjacent to status but Hardin's own income is the relevant variable, not his father's; the family wealth is also explicitly a *source of pain* in the narrative, not a flex.

## Joint Probability

- **Method:** threshold (female-stack)
- **Naive product of marginals:** 0.04 × 0.20 × 0.02 = **1.6 × 10⁻⁴** (≈ 1 in 6,250)
  - If intelligence is excluded (low-confidence trait), the floor is just height × fitness = 0.04 × 0.20 = **8.0 × 10⁻³** (≈ 1 in 125).
- **Correlation adjustment:** Height and intelligence are mildly positively correlated (r ≈ 0.10–0.20 from Sundet et al. 2005, Silventoinen et al. 2012); base-rates.md notes this overstates rarity by ~1.2–1.4x for that pair. Height ↔ fitness is weakly correlated (taller-leaner pairs slightly favored at age 19–21). Net effect: naive product slightly OVERSTATES rarity by ~1.3–1.5x. With only three traits and no time-budget-binding skill stack, the correlation adjustment is modest in magnitude. Using a 1.4x reduction:
- **Adjusted estimate:** ~1.1 × 10⁻⁴ (≈ 1 in 9,000) **with intelligence**, or ~8 × 10⁻³ (≈ 1 in 125) **without**.
- **Population implication:** Among ~127M US adult men (or equivalent base population for a tall lean smart college-aged male), roughly 14,000–1,000,000 plausibly match Hardin's universal-desirable stack — depending on whether intelligence is counted. Hardin lives close to the LOW-RARITY end of the female-stack distribution; he is the canonical example of a "personality-coded heartthrob" rather than a "slider-maxed heartthrob." This is exactly what the project hypothesized for archetypes like Jack Dawson: when the appeal is brooding personality + dark past + bad-boy aesthetic, the universal-desirable stack stays small and the joint probability stays comparatively HIGH.

## Notes / Edge Cases

- **Light universal-desirable stack is the point.** Hardin Scott is one of the female-stack heartthrobs whose appeal sits almost entirely in personality / characterization rather than slider-maxed universal traits. Including him in the dataset is informative because he should land toward the LESS-improbable tail of the female distribution and demonstrate the project's "personality-coded vs. achievement-coded" axis spread. He is the female-stack analogue of a romance lead like Mr. Big or Edward Lewis (also relatively light on raw stack, heavy on personality / situational appeal).
- **Age window mismatch.** The plan's prime-window default of 28–38 is calibrated for adult-romance leads (Christian Grey, Edward Cullen-as-adult-vampire, Mr. Darcy). Hardin is 19–21 — too young for the 28–38 prime window AND old enough that "young AND attractive" is not unusual. This trait simply doesn't apply, rather than being a "fail." For New-Adult subgenre leads (Hardin, Cardan, the Kingdom of the Wicked male leads), the age trait may need a younger window or be omitted; this is a methodology consideration for the final graph annotations.
- **Wealth-adjacent vs. wealth.** Hardin's biological father Christian Vance is a successful publisher and his stepfather Ken Scott is a university chancellor — Hardin moves in moderately wealthy circles and inherits some social capital, but he himself has no significant personal income within the canonical romance arc. The methodology measures the character's traits, not their family's, so this does not enter the calc.
- **One Direction fanfic origin.** Worth flagging in the final write-up: Hardin began as a fictionalized Harry Styles. The "real-person fanfic → original property → film franchise" pipeline is a specific 2010s phenomenon and Hardin is the most commercially successful example. Cultural reach is partially inherited from the 1D fanbase rather than purely earned by the character — but the print sales and film franchise across multiple years confirm the character has independent staying power post-Wattpad.
- **Intelligence is the most borderline call.** Hardin is well-read and articulate, but the leap from "literature major who quotes Brontë" to "≥ 130 IQ (top 2%)" is real. Bookish ≠ psychometrically gifted. Reported with low confidence, and the bracket "with intelligence vs. without intelligence" is given so the final visualization can show the sensitivity of his placement on the distribution to this single call.
