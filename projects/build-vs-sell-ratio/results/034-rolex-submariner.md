## Methodology notes

**Data foundation.** Rolex SA is wholly owned by the Hans Wilsdorf Foundation and files no public financial statements. Every number here is an estimate. The single most authoritative external source is the annual Morgan Stanley/LuxeConsult Swiss Watch Industry Report, which uses Federation of the Swiss Watch Industry (FH) export data plus proprietary modeling to estimate brand-level revenues. Their February 2024 report estimated Rolex at CHF 10.1B in 2023, with 1.24M timepieces at 32% of total Swiss watch market value. I used this as the revenue anchor. The USD/CHF conversion of 1.12 reflects the 2023 annual average.

**Gross margin assumption (key uncertainty).** Rolex's gross margin is estimated at 62–68% by analysts, based on: (a) Richemont FY2024 peer gross margin of 65.35%, (b) the known wholesale-to-MSRP relationship (ADs purchase at roughly 60–63.5% of MSRP, implying Rolex's own cost-to-wholesale-price gap is the source of their margin), and (c) the production intensity of in-house manufacturing. I used 35% COGS (65% gross margin) as the central estimate. If gross margin is 62%, COGS rises to $4,294M; if 68%, COGS falls to $3,616M. This is the single largest lever in the model.

**Operating margin.** Estimated at 36.3% in my central case, within the analyst range of 33–40% (Atelier de Griff, Lonely Wrist cite ~40%; applying peer comparisons against Richemont at 32.5% and Patek Philippe at ~35% suggests a credible upper bound of ~40%). If operating margin is 40%, profit rises to ~$4,520M and overhead compresses to ~$1,670M; if 33%, profit is ~$3,729M and overhead expands to ~$2,461M. The ratio itself (build_pct) is not highly sensitive to this assumption since overhead and profit are excluded from the denominator.

**Why build_pct comes out ~83%, not 50–65%.** The ticket hypothesized 50–65% build based on the assumption that Rolex's sponsorship and AD network costs are comparable in weight to its manufacturing. The data does not support this. Even using generous sell estimates (every sponsorship dollar counted at top-end, full AD boutique program, ambassadors), sell totals ~7.7% of revenue. COGS alone is 35% of revenue. For build_pct to reach 65%, sell would need to be ~23% of revenue — comparable to P&G's selling intensity, which Rolex clearly does not approach. The honest estimate is in the 73–87% range. The midpoint I report (83%) reflects a moderate AD-support estimate; the floor (73%) reflects classifying all boutique-related costs as sell and using peak sponsorship figures.

**Sell classification choices.** Physical distribution to ADs was classified as build (getting the product to the dealer). AD boutique refurbishment contributions were classified as sell (motivating the dealer to showcase Rolex; this is a form of dealer incentive). Warranty and service costs were excluded (negligible; Rolex warranties are primarily handled at the factory level and are a very small cost relative to watch price). Customer support is effectively zero for the Submariner — no call centers, no recalls.

**R&D.** CEO Jean-Frédéric Dufour disclosed CHF 100M/yr on "watchmaking technology" in a 2024 WatchPro interview. This covers machinery upgrades and movement R&D but may understate total product development spend. Used as stated: $110M.

**Manufacturing capex.** Rolex announced a CHF 1B new Bulle facility (2029 target). This future capex is not in 2023 income-statement depreciation. Existing facility depreciation is embedded in COGS (consistent with Swiss manufacturing accounting conventions and the plan.md rubric). Not separately broken out.

**Submariner-specific note.** The analysis is of Rolex SA consolidated, not the Submariner SKU. Submariner estimated at ~10–12% of total production (100,000–150,000 units of 1.24M total). At retail MSRP $10,800 (steel Sub Date), wholesale ~$6,500/unit, and COGS estimated at ~$3,500–4,200/unit manufacturing cost, the per-unit margin structure is consistent with a 62–68% gross margin on wholesale revenue. No separate Submariner-only financials are possible.

**Confidence: low.** Every number is an estimate. Revenue is the best-supported figure (two independent analyst firms converge on CHF 10.1B for 2023). COGS%, operating margin, and especially the sell breakdown are all triangulated from peers and public statements, not disclosed financials. Treat the ratio as directionally reliable, not precise.
