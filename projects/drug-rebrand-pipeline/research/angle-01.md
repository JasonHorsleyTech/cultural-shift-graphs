# Angle 01 — Framing the rebrand pipeline

This is a transcript-style summary of the live workshopping with Jason, captured for downstream agents. The explore phase was done conversationally rather than via separate agent passes.

## The original hook (Jason's words, paraphrased)

"All the drugs that came out and were bad, and then what they turned into eventually, and how normal it is. Like the Nazi meth chocolate that they did for blitzkrieg that eventually got turned into Adderall. I know that Pervitin is not Adderall, but Adderall is slow-release Pervitin. It's not *not* Pervitin. Same chemical compound, same properties. And you do that same shit for methadone or Suboxone — methadone maintenance therapy — where it's like, 'Oh, we got this new drug for you, and it's medicine. It's gonna keep you okay.' And it's just heroin, but it's slow-release heroin, so it's fine."

The question, sharpened: for every drug people know as a big scary drug that turned into "it's a miracle medicine," what % of people know the connection, how long it took for each drug to do that, and how successful was the rebrand?

## The visceral angle

The fun of this graph is the *amnesia* — people know both names, they just don't think about the fact that they're the same drug. The graph makes that invisible connection visible. Like the build-vs-sell project, the point is to surface a hidden truth that lands differently when you see it on a chart.

## Two distinct rebrand arcs

During workshopping a second pattern emerged that's structurally different but related:

1. **Street drug → respectable medicine.** The original idea. Cultural demon gets a prescription identity.
2. **Failed / scandalous medicine → rebranded medicine.** A drug got pulled, shamed, or quietly failed, then came back under a different name — often for a totally different problem.

Both arcs share the *cultural amnesia* mechanic. Different starting points, same endgame.

Some drugs span both arcs (heroin was originally a Bayer cough syrup, MDMA was originally a psychotherapy adjunct, LSD was Sandoz's Delysid). Those get full-arc tickets that cover the whole journey.

Jason's framing question at the close of workshopping: "Are there any other kinds?" — we considered medicine→street→medicine (which is captured as "spanning both arcs") and medicine→street with no rebrand (which is a different graph — out of scope).

## The 2x2 we landed on

|  | Same indication, just renamed | Different indication |
|---|---|---|
| **Started as street drug** | Heroin → Methadone (same job: opioid maintenance) | MDMA → PTSD therapy |
| **Started as medicine** | Prozac → Sarafem (just patent games) | Thalidomide → cancer; Viagra ex-heart-drug |

The diagonal — street drug rebranded for a totally different medical problem — is the rarest and most interesting quadrant. MDMA for PTSD, ketamine for depression, psilocybin for treatment-resistant depression. Worth surfacing this visually in the graph.

## Pharma terminology for downstream agents

- **Drug repurposing / drug repositioning** — the formal name for "found it works for a different disease."
- **Evergreening** — the patent-extension version (Sarafem-from-Prozac).
- **Drug substitution therapy** — methadone-style "replace the bad version with the slow version" approach. Sometimes called maintenance therapy or opioid agonist therapy.

These are search terms research agents should hit when looking for primary sources.
