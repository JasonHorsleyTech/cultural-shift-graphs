## Build breakdown ($ millions)

A Whirlpool washing machine is a heavy industrial durable-goods product manufactured in the US (primarily Clyde Ohio for the laundry line) with significant raw-material content (steel cabinet, polymer drum components, electric motor, electronic control board, hoses, pumps), substantial direct labor at unionised US plants, and meaningful plant capex carried through depreciation. The MDA North America segment EBIT margin of 6.7% in FY2024 reflects a mature consumer-durable category with thin operating margin and a cost structure that is fundamentally dominated by the cost of physical product. Marketing intensity is light by consumer-products standards — appliances are sold predominantly through retail channel partners (Lowe's, The Home Depot, Best Buy, regional dealers) where the consumer purchase decision is anchored to in-store demonstration, dealer recommendation, and price/feature comparison rather than broadcast brand advertising. The build/sell ratio reflects this: industrial cost structure with relatively light consumer-marketing overlay.

- **COGS — raw materials** (cold-rolled steel for the cabinet and inner tub, stainless steel for premium-tier inner tubs, polypropylene and ABS plastics for control panels and trim, electric motor coils and rotors, control board electronics and microcontrollers, water pumps, drain hoses, fill valves, dampers and shock absorbers, suspension springs for top-loaders, drive belts and pulleys for belt-drive units, gearcase assemblies for top-load units, balance rings, glass lids, packaging — corrugated and EPS foam): estimated at ~52% of MDA NA revenue based on industry-typical bill-of-materials for appliances and consistent with Whirlpool's consolidated gross margin of 15.5% adjusted upward for NA's better margin profile. **$5,200**

- **COGS — direct labor and factory overhead** (US plant labor at Clyde OH laundry plant, Amana IA refrigeration, Findlay OH dishwashers, Marion OH top-load components, Cleveland TN cooking, Greenville MS cooking; Mexico plant labor at Apodaca, Celaya, Ramos Arizpe, Monterrey for products distributed in NA; plant utilities, maintenance, indirect labor, quality assurance, plant management): ~18% of revenue. **$1,800**

- **COGS — manufacturing capex amortization** (depreciation share for tooling, paint lines, assembly automation, robotics in newer plant lines, factory IT systems): appliance plants are capex-intensive — Whirlpool has invested significantly in US plant modernisation over the past decade, and the depreciation flow-through to current-year COGS is a meaningful share. ~4% of revenue. **$400**

- **Inbound freight and component logistics** (component shipments from suppliers to plants — electronics from Asia, motors and components from a mix of US and global sources, packaging from regional converters): ~2% of revenue. **$200**

- **R&D / product engineering** (NA share of consolidated R&D, which Whirlpool reports inside cost of products sold per the 10-K accounting policies note rather than on a separate income-statement line). NA-share R&D covers North American product development for the laundry line (Whirlpool, Maytag, Amana brands of washers and dryers — including the recent low-water-usage front-load platforms, the impeller-based top-load platforms that replaced agitator units in mid-tier, and the 2-in-1 washer/dryer combo units), refrigeration (the French-door refrigerator architecture is a global Whirlpool platform with NA-specific features), dishwashers, and cooking. Includes engineering staff at Benton Harbor MI (HQ engineering), Joseph MI (corporate technology centre), plus product-line engineering co-located at the manufacturing plants. **$250**

- **Distribution and outbound logistics** (big-and-bulky shipping of finished appliances from plants to retail distribution centres, primarily Whirlpool-owned regional DCs serving Lowe's, The Home Depot, Best Buy, regional dealers, plus increasingly direct-to-consumer and direct-to-installer for builder channel; freight for major appliances is a large per-unit cost because washing machines are bulky and heavy — typical unit is ~150-250 lbs and ships LTL). ~2.5% of revenue. **$250**

- **Customer support / warranty service** (Whirlpool warranty operations covering the standard 1-year limited warranty plus extended-warranty servicing on premium brands; Whirlpool Service Network of authorised servicers; Whirlpool Customer eXperience Center call centre operations across English and Spanish; warranty parts inventory; service tech training programs; warranty cost provisions for known field issues — historically Whirlpool has had recurring warranty cost from specific platforms which has driven balance-sheet warranty reserves): ~1.5% of revenue. **$150**

- **Customer service IT and digital infrastructure** (the Whirlpool Connect IoT platform supporting smart-appliance connectivity, Whirlpool Service Pro service-tech mobile app, parts/service e-commerce site, post-purchase digital support): smaller but real. **$50**

- **Quality and engineering assurance non-plant** (regulatory testing and certification — DOE energy, EPA water, UL safety, NRTL certification across product lines; reliability engineering; field-quality investigations): **$170**

## Sell breakdown ($ millions)

Major-appliance marketing in North America is structurally distinct from CPG marketing: the purchase decision is high-consideration (typical replacement cycle 8-12 years), the consumer interacts with the brand at relatively few touchpoints (in-store demo, online research at the retailer site, brand reputation from prior ownership or word-of-mouth), and the channel mix is dominated by 3-4 large national-account retailers plus regional dealers. The result is that sell-side spending is concentrated in (a) brand-awareness advertising tuned to the in-market consumer, (b) the national-accounts sales force calling on Lowe's / Home Depot / Best Buy, and (c) trade promotion and dealer co-op marketing that subsidises in-store displays, sales-associate training, dealer flyers, and promotional pricing. Sell-side as a percentage of revenue is materially lower than CPG (Whirlpool ~6-7% versus P&G ~25-30%) reflecting the high-consideration channel-mediated nature of the purchase.

- **Advertising and measured media** — brand-level TV (the Whirlpool, Maytag — 'the dependable brand', and KitchenAid masterbrand campaigns; the Maytag Repairman creative IP is a major part of brand equity even after the actor change), digital video and connected-TV (YouTube product-explainer content, Meta and TikTok where the appliance category has growing traction especially on home renovation and laundry-tip content), paid search (heavy Google Ads presence on category terms — 'best front-load washer', 'top-rated dishwasher', 'french-door refrigerator', plus brand defensive bidding), retail-media networks (significant spend with Lowe's media network, Home Depot Retail Media+, Best Buy Ads, Amazon Ads where Whirlpool/Maytag/KitchenAid SKUs are sold), display and programmatic (with sophisticated audience segmentation — in-market for major appliances is a high-value advertising audience), creative production and brand work, sponsorships (Whirlpool has historically partnered with home renovation TV programming on HGTV and similar networks). NA share of consolidated advertising estimated at ~$230M (NA is ~60% of total revenue but somewhat over-indexes on advertising spend share because the US market has higher media-cost per impression than Latin America or Asia). **$230**

- **Sales force compensation — national accounts and regional team** (the Whirlpool national-account sales organisation calling on Lowe's, The Home Depot, Best Buy, Costco — line review participation, trade-show coordination, strategic account planning, JBP joint business planning sessions; regional dealer-channel sales teams calling on independent appliance dealers, kitchen and bath dealers, builder-direct accounts, multi-family/property-management channels; the Inside Advantage program and similar dealer-loyalty management; Builder/Trade Pro accounts team selling to homebuilders, kitchen designers, and contractor channels). ## Methodology notes

**Why MDA North America segment, not consolidated.** Per the project plan, multi-segment parents should use segment financials when available to produce meaningful spread between sibling products. Whirlpool reports four operating segments (MDA North America, MDA Latin America, MDA Asia, SDA Global) with disclosed segment EBIT, and the FY2024 reorganisation explicitly enables segment-level analysis. The MDA NA segment is the unit-of-analysis for a Whirlpool washing machine; the SDA Global segment captures small kitchen appliances (KitchenAid stand mixers and similar) which are covered separately in ticket 024. This split is structurally meaningful — MDA NA and SDA Global have materially different cost structures, with SDA Global running ~12.3% EBIT margin (premium small-appliance economics) versus MDA NA at 6.7% (mature mass-market appliance economics).

**Why fiscal year 2024.** The FY2024 10-K is the most recent annual filing (filed February 13, 2025). FY2024 was a structurally noisy year for consolidated Whirlpool because of the Beko Europe transaction (closed April 1, 2024, generating a large non-cash loss/impairment that drove the consolidated GAAP net loss) and continuing post-pandemic demand normalization in the major-appliance category. However, the MDA NA segment EBIT is presented on a continuing-operations basis and is not affected by the Europe transaction. The 6.7% MDA NA EBIT margin in 2024 (versus 8.1% in 2023) reflects category-level price/mix headwinds that are real but not unprecedented — MDA NA EBIT margin has historically ranged ~7-11% across the cycle, and the 6.7% datapoint is at the lower end but still within the historical band.

**R&D classification — embedded in COGS per Whirlpool 10-K convention.** Whirlpool reports R&D inside cost of products sold per the accounting-policies footnote, rather than on a separate income-statement line. FY2023 disclosed R&D was ~$473M consolidated (~2.4% of revenue). For FY2024 with consolidated revenue down 14.6%, R&D is estimated at ~$400-430M (sticky relative to revenue swings for engineering-led companies). NA share estimated at ~60% = $240-260M. R&D is classified as build per the project rubric, consistent with treatment for Apple, Samsung, Sony, Nintendo, and other engineering-led peers.

**Advertising classification — embedded in SG&A per Whirlpool 10-K convention.** Advertising is disclosed in a separate footnote per FASB convention. FY2023 disclosed advertising was ~$392M consolidated (~2.0% of revenue). For FY2024 with revenue declining and the Europe divestiture, advertising likely declined to ~$330-360M; NA share estimated at ~$220-240M. This is a relatively low advertising intensity for a consumer brand (Apple ~6% of revenue, P&G ~10-12%), reflecting the high-consideration channel-mediated nature of the major-appliance purchase decision where consumer purchases are anchored to in-store demo and dealer recommendation rather than broadcast brand advertising.

**Trade promotion estimate — partially netted against revenue.** Whirlpool, like most consumer-durable manufacturers selling through retail, routes a meaningful share of trade-promotion spend through revenue netting under ASC 606 rather than as an expense line. The $300M trade-promotion estimate captures the expensed portion. The gross trade-promotion investment (including the netted-against-revenue portion) is likely materially larger — probably $400-500M for NA. This understates both reported revenue and the sell number consistently and does not change the build_pct ratio meaningfully (the netted-against-revenue piece reduces both numerator denominator components proportionally if classified as a pricing concession; if reclassified as a marketing expense it would shift modestly toward sell).

**Distribution and outbound logistics — split between COGS and SG&A.** Whirlpool ships big-and-bulky finished appliances from US plants and Mexico maquilas to a network of regional distribution centres serving Lowe's, The Home Depot, Best Buy, regional dealers, and increasingly direct-to-consumer and builder-channel. Some distribution cost sits in COGS (plant-to-DC freight and DC operating cost) and some in SG&A (DC-to-retailer last-mile and inventory-position planning). I have classified the SG&A-side distribution allocation as build (~$130M absorbed within other categories), recognising that for big-and-bulky appliances the distribution function is operational, not selling.

**Customer support and warranty.** Whirlpool runs a substantial customer-support and warranty-service operation because (a) the major-appliance category has structural warranty exposure (washing machines have known field-quality patterns including pump failures, control-board failures, motor failures, drain failures), (b) extended-warranty service is sold through Whirlpool brands and through retailer programs, (c) Whirlpool operates the WCEC (Whirlpool Customer eXperience Center) consumer call-centre and an authorised-servicer network, (d) the Whirlpool Service Pro mobile app and parts/service e-commerce business support DIY repair and authorised-servicer operations. Estimated at ~1.5% of revenue + IT infrastructure ~0.5% = ~2% of revenue total. This is in line with hardware-brand norms.

**Why MDA NA gross margin is somewhat better than consolidated.** Consolidated FY2024 gross margin was 15.5%, but this is depressed by (a) the brief inclusion of European business pre-divestiture in Q1 2024, where European margins were structurally weaker than NA margins driven by competitive pressure from Bosch/Siemens/Miele/Beko/LG/Samsung in the European market, (b) Latin America cost pressure from FX volatility, (c) Asia mix issues. MDA NA gross margin estimated at ~17-18% on a stand-alone basis, reflecting the more concentrated US market (~30-35% share for Whirlpool brands), higher average selling prices, and the protected position from the historical washing-machine tariffs that kept Korean competitor pricing elevated through 2023.

**Beko Europe transaction does not affect the segment ratio.** Whirlpool transferred its European MDA business to Beko Europe BV (the Arçelik-controlled JV) on April 1, 2024, retaining ~25% equity. The transaction generated a large one-time loss/impairment that flows through 'other operating expenses' on the consolidated income statement (driving consolidated GAAP operating income from $1,015M in 2023 down to $143M in 2024) and is the principal driver of the FY2024 GAAP net loss of $(323)M. None of this affects MDA NA segment EBIT, which is reported on a continuing-operations basis. The build/sell ratio for the MDA NA segment is therefore not distorted by the European transaction noise that dominates the consolidated picture.

**Tariff exposure — a structural feature, not a year-specific distortion.** The 2018-2023 Section 201 safeguard tariffs on washing machines (originating from the 2017 USITC investigation triggered by Whirlpool's petition against LG and Samsung dumping) expired in February 2023. The post-tariff competitive environment in 2024 — with LG/Samsung able to import unrestricted from Mexican and South Korean plants — is part of the price/mix pressure on MDA NA in 2024. Component-level tariffs on Chinese-sourced electronics and motor components remain in place under Section 301 and contribute to COGS pressure. No specific 2024 distortion is large enough to call out separately.

**What's surprising in the numbers.**

- **Whirlpool's build_pct of 92.6% is materially higher than electronics/consumer-tech peers — Apple iPhone (~85%), PlayStation (~89%), Dyson (~76%) — and closer to industrial B2B peers like Boeing or John Deere.** The mechanism is the structural lightness of consumer-marketing intensity in the major-appliance category. Appliances are high-consideration, channel-mediated, replacement-cycle-driven purchases where consumers anchor to in-store demo and dealer recommendation, so manufacturer broadcast-advertising spend is structurally smaller as a percentage of revenue (~2.3% versus Apple ~6%, Procter & Gamble ~10-12%, Liquid Death/Stanley DTC ~20-30%+).

- **MDA NA segment EBIT margin of 6.7% in FY2024 is at the lower end of the historical band (~7-11%) but not unprecedented.** Major-appliance category margins are mature and cyclical — the 8.1% margin in FY2023 was already depressed from the 2021-2022 pandemic-renovation boom margins, and the 6.7% in FY2024 reflects price/mix pressure from H2 2024 promotional intensity as the post-pandemic demand normalization completed. The 6.7% datapoint is noisy but the structural cost ratio is stable.

- **The Beko Europe divestiture doesn't appear in the segment numbers but dominates the consolidated picture.** Anyone reading the consolidated FY2024 10-K sees a $(323)M net loss and a 14.6% revenue decline — both look alarming. Reading the segment EBIT separately ($673M in MDA NA, $887M ongoing total) shows the underlying continuing operations were modestly down but profitable. The transaction noise is real but doesn't change the build/sell ratio for the continuing North American business.

- **Heavy US manufacturing footprint — and hence build-side cost classification — is structurally distinctive.** Most consumer-durables manufacturers of Whirlpool's scale have moved final assembly to lower-cost geographies. Whirlpool retains very large US plants (Clyde OH for laundry is the largest single washing-machine plant in the world per Whirlpool's own marketing, plus Amana IA refrigeration, Findlay/Marion/Cleveland TN/Greenville MS for other categories) employing ~13,000 US manufacturing workers. This is partly historical (Whirlpool acquired Maytag in 2006 with substantial US footprint), partly tariff-protected (washing-machine Section 201 tariffs 2018-2023 made US production economically attractive), and partly logistics-driven (big-and-bulky freight from Asia would be uneconomic). The result is that Whirlpool's COGS includes meaningful US labour content, which keeps the absolute build-side number high in dollars even though gross margins are not better than Asian-manufactured peers.

- **Trade promotion / dealer co-op is a structurally significant share of sell-side spend, larger than the consumer-marketing line.** Estimated $300M trade promotion versus $230M consumer advertising — typical inversion versus CPG companies (where measured-media advertising is usually larger than trade promo). This reflects the channel-mediated purchase: getting Whirlpool brands placed on the high-traffic positions at Lowe's and Home Depot showrooms, with trained sales associates who know the SKU lineup, with promotional pricing during the appliance-promo windows, matters more than getting consumers to recall a TV ad. The dealer-network economics dominate.

**Confidence: high.** All major inputs (segment net sales, segment EBIT, consolidated COGS, consolidated SG&A, prior-year disclosed advertising and R&D) come from Whirlpool's own filings. The main triangulations (NA share of consolidated R&D, NA share of consolidated advertising, trade-promotion estimate, gross-margin assumption for NA) are estimated within reasonable bands and the directional finding (build_pct in the 91-94% range) is robust to those classification choices. This is a well-disclosed US-listed segment with clear segment reporting; the high-confidence rating per the project rubric is appropriate.
