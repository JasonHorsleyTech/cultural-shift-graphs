# Cirilla "Ciri" Fiona Elen Riannon

## Character Summary
- **Source work:** The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt (CD Projekt Red, 2015) — the canonical anchor — plus its expansions Hearts of Stone (2015) and Blood and Wine (2016). Originally a literary character from Andrzej Sapkowski's novel cycle (1986–1999, *Sword of Destiny* through *The Lady of the Lake*). Subsequently in the Netflix Witcher series (2019+, Freya Allan). Background presence in The Witcher (2007) and The Witcher 2 (2011), but elevated to playable deuteragonist and central plot role in W3.
- **Audience:** male-targeted
- **Decade slot:** 2020s (per heartthrobs.md — "carryover, but cultural peak with Netflix series")
- **Cultural reach proxy:** The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt has sold 60M+ copies as of 2024 (CD Projekt Red FY2023 reports cited 50M+ in May 2023, with steady growth since); the Witcher franchise (games + books) has surpassed 85M units. W3 holds 250+ Game of the Year awards across publications. The Netflix Witcher series Season 1 (2019) was Netflix's most-watched original series at launch with ~76M household views in its first 28 days. Ciri is consistently among the most-cosplayed female video-game characters of the late 2010s/2020s, and CD Projekt Red has announced The Witcher IV (in development) with Ciri as the full protagonist — confirming her status as the franchise's torch-bearer character.
- **Canonical version used:** Per ticket instruction, **adult Witcher 3 era specifically** — Ciri is canonically born in 1251, and W3 is set in 1272, placing her at age **21** during the playable adult sections. The childhood Kaer Morhen training flashback (~age 10) is not the canonical reference. The book version (canonically aged from infant to ~16) and the Netflix series version (S1: ~12–13, aging up across seasons) are not the dominant pop-culture image and are not the male-fantasy reference. The W3 ash-blonde, scar-on-cheek, swordswoman version is the load-bearing canonical image for the heartthrobs slot.

## Joint Probability

- **Method:** filter (male-target stack)
- **Naive product of marginals:**
  0.20 × 0.22 × 0.34 × 0.70 × 0.25
  = (0.20 × 0.22) = 0.044
  × 0.34 = 0.01496
  × 0.70 = 0.01047
  × 0.25 = **2.618e-3**
  (≈ 1 in 382, applied within a base population of US women aged 20–28)
- **Correlation adjustment:** Mixed-direction adjustments. Same structural pattern as Wanda Maximoff (ticket 012) — a 5-filter stack that misses the Cool Girl + low neuroticism halo cluster:
  - Top-quintile attractiveness ↔ BMI 18.5–22: positive (slim figures rate higher; r ≈ 0.20–0.30). Naive product OVERSTATES rarity by ~1.2–1.5x for this pair.
  - No-kids ↔ currently single: positive (~1.2x OVERSTATES).
  - Top-quintile attractiveness ↔ currently single: NEGATIVE in real world (more attractive women more likely partnered). Naive product UNDERSTATES rarity by ~1.2–1.4x.
  - Top-quintile attractiveness ↔ doesn't-follow-girl-culture: NEGATIVE in real world (Mean-Girls / popular-girl framing maps onto high mainstream-girl-culture engagement). Naive product UNDERSTATES rarity by ~1.3–1.5x — and the Ciri stack DELIBERATELY violates this real-world correlation (the male-fantasy "top-tier hot AND not-into-mainstream-girly-stuff AND warrior-coded" combination is the same genre-violation pattern as Black Widow's "hot AND not-girly").
  - **Net direction:** The positive halos (attractiveness↔BMI, no-kids↔single) push toward "less rare than naive product." The negative correlations (attractiveness↔single, attractiveness↔not-girly) push toward "more rare than naive product." Without the Cool Girl + low-N personality-halo cluster (which Black Widow / Wonder Woman have but Ciri does not), the positive-halo stack is thinner and the negative-correlation effect modestly DOMINATES. Net: roughly **1.3x rarer** than the naive product.
- **Adjusted estimate:** **~3.4e-3** (≈ 1 in 290 women aged 20–28). Bracket: [1.5e-3, 5e-3].
- **Population implication:** Out of ~14M US women aged 20–28, roughly **48,000** match Ciri's universal-desirable stack (range 21,000–70,000 across the bracket). This places Ciri in the **moderate band** of the male-target distribution — substantially less rare than Black Widow (~1,190 matches) and Wonder Woman (~1,190 matches) because Ciri does NOT clear the Cool Girl / low neuroticism filters, and roughly comparable in stack-size to Wanda Maximoff (~38,000 matches at adjusted 2.7e-3). The structural pattern: Ciri's appeal is *characterization-heavy* (Elder Blood, royal bloodline, witcher training, surrogate-daughter arc, Wild Hunt trauma) and *under*-loaded on universal sliders relative to the 2010s warrior-princess Cool-Girl-low-N template. The methodology correctly differentiates the *type* of appeal: Ciri is a "fierce warrior heroine fans love for her arc" rather than a "Cool Girl optimized for low-friction male fantasy."

## Notes / Edge Cases

- **Surrogate-daughter framing — the central methodological caveat:** Ciri is canonically Geralt's surrogate daughter, NOT his love interest. Both Sapkowski (in the novels) and CD Projekt Red (in the games) explicitly treat the Geralt–Ciri dynamic as paternal — the franchise's romance options for Geralt are Yennefer or Triss, never Ciri, and CDPR has been unambiguous on this in interviews and official materials. The "male-target heartthrob" placement here therefore relies on the heartthrobs.md operational definition: *"fictional female character functioning as fantasy object regardless of strict plot role"* — same definition that includes Lara Croft (protagonist, no canonical love interest), Buffy (protagonist), Bayonetta (protagonist), Princess Peach (kidnap-victim, not love interest in the slot-machine sense). Ciri's appeal in male-fantasy / cosplay / fan-art circles is real and substantial despite the canonical surrogate-daughter framing — but the placement is more debatable than for characters like Yennefer (Geralt's actual romantic partner) or Triss. This is a borderline case and warrants annotation in the final-graph review. **A reviewer who applied a stricter "must be a canonical romantic/sexual figure" filter would remove Ciri from the male-target list and substitute another 2020s character.** The dataset proceeds with Ciri on the list because (a) the heartthrobs list is locked at graduation per plan, (b) the operational definition explicitly includes protagonist-as-fantasy-object cases, and (c) Ciri's male-fantasy footprint in cosplay/fan-art/community-rankings is large enough to qualify under the "functions as fantasy object" framing. Flag this for Jason's attention in the final review.

- **Multiple canonical versions and the W3 anchor:** Ciri exists in three main canonical layers: Sapkowski's books (1986–1999, where she ages from infant to ~16), The Witcher 3 (2015, age 21), and the Netflix series (2019+, where she starts at ~12–13 and ages up across seasons). The book version is canonically a teenager throughout, and the Netflix version is also younger than the W3 anchor. **The W3 version (age 21, adult swordswoman, ash-blonde with scar) is the load-bearing pop-culture image and the only canonical layer where the male-target heartthrob framing is even arguably appropriate**, because the books and Netflix series center on her childhood and adolescence. Anchoring on the W3 version is necessary and is consistent with the ticket instruction ("anchor canonical version on adult Witcher 3 era specifically"). A reviewer who insisted on book-or-Netflix anchoring would invalidate the heartthrob placement entirely. The W3 anchor is the only defensible reading for this slot.

- **Skill-stack methodological asymmetry — same as Black Widow / Wonder Woman:** Ciri's franchise appeal includes her witcher training, master-tier swordfighting, Elder Blood teleportation/time-travel powers, royal lineage across multiple thrones (Cintra, Nilfgaard, Skellige, Brugge, Sodden), and multilingualism (Common, Elder Speech, fragments of others). If the male-target trait list MIRRORED the female-target list (counting expert-combatant status, top-quintile fitness, multiple expert skills, multilingualism, royal/aristocratic income proxies), Ciri would stack 5–8 additional rarity multipliers and her joint would collapse by 5–8 orders of magnitude — potentially placing her below Christian Grey on the rarity axis. The plan explicitly does NOT mirror trait lists between genders, on the grounds that male-target media doesn't typically *stack* skills onto female love interests as universal-desirable sliders the way female-target media stacks them onto male leads. For Ciri specifically, this asymmetry is testable: her warrior/magical capability IS part of her appeal, but the question is whether the median male audience reads it as universal-desirable (slider up = better) or as characterization (a feature of her specific archetype). The plan's call is the latter, and Ciri's "warrior-princess-with-magic-and-bloodline" iconicity is best read as the *characterization* of the dark-fantasy heroine archetype rather than a separately stackable universal slider. **Same methodological asymmetry note as Black Widow ticket 010 and Wonder Woman ticket 020 — flagged here for final-graph annotations.** A symmetric-trait-list calculation would migrate Ciri substantially leftward (rarer) on the log-probability axis, into or below the Christian Grey / Edward Cullen band.

- **Comparison to Wanda Maximoff (ticket 012) — the closest within-dataset analogue:** Ciri is structurally similar to Wanda on this metric — both are 5-filter stacks (clear attractiveness, BMI, single, no-kids, not-girly; FAIL Cool Girl and low-neuroticism due to trauma-burdened fierce-personality coding). The methodology correctly separates these two appeal-types: Wanda and Ciri are "fierce magical women with deep trauma arcs"; Black Widow and Wonder Woman are "Cool Girl warrior-princesses with stoic emotional regulation." Both are male-fantasy figures; the joint-probability math captures the structural difference cleanly.
