## Build breakdown ($ millions)

Nintendo is structurally build-dominated for three reasons that distinguish it from Sony's PlayStation business: (a) no third-party royalty gross-up — Nintendo books net take on third-party publisher revenue (per the eShop revenue-recognition treatment), so the revenue denominator is not inflated by passthrough, (b) Nintendo manufactures hardware for genuine profit per unit (unlike PlayStation, which sold PS5 at a loss for most of the cycle), shifting the build-to-sell mix, and (c) first-party game development with a relatively lean studio system (vs Sony's 17 first-party studios with 4,000+ developers) and Switch 2 platform R&D ramp during FY25 just before launch.

- **Cost of goods sold (consolidated)** — Switch hardware cost of goods (custom NVIDIA Tegra X1 chips manufactured at TSMC, LCD panels for Switch and OLED panels from Samsung Display for the OLED model, Joy-Con controllers manufactured by Nidec/Hosiden, Switch dock, AC adapter, packaging, ocean freight from contract assembly in China and Vietnam to regional distribution centers); Switch 2 inventory build-up costs (FY25 absorbed pre-launch inventory and component-prebuild — Switch 2 launched June 5, 2025, after FY25 close); first-party software production costs (cartridge manufacturing for physical SKUs, packaging, shipping); third-party physical software wholesale COGS pass-through; first-party content amortization (capitalized portion of game development amortized to COGS over post-launch tail); accessory product COGS (Pro Controller, Joy-Con replacements, charging docks, carrying cases, Splatoon-themed gear, Amiibo figures); warranty reserves and refurbishment for Switch returns. FY25 COGS ¥454.8B / 152.48 = **2,985**

- **R&D (consolidated)** — Nintendo Entertainment Planning & Development (EPD, Kyoto) and integrated first-party studios (Nintendo Software Technology Redmond, Retro Studios Austin, Next Level Games Vancouver, 1-UP Studio Tokyo, Monolith Soft Tokyo); console hardware engineering (Switch 2 custom NVIDIA T239 SoC partnership, system architecture, joycon and dock engineering, screen and audio systems, backward-compatibility layer); system software development (Switch OS, eShop backend, Nintendo Account identity stack, Switch Online subscription stack, parental-control infrastructure); online platform engineering (Nintendo Switch Online infrastructure for matchmaking, voice chat via the Nintendo Switch Online mobile app, save-data cloud, NES/SNES/N64/GameCube classic game streaming services); peripheral and accessory engineering. FY25 R&D ¥143.7B includes the late-stage Switch 2 platform R&D ramp before the June 2025 launch. ¥143.7B / 152.48 = **945**

- **Distribution / logistics (allocated from Other SG&A)** — third-party freight (ocean freight Asia-to-Americas, intra-region trucking, last-mile to retail), regional distribution center operations (Redmond Washington for the Americas, Frankfurt for Europe, Kyoto/Tokyo for Japan, Hong Kong for non-Japan Asia), inventory carrying costs, e-commerce fulfillment for direct-to-consumer Nintendo Store sales (mynintendo.com), import/export and customs operations. Estimated from headcount and industry-typical logistics intensity: **310**

- **Customer support (allocated from Other SG&A)** — Nintendo Support call centers and online chat support across English, Japanese, French, German, Spanish, Italian, Dutch, Portuguese, Russian, Chinese, and Korean covering hardware warranty (Joy-Con drift was a notable warranty issue Nintendo handles via free repair program; Switch panel issues; dock issues), Nintendo Account recovery, eShop transaction disputes, parental controls assistance, Nintendo Switch Online subscription support, and trust and safety operations (player conduct moderation, child safety, gambling/loot-box absence — Nintendo's first-party games largely avoid loot-box mechanics). Customer support headcount ~600-800 globally plus outsourced overflow. Estimated: **80**

**Build total: 4,320**

## Methodology notes

**Why we used consolidated, not segment.** Nintendo files Japanese GAAP consolidated financials and does not provide segment reporting because the company is functionally a single segment (dedicated video game platform). The minor revenue lines — Mobile/IP-related income (¥67.6B in FY25, mostly Super Mario Bros Movie residuals continuing to monetize plus mobile-game royalties from Mario Kart Tour, Animal Crossing Pocket Camp, etc.) and Other (¥13.7B, mostly Nintendo TOKYO/OSAKA/KYOTO retail-store merchandise sales plus traditional hanafuda/karuta playing-card sales which date back to Nintendo's pre-1980s origins) — together represent ~7% of revenue and have somewhat different cost structures (Mario Movie residuals are very high-margin pass-through; retail stores have higher selling-cost intensity). Including them in the consolidated ratio is the right call given they share the brand-level and platform-level support infrastructure. A pure-platform-only ratio would be marginally higher build_pct.

**Why FY25 (year ending March 2025) rather than FY24 (year ending March 2024).** FY25 is the most recent full-year reporting and aligns with the PlayStation result's reporting window (Sony FY24 = year ending March 2025 — Sony labels by fiscal start year, Nintendo labels by fiscal end year, but the calendar period is identical). FY25 is structurally a trough year for Nintendo — Switch is in its 8th year and consumers were waiting for Switch 2 (officially announced January 2025, launched June 5, 2025), so hardware sell-in fell -31.2% YoY and software sell-in fell -22.2%. R&D ramped (Switch 2 platform development) while revenue fell, so the build-side absolute spending was structurally elevated relative to revenue. Stress test: re-running the same classification on FY24 (year ending March 2024, the steady-state Switch year) yields revenue $11,569M, build $6,275M, sell $1,115M, overhead $520M, profit $3,659M, build_pct = 84.9%. FY25 build_pct of 82.2% is therefore ~3pp lower than steady-state because (a) revenue dropped 30% but R&D rose 4% (Switch 2 ramp), and (b) advertising was deliberately compressed in the pre-launch trough. The directional finding is robust — Nintendo is strongly build-dominated either way.

**Japanese GAAP R&D classification.** Japanese GAAP includes R&D within SG&A by convention; this differs from US GAAP and IFRS where R&D is typically reported as a separate line above operating profit. Nintendo discloses the R&D sub-line within SG&A. Per the project rubric R&D is classified as build. The disclosed FY25 R&D of ¥143.7B is allocated entirely to build with no portion shifted to overhead — Nintendo's R&D is essentially all platform/game/system development, not corporate R&D. The line includes both first-party studio operating cost (personnel + content production) and console silicon/system engineering for Switch 2.

**The Switch 2 R&D ramp distorts FY25 build-side spending.** Switch 2 platform R&D was ramping heavily during FY24 and FY25 in preparation for the June 2025 launch. The ¥143.7B FY25 R&D figure incorporates substantial Switch 2 development costs being expensed before any Switch 2 revenue arrived (Switch 2 launched June 5, 2025, two months after FY25 close). This is genuine pre-launch R&D investment, not a one-time accounting anomaly. FY26 forecast R&D will continue rising (per the FY26 commentary mentioning higher R&D guided) before normalizing in the post-launch steady-state. For build vs sell ratio purposes, this is correctly classified as build — but the timing distortion means FY25's build-side absolute spending is somewhat ahead of FY25 revenue.

**Other SG&A allocation methodology.** Nintendo discloses two sub-lines within SG&A — R&D (¥143.7B, build) and advertising (¥86.5B, sell) — leaving ¥197.4B (~$1,294M) of 'Other SG&A' to allocate. I used a bottom-up estimate based on Nintendo's known headcount (~7,724 employees globally per the FY25 Annual Report — small for a $7.6B revenue company), Japanese-GAAP typical SG&A category breakdowns, and industry-typical logistics intensity:
- Distribution/freight (third-party): ~$310M (build)
- Customer support: ~$80M (build, based on ~600-800 customer-service heads globally plus outsourced overflow)
- Sales force/commercial operations: ~$170M (sell, based on ~1,500 commercial-org staff globally)
- Sales promotion (retailer co-op, displays, demos, in-store): ~$200M (sell)
- Corporate G&A (executives, finance, legal, IT, HR, real estate, professional fees, depreciation on SG&A assets): ~$530M (overhead)
Total: $1,290M — within the ¥197.4B / ~$1,294M Other-SG&A bucket.

**Marketing intensity vs PlayStation.** Nintendo's marketing-to-revenue ratio is ~7.4% in FY25 (advertising 565 + sales promo 200 / revenue 7640) — meaningfully higher than Sony G&NS (~5.9% advertising-to-revenue) on a revenue-normalized basis. Three structural reasons: (a) Nintendo lacks the third-party royalty gross-up that inflates Sony's revenue denominator (Sony books gross digital revenue with publisher royalty as COGS; Nintendo books net take, which is industry-norm for digital distribution but specific to how each marketplace recognizes revenue), (b) Nintendo's marketing concentrates on first-party tentpole launches (Mario, Zelda, Pokemon) which require platform-level brand-building because Nintendo's IP is the platform's main draw, vs Sony's third-party-heavy library where individual publishers do their own game marketing, (c) Nintendo Direct format and Nintendo's first-party IP do drive some organic marketing leverage but the absolute advertising spend per first-party AAA launch is comparable to Sony first-party launches. The FY25 advertising decline of -17% reflects deliberate pre-Switch-2 compression, not a structural shift.

**Per-unit hardware economics and build_pct sensitivity.** Switch hardware has been profitable per unit for essentially the entire platform lifecycle (industry consensus and Nintendo investor commentary both confirm this). Per-unit Switch hardware contribution margin estimated at ~$30-50 base model, ~$60-90 OLED model, ~$15-25 Lite. On 10.80M Switch units in FY25, hardware contribution to operating income is ~$400-600M of $1,855M segment OI (~25-30% of OI). The remaining ~70-75% of OI comes from first-party software (highest-margin), Nintendo Switch Online subscription (high-margin recurring), and third-party royalties (moderate-margin platform fee). This mix is structurally different from Sony G&NS (where hardware is ~25% of revenue but only ~20-25% of OI because hardware operates near breakeven) — Nintendo's hardware-profit posture is part of why the build/sell ratio differs from Sony's.

**Currency and reporting period.** Nintendo reports in JPY with a consolidated FY ending March 31 each year. Nintendo's labeling: FY25 = April 2024 to March 2025 (Nintendo labels by fiscal end year). Sony's labeling for the same calendar period is FY24 (Sony labels by fiscal start year). The PlayStation result used fiscal_year: 2024 to mean year ending March 2025; this Nintendo result uses the same fiscal_year: 2024 designation for the equivalent calendar period (year ending March 2025) for cross-comparability within the dataset, despite Nintendo's own internal labeling being FY25. Used average FY25 USD-JPY of 152.48 yen per dollar (Nintendo's disclosed average reference rate) for translation. JPY weakened from ~149 at start of FY25 to ~150 at end of FY25, with intra-year volatility around 145-160. The strong yen weakness during FY25 boosted Nintendo's reported revenue and SG&A in JPY terms (foreign-currency-denominated expenses translated higher) — this is partly responsible for SG&A staying flat in JPY while revenue dropped 30%.

**Sales force compensation classification.** Nintendo reports SG&A as a single consolidated line plus the disclosed R&D and advertising sub-lines. There is no separate disclosure of selling vs general/administrative within Other SG&A. I used a bottom-up estimate of ~1,500 commercial-organization staff globally at ~$110-120K loaded comp = ~$170M for sales force. This is more defensible than a top-down 60/40 split given the disclosure gap.

**First-party game development capitalization vs expensing.** Nintendo first-party game development is partially capitalized (the portion clearly identifiable as creating future-revenue-generating intangible assets) and partially expensed. Capitalized development is amortized to COGS over the post-launch revenue tail. Both treatments land in build per the rubric — capitalized portion flows through COGS, expensed portion flows through R&D. Nintendo's first-party AAA development budgets are reportedly $50-150M per AAA title — materially below Sony first-party AAA budgets ($200-300M+) — because Nintendo's IP-driven approach reuses art assets, engines, and characters across titles (e.g., Mario Kart 8 base reused for Mario Kart 8 Deluxe; Splatoon engine reused across the trilogy). First-party content amortization in FY25 estimated at $300-500M (running through COGS); current-year R&D expense is $945M (running through R&D). All build either way.

**Confidence: medium.** Nintendo Co., Ltd. is publicly listed on TSE (7974) Prime Market and on OTC ADR (NTDOY), files detailed Japanese Securities Reports (Yukashoken Hokokusho) and English Annual Reports, and discloses consolidated R&D and advertising as separate sub-lines within SG&A. The advertising and R&D disclosure granularity is unusually clean for a Japanese filer (better than Toyota or Sony Group at the corporate level — Sony only breaks out R&D by segment in supplementary materials), but the SG&A sub-classification beyond those two disclosed lines requires triangulation. The build/sell ratio is robust to ±2pp under all plausible alternative classifications of Other SG&A. The fiscal-period choice (FY25 trough vs FY24 steady-state) shifts build_pct by ~3pp; both endpoints are within the ticket's anticipated 70-85% range. The largest single driver of uncertainty is the trough-year timing (Switch 2 R&D pre-spend without Switch 2 revenue), which I have documented above as a structural feature of FY25 rather than an estimation error.

**What's surprising in the numbers.**

- **Nintendo build_pct of 82.2% is below PlayStation's 89.1% despite Nintendo being the more 'first-party-heavy' platform.** This is counterintuitive because Nintendo is famously dependent on first-party IP (Mario, Zelda, Pokemon) while Sony has a much larger third-party-revenue mix. The mechanism is the gross-vs-net royalty treatment: Sony's third-party digital revenue is grossed-up (publisher royalty flows through COGS, inflating both revenue and build), Nintendo's is netted. Adjusting for this (re-running Sony G&NS on net-take) would put PlayStation at ~85.8% build_pct, slightly above Nintendo's 82.2% — the directional finding (build-dominated platform) holds but the gap closes. Nintendo's somewhat lower build_pct is real and reflects (a) higher marketing intensity per revenue dollar without the third-party-royalty gross-up offset, and (b) Nintendo's smaller first-party studio system (~3,000 developers across NTD/EPD vs Sony's ~4,000+ across PlayStation Studios) — though Nintendo's per-title development cost discipline keeps the ratio reasonable.

- **R&D-to-revenue at 12.4% is high for a hardware company.** Most consumer-electronics companies run R&D-to-revenue at 5-8% (Apple ~7%, Samsung ~9% consolidated). Nintendo's 12.4% reflects the platform-development model: continuous first-party game development plus Switch 2 platform R&D ramp. In a steady-state year (FY24), R&D was 8.2% of revenue — closer to peer levels but still elevated. Nintendo runs R&D heavy because the platform is the product.

- **Nintendo is hardware-profitable by design.** Unlike PlayStation (which sold PS5 at a loss for most of the cycle), Nintendo has been hardware-profitable per unit essentially since launch. This is a deliberate strategic choice — Nintendo prices hardware to be profitable per unit and accepts a smaller install base in exchange for unit economics. The cost-structure ratio reflects this: hardware contribution to OI is ~$400-600M (~25% of OI) on positive per-unit margin, vs PlayStation's near-breakeven hardware that depends on software and services for OI.

- **Cash pile generates non-operating income but doesn't affect the build/sell ratio.** Nintendo's ~¥1.7T cash and short-term investment balance generated ¥98.3B in non-operating income in FY25 (~$650M, mostly interest and dividends from JGB and corporate-bond holdings plus FX gains in some years). This flows to ordinary profit and net profit but is excluded from operating profit and therefore from the build/sell ratio. The net result is that Nintendo's ordinary profit ratio (32.0% in FY25) and net profit ratio (23.9%) are higher than the operating margin (24.3%) — a feature of conservative balance-sheet management, not platform economics.

- **Mobile/IP income (Mario movie residuals) is a small but interesting line.** The Super Mario Bros. Movie (released April 2023, $1.36B global box office) drove ¥92.7B (~$640M at FY24 FX) of mobile/IP income in FY24, falling to ¥67.6B (~$443M) in FY25 as the residuals tapered. This is highest-margin passthrough revenue (Universal/Illumination did the production work; Nintendo's licensing royalty is essentially pure margin). A second Mario Bros movie was announced for April 2026 release; theme-park royalties (Super Nintendo World Hollywood opened February 2023; Super Nintendo World Orlando opened May 2025 at Universal's Epic Universe) add another modest residual revenue line. None of these are large enough to materially shift the consolidated build_pct but they are structurally accretive to gross margin over time.

- **The Switch 1 platform is winding down profitably.** Switch lifetime cumulative sell-through approaching 150M units as of March 2025 — making it Nintendo's best-selling console platform ever (surpassing the original Wii's ~101M and DS's ~154M; Switch overtook DS during FY26). Software lifetime cumulative on Switch is well over 1.4B units. The platform is exiting profitably, with Switch hardware still selling 10.80M units in FY25 even with Switch 2 announced — atypical end-of-cycle behavior reflecting Switch's strong evergreen software library (Mario Kart 8 Deluxe, Animal Crossing New Horizons, Super Mario Bros Wonder).
