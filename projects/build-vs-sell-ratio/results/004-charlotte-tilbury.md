## Methodology notes

**Segment selection.** The brief asks to use Charlotte Tilbury within the Puig prestige-beauty segment. Puig reports three product segments — Fragrance & Fashion (€3,538M, 73%), Makeup (€763M, 16%), and Skincare (€516M, 11%). Charlotte Tilbury is the largest single contributor to the Makeup segment per Puig's FY2024 commentary (it "led" the segment), so the Makeup segment is the cleanest available proxy for Charlotte Tilbury's economics. Other Makeup-segment brands (Carolina Herrera makeup line, Penhaligon's makeup line, Christian Louboutin Beauty) are smaller, leaving Charlotte Tilbury as the dominant share — likely 70%+ of segment revenue based on industry trade-press estimates of brand sales (£487M UK alone in FY24, plus US/EU/APAC). Used segment net revenue €763M as the revenue base.

**Why not the UK Companies House standalone.** Charlotte Tilbury Beauty Ltd. files separate UK accounts (turnover £448.5M FY23 → £487.3M FY24, EBITDA £18.3M FY23 — only ~4% margin). The UK entity is one legal entity within a global brand; it bears most of the brand's central cost (London headquarters, global brand-building A&P) but books only a fraction of global revenue (US, EU, APAC sit in other Puig legal entities). Using the UK standalone would dramatically overstate the sell% (because central A&P sits there) and understate scale. Puig's segment-level disclosure is materially more representative of the brand's true global economics.

**Currency conversion.** Used the calendar-2024 average EUR/USD rate of 1.082 to convert all euro figures to USD, consistent with project convention. Sensitivity: ±5% FX move would shift all USD values proportionally but does not change build_pct.

**A&P allocation: pro-rata vs. category-weighted.** Puig discloses consolidated A&P of €1,551.3M = 32.4% of revenue. Pro-rata to Makeup gives €247M = $267M. Counter-argument: prestige cosmetics is famously the most sell-heavy beauty category (more than fragrance, since fragrance benefits from celebrity-licence economics where the A&P is partly capitalised in the licence fee). If Makeup actually runs A&P of 35-38% of revenue (Estée Lauder-style), Puig's true Makeup A&P could be €270-290M ($292-314M), shifting build_pct down ~1-2 points to ~42%. Counter-counter: Puig's fragrance brands (Rabanne, Jean Paul Gaultier, Carolina Herrera) include continuous celebrity-fronted multi-million-euro media campaigns that may run higher A&P intensity than Makeup, meaning Makeup may actually run *below* the corporate average. Defaulted to pro-rata as the neutral choice and flagged as the largest single uncertainty.

**Operating-profit allocation.** Puig discloses consolidated Adjusted EBITDA of €969M (20.2% margin) and notes that Makeup's "operating margin decreased by (227) basis points" in 2024. The press release does NOT disclose absolute segment-level EBITDA or EBIT. Used a Makeup operating margin of ~12.5% (vs. corporate ~15.6% EBIT) reflecting (a) Makeup underperforming the rest of the portfolio per the -227bps commentary, (b) Makeup revenue being flat / slightly down (-1.3%) on negative operating leverage, and (c) the Charlotte Tilbury Airbrush Flawless Setting Spray batch recall in Q4 2024 which absorbed margin. This yields €95M = $103M. If Makeup actually ran at the corporate EBIT margin (15.6%), profit would be ~$129M and overhead would be reduced by the same amount — does not affect build_pct.

**Build / sell residual classification.** After allocating COGS pro-rata and A&P pro-rata, the residual segment opex ($300M USD) covers R&D, distribution, sales-force/counter, trade promo, G&A, depreciation, and IPO-cost share. Used industry-standard ratios from Estée Lauder and L'Oréal Luxe to split: R&D ~2.5% of revenue, Distribution ~4%, Sales-force/counter ~5%, Trade promo ~3%, G&A residual to overhead. The split between build-like ($53M added to COGS to total $260M build) and sell-like ($66M added to A&P to total $333M sell) is the classification work. Plan.md's 50/50 default residual split would have given build_pct of ~45%; my category-informed split gives 43.8%. Both round to "low 40s" — the headline conclusion is robust to the split choice.

**Sales-force / beauty-advisor reality.** Prestige cosmetics has a meaningful in-store labour cost that doesn't appear in mass-market CPG: trained beauty advisors at department-store counters and Sephora shop-in-shops, often paid jointly by brand and retailer with some commission overlay. Puig does not separately disclose this. Estimated at ~5% of segment revenue based on Estée Lauder MD&A discussion of "store operations" and L'Oréal's segment commentary. Booked into Sell (it is direct selling labour, not corporate G&A), consistent with plan.md's classification convention.

**Influencer / digital-marketing under-reporting in Kantar.** Charlotte Tilbury was an early and aggressive influencer-marketing brand (long pre-dating most prestige peers — the brand grew up on YouTube tutorial culture). Pre-2022 Kantar / Ad Age "measured media" data systematically under-counted influencer spend, paid TikTok placement, and direct-to-consumer Meta/Google ads, which together likely make up a meaningful share of Charlotte Tilbury's sell-side spend today. Because we used Puig's own A&P disclosure (not Kantar) — and Puig's accounting captures all paid promotional activity regardless of channel — this caveat does not bite for our number, but it would for any Kantar-derived triangulation.

**Charlotte Tilbury vs. Makeup segment.** If Charlotte Tilbury is ~75% of segment revenue (estimate based on the brand's stated #1 contribution and the segment's other smaller brands being licensed celebrity makeup lines), then Charlotte Tilbury alone is ~$620M USD revenue with proportionate cost structure. The brand-specific build_pct should be similar to or slightly lower than the segment (Charlotte Tilbury's heavier influencer/DTC spend would push sell% higher, lowering build_pct). Did not attempt brand-only carve-out because that requires non-public allocation; flagged for future refinement.

**Recently IPO'd parent.** Puig listed in Madrid in May 2024. FY2024 reported numbers carry one-time IPO costs (employee award + listing fees + legal & advisory) — Puig discloses these in adjusted-vs-reported reconciliations. Allocated a pro-rata share to Makeup overhead. These will not recur in 2025+, so today's overhead share is ~$10-15M overstated relative to steady-state.

**Confidence: medium.** Segment net revenue and consolidated cost lines (COGS, A&P, total opex) come directly from Puig's audited 2024 Consolidated Annual Accounts and FY2024 results press release — high confidence on those inputs. Allocation to Makeup requires (a) pro-rating consolidated A&P and other opex by segment revenue share, (b) imputing a Makeup operating margin from a YoY-change disclosure rather than absolute disclosure, (c) splitting residual opex between build / sell / overhead using prestige-beauty peer benchmarks, and (d) applying an EUR/USD conversion. None of those steps is wild, but each carries uncertainty. Calling this "high" would overclaim — Puig does not disclose segment-level EBITDA or A&P, unlike Estée Lauder which discloses both. Calling this "low" would understate — Puig is a public company with audited segment revenue and a credible A&P line; this is materially better data than Dyson, Rolex, or IKEA where everything is triangulated. Medium is the honest read.
