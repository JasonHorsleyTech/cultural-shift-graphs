# Ticket 034 — Bath salts / synthetic cathinones (null case: failed rebrand)

## Demon identity

"Bath salts" — synthetic cathinones (mephedrone, MDPV, methylone, alpha-PVP, others). Cultural demonization peaked 2010–2012 with the "Miami zombie" / Rudy Eugene case (May 2012) and sensational headlines about cathinone-induced violent behavior. DEA emergency-scheduled mephedrone, MDPV, and methylone in October 2011; subsequent scheduling of other analogs followed.

## Medicine identity

Effectively **none**. Bupropion (Wellbutrin) is technically a substituted cathinone but it predates the bath-salts cultural moment and is not framed as a rebrand of synthetic cathinones. No FDA approval has emerged from the synthetic-cathinone family specifically as a result of the bath-salts era.

## Arc type

`failed_medicine_rebranded` with `medicine_year: null` — like Quaaludes (ticket 032), this is a null case. The synthetic cathinone class never got a medicinal rebrand.

## Chemistry hypothesis

Bupropion is technically a substituted cathinone (it's a β-keto amphetamine derivative). But it was developed independently and predates the bath-salts moral panic by ~25 years. The conceptual lineage is loose; this is closer to a chemistry-family coincidence than a rebrand.

## Caveats

- Another **null case** for the project. The graph needs these to show rebrand isn't automatic.
- The "Miami zombie" case may not have actually involved bath salts — toxicology found only marijuana. Worth flagging in the honesty section as an early "fake news" story driving drug policy.
- Why no rebrand? Likely: (a) synthetic cathinones are a *class*, not a single famous molecule; (b) no clear therapeutic indication exists for the dopamine/serotonin profile they produce; (c) bupropion already covers any plausible therapeutic niche.
