## Methodology notes

**Why proxy-based estimation.** Trader Joe's Company is private and discloses no public financial statements. Aldi Nord (the German family trust that owns Trader Joe's through the Albrecht family holdings) files limited consolidated disclosures in Germany, but Trader Joe's operates as a structurally separate US entity. No audited Trader Joe's revenue, COGS, or SG&A figures exist in any public source. All numbers here are triangulated from (a) trade press revenue estimates, (b) public-peer cost structures, and (c) known qualitative facts about the TJ's operating model. The confidence rating is **low** by design. This entry's value is the contrast it provides against the broader dataset, not its precision.

**Revenue anchor.** Trade press sources (Supermarket News, Forbes, Progressive Grocer) have consistently cited Trader Joe's revenue in the $16–20B range for 2023–2024, with the company operating approximately 560–580 stores in that period. This analysis uses **$18,000M** as the central estimate — approximately $32M in average annual sales per store, consistent with: (a) a 10,000–15,000 sqft average store footprint at $1,750–2,000/sqft annual sales productivity (= $17.5–30M per store); (b) scale-weighted toward larger newer stores. Any individual estimate from trade press carries ±15–20% uncertainty; the range $15,000–21,000M is plausible without additional primary data.

**Gross margin anchor: Kroger, not Costco.** The primary peer for TJ's cost structure is Kroger rather than Costco, because TJ's and Kroger share similar gross-margin territory. Kroger's FY2024 10-K (ended February 3, 2024) reports a gross margin of approximately 22.2% (COGS ~77.8% of net sales, inclusive of buying and occupancy costs). Trader Joe's is estimated to have a modestly better merchandise cost ratio (~75%) for two reasons: (1) ~80% private-label penetration eliminates national-brand pricing power — TJ's negotiates directly with co-manufacturers and captures the margin normally captured by brand owners; (2) a narrow SKU count (~4,000 SKUs vs. Kroger's ~30,000) drives higher volume per SKU and better supplier leverage. Some of that cost advantage is passed to consumers in lower shelf prices, which is what drives TJ's exceptional 4× industry-average sales-per-sqft productivity. Net merchandise COGS estimate: **75% of revenue = $13,500M**. Costco is a secondary reference for model logic (high private-label, low advertising, efficiency-driven) but not for cost ratios: Costco's gross margin is structurally different (~13–15%) because it caps markups at 14–15%, subsidizing merchandise margins with $4.8B in membership fee income. TJ's has no membership fees.

**Advertising: confirmed near-zero.** Multiple trade press sources confirm Trader Joe's spends no material dollars on traditional advertising (no TV commercials, no radio, no outdoor, no paid digital acquisition at scale). The sole marketing vehicle is the **Fearless Flyer** — a print-and-email newsletter, six issues per year, covering new and seasonal products in the brand's distinctive voice. Production and postage/distribution costs for ~10M circulation across six issues are estimated at ~$30–40M/year. A minimal social media presence and occasional podcast/organic PR add another $10–20M. Total advertising and marketing: **~$50M/year**, or approximately 0.3% of estimated revenue. For comparison, a typical CPG company spends 8–15% of revenue on advertising; even value-focused peers like Kroger embed ~1–2% in SG&A. TJ's ratio is 30–50× lower.

**Store occupancy as sell.** Per the project rubric, storefronts are classified as sell. TJ's stores (avg ~10,000–15,000 sqft) are in prime retail corridors — suburban strip malls, urban mixed-use, and college-town locations with above-average foot traffic. Estimated occupancy: ~560 stores × ~$1.4M/year average (mix of owned and leased, including property taxes and CAM) = **~$800M**. This estimate is rough; TJ's does not disclose its owned/leased split or average lease terms. A ±30% range on the occupancy estimate would shift build_pct by approximately ±1.5 percentage points.

**Store labor as overhead.** Following the Costco precedent (ticket 040), store employee wages are classified as overhead rather than sell. TJ's store crew members — called "crew members" internally, not associates — handle stocking, sampling, checkout, and customer interaction. They are service staff executing the retail operation, not a sales force in the plan.md sense (no trade promotion negotiation, no broker channels, no door-to-door selling). TJ's is well-known for paying above-market retail wages ($22–28/hr reported by multiple sources) and offering full benefits to part-time workers, which drives low turnover and builds the brand's cult reputation indirectly. This labor cost ($2,200M estimated, 12.2% of revenue) is the largest single cost line after merchandise COGS.

**Absent data: trade promotion / slotting fees.** Slotting fees and trade promotion are a significant sell-side cost for traditional CPG brands selling through grocery retailers. TJ's inverts this: as a retailer with 80% private label, it does not pay slotting fees — it *collects* them from the national brands occupying the remaining 20% of shelf space. Those fees would appear as an offset to cost/revenue. This is ignored here (insufficient data; the offset likely reduces effective merchandise COGS slightly, which is already modeled as better than Kroger's).

**The ±10pp caveat.** The build_pct of 94.3% should be interpreted as 94% ± 10 percentage points. The three primary sources of uncertainty are: (1) revenue estimate (±15–20% from true value); (2) merchandise COGS ratio (anchored to Kroger at 75%, but TJ's may be 72–78%); (3) occupancy estimate (rough first-principles, ±30%). Within those uncertainty bounds, build_pct could plausibly range from 88% to 97%. The directional conclusion — that TJ's is extremely build-heavy, with sell spending at roughly 5% of the build+sell base rather than 20–30% for typical CPG brands — is robust to any reasonable assumption combination.

**Structural contrast vs. CPG brands.** The Tide pod entry (ticket 001) shows P&G's Fabric & Home Care segment at 73.5% build. Trader Joe's at ~94% reflects a fundamentally different cost structure: TJ's *is* the distribution channel, so it doesn't pay someone else to sell its goods. There is no advertising agency, no slotting fee, no national sales force visiting grocery buyers — TJ's is the buyer and the seller simultaneously. The sell costs that remain (occupancy + Fearless Flyer) are tiny relative to the merchandise procurement machine underneath. This is the point of including TJ's in the dataset: it anchors the "maximum build" end of the consumer panel alongside Costco (98.3%), demonstrating that a near-zero advertising model is viable at scale in consumer goods when the retailer is also the brand.
