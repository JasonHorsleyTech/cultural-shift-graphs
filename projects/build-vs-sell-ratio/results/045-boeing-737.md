## Methodology notes

**Segment selection.** Used Boeing's Commercial Airplanes (BCA) reportable segment exclusively. Boeing reports three segments: BCA (commercial aircraft, 737/787/777/767), BDS (defense and government), and BGS (aftermarket services). The 737 MAX family sits entirely within BCA. BCA segment revenue of $22,861M and segment operating loss of ($7,969M) are taken directly from Note 21 (Segment and Revenue Information) of the FY2024 10-K. FY2024 was an exceptionally difficult year: a 53-day IAM machinists strike halted 737 production, deliveries fell from 528 frames (FY2023) to 348 frames (FY2024), and the segment absorbed ~$1.1B in charges on 777X and 767 programs. The operating loss is real and reported as-is — the build_pct calculation is unaffected by profitability.

**R&D allocation.** Boeing's Q4 2024 earnings release discloses segment-level R&D. BCA-allocated R&D was $2,386M in FY2024 (out of $3,812M consolidated). This is the dominant build driver after COGS and reflects continued investment in 737 MAX airworthiness compliance, 777X certification work, and other program engineering. All R&D is classified as build per the project rubric.

**COGS derivation (program accounting caveat).** Boeing uses program accounting (block accounting) for commercial aircraft — production costs are spread over a large accounting quantity of frames rather than expensed as incurred. This means COGS in any given year does not equal the cash cost of planes delivered in that year; favorable or adverse cumulative cost performance gets amortized across the block. For our purposes, the reported segment cost of revenue is the closest available proxy for "what it cost BCA to produce and deliver aircraft." It is not distorted for our ratio because we are computing a structural cost-mix ratio, not a cash-flow ratio. The residual COGS figure ($26,717M) is derived as: BCA total implied costs ($30,830M) minus BCA R&D ($2,386M) minus BCA-allocated G&A ($1,727M). The $26,717M includes manufacturing labor, materials, supplier parts, factory depreciation, program provisions, write-downs, and delivery/distribution costs.

**G&A allocation to BCA.** Boeing does not disclose G&A at the segment level. Allocated using BCA revenue share ($22,861M / $66,517M = 34.4%) of consolidated G&A ($5,021M), yielding ~$1,727M. The unallocated corporate line in Boeing's segment reconciliation (approximately ($943M), reflecting items like share-based compensation, pension actuarial adjustments, and deferred compensation held at the corporate level) is excluded from BCA's cost pool, consistent with Boeing's own reporting structure.

**Sell-line minimization: discounts netted from revenue.** Boeing sells aircraft at steep discounts to list price. List price for a 737 MAX 8 is ~$121M; realized prices are commonly 40–60% of list. Per ASC 606, these volume discounts and fleet incentives are netted against revenue rather than recorded as a selling expense. This means the true economic "cost to win the sale" is structurally understated in our sell figure — the discount IS the incentive, but it compresses the revenue line, not the expense line. Flagging this as a consistent systematic understatement of sell for all aerospace OEMs in this panel.

**Sales force estimate.** Boeing's commercial sales organization is not separately disclosed. Estimated at 1.5% of BCA revenue ($343M) based on the ticket rubric's 1–3% guidance. Boeing's selling headcount is small relative to revenue scale (each transaction involves hundreds of aircraft worth billions), but senior account teams, technical sales, regional offices, and customer financing structuring add up. The 1.5% figure is deliberately mid-conservative — the true number could be 1–2%.

**Advertising.** Boeing does not separately disclose advertising expense (standard for B2B industrials; FASB requires disclosure only above materiality thresholds). The company has minimal consumer advertising. The $20M institutional estimate covers aviation trade press insertions, corporate brand maintenance, and Boeing's web/digital presence — not commercial media. Major sell-side spend is airshow presence (Paris and Farnborough biannually): Boeing maintains large static displays, flight demonstration participation, and executive hospitality tents; $80M annually is a rough estimate for the airshow line normalized across years.

**BGS / customer support boundary.** Boeing's Global Services segment (BGS) handles a large portion of ongoing airline support, spares, training, and MRO work as a separate revenue stream. BGS revenue ($19,954M, 18.1% operating margin) is excluded from this analysis. This means the BCA entry here likely understates the true "build" investment in the 737 platform, because ongoing support infrastructure and aftermarket engineering that Boeing bundles into BGS is economically related to the aircraft product. Noted but not adjusted — the ticket explicitly instructs to keep BCA-only.

**Confidence: high.** BCA is a US-listed public company with detailed segment reporting in a 10-K and quarterly earnings releases. R&D is disclosed at segment level. COGS derivation is slightly indirect (residual method) but grounded in disclosed segment earnings and the segment R&D number. The only material estimation is the sell line, which is small enough that ±50% uncertainty on that estimate moves build_pct by less than 0.5 percentage points.

**build_pct context.** At 98.5%, BCA is the most build-heavy entry in the panel, confirming its role as the structural B2B baseline. The range implied by ticket (92–98%) was slightly conservative; in practice, aerospace OEM selling costs are even thinner than expected because (a) discounts are revenue-netted and (b) the sales cycle is a small specialist team on enormous transactions. Even in a catastrophically loss-making year, the cost structure is overwhelmingly manufacturing, engineering, and R&D — not selling.
