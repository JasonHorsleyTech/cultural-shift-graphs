# Will Turner

## Character Summary
- **Source work:** Pirates of the Caribbean: The Curse of the Black Pearl (2003), Dead Man's Chest (2006), At World's End (2007), with brief return appearance in Dead Men Tell No Tales (2017). Played by Orlando Bloom across all appearances. Underlying canon: Disney/Bruckheimer/Verbinski franchise; novelizations by Irene Trimble and Tui T. Sutherland are derivative.
- **Audience:** female-targeted
- **Decade slot:** 2000s
- **Cultural reach proxy:** The Curse of the Black Pearl grossed ~$654M worldwide on a ~$140M budget — a sleeper smash that launched a billion-dollar franchise. Dead Man's Chest grossed ~$1.066B (the third film ever to clear $1B at that time) and At World's End grossed ~$963M; cumulative box office across the three Will Turner-era films ≈ $2.68B. The franchise total across all five films is ~$4.5B. Will Turner remains a fixture on "best movie boyfriends of all time" rankings (Cosmopolitan, Glamour, Marie Claire, BuzzFeed, Vulture, Empire) for the 2000s decade — frequently paired with Aragorn (LOTR) and Edward Cullen (Twilight) as the defining female-targeted heartthrobs of that decade.
- **Canonical version used:** Original trilogy (2003–2007) as primary canon — the Will-Turner-as-romantic-lead arc with Elizabeth Swann across these three films. The 2017 cameo (post-Flying-Dutchman captaincy) is post-canonical to the romantic arc and not used as the trait-stack baseline. Where the films establish a trait, that establishment is treated as canon (e.g. "I practice three hours a day with a sword" — establishes master-swordsman threshold). Orlando Bloom's portrayal is the cultural reach driver.

Traits considered and excluded:

- **Height (≥ 6'2")**: Orlando Bloom is 5'11" (180 cm); Will as a character is not given a canonical height and is not framed as notably tall on screen — he and Jack Sparrow are filmed at roughly equal height, and Sparrow (Depp) is 5'10". Does NOT clear ≥6'2". Excluded.
- **Income (≥ $10M/yr)**: Blacksmith's apprentice through the start of COTBP (low-to-moderate working-class income), then pirate (variable, occasionally substantial plunder but not stable wealth), then Captain of the Flying Dutchman (not a wealth role — duty/curse). At no canonical point does Will hold $10M-tier earned income or net worth. Excluded.
- **Intelligence (≥ 130 IQ — top 2%)**: Clever, resourceful, tactically sharp ("we'll commandeer that ship — *this* one… we'll commandeer this ship!"), and pulls off non-trivial plans (the Black Pearl rescue in COTBP, the Dead Man's Chest race in DMC, the multilateral Brethren-Court politics in AWE). But not framed as exceptional cognitive intellect — closer to "earnest competent" than to a top-2% intellectual register. A reviewer applying a generous read could include this trait at 0.02; primary calc excludes per the strict-threshold reading.
- **Musical/artistic skill**: None depicted. The smithing trade is metal-craft, not visual-art or musical-virtuoso tier; folded under the artisan-smith trait, not double-counted. Excluded.
- **Languages beyond English**: Not depicted as multilingual. The franchise's polyglot is Sparrow (a few words in Spanish, Mandarin, etc.); Will is monolingual in canon. Excluded.
- **Age in prime window (28–38)**: Canonically early-to-mid 20s in COTBP. Bloom was 26 during the first film's production. Will is approximately 22–26 across the trilogy — below the 28–38 prime-window threshold. Excluded under strict read. Same situation as Jack Dawson (canonically 20) and Johnny Castle (canonically 25) — youthful-golden-boy mode below the threshold.
- **Wealth status / Captain of the Flying Dutchman**: At the END of At World's End, Will takes Davy Jones's role as the immortal ferryman of souls who died at sea, captaining the Flying Dutchman with a crew of the dead, allowed ashore for one day every ten years. This is supernatural sovereign-of-the-dead status, not real-world wealth or earned position, AND it occurs after the romantic-lead arc concludes (Will and Elizabeth marry on the deck of the Black Pearl during the maelstrom battle, mid-AWE — before the captaincy curse takes effect). Not counted as a wealth/status slider trait. Same logic as not counting Aragorn's coronation as a separate trait (his pre-existing royal lineage is the actual slider, which Will lacks). Excluded.

## Personality / Characterization (Excluded from Calc)

- **Devoted-romantic / "I love her" earnestness**: Defining personality trait (driving force of the entire trilogy). Audience preferences split — many find devoted earnestness deeply attractive, some find it too earnest or "boy-scoutish." Not a slider.
- **Honor-bound principled hero**: Archetype, not a slider. The "I won't surrender to pirates" → "I AM a pirate" arc is characterization.
- **Reluctant-pirate-by-blood discovery / "Bootstrap Bill is my father"**: Plot architecture, not a universal-desirable trait. Mysterious-paternal-past per plan = personality, excluded.
- **Brave but inexperienced underdog**: Plot framing, not a slider.
- **"Will the Eunuch" / running-joke earnest stiffness**: Comedic characterization, not a slider.
- **Forgiveness arc with Bootstrap Bill / filial-loyalty drive**: Plot/personality, not slider.
- **Ten-year separation from Elizabeth (post-AWE Flying Dutchman captaincy)**: Tragic-romance framing, not a universal-desirable trait. Some audiences find the separation deeply romantic; others find it bleak.
- **Marries Elizabeth during the maelstrom battle (officiated by Barbossa mid-swordfight)**: Iconic franchise moment, plot beat, not a slider.

## Joint Probability

- **Method:** threshold (female-target stack)
- **Naive product of marginals:**
  0.20 × 1e-3 × 1e-3 × 0.05
  = 0.20 × 1e-3 = 2e-4
  × 1e-3 = 2e-7
  × 0.05 = **1e-8 (≈ 1 in 100 million)**
- **Correlation adjustment:**
  - **Combat × physical-fitness top quintile (positively correlated, pulls less rare).** P(top-quintile fitness | master-tier swordsman) ≈ 0.95 per base-rates.md combat entry. Naive product OVERSTATES rarity by ~4–5x for this pair. Adjustment: **~4x less rare** on this pair.
  - **Smith × physical-fitness top quintile (positively correlated, pulls less rare).** Sustained smithing builds upper-body strength and stamina; P(top-quintile fitness | active artisan smith) ≈ 0.5–0.7 per the new base-rate entry. Naive product OVERSTATES rarity by ~3x for this pair. The fitness trait is being shared across two correlated skills, so the headline correction here is partly already absorbed into the combat × fitness adjustment — net additional ~1.5x less rare.
  - **Combat × smith (positively correlated, pulls less rare).** Period maker-user tradition (smiths historically forged the swords they used); modern HEMA/historical-weaponry subculture overlap; r ≈ 0.15–0.25 estimated. Naive product OVERSTATES rarity by ~2x. Adjustment: **~2x less rare** on this pair.
  - **Time-budget penalty for skill-stacking (combat-expert + smith-expert, NEGATIVELY correlated, pulls rarer).** Two expert-skill traits at ~10,000-hour deliberate-practice tier. The base-rates time-budget warnings (5–15x understatement) apply most severely at 3+ skill counts; with only 2 expert skills, the penalty is mild — closer to ~1.5–2x rarer than naive. Adjustment: **~1.5x rarer** for time-budget.
  - **Family-unencumbered ↔ all others:** approximately independent to first order. **No adjustment.**
  - **Net direction:** Three positive-correlation adjustments (combat × fitness ~4x, smith × fitness ~1.5x marginal, combat × smith ~2x) collectively pull ~8–12x less rare than naive. The single negative-correlation adjustment (time-budget) pulls ~1.5x rarer. Net adjustment: **~5–7x less rare than naive product**, with positive correlations dominating because the smith-and-warrior trade pair sits in a coherent "physical-craft cluster" that real-world practitioners do co-occupy (HEMA hobbyists, professional armorers, period-reenactor master smiths). The time-budget penalty bites less hard with only 2 skills than at the Aragorn/Edward 4+ skill tier.
- **Adjusted estimate:** ~**2e-8** (≈ 1 in 50 million). Bracket: [5e-9, 1e-7].
- **Population implication:** Out of ~127M US adult men, expected matches ≈ **2–3 individuals**. Out of ~3.5B global adult men, expected matches ≈ **70 individuals**. Will Turner's universal-desirable stack is real-but-rare — orders of magnitude more achievable than Christian Grey (~10⁻²⁴), Edward Cullen (~10⁻¹⁸), or Aragorn (~10⁻¹⁹–10⁻²⁰), and meaningfully rarer than Jack Dawson (~10⁻⁵) or Han Solo (~10⁻¹⁰). Within the female-target distribution his position is **moderate-to-extreme tail** — closer to the action-hero athletic-craftsman cluster (Han Solo, Aragorn) than to the slider-maxing fantasy cluster (Edward, Christian, Rhysand). The fantasy element of his appeal is concentrated in the *period setting* (a real human master swordsman + master smith + orphan + athletic in modern-US population terms is rare; in 1729 Caribbean colonial society it would be much more common — but the project uses modern US base rates as the comparison frame, per methodology).

## Confidence Assessment
- **Overall:** medium — trait identification is well-supported by film canon, but the headline number's precision is bounded by judgment calls on which skills count at expert tier (smithing), and the period-setting-vs-modern-base-rate framing introduces a layer of interpretive uncertainty.
- **Trait identification:** medium-high — film canon is unambiguous on combat-expert ("I practice three hours a day with a sword") and on smith-expert (he made every sword sold by the Port Royal smithy, then forges blades aboard the Black Pearl and Flying Dutchman). Family-unencumbered is unambiguous at the canonical romantic-lead moment. Fitness is unambiguous from the visible-on-screen athletic build. Excluded traits (height, income, intelligence, languages, age, wealth) are also unambiguously failed.
- **Base rates:** medium — combat-expert (1e-3) and family-unencumbered (0.05) are well-established in base-rates.md with provenance. Fitness top quintile (0.20) is definitional. Smith-expert (1e-3) is a new entry added with this ticket; calibrated to the chef/mechanic/pilot/combatant/healer expert-tier convention with BLS, ABANA, and AFA provenance, but it is a derived midpoint with factor-of-2 calibration uncertainty (true rate could be 5e-4 to 2e-3 depending on whether broader artisan-metalworker subsets are counted).
- **Correlation adjustments:** medium — directional reasoning is well-supported (combat × fitness positive; smith × fitness positive; combat × smith positive via period and HEMA traditions; time-budget penalty for 2 skills is mild). Magnitudes (5–7x net less rare than naive) are judgment, anchored to the comparable adjustments for Han Solo (~3–5x less rare for skills cluster) and Aragorn (~2–3x net rarer with much heavier skill-stacking).

## Notes / Edge Cases

- **Period-setting bias.** Will Turner's stack of "master swordsman + master smith + athletic + orphan" was significantly more *common* in 1729 colonial Caribbean society than in modern US society. A skilled blacksmith-apprentice who also practiced swordplay daily was not unusual in the Age of Sail; the project uses modern US base rates as the comparison frame because the question is "out of N modern people, how many match this stack?" — but the period-setting bias should be flagged. This is structurally similar to how Mr. Darcy and Bridgerton-Simon were treated (their inherited-aristocrat stacks read as more extreme in modern terms than they would in Regency terms). For Will, the period bias works in the opposite direction from the wealth-aristocrat case: rather than amplifying his rarity (Darcy), the period setting deflates the *effective* rarity of his trade-skill stack. Either way, both the period and modern framings are informative, and the modern framing is the project's chosen yardstick.

- **Smith-expert vs. amateur-blacksmith sensitivity.** I set smith-expert at 1e-3 to match the chef/mechanic/pilot/combatant/healer expert-tier calibration. A reviewer applying a stricter "renowned/master-tier swordsmith" reading (1e-4 — comparable to Forged in Fire winners or museum-collected armorers) would shift the joint by 10x to ~2e-9. A reviewer reading Will as "a talented apprentice working under Mr. Brown's name" rather than full master tier would soften the rate to ~5e-3 (skilled-tradesman tier without master credentials), shifting the joint by 5x in the other direction to ~1e-7. The film canon "I made every one of them" line strongly supports the master-tier read, so 1e-3 is the honest middle.

- **Combat-expert calibration.** Will is canonically "the second-best swordsman in the Caribbean" (after Jack Sparrow, who concedes he wins by trickery rather than skill). The 1e-3 expert-combatant rate is appropriate, matching how Aragorn and Christian Grey's martial skills are calibrated. A "renowned/legendary" tier (1e-4) is defensible given he's framed as the best swordfighter Port Royal has produced — but parity with the trilogy's other combat-expert characters argues for 1e-3.

- **Family-unencumbered nuance.** The Bootstrap Bill discovery in DMC complicates the orphan reading, but Bootstrap is canonically bound to the Flying Dutchman with no real-world claim on Will's life, and he was unknown-to-Will at the canonical romantic-lead moment (start of COTBP). The 5% rate stands; a stricter reviewer who excludes this trait because Will's father is technically alive (just supernaturally constrained) would shift the joint by 20x to ~5e-7 (~280 expected matches in US adult men). The honest read is that at the canonical romantic-lead moment, Will is functionally orphan-coded — included.

- **Fitness threshold call.** Bloom in 2003–2007 was lean-cut rather than action-hero bulky (the Aragorn/Hugh-Jackman-Wolverine register). The "visible muscle, athletic" reading per NHANES is borderline; Will clears it on athletic capability (sustained sword combat, climbing, swimming, free-running across rigging) but a reviewer applying a strict "body-builder muscle" reading could exclude him. Excluding fitness would shift the joint by 5x to ~1e-7 (~13 expected matches in US adult men). Included under the "athletic" reading.

- **Age below window.** Like Jack Dawson (canonically 20) and Johnny Castle (canonically 25), Will Turner sits below the 28–38 prime-window threshold. This is the third "youthful golden boy" mode example in the female-target dataset. The pattern is real signal: a meaningful subset of female-target heartthrobs are under-30 and the prime-window threshold misses them. Don't reweight retrospectively per ticket-by-ticket convention, but flag for the final graph annotations — there may be a "young athletic apprentice / journeyman" sub-distribution within the female-target stack that complements the "established billionaire-virtuoso" sub-distribution.

- **Wealth/status: the Captain-of-the-Flying-Dutchman question.** At the END of At World's End, Will inherits Davy Jones's role as the immortal psychopomp of the seas, captaining the Flying Dutchman with a crew of the dead, allowed ashore for one day every ten years. This is supernatural sovereign-of-the-dead status — not real-world wealth, not earned position, and not a slider trait the way Aragorn's pre-existing royal lineage is. Crucially, the romantic-lead arc concludes BEFORE the captaincy curse takes effect (Will and Elizabeth marry mid-maelstrom in AWE, before the role transfer). Counting Flying-Dutchman-captaincy as a wealth/status trait would be similar to counting Aragorn's coronation as separate from his lineage — but Will lacks any pre-existing wealth/status to elevate. Excluded.

- **Comparison to other female-target leads in this dataset:**
  - **Jack Dawson (~1.5e-5):** Will is ~1,000x rarer because Will adds two expert skills (combat + smith) and athletic fitness on top of the orphan-coded baseline. Jack's stack is two-trait (artist + orphan); Will's is four-trait. Both are personality-archetype-driven heartthrobs with thin-but-real universal-desirable stacks.
  - **Johnny Castle (~3e-7):** Will is ~10x rarer — comparable structural stack (athletic + expert skill + working-class), but Will adds a second expert skill (smithing) where Johnny only has the dance-virtuoso tier. Both are below-prime-window youthful golden boys.
  - **Han Solo (~1e-10):** Will is ~5,000x *less* rare — Han adds pilot expertise + family-unencumbered + 2+ languages stack on top of athletic + combat. Will's stack is shorter.
  - **Aragorn (~4e-20):** Will is ~10¹² *less* rare — Aragorn adds height + billionaire-tier inherited wealth + intelligence + healing + 3+ languages on top of combat + fitness + family-unencumbered. Same decade slot (2000s), opposite ends of the female-target rarity axis.
  - **Edward Cullen (~1e-18):** Will is ~10¹⁰ *less* rare. Same decade pattern as Aragorn comparison: 2000s female-target heartthrobs span 13+ orders of magnitude in joint probability, with Will at the achievable end and Edward/Aragorn at the fantasy-framing tail.

- **Personality dominance.** Will Turner's enduring "best 2000s heartthrob" status across 20+ years of fan retrospective writing is overwhelmingly driven by *personality, plot, and archetype* — the earnest blacksmith who turns pirate for love, the devoted-to-the-end-of-the-world commitment to Elizabeth, the principled honor-bound hero who discovers his pirate heritage and embraces it, the tragic 10-year-separation Flying-Dutchman ending. Like Jack Dawson and Han Solo, the universal-desirable rarity number undersells his cultural footprint by a substantial margin. Will is a strong example of the personality/archetype heartthrob rather than the slider-maxing-fantasy heartthrob — his rarity comes from "rare combination of athletic skill-trade hero with orphan-romantic personality," not from being a billionaire-virtuoso-polyglot.

- **2000s decade balance signal.** Within the 2000s slot, Will Turner sits at the achievable end (~2e-8) opposite Aragorn (~4e-20) and Edward Cullen (~1e-18) at the fantasy-framing end. This is a real within-decade range finding: the 2000s female-target heartthrob distribution is bimodal — period-action (Will, Aragorn-without-the-fantasy-stack) at one mode, supernatural-fantasy (Edward, Aragorn-with-the-fantasy-stack) at the other. Will is the cleanest "achievable hero" anchor for the 2000s decade in this dataset.
