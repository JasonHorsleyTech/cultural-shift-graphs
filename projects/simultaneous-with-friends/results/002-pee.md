## Setup

Peeing is one of the most frequent involuntary-ish bodily acts adults perform — about 7 times a day, around 21 seconds each. The question is: how often are you and a close friend both mid-stream at the exact same moment?

## Inputs

- **Frequency:** 6–8 times/day is the clinical normal range (NHS, Cleveland Clinic). Midpoint: **7/day**, waking hours only. Night-time urination is suppressed by antidiuretic hormone (ADH) circadian rhythm; healthy adults produce far less urine during sleep.
- **Duration:** The "Law of Urination" (Yang et al., Georgia Tech, 2014, *PNAS*) found that all mammals over ~1 kg empty their bladders in approximately **21 seconds**, regardless of bladder size — larger animals have longer, wider urethras that increase flow velocity. Sharp HealthCare and urologists cite 20–22 seconds as the healthy human norm. Using **21 seconds**.
- **Active window:** 16 hours/day (waking). Peeing is spread across this window with mild clustering: first void on waking, one after morning coffee, one after each main meal, and a few miscellaneous ones. No single tight cluster dominates, so `h = 16` is appropriate.
- **Caveats:** Frequency rises with hydration (heavy drinkers can hit 10+/day), drops with dehydration or concentrated diet. Older adults and women trend slightly higher. Gender differences in flow rate (women average ~18 mL/s, men ~21 mL/s) don't materially affect duration. The 21-second figure holds across healthy adults.

Sources: Medical News Today, NHS, Georgia Tech/PNAS Law of Urination, Sharp HealthCare, PMC circadian rhythms study.

## Math

Using the repeatable-activity formula from the plan:

```
r = f_day / (h × 3600)
r = 7 / (16 × 3600) = 7 / 57600 ≈ 1.215 × 10⁻⁴ per second

t_overlap = (24 / h) × 1 / (2 × r² × d × N)
```

**t_50 (any of 50 friends):**
```
= (24/16) × 1 / (2 × (1.215e-4)² × 21 × 50)
= 1.5 × 1 / (2 × 1.477e-8 × 21 × 50)
= 1.5 × 1 / (3.101e-5)
= 1.5 × 32,247
≈ 48,364 seconds
≈ 13.4 hours
```

**t_1 (best friend only, N=1):**
```
= (24/16) × 1 / (2 × (1.215e-4)² × 21 × 1)
= 1.5 × 1 / (6.202e-7)
≈ 2,418,192 seconds
≈ 28 days
```

## Result

- **With any of your 50 close friends:** You're both peeing at the same time roughly **every 13–14 hours** — about twice a day.
- **With your single best friend:** A simultaneous pee happens roughly **every 4 weeks**.

## Surprise check

The 13-hour figure for 50 friends is faster than most people would guess — it sounds like peeing should be rare overlap territory. But 7 events × 21 seconds each = 147 seconds of peeing per 16-hour day, and 50 friends simultaneously casting that net makes overlaps surprisingly frequent. The monthly cadence for a single best friend feels more intuitively right and confirms the math is in the right neighborhood.
