# Lara Croft

## Character Summary
- **Source work:** Tomb Raider series (Core Design / Eidos), debuting with Tomb Raider (1996, PlayStation / Sega Saturn / PC) and continuing across Tomb Raider II (1997), III (1998), The Last Revelation (1999), and Chronicles (2000) — the Core Design "classic" run that defines the 1990s decade-slot reference. Subsequently the Crystal Dynamics reboot trilogy (Tomb Raider 2013, Rise 2015, Shadow 2018) and the live-action films (Tomb Raider 2001 with Angelina Jolie, The Cradle of Life 2003, Tomb Raider 2018 with Alicia Vikander). Per the heartthrobs.md operational definition, Lara is included as "protagonist functioning as fantasy object regardless of strict plot role" — same operational basis as Buffy / Bayonetta / Princess Peach.
- **Audience:** male-targeted
- **Decade slot:** 1990s
- **Cultural reach proxy:** Tomb Raider franchise has surpassed **100 million units sold** as of the early 2020s (Square Enix / Embracer cumulative reporting). The original 1996 game is consistently cited as one of the most influential video games of all time and as the title that brought 3D action-adventure into the mainstream. Lara Croft holds **6 Guinness World Records**, including "Most Successful Human Videogame Heroine" and "Most Detailed Videogame Heroine," and has appeared on **over 2,300 magazine covers** — far more than any other video-game character and competitive with top-tier human celebrities. The character was the explicit cover-model of late-1990s gaming magazines (PSM, GamePro, Edge, etc.), and the Tomb Raider 2001 film grossed ~$275M worldwide on a ~$115M budget — establishing her as the first video-game character to anchor a tentpole live-action film with mainstream success. Cosplay / fan-art presence has been continuously top-tier since 1997. By any measure Lara is the **most iconic female video-game character of all time** and one of the defining male-fantasy figures of the 1990s.
- **Canonical version used:** Per ticket instruction and the project's anchor-on-original convention, the **Core Design 1990s Lara** is the canonical reference. Per Core Design's official 1996 biography (the canonical source for the original run): born February 14, 1968 in Wimbledon, London → **age 28 in the original 1996 game**, fitting cleanly within the project's 20–28 base population. Official Core Design vital statistics: 5'9", 130 lbs, brown hair, brown eyes, measurements 34D–24–35, status "Single," AB– blood type. The 1990s Core Design Lara is the load-bearing male-fantasy projection (turquoise leotard, dual pistols, exaggerated 3D-model proportions, aristocratic-British-explorer aesthetic). The 2013 Crystal Dynamics reboot deliberately walked back the exaggerated proportions and re-aged the character to early-20s for tonal reset; the reboot Lara is a different fantasy projection (gritty survivor rather than confident sex symbol) and is not the load-bearing 1990s reference.

By Tomb Raider II (1997, age 29) she edges out of the window, but the 1996 reference is the load-bearing decade-slot anchor.
- **Plays the protagonist's hobbies / aligned interest profile:** N/A in the standard sense. Lara IS the player character and the audience-aligned hobby IS adventuring. Per the convention used for Black Widow (ticket 010), Wonder Woman (ticket 020), and Ciri (ticket 022), this filter is excluded for action/adventure protagonist characters when there is no specific niche-hobby alignment beat. ## Personality / Characterization (Excluded from Calc)

- **Aristocratic-British detachment / dry wit / aloof demeanor:** Defining characterization beat. Some male audience finds the cool-aristocrat framing attractive; preferences split.
- **Loner / "only truly alive when travelling alone":** Self-sufficient/solitary archetype. Some find this attractive; some find it cold. Preferences split → personality, not universal.
- **Adventurer / treasure-hunter / Indiana-Jones-but-female aesthetic:** Plot/genre framing, not a slider trait. The career identity itself is personality (similar to how "writer with a heart of gold" is Jack Dawson's personality).
- **Mysterious/dark-past origin (Himalayan plane crash, abandoned aristocratic life, disowned by father):** Per plan, "mysterious dark past" is explicitly flagged as personality and excluded from the calc. Lara's origin story is one of the canonical examples of this trope.
- **Iconic visual styling (turquoise leotard / dual pistols / signature plait / fingerless gloves / boots):** Visual/staging characterization — load-bearing fantasy ornament for the character. Same category as Slave Leia outfit, Black Widow catsuit, Mikaela engine-hood scene.
- **Aristocratic refusal of the "expected upper-class life" / disownment by father:** Backstory characterization, not a universal slider.
- **Asexual-functioning / no canonical romantic interests:** Personality coding (deliberate authorial choice — Toby Gard explicitly designed her to be "sexy only because of her power"). Some male audience finds the unattainable-loner framing attractive; preferences split.
- **Confidence / cocksure / unbothered:** Defining personality beat. Distinguishes her from Cool Girl (which is agreeable-low-friction) — Lara is confident-unattached. Some find this attractive; some find it intimidating.

## Joint Probability

- **Method:** filter (male-target stack)
- **Naive product of marginals:**
  0.20 × 0.02 × 0.34 × 0.70 × 0.25 × 0.25
  = (0.20 × 0.02) = 0.004
  × 0.34 = 0.00136
  × 0.70 = 9.52e-4
  × 0.25 = 2.38e-4
  × 0.25 = **5.95e-5**
  (≈ 1 in 16,800, applied within a base population of US women aged 20–28)
  Note: the body-composition factor (0.02) is already a joint rate for BMI 18.5–22 AND DD+, NOT a product of marginals. Multiplying separate marginals would have given a rarer naive estimate by ~5–10x.
- **Correlation adjustment:** Mixed-direction adjustments, with the body-composition joint already applied at the joint rate (so no further BMI×bust adjustment needed). Remaining pairs:
  - Top-quintile attractiveness ↔ body-composition joint: positive halo (slim-and-busty body type already contributes to high attractiveness ratings in male-rating studies, partial overlap). Naive product OVERSTATES rarity by ~1.2–1.4x for this pair.
  - Top-quintile attractiveness ↔ low neuroticism: positive halo (r ≈ 0.10–0.15). Naive product OVERSTATES by ~1.2x.
  - No-kids ↔ currently single: positive (~1.2x OVERSTATES).
  - Top-quintile attractiveness ↔ currently single: NEGATIVE in real world (more attractive women more likely partnered). Naive product UNDERSTATES rarity by ~1.2–1.4x.
  - Top-quintile attractiveness ↔ doesn't-follow-girl-culture: NEGATIVE in real world (Mean-Girls / popular-girl framing maps onto high mainstream-girl-culture engagement). Naive product UNDERSTATES rarity by ~1.3–1.5x — and the Lara stack DELIBERATELY violates this real-world correlation (the male-fantasy "top-tier hot AND not-into-mainstream-girly-stuff AND adventurer-coded" combination is the same genre-violation pattern as Black Widow / Wonder Woman / Mikaela / Ciri).
  - **Net direction:** The positive halos (attractiveness↔body-comp, attractiveness↔low-N, no-kids↔single) push toward "less rare than naive product." The negative correlations (attractiveness↔single, attractiveness↔not-girly) push toward "more rare than naive product." For the FICTIONAL Lara stack as written, the negative-correlation effect modestly DOMINATES because the adventurer-aristocrat archetype is constructed precisely on the violation of real-world tradeoffs between conventional attractiveness and tomboy/expedition-coded interest profiles. Net: roughly **1.3x rarer** than the naive product.
- **Adjusted estimate:** **~7.7e-5** (≈ 1 in 13,000 women aged 20–28). Bracket: [3e-5, 2e-4].
- **Population implication:** Out of ~14M US women aged 20–28, roughly **1,080** match Lara Croft's universal-desirable stack as captured by the male-target methodology (range ~420–2,800 across the bracket). This places Lara structurally in the **same band as Black Widow (~1,190 matches) and Wonder Woman (~1,190 matches)** despite reaching that scarcity through a *different* trait combination: Lara stacks body-composition joint (0.02) + low-N + not-girly without the Cool Girl filter, while BW/WW stack Cool Girl (0.10) + low-N + not-girly without the body-composition joint. The arithmetic resolves to similar joint probabilities, demonstrating that the 1990s "buxom adventurer" template (Lara), the 1990s "buxom lifeguard" template (CJ Parker), and the 2010s "warrior-princess Cool Girl" template (BW/WW) are different routes to the same male-fantasy rarity band. **Critically: this male-target-methodology number radically under-counts Lara's total stack — see Notes / Edge Cases for the symmetric-trait-list comparison, which would put her among the very rarest characters in the entire dataset.**

## Confidence Assessment
- **Overall:** medium
- **Trait identification:** high — Lara Croft's classic Core Design traits are exhaustively documented across Wikipedia, WikiRaider, Tomb Raider Wiki / Fandom, the Core Design official biography (1996 onward, mirrored on Tomb Raider Chronicles), Square Enix / Embracer franchise materials, and decades of cultural-criticism coverage. Canon is unambiguous on age (28 in 1996), single status (official biography lists "Single"), no-kids, body composition (5'9" / 130 lbs / 34D–24–35 per official bio + iconically exaggerated 1996 3D model), low-neuroticism characterization (Himalayan-survivor origin and aristocratic-stoic coding), and tomboy-coded interest profile.
- **Base rates:** medium-high — all six base rates used are already in `base-rates.md` from prior tickets, with strong NHANES / Pew / Census / Big-5 / bra-fit / OkCupid provenance. The body-composition joint (0.02 for BMI 18.5–22 AND DD+) is a composite estimate rather than a single-source measurement, which is the lowest-confidence base rate in the stack — same caveat as CJ Parker (ticket 016) which used the same joint.
- **Correlation adjustments:** medium-low — the directional claim that the fictional Lara stack violates real-world negative correlations between attractiveness and tomboy-coded interests is well-supported in mate-market literature, but the exact magnitude (~1.3x net rarer) is judgment, not a measured joint distribution. Bracketing of [3e-5, 2e-4] is wide for that reason.
- **Methodological-asymmetry caveat:** **high relevance, medium confidence reduction.** Lara is the **most-skill-stacked male-target character in the dataset by a meaningful margin** — aristocratic wealth, polyglot, expert archaeologist, expert combatant, expert mountaineer, expert marksman, big-game hunter, master thief, top-tier physical fitness, two elite educations. The male-target trait list captures NONE of these as universal-desirable filters. A symmetric-trait-list analysis would migrate Lara dramatically leftward on the rarity axis (see Notes / Edge Cases for a numerical estimate). Same caveat structure as Black Widow / Wonder Woman / Ciri but more pronounced.

## Notes / Edge Cases

- **Methodological asymmetry — THE central caveat for Lara:** Lara Croft is the most skill-stacked male-target character in the entire dataset. The Core Design canonical bio explicitly stacks: aristocratic wealth (vast inherited fortune from Lord Henshingly Croft), elite double-education (Gordonstoun + Swiss finishing school), polyglot ("fluent in several languages"), expert archaeologist, expert mountaineer / rock climber, expert marksman / mercenary work, expert big-game hunter, master thief, extreme skier, top-tier physical fitness. **The male-target trait list captures NONE of these as universal-desirable filters** because the project's premise is that male-target media doesn't typically *stack* skills onto female love interests as universal-desirable sliders the way female-target media stacks them onto male leads. For Lara specifically, this asymmetry is uniquely consequential because she is essentially Indiana Jones genderswapped (a male-fantasy version of the Christian-Grey / Edward-Cullen achievement stack: aristocrat-billionaire-polyglot-master-of-multiple-disciplines). If a SYMMETRIC trait-list analysis were applied — counting wealth, intelligence, multilingualism, multiple expert skills, top-quintile fitness — Lara would gain roughly **6–8 additional rarity multipliers** (e.g. ~1e-4 for billionaire-tier × 0.03 for 2+ languages × ~1e-3 for archaeology-expertise × ~1e-3 for marksman/combatant × ~0.20 for fitness × ~0.02 for IQ ≥130 implied by archaeologist career path), and her joint probability would collapse by **5–7 orders of magnitude** — putting her in the **same band as Christian Grey / Edward Cullen / Mr. Darcy on the rarity axis**, near the floor of the entire dataset. This is a defensible alternative analysis. The plan's call (asymmetric trait lists) is taken because the male-target *audience* does not appear to stack those traits as universal sliders — Lara is a male-fantasy figure first because she is hot-and-buxom-and-tomboy-coded, second because she is competent. The math captures the sliders that actually drive male-fantasy projection. **Flag this asymmetry in the final-graph annotations as the most consequential single methodology choice in the dataset.** A reviewer who insists on symmetric analysis would put Lara among the rarest 3 characters overall.

- **Body composition joint and the Toby Gard 150% anecdote:** The widely-circulated anecdote that Toby Gard accidentally increased Lara's chest dimensions by 150% in the original 1996 design appears to be apocryphal — Gard himself has stated the figure came from a "silly remark in an interview." Regardless of literal accuracy, the cultural perception of Lara's iconically exaggerated 3D-model proportions IS the male-fantasy projection on the character, and the Core Design official biography's measurements (34D–24–35 with BMI 19.2) place her at the upper edge of the BMI 18.5–22 AND large-bust joint regardless. The 0.02 joint base rate from `base-rates.md` (calibrated to BMI 18.5–22 AND DD+ proxy as a male-fantasy projection) is the appropriate input. Same treatment as CJ Parker (ticket 016), where Pamela Anderson's bust was famously augmented but the male-fantasy projection was on the canonical-natural reading of the character.

- **Protagonist-as-fantasy-object framing:** Same caveat as Buffy / Bayonetta / Princess Peach / Ciri (ticket 022). Lara is the player character with no canonical romantic partner — her "love interest" placement on the male-target list relies on the heartthrobs.md operational definition: *"fictional female character functioning as fantasy object regardless of strict plot role."* For Lara this framing is uncontroversially correct in the cultural sense (1990s/2000s male-fantasy iconicity, 2,300+ magazine covers, sex-symbol status across multiple male-target media platforms) but is non-standard in the canonical-romance-lead sense. A reviewer who applied a stricter "must be a canonical romantic figure" filter would remove Lara from the list and substitute another 1990s character — but this would be a much harder defense for Lara than for Ciri, given Lara's vastly larger male-fantasy footprint and zero competing canonical-romance interpretation.

- **Multiple canonical versions and the Core-Design anchor:** Lara exists in at least three major canonical layers — Core Design 1996–2003 (the classic exaggerated-proportions buxom-aristocrat version), Crystal Dynamics 2006–2008 (Anniversary / Legend / Underworld, a softer reboot of the classic), and Crystal Dynamics 2013–2018 (the gritty-survivor reboot trilogy with deliberately walked-back proportions and re-aged early-20s framing). Per ticket instruction and project decade-slot anchoring, the **Core Design 1990s version is the load-bearing reference**. The 2013 reboot Lara is a distinct fantasy projection (gritty survivor / vulnerability-coded protagonist) that would NOT clear the body-composition joint (deliberately not-DD+) and would partially clear vulnerability/damsel coding — anchoring on the reboot would yield a substantially different joint probability (~3e-3 / ~1 in 330, comparable to Wanda or Ciri) within a different base population (re-aged to 21). The 2001 / 2018 film versions present yet other variants. Anchoring on the 1990s Core Design version is consistent with the project's decade-slot logic and the ticket instruction, and is necessary for "1990s heartthrob" placement.

- **Age boundary at 28:** Lara is canonically 28 in the 1996 game — exactly at the upper edge of the 20–28 base population window. By Tomb Raider II (1997) she is 29 and technically out of the window. Per project convention (anchor on the decade-slot release year), 28 in 1996 is the canonical reference and the joint applies cleanly. A reviewer who insisted on average-canonical-age across the 1990s Core Design run (~30) would shift the base population to 28–38 (~12M US women) and adjust the no-kids and currently-single filters slightly downward, producing a marginally different joint at the same order of magnitude.

- **Comparison to other 1990s male-target heartthrobs in the dataset:** Lara's adjusted joint (~7.7e-5) is structurally comparable to but slightly less rare than CJ Parker (~1.1e-6, ticket 016), because CJ adds the Cool Girl + low-N + high-SOI personality cluster on top of the body-composition joint that both characters share. Within the 1990s decade, CJ's stack is rarer because Pamela Anderson / CJ adds three personality filters (Cool Girl, high-SOI, plus the SOI is itself another 0.25) that Lara does not clear. Lara's stack is, however, dramatically rarer than Linda Barrett (ticket 024, ~1.7e-2 / ~1 in 60) because Linda lacks both the body-composition joint AND the not-girly filter that Lara clears. The 1990s male-target distribution thus spans roughly 4 orders of magnitude across its 5 character slots, with Lara sitting in the rare-but-not-rarest band.

- **Comparison to Black Widow (ticket 010) and Wonder Woman (ticket 020) — the cross-decade structural twin:** Lara's adjusted joint (~7.7e-5) lands within ~1.5x of Black Widow's and Wonder Woman's (both ~8.5e-5), DESPITE Lara stacking through a different combination (body-composition joint + low-N + not-girly, no Cool Girl) vs. BW/WW (Cool Girl + low-N + not-girly, no body-composition joint). The arithmetic resolves to the same band, demonstrating that the male-fantasy "stacked rare-girl" template is *structurally consistent* across the 1990s adventurer-archetype, the 2000s/2010s warrior-princess archetype, and the 2010s super-spy archetype — all reach roughly the same joint probability through different specific filter combinations. This is a strong dataset-level finding for the final graph: the male-fantasy stack appears to converge on a characteristic ~1e-4 rarity floor regardless of decade, with characterization (adventurer vs. warrior-princess vs. spy) doing the differentiating work rather than the universal-slider profile.

- **Comparison to female-target stacks of the same era:** A 1990s female-target counterpart to Lara on the rarity axis would be Mr. Darcy (ticket 005, ~1e-9 / 1 in a billion in the symmetric analysis) or Han Solo (ticket 003) — comfortably orders of magnitude rarer on the FEMALE-target methodology because that methodology counts the achievement stack (income, intelligence, languages, expert skills, etc.) that the MALE-target methodology deliberately omits. The within-Lara symmetric-trait-list comparison (see methodological asymmetry caveat above) suggests Lara would land near Christian Grey / Mr. Darcy on the rarity axis if treated symmetrically — making Lara the **strongest single test case in the dataset for the project's central methodological asymmetry note**.
