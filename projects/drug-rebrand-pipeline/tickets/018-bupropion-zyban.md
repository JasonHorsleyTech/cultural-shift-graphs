# Ticket 018 — Bupropion (Wellbutrin → Zyban)

## Demon identity

Not a street drug. The original identity is **Wellbutrin** (bupropion HCl). FDA approved 1985 for depression by Burroughs Wellcome (now GSK). Briefly withdrawn 1986 due to seizure concerns at high doses, reintroduced 1989 with lower-dose labeling. So the "demon" component is a *clinical-safety scandal* rather than a street-drug one — this is the project's milder rebrand example.

## Medicine identity

**Zyban** (sustained-release bupropion HCl, 150 mg). FDA approved May 1997 for smoking cessation. Same active molecule as Wellbutrin SR. GSK's evergreening play — discovered that bupropion reduced nicotine cravings during depression trials, then submitted a new NDA for smoking cessation under a new brand.

## Arc type

`failed_medicine_rebranded`. Bupropion didn't *fail* — Wellbutrin is one of the more successful antidepressants — but the rebrand is into a different indication entirely, fitting the second arc pattern.

## Chemistry hypothesis

**Identical.** Bupropion HCl in both Wellbutrin and Zyban. Same molecule, same SR formulation, just different brand and labeled indication.

## Source hierarchy

1. **FDA Wellbutrin approval (1985), withdrawal (1986), reintroduction (1989), Zyban approval (1997)**.
2. **GSK / Burroughs Wellcome historical materials** for the discovery-of-smoking-cessation-effect story.
3. **IQVIA data** for Wellbutrin vs. Zyban prescription split.
4. **Smoking cessation guidelines** showing Zyban's position.

## Caveats

- This is one of the lower-intensity rebrand cases — the original wasn't demonized in any meaningful cultural sense, just had a clinical-safety hiccup. Reflect that in result framing.
- Zyban prescriptions have declined significantly with the launch of Chantix (varenicline) in 2006 — the rebrand was successful but is being eaten by newer competition.
- Awareness that Wellbutrin and Zyban are the same molecule is probably near zero outside pharmacy / clinical circles.
