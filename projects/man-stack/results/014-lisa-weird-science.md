# Lisa

## Character Summary
- **Source work:** Weird Science (1985), live-action feature film written and directed by John Hughes; Universal Pictures; portrayed by Kelly LeBrock. Based loosely on the 1951 EC Comics story of the same name.
- **Audience:** male-targeted
- **Decade slot:** 1980s
- **Cultural reach proxy:** Weird Science grossed ~$23.8M domestic on a ~$7.5M budget (modest theatrical, but a Hughes-era cult classic with a 40-year afterlife in retrospectives, syndication, and meme culture). Kelly LeBrock as Lisa cemented her as one of the foundational male-fantasy icons of the 1980s; she had already done the "The Woman in Red" (1984) and was repeatedly cited in male-targeted retrospectives and "sexiest of the 80s" lists for the rest of the decade. The character's premise — two teenage boys use a computer to generate the perfect woman — has had outsize cultural impact as a *meta-textual* artifact: Lisa is one of the most-cited reference points whenever cultural critics talk about the male fantasy stack as a construct (compare Stepford Wives, Galatea, Pygmalion). The 1994 USA Network sitcom adaptation ran 88 episodes (1994–1998) but is not the canonical version. The character is a recurring shorthand for "the perfect woman built by male fantasy" in cultural criticism (Vox, Atlantic, Vulture retrospectives on John Hughes; multiple academic treatments of the male-gaze visual grammar of the 1980s).
- **Canonical version used:** Per ticket instruction, Weird Science (1985) film. Lisa is portrayed as an adult woman in approximately her mid-20s (Kelly LeBrock was 25 at filming). Her "age" within canon is technically zero (she has just been created via lightning-strike-on-Barbie-doll-circuitry-with-Albert-Einstein's-brain-scanned-from-a-newsreel) but she is coded as fully adult and presents as 20–25 throughout. I treat her as 25 for base-population purposes, in the standard 20–28 male-fantasy projection window.

## Personality / Characterization (Excluded from Calc)

- **Constructed-by-protagonists / wish-fulfillment ontology:** Lisa's defining feature is that the protagonists *literally specified her into existence* via a computer using photos of fashion models, Barbie-doll circuitry, and a scan of Albert Einstein's brain. This makes her a *meta-textual* fantasy object — she is the male-fantasy stack made flesh, with the protagonists as her authors. Methodologically this matters: the joint probability calc below treats Lisa as if she were a real human matching her observable traits, but the film's premise is that she is *not* a possible real human. She is a wish granted by a computer. The probability number understates the meta-textual fantastical level because the film acknowledges the impossibility within its own diegesis. This is the most important characterization beat for the project — Lisa is the manifesto-character of the male-fantasy-stack hypothesis. (See Notes / Edge Cases for further treatment.)
- **Magical / supernatural powers:** Lisa can rewrite reality — she creates a Porsche from thin air, blows out walls of the house, manipulates time/parents' memories, transforms Wyatt's brother Chet into a blob and back. Plot-functional capability, not a universal-desirable slider trait.
- **Mentor-figure / matures-the-boys arc:** Lisa's narrative function is to teach Gary and Wyatt confidence with women, not to be their girlfriend. She departs at the film's end, leaving them paired with their own age-appropriate love interests (Deb and Hilly). This is a *characterization beat* that pushes Lisa toward the "fairy godmother" / "guardian-mentor" role rather than the "romantic partner" role. Some viewers find the mentor framing attractive; preferences split. Excluded from calc.
- **English accent / cosmopolitan-sophisticate styling:** LeBrock's actual British accent gives Lisa a coded sophistication that is genre-typical for 1980s "exotic adult woman" male-fantasy figures (compare Linda Barrett, the bond girls of the era). Characterization, not a universal slider — the male audience does not stack "British accent" as a universal desirable in the way they stack "attractive."
- **Openly sexual / openly nurturing duality:** Lisa simultaneously codes as sexual-temptress AND maternal-guide. This duality (sex object + mentor + protector) is the load-bearing characterization of the construct, and it is both psychologically interesting (the mother/madonna conflation) and structurally specific to wish-fulfillment fantasies. Personality-coded, not a slider.
- **No discernible personal history, taste, or preferences of her own:** Lisa never expresses a personal preference that conflicts with the boys' wishes; never describes a past, family, hobbies, or future plans of her own. Her "no needs of her own" framing is not a Big-5 measurable trait but a structural feature of her being a wish-fulfillment construct. This is captured definitionally by the Cool Girl Agreeableness slider but *operates beyond* what the survey-norm cutoff measures. Flagged here for completeness.

  (≈ 1 in 61,000, applied within a base population of US women aged 20–28)
- **Correlation adjustment:** Mixed-direction, with the dominant net effect being POSITIVE halo correlations (naive product overstates rarity):
  - Top-quintile attractiveness ↔ BMI 18.5–22: positive (slim figures rate higher; r ≈ 0.20–0.30). OVERSTATES rarity by ~1.2–1.5x.
  - Top-quintile attractiveness ↔ low neuroticism: positive halo (r ≈ 0.10–0.15). OVERSTATES by ~1.2x.
  - Top-quintile attractiveness ↔ Cool Girl Agreeableness: positive halo (r ≈ 0.10–0.15). OVERSTATES by ~1.2x.
  - Top-quintile attractiveness ↔ high SOI: weak positive in mate-market data (more attractive women report more short-term opportunities). OVERSTATES by ~1.2–1.3x.
  - Cool Girl Agreeableness ↔ low neuroticism: positive (both load on stability/positive-affect factor; r ≈ 0.20–0.25). OVERSTATES by ~1.2–1.3x.
  - No-kids ↔ currently single: positive (~1.2x OVERSTATES).
  - Top-quintile attractiveness ↔ currently single: NEGATIVE in real world (more attractive women more likely partnered). UNDERSTATES rarity by ~1.2–1.4x.
  - Top-quintile attractiveness ↔ doesn't-follow-girl-culture: NEGATIVE in real world (Mean-Girls / popular-girl framing maps onto high mainstream-girl-culture engagement). UNDERSTATES rarity by ~1.3–1.5x — and the Lisa stack DELIBERATELY violates this real-world correlation (the male-fantasy combination of "top-tier hot AND cosmopolitan-above-mainstream-girl-culture").
  - Cool Girl Agreeableness ↔ high SOI: weakly NEGATIVE in some samples (agreeable women lean serial-monogamous). UNDERSTATES rarity by ~1.2x.
  - **Net direction:** Same general structural pattern as Black Widow (ticket 010). The positive halo cluster (attractiveness↔low-N, attractiveness↔Cool-Girl, low-N↔Cool-Girl, attractiveness↔BMI, attractiveness↔SOI, no-kids↔single) collectively pushes toward "less rare than naive product." The negative correlations (attractiveness↔single, attractiveness↔not-girly, Cool-Girl↔SOI) push toward "more rare than naive product." For Lisa specifically, the deliberate wish-fulfillment construction violates the real-world negative correlations (top-tier hot AND cosmopolitan-not-girly AND simultaneously agreeable-Cool-Girl AND high-SOI is itself a contradiction stack — agreeable women rarely score high-SOI in survey data, attractive women are rarely single, etc.). The net effect of the correlation adjustments is approximately balanced, with a slight lean toward "rarer than naive product" because the contradictory pairs (Cool-Girl + SOI; attractiveness + not-girly + single) are central to her construction. Net: roughly 1.0–1.3x rarer than the naive product. Adjustment: ~1.2x.
- **Adjusted estimate:** ~2.0e-5 (≈ 1 in 50,000 women aged 20–28). Bracket: [8e-6, 5e-5].
- **Population implication:** Out of ~14M US women aged 20–28, roughly **280** match Lisa's universal-desirable stack (range 110–700 across the bracket). This places Lisa in the **moderate-to-rare** band of the male-target distribution — substantially less rare than Mikaela Banes (ticket 008, ~24 matching women, driven by the additional car-expertise filter at 2%), comparable to Black Widow (ticket 010, ~1,190 matching women on a 7-trait stack), and substantially rarer than Princess Leia and Rachel Green (4-trait stacks at ~175,000 matching women each). Lisa's stack adds Cool Girl + low neuroticism + high SOI + doesn't-follow-girl-culture on top of the floor of attractive/slim/single/no-kids — four extra sliders all loaded toward the male-fantasy compliance axis. **The numerical answer here is the most informative when paired with the meta-textual point**: Lisa's measurable trait stack lands in the moderate-to-rare band, but her *characterization* is the platonic male-fantasy stack made literal — she is the manifesto character for the project hypothesis. The fact that even a deliberately-engineered wish-fulfillment construct lands at "1 in 50,000" rather than "1 in a billion" is itself a finding: the universal-desirable trait list captures only a portion of what makes Lisa Lisa. The real fantasy-stack for this character is in the *characterization* (constructed for total alignment, mentor-mother-temptress duality, no needs of her own) — which the calc explicitly excludes. This is consistent with the project's Princess Leia / Rachel Green pattern (male-target heartthrobs are made by ICONIC SPECIFIC IMAGERY and CHARACTERIZATION, not by maxing universal sliders) — but Lisa is the strongest example yet, because she is the male-fantasy stack *as the explicit conceit of the film*.

## Notes / Edge Cases

- **Lisa is the manifesto character for the project hypothesis.** This is the single most important point for this profile. Lisa is *literally constructed by the male protagonists* using a computer, photos of fashion models, Barbie-doll components, and a scanned brain of Albert Einstein. She is the male-fantasy stack made flesh, with the protagonists as her authors. The calc treats her as if she were a real human matching her observable traits — but the film's premise is that she is *not* a possible real human. She is a wish granted by a computer. The numerical joint probability (~2e-5, ~1 in 50,000) understates the meta-textual fantastical level by orders of magnitude, because the film *acknowledges within its own diegesis* that she is impossible. For the graph and final write-up, Lisa should be flagged as the meta-textual reference character for the male-fantasy stack — the character whose existence the project is, in a sense, about. (Compare to Christian Grey on the female-target side: Christian Grey is the manifesto character for the female-target slider-max stack, but the film/book treats him as a real-if-extremely-improbable human; Lisa goes one further and openly admits the impossibility.)
- **Why not include "aligned interest profile" as a filter?** Lisa's *total* alignment with the protagonists is the conceit of her construction, but her alignment is not a niche-specific hobby filter (no car expertise, no specific media interest, no programming skill). She is generically aligned — she enables their wishes rather than sharing their specific hobbies. This is structurally different from Mikaela Banes (deep car expertise, an explicit niche filter) and is closer to Black Widow / Wanda Maximoff (excluded for non-specific alignment). I have followed the established precedent and excluded the niche-hobby filter. The total-alignment beat is captured in the personality / characterization section as "constructed-by-protagonists / wish-fulfillment ontology." A reviewer who insists on including a generic "total alignment with protagonist's interests" filter at, say, 0.05 would push the joint to ~1e-6 (~1 in 1 million) — meaningfully rarer but still nowhere near the female-target slider-max heartthrobs (Christian Grey, Edward Cullen at ~1e-9 to 1e-11 territory).
- **Cool Girl coding 27 years before Gone Girl named the trope.** Lisa in 1985 is one of the platonic Cool Girl precursors — uncomplaining, totally aligned, no needs of her own, openly sexual but also nurturing-mentor, never an obstacle. Gillian Flynn's Gone Girl (2012) crystallized the term, but the trope itself is structurally present in Lisa, and the project's top-decile Agreeableness filter captures part of it. The other part of the Cool Girl coding (high-SOI, no-needs-of-her-own) is captured by the high-SOI filter and the characterization-only "no needs of her own" beat. Even with two slider filters, the Cool Girl coding in Lisa is so extreme that the calc understates her — she is the *limit case* of the Cool Girl construct.
- **Ontological age vs. presented age.** Lisa is canonically zero years old (she is created during the film's runtime). She presents as an adult woman in her mid-20s; LeBrock was 25 at filming. The ticket plan's standard 20–28 base population is appropriate because (a) she is coded as an adult, (b) the male-fantasy projection in the film treats her as a fully adult woman, and (c) her ontological age is incompatible with the trait-stack methodology (a zero-year-old could not have any of the trait stack — the methodology requires treating her as an adult). This is a minor caveat; the standard treatment is the only sensible one.
- **The "fairy godmother" arc and its narrative function.** Lisa departs at the film's end, leaving Gary and Wyatt paired with their own age-appropriate love interests (Deb and Hilly). Her arc is closer to the *guardian-mentor* role than to the *romantic-partner* role. The character is uncoupled with the protagonists across the film's runtime; her sexual propositioning is rebuffed by the boys themselves (they are too nervous). For the project's purposes, she still profiles cleanly as a male-fantasy heartthrob — she is the character on whom the male audience's fantasy-projection is openly directed by the film's camera and dialogue, regardless of the protagonist-pairing endgame. The narrative function is a characterization beat, not a slider.
- **Comparison to Princess Leia (Slave Leia, ticket 004).** Both are 1980s male-target heartthrobs. Leia is carried by ICONIC IMAGERY (the metal bikini) on a 4-trait stack; Lisa is carried by THE METATEXTUAL CONSTRUCTION CONCEIT on an 8-trait stack. Numerically Lisa is roughly 100x rarer than Leia (~2e-5 vs. ~1.3e-2) because she stacks four extra sliders — but qualitatively, both characters illustrate the project's recurring finding that male-target heartthrobs derive their cultural weight from characterization and imagery rather than from universal-desirable trait stacking alone. For Lisa specifically, the meta-textual construction conceit is the load-bearing characterization beat that the calc cannot capture.
- **Actress-vs-character conflation:** As with Mikaela / Megan Fox and Rachel / Jennifer Aniston, much of Lisa's cultural reach is actually Kelly LeBrock's reach (her career-wide iconicity in 1980s male-targeted cinema, "Woman in Red" + Weird Science + retrospective coverage). For the trait-stack purposes the calc is on the character; the reach annotation conflates partly with the actress. Not adjusted.

The sitcom adaptation re-cast Lisa (played by Vanessa Angel) and re-tooled the premise as a buddy-comedy — a substantial departure from the film's wish-fulfillment-construct conceit. The film's Lisa is the canonical fantasy-object for the project.
