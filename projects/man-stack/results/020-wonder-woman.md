# Diana Prince / Wonder Woman

## Character Summary
- **Source work:** DC Extended Universe — Batman v Superman: Dawn of Justice (2016), Wonder Woman (2017), Justice League (2017), Wonder Woman 1984 (2020), Zack Snyder's Justice League (2021). Portrayed by Gal Gadot. Per the ticket, the canonical reference is the DCEU film run, anchored on Wonder Woman (2017) as the breakout/origin film and Wonder Woman 1984 (2020) as the explicit decade-period sequel.
- **Audience:** male-targeted
- **Decade slot:** 2010s
- **Cultural reach proxy:** Wonder Woman (2017) grossed ~$822M worldwide on a ~$150M budget — at release, the highest-grossing live-action film directed by a woman (Patty Jenkins) in cinema history, a milestone widely cited as a tipping point for big-budget female-led superhero films. Batman v Superman (2016) grossed ~$874M, Justice League (2017) ~$657M, Wonder Woman 1984 (2020) ~$169M (compressed by pandemic-era release with same-day HBO Max launch). Gal Gadot was named Maxim's "Hot 100" #1 in 2018 and has appeared on Time 100, Vanity Fair Hollywood Issue, and multiple "sexiest" rankings throughout the late 2010s. The "No Man's Land" scene from Wonder Woman (2017) is consistently cited in retrospectives of 2010s superhero cinema as one of the decade's defining cinematic moments. Wonder Woman the character is a top-tier cosplay subject, with the iconic gold-and-red armor / lasso / tiara silhouette being the most recognizable female superhero visual of the 2010s.
- **Canonical version used:** DCEU Earth-Prime (later Snyderverse) across 2016–2021. Per ticket, "demigod — fantasy framing" with physical traits / combat training / age-window-but-actually-immortal mapped explicitly. Diana is canonically a daughter of Zeus and Hippolyta, an immortal Amazon trained on Themyscira, and depending on canonical layer is either ~5,000+ years old or has spent millennia on Themyscira before entering the World of Men in 1918 (Wonder Woman 2017 origin). Across all DCEU appearances she presents as a young adult woman; Gal Gadot was 30–35 across the role's run. The fantasy-framing note from the ticket means the immortal age and demigod heritage are characterization (excluded from calc) while the visible-on-screen traits (physical, body composition, single-status, etc.) drive the universal-desirable assessment.

## Joint Probability

- **Method:** filter (male-target stack)
- **Naive product of marginals:**
  0.20 × 0.22 × 0.34 × 0.70 × 0.10 × 0.25 × 0.25
  = (0.20 × 0.22) = 0.044
  × 0.34 = 0.01496
  × 0.70 = 0.01047
  × 0.10 = 1.047e-3
  × 0.25 = 2.618e-4
  × 0.25 = **6.55e-5**
  (≈ 1 in 15,300, applied within a base population of US women aged 20–28)
- **Correlation adjustment:** Mixed-direction adjustments, with the dominant effect being the male-fantasy violation of real-world negative correlations between conventional-attractiveness and tomboy-coded interest profiles. Same structural pattern as Black Widow (ticket 010):
  - Top-quintile attractiveness ↔ BMI 18.5–22: positive (slim figures rate higher; r ≈ 0.20–0.30). Naive product OVERSTATES rarity by ~1.2–1.5x for this pair.
  - Top-quintile attractiveness ↔ low neuroticism: positive halo (r ≈ 0.10–0.15). OVERSTATES by ~1.2x.
  - Top-quintile attractiveness ↔ Cool Girl Agreeableness: positive halo (r ≈ 0.10–0.15). OVERSTATES by ~1.2x.
  - Cool Girl Agreeableness ↔ low neuroticism: positive (both load on stability/positive-affect factor; r ≈ 0.20–0.25). OVERSTATES by ~1.2–1.3x.
  - No-kids ↔ currently single: positive (~1.2x OVERSTATES).
  - Top-quintile attractiveness ↔ currently single: NEGATIVE in real world (more attractive women more likely partnered). Naive product UNDERSTATES rarity by ~1.2–1.4x.
  - Top-quintile attractiveness ↔ doesn't-follow-girl-culture: NEGATIVE in real world (Mean-Girls / popular-girl framing maps onto high mainstream-girl-culture engagement). Naive product UNDERSTATES rarity by ~1.3–1.5x — and the Wonder Woman stack DELIBERATELY violates this real-world correlation (the male-fantasy "top-tier hot AND not-into-mainstream-girly-stuff AND classical-warrior-coded" combination is the same genre-violation pattern as Black Widow's "hot AND not-girly").
  - **Net direction:** The positive halos (attractiveness↔low-N, attractiveness↔Agreeableness, low-N↔Agreeableness, no-kids↔single, attractiveness↔BMI) push toward "less rare than naive product." The negative correlations (attractiveness↔single, attractiveness↔not-girly) push toward "more rare than naive product." For the FICTIONAL Wonder Woman stack as written, the negative-correlation effect modestly DOMINATES because the warrior-princess archetype is constructed precisely on the violation of real-world tradeoffs between conventional attractiveness and tomboy-coded / classical-warrior-coded interest profiles. Net: roughly **1.3x rarer** than the naive product.
- **Adjusted estimate:** **~8.5e-5** (≈ 1 in 11,800 women aged 20–28). Bracket: [4e-5, 2e-4].
- **Population implication:** Out of ~14M US women aged 20–28, roughly **1,190** match Diana Prince's universal-desirable stack (range 700–3,500 across the bracket). This places Wonder Woman in the **moderate-to-rare** band of the male-target distribution, structurally identical to Black Widow (ticket 010, ~1,190 matching women) — both characters clear exactly the same 7-filter stack (attractiveness, BMI, single, no-kids, Cool Girl, low-N, not-girly). This is a meaningful within-decade signal: the 2010s super-spy / superhero / warrior-princess archetypes converge on a common universal-desirable trait stack, distinguished by their characterization (espionage vs. mythology vs. magic) rather than by their universal-slider profile. Wonder Woman is structurally a "Black Widow with sword and tiara instead of catsuit and pistols" on this metric — both are Cool Girl + low-N + tomboy-coded super-women, both are 7-filter stacks, both come out at ~8.5e-5. Notably this is substantially less rare than Mikaela Banes (ticket 008, ~24 matching women, driven by the additional car-expertise filter at 2%) but substantially rarer than Princess Leia and Rachel Green (4-trait stacks at ~1.3e-2, ~175,000 matching women each). The Cool Girl + low neuroticism + non-mainstream-girly trio is the load-bearing increment, and it is the structural fingerprint of the 2010s super-women male-fantasy template.

## Notes / Edge Cases

- **"Physically dominant + still feminine" — the ticket-flagged combo and methodological asymmetry:** The ticket explicitly notes that the male-fantasy appeal of Wonder Woman includes "physically dominant + still feminine" and asks the agent to consider that combo's joint probability. In the dataset's male-target methodology, this combo is *implicitly* handled — top-quintile attractiveness captures the "feminine" axis, and the male-fantasy stack does not separately quantify physical dominance / combat capability (per plan, those traits live on the FEMALE-target stack only). If the male-target trait list MIRRORED the female-target list (counting expert-combatant status, top-quintile fitness, multilingualism, etc.), Wonder Woman would stack 4–6 additional rarity multipliers and her joint would collapse by 4–6 orders of magnitude — putting her in or below the Christian Grey / Edward Cullen band. The plan explicitly does NOT mirror trait lists between genders, on the grounds that male-target media doesn't typically *stack* skills onto female love interests as universal-desirable sliders the way female-target media stacks them onto male leads. For Wonder Woman specifically, this asymmetry is testable: her warrior capability IS part of her appeal, but the question is whether the median male audience reads it as universal-desirable (slider up = better) or as characterization (a feature of her specific archetype, not a trait the audience would universally want stacked on every female love interest). The plan's call is the latter, and Wonder Woman's "physically dominant + still feminine" iconicity is best read as the *characterization* of the warrior-princess archetype rather than a separately stackable universal slider. **Same methodological asymmetry note as Black Widow ticket 010 — flagged here for final-graph annotations.** A symmetric-trait-list calculation would migrate Wonder Woman substantially leftward (rarer) on the log-probability axis. Either methodological choice is defensible; the project chose the asymmetric one because it captures the *axes* on which each gender's media actually stacks.

- **Immortality and the age-window filter:** Diana is canonically thousands of years old in the DCEU — but presents as a young adult woman across the run. Per ticket note ("age-window-but-actually-immortal"), the canonical age is fantasy framing and excluded from the calc; the joint is computed against the 20–28 base population per the dataset convention used for Black Widow (whose canonical run drifts above 28) and Wanda Maximoff (canonically ~33–34 in the 2020s). A reviewer who strictly enforced canonical age would either reject the character (immortal does not fit any age window) or treat her as the visual-presentation age. The dataset convention is the latter; the joint estimate is robust to either reasonable choice within an order-of-magnitude bracket.

- **Steve Trevor's death and the single-status interpretation:** Steve Trevor dies at the end of Wonder Woman (2017) in 1918. Across the contemporary-set DCEU films (BvS 2016, Justice League 2017/2021, parts of WW84 2020), Diana has been a long-grieving single woman for ~98–100 years. WW84 (2020) revisits Steve via a Dreamstone wish that brings him back briefly, but the film resolves with Diana renouncing the wish and Steve returning to death — the canonical end-state is single. The single-status interpretation is unambiguous and consistent across canon. A reviewer who counted the Dreamstone-arc Steve as canonical would zero out the single filter for the 2020 portion of WW84 specifically, but the overall canonical run is overwhelmingly single. Same single-default convention used for Black Widow.

- **DCEU / Snyderverse continuity caveats:** The DCEU went through significant continuity revision during the 2017–2021 run (theatrical Justice League 2017 vs. Zack Snyder's Justice League 2021, with major character-arc differences for some of the male leads). For Diana specifically, the two versions of Justice League are largely consistent on character traits — both portray her as the same Cool Girl + warrior-princess + Steve-grieving archetype. The trait stack is stable across the continuity revision.

- **Gal Gadot conflation:** Same caveat as Megan Fox / Mikaela, Jennifer Aniston / Rachel, Scarlett Johansson / Black Widow, Elizabeth Olsen / Wanda. A meaningful share of Wonder Woman's "cultural reach" is partly Gal Gadot's career-wide reach (Miss Israel 2004, Fast & Furious franchise, Death on the Nile, Heart of Stone). For trait-stack purposes, the calc is on the character — the reach annotation conflates somewhat with the actress.

- **Comparison to Black Widow (ticket 010) and other 2010s heartthrobs:** Wonder Woman's adjusted joint (~8.5e-5) is structurally *identical* to Black Widow's (~8.5e-5), because both characters clear the same 7-filter stack (attractiveness, BMI, single, no-kids, Cool Girl, low-N, not-girly). This is a **strong within-decade convergence signal**: the 2010s super-women male-fantasy template (whether espionage-coded or mythology-coded) lands on the same universal-desirable trait set, with characterization (catsuit-and-spy-craft vs. tiara-and-mythic-warrior) doing the differentiating work. Both characters are substantially rarer than Princess Leia (1980s, ~5-trait stack) and Rachel Green (1990s, ~4-trait stack) by virtue of stacking Cool Girl + low neuroticism + non-mainstream-girly on top of the visual-and-availability base. This convergence is a useful finding for the final-graph: the 2010s "stacks more universal sliders" signal is consistent across the decade's super-women, and Wonder Woman + Black Widow are essentially structural twins on this metric. The 2010s decade signal: the male-fantasy stack tightens meaningfully relative to simpler 1990s templates by adding the Cool-Girl-coded personality cluster on top of the visual-and-availability base.

- **Comparison to Wanda Maximoff (ticket 012) — the most informative within-genre contrast:** Wanda is structurally distinct from Wonder Woman on this metric, despite being from the same broader genre (superhero / female-coded magic-user). Wanda clears 5 filters (NOT Cool Girl, NOT low neuroticism); Wonder Woman clears 7. Wanda's joint is ~3.1e-3 (~1 in 320), Wonder Woman's is ~8.5e-5 (~1 in 11,800) — Wonder Woman is ~37x rarer on the universal-desirable axis. The structural difference is exactly what the methodology is designed to capture: Wanda's appeal is *personality-heavy* (tragic-widow + witch + maternal-grief) and *under*-loaded on universal sliders, while Wonder Woman's appeal is *universal-slider-heavy* (Cool Girl + low-N + warrior-feminine) and less personality-loaded. Both characters are culturally enormous; the methodology correctly differentiates the *type* of appeal rather than collapsing both into a single "this character is hot" metric. This is a useful triangulation point alongside Black Widow.
